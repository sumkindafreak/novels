# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Five — Wrong

“Wrong.”

Jamie stopped turning the screwdriver.

He looked over his shoulder.

Evelyn stood in the doorway of the morning room with both hands resting on the silver top of her walking stick.

“What’s wrong?”

“The hinge.”

“What about it?”

“It is wrong.”

Jamie stared at her.

“That is not information.”

“It is perfectly adequate information.”

“No, it isn't. Is it too high? Too low? Bent? Am I using the wrong screw?”

“Yes.”

“To which one?”

Evelyn considered.

“Several.”

Jamie dropped his head against the doorframe.

It was his third Saturday at Ashcroft House.

He had been there for fifty-one minutes.

He was already considering escape.

The door between the morning room and the hall had refused to close properly for years. According to Izzy, everyone dealt with this by kicking the bottom corner until the latch caught.

Evelyn had decided Jamie could fix it.

Jamie had pointed out that he had never fixed a door.

Evelyn had replied that there was presumably a first occasion for everything.

He had removed the bottom hinge, packed behind it with a thin piece of timber his father had cut for him, and was now attempting to put the door back together under what he considered unnecessary levels of surveillance.

Evelyn tapped the hinge with one finger.

“You have placed the screws back into the damaged holes.”

“That’s where they came from.”

“Precisely why they are damaged.”

Jamie looked closer.

The screw turned loosely beneath his fingers.

“Oh.”

“Yes. Oh.”

“So what do I do?”

Evelyn shrugged.

“You are the repairman.”

“I’m fifteen.”

“I had noticed.”

Jamie reached for his phone.

Evelyn's eyebrow rose.

“I’m ringing Dad.”

“I see.”

“You told me not to do anything I don't know how to do.”

“That seems unusually sensible.”

“It was Dad.”

“Ah.”

Jamie called.

Gareth answered on the fourth ring.

“What’ve you broken?”

“Nothing.”

A pause.

“That sounded suspiciously quick.”

“I’m fixing a hinge.”

“Are you?”

“Trying to.”

“What’s the problem?”

Jamie explained.

Gareth told him to wait.

Twenty minutes later, his father's van appeared through the gates.

Jamie met him outside.

“You came all the way up here for a hinge?”

“You called.”

“Yeah, but I thought you'd tell me what to do.”

Gareth opened the back of the van.

“I did. Wait.”

He removed a small box of wooden dowels, glue and a drill.

Jamie followed him inside.

Evelyn was exactly where he had left her.

“Mr Morgan.”

“Evelyn.”

Jamie looked between them.

“Still weird.”

Neither paid attention.

Gareth crouched beside the hinge.

“Right. See these?”

He pointed at the worn holes.

Jamie nodded.

“You can’t tighten into nothing. So we give the screw something solid to bite into again.”

He showed Jamie how to clean the damaged holes, glue in small pieces of hardwood and trim them flush once set.

Jamie watched closely.

“You could use matchsticks for a tiny repair,” Gareth said, “but this door weighs about as much as your grandmother’s Renault, so we’re doing it properly.”

“My grandmother hasn't got a Renault.”

“You know what I mean.”

From behind them Evelyn said, “My first car was a Renault.”

Gareth looked around.

“What model?”

“A Dauphine.”

“Really?”

“Unfortunately.”

“Those were terrible.”

“Appalling.”

Jamie stared at both of them.

“You two are going to become friends, aren't you?”

“No,” they said together.

Jamie sighed.

---

Once the glue had set, Gareth made Jamie rehang the door himself.

The first time it scraped against the floor.

“Wrong,” Evelyn said.

Jamie glared at her.

The second time it closed but the latch missed the strike plate.

“Wrong.”

“Thank you.”

The third time Jamie lifted the handle, pushed gently and the door swung shut with a soft, perfect click.

He froze.

Then opened it.

Closed it again.

Click.

Jamie grinned.

“Look at that.”

Gareth nodded.

“Nice.”

Jamie opened it a third time.

“Actual door.”

“It was an actual door before,” Evelyn said.

“It was a wall you kicked.”

“Language.”

“You told me Izzy kicked it.”

“Izzy has many defects.”

From upstairs came a distant shout.

“I HEARD THAT.”

Evelyn raised her voice without looking up.

“GOOD.”

Gareth laughed.

Jamie closed the door again.

That little click did something strange to him.

It was stupid.

He knew that.

Nobody should feel proud of a door closing.

Doors had been closing successfully for thousands of years.

But an hour ago it hadn't.

Now it did.

Because of him.

Gareth must have recognised the expression on his face.

“Good feeling, isn’t it?”

Jamie shrugged immediately.

“It’s all right.”

His father smiled.

“Yeah.”

Jamie opened the door again.

“Could probably do the one in our bathroom.”

“You could definitely do the one in our bathroom.”

“That sounds like unpaid work.”

Gareth packed away the drill.

“You've discovered home ownership.”

---

Gareth left just before lunch.

Jamie returned the tools to the kitchen and found Evelyn making sandwiches.

He stopped in the doorway.

“You cook?”

She didn't turn around.

“I have survived eighty-one years without starving.”

“I thought Izzy did it.”

“Isabella once put a frozen pizza under the grill with the plastic wrapping intact.”

Jamie laughed.

“I was fourteen!” Izzy shouted from somewhere nearby.

“That improves nothing.”

Evelyn placed three plates on the table.

Jamie sat.

“What did you used to do?” he asked.

Evelyn continued slicing cheese.

“For a living.”

“I understood the question.”

“You said that thing last week. About thirty-five years.”

“I said many things last week.”

“You said you spent thirty-five years hearing people promise not to tell anyone.”

Evelyn put the knife down.

Jamie waited.

Izzy entered the kitchen and immediately noticed the atmosphere.

“Oh, good. He asked.”

“Asked what?”

“What Gran did.”

Jamie pointed at her.

“You know.”

“Bits.”

“What bits?”

“Classified bits.”

Evelyn sighed.

“Neither of you understands what classified means.”

Izzy stole a piece of cheese from the chopping board.

“You worked for the government.”

Jamie looked at Evelyn.

“What sort of government?”

“The British one, generally.”

“Doing what?”

“Work.”

“What kind of work?”

“Government work.”

“That means nothing.”

“It has meant very little on several occasions.”

Izzy laughed.

Jamie leaned across the table.

“Were you police?”

“No.”

“Army?”

“No.”

“Politician?”

Evelyn looked offended.

“Certainly not.”

Jamie tried again.

“Civil servant?”

A pause.

“Technically.”

Izzy's grin widened.

Jamie noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You're both annoying.”

Evelyn placed a sandwich in front of him.

“You came here to repair a door, not interrogate me.”

Jamie picked it up.

“Did people interrogate you?”

Evelyn stared at him.

Jamie smiled.

For several seconds nobody moved.

Then Evelyn sat opposite him.

“You are improving.”

“At doors?”

“At asking better questions.”

Jamie took a bite.

“What’s the answer?”

“No.”

He groaned.

Izzy laughed so hard she nearly choked on her sandwich.

---

At one thirty Jamie put his coat on.

He was only meant to work mornings.

Evelyn inspected the door once more.

“It closes.”

Jamie waited.

“That’s your review?”

“Yes.”

“Not ‘good job’?”

“It is a door. It closes. That is the job.”

Jamie shook his head and walked towards the front entrance.

He had one shoe on when Evelyn spoke behind him.

“Jamie.”

He turned.

She was looking at the repaired door.

“Not dreadful.”

Jamie stared at her.

Then smiled.

“Careful. You'll spoil me.”

He opened the front door.

“Ten o’clock next Saturday,” she said.

Jamie stepped outside.

For the first time, he didn't mind.
