# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-Six — The House

Jamie looked at the locked study door.

Then at Izzy.

“Give me the key.”

She held it inside her closed fist.

“No.”

“I’m not going to fight you.”

“Then stay.”

“For what?”

“Until we work this out.”

“There is nothing to work out.”

“You don't know that.”

“I know you pushed your grandmother down the stairs.”

Izzy flinched.

Jamie lifted his phone.

“And I know I'm calling the police.”

Her eyes dropped to it.

Jamie saw the decision happen.

Evelyn had taught him to notice that too.

A person's face often changed before they moved.

Izzy lunged.

Jamie stepped backwards.

She grabbed his wrist.

“Give me the phone.”

“No.”

“Jamie!”

He twisted away.

The phone slipped from his fingers, hit the edge of the desk and landed face down on the wooden floor.

The screen cracked with a sharp snap.

Both of them looked at it.

Jamie moved first.

He grabbed for the key in Izzy's hand.

She pulled away.

He caught her fingers.

For several seconds they struggled silently.

Not like a fight on television.

No punches.

No dramatic moves.

Just two frightened teenagers wrestling beside a desk while papers slid to the floor.

Izzy scratched his hand.

Jamie swore.

The key dropped.

He kicked it away from her.

It skittered beneath a chair.

Izzy shoved him.

Jamie crashed against the cabinet.

The hidden panel swung open.

The old Minox camera fell from the desk and hit the carpet.

“Stop!” Jamie shouted.

Izzy froze.

Both of them were breathing hard.

Jamie stared at her.

“This is insane.”

“I know.”

“Then let me go.”

“I can't.”

“Yes, you can.”

“You'll tell them.”

“Yes.”

Izzy started crying again.

Jamie hated how familiar that face was.

How badly he wanted to make the crying stop.

He hated himself for still caring.

“Iz,” he said quietly. “It’s done.”

“No.”

“It is.”

“No.”

“You can't undo it by doing something worse.”

Her eyes moved towards his phone.

Jamie followed them.

Then towards the key under the chair.

He moved.

So did she.

Jamie reached it first.

He rolled onto one knee, grabbed the key and shoved it into the lock.

Izzy caught the back of his hoodie.

He turned the key.

The door opened.

Jamie pulled forward.

The hood tightened around his throat.

He yanked his arms backwards out of the sleeves and left the hoodie in Izzy's hands.

Then he ran.

---

The entrance hall opened in front of him.

The front door was ten metres away.

Jamie sprinted.

He reached the first lock.

Behind him, the study door slammed against the wall.

“Jamie!”

He turned one lock.

Second.

The third stuck.

It always stuck in cold weather.

Evelyn had complained about it.

Jamie had meant to repair it.

He never had.

His fingers slipped on the brass.

Izzy grabbed him from behind.

Jamie spun.

“Get off!”

She pulled at his arm.

“Just stop!”

“You stop!”

He pushed her away.

Izzy stumbled into the hall table.

A vase tipped and smashed on the floor.

Jamie turned back to the lock.

Izzy grabbed his shirt.

He lost his balance.

They fell against the bottom of the staircase.

Jamie struck his shoulder on the banister.

Pain shot down his arm.

He shoved himself upright and ran up the stairs.

It was the worst possible direction.

He knew that immediately.

The front door was escape.

Upstairs was the house.

But Izzy was between him and the door now.

So Jamie climbed.

Past the fifth step.

Past the sixth.

Past the section of runner where the brass rod was missing.

He refused to look down.

Izzy followed.

“Jamie, wait.”

He reached the first-floor landing beneath Catherine's window.

Coloured afternoon light covered the floor.

Red.

Blue.

Amber.

Jamie turned towards the servants' corridor.

Izzy caught him.

Her hand closed around his arm.

The bruise on Evelyn's arm flashed through his mind.

Jamie pulled free.

Izzy pushed him.

Hard.

His heel struck the edge of the top stair.

For one terrifying second there was nothing behind him.

Jamie fell backwards.

His hand caught the banister.

Wood slammed into his ribs.

His body twisted over empty space.

Below him, the staircase dropped to the hall.

The exact staircase.

The exact place.

Jamie stared at Izzy.

She stared back.

Her face had gone white.

“You tried to push me down.”

“I—”

“You tried to kill me.”

“No.”

“You did.”

“I didn't mean—”

“Stop saying that!”

Jamie hauled himself upright.

Izzy stepped towards him.

He ran.

---

The servants' door was halfway down the corridor.

Jamie reached it and pulled.

It opened.

The narrow staircase fell steeply into darkness.

He took the steps two at a time.

Behind him, Izzy shouted his name.

She knew this staircase better than he did.

She had shown it to him.

But Jamie knew what had changed.

The sixth step had split in October.

He had repaired it with Gareth, but the third step from the bottom still dipped sharply on the right side.

Jamie kept left.

Behind him came a thud and a curse.

Izzy had forgotten.

That bought him three seconds.

Jamie burst into the morning room.

The door to the hall stood open.

He ran through it and slammed it behind him.

Click.

The latch caught perfectly.

The first door he had ever repaired in Ashcroft House.

Izzy hit it from the other side.

It held.

Jamie stared at it for half a second.

Then almost laughed.

“Not dreadful,” he whispered.

Izzy kicked the door.

The frame shuddered.

Jamie ran.

---

The front door was still locked.

He did not try again.

Instead he headed for the library.

One window faced the side garden.

The west window.

The one he had spent weeks repairing.

He reached it.

Turned the latch.

Nothing.

His stomach dropped.

Then he remembered.

Lift before turning.

Old frame.

He lifted the sash slightly, turned the latch and pushed.

The window opened smoothly.

Cold air hit his face.

Behind him, the morning-room door crashed open.

Jamie climbed onto the sill.

“Jamie!”

He looked back.

Izzy stood in the library doorway.

There was blood on one knee where she had hit the servant stair.

She looked terrified.

Not angry anymore.

Terrified.

“Don't,” she said.

Jamie looked at her.

“I loved you.”

“I love you.”

“That isn't enough.”

He climbed out.

---

The drop to the side garden was less than two metres.

Jamie landed badly, rolled onto wet grass and came up with pain shooting through his ankle.

He ran anyway.

Around the side of the house.

Past the conservatory.

Past the stone bench where Evelyn had warned him about confusing love with need.

The irony was almost unbearable.

Behind him, the conservatory door opened.

Izzy had taken the faster route.

Jamie heard her running.

He reached the front path.

The iron gates were twenty metres away.

“Jamie!”

He kept going.

“Please!”

He reached the gates and pulled one open.

A hand caught the back of his shirt.

Jamie spun.

Izzy grabbed both his arms.

“Just listen.”

“I did.”

“You'll ruin everything.”

“You already did.”

She tried to pull him back towards the house.

Jamie shoved her away.

Izzy lost her footing on the wet stone and fell against the gate pillar.

She cried out.

Jamie froze.

For one stupid second, he nearly went back to help her.

Then she stood.

And came towards him again.

Jamie ran into the lane.

A woman walking a spaniel stopped dead.

“Are you all right?”

Jamie nearly collided with her.

“Call the police.”

The woman stared.

“Please. Call the police.”

Izzy stopped inside the gates.

The woman looked from Jamie to her.

Then reached for her phone.

Izzy did not follow.

Jamie backed away until he reached the chapel wall.

His legs gave out.

He sat on the wet pavement.

The woman spoke rapidly into her phone.

Jamie looked at Ashcroft House.

Izzy stood behind the gates.

The same gates he had thrown a stone over months earlier.

She was crying.

Jamie was too.

Neither moved.

---

Police arrived in less than ten minutes.

Jamie barely remembered it.

Two cars.

Then an ambulance.

Then Detective Sergeant Morris.

Gareth arrived so quickly Jamie suspected he had broken several laws.

He ran from the van and pulled Jamie into his arms.

Jamie held onto him.

“I'm sorry.”

Gareth's voice shook with anger.

“Do not apologise to me.”

“I went alone.”

“I know.”

“You told me not to.”

“I know.”

“I’m sorry.”

Gareth held the back of his head.

“Just shut up for a minute.”

Jamie did.

Across the lane, officers entered Ashcroft House.

Izzy did not fight them.

She walked out between two officers with her hands cuffed in front of her.

She looked smaller than Jamie had ever seen her.

When she reached the police car, she turned.

Their eyes met.

Jamie could still see the girl in the conservatory.

The girl on the window sill.

The girl who had waved at him through the upstairs glass on the first night.

He could also see Evelyn at the bottom of the stairs.

He looked away.

The police car door closed.

Ashcroft House stood behind it.

The repaired morning-room door.

The servant staircase.

The library window.

Every piece of the house Jamie had learned, fixed or cursed had carried him towards the road.

He had once damaged Ashcroft House because it meant nothing to him.

Today, it had saved his life.
