# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Fifteen — The Argument

Jamie heard the argument before he reached the front door.

That alone was unusual.

Ashcroft House swallowed sound.

Rooms were too large, walls too thick, corridors too long. You could have a television playing in one part of the house and hear almost nothing two doors away.

But Evelyn's voice carried through the entrance hall and out beneath the old green door.

“No.”

Jamie stopped on the top step.

Izzy answered, louder.

“You haven't even listened.”

“I have listened repeatedly.”

“You've decided already.”

“Yes.”

Jamie stood with one hand raised towards the bell pull.

He had come after school to return a book Evelyn had lent him.

He considered leaving.

Then Izzy shouted, “It’s my life!”

Evelyn's answer was quieter.

“That does not make it my responsibility to finance every bad decision you wish to make.”

Jamie lowered his hand.

He should definitely leave.

Instead the door opened.

Izzy stood there.

Her eyes were wet.

Jamie froze.

She stared at him.

“Oh.”

“Sorry.”

“What are you doing here?”

Jamie held up the book.

“Returning this.”

Izzy wiped one eye angrily with her sleeve.

“Great timing.”

Behind her, Evelyn appeared at the bottom of the stairs.

Her expression was controlled.

Too controlled.

Jamie had learned what that meant.

“I can come back,” he said.

“No,” Evelyn said.

“Yes,” Izzy said at the same time.

Jamie looked between them.

“Excellent.”

Izzy grabbed her coat from the hall stand.

“I’m going out.”

Evelyn said, “We are not finished.”

“I am.”

“Isabella.”

“Don't.”

The word came out sharp enough to make Jamie flinch.

Izzy pushed past him and started down the steps.

Jamie looked at Evelyn.

She said nothing.

He hesitated, then followed Izzy.

---

He found her sitting on the low stone wall near the old chapel.

Cars moved below them on the main road.

The sky was already darkening even though it was barely four.

Izzy did not look at him when he sat beside her.

“You don't have to do the silence thing,” she said.

“I wasn't.”

“You were about to.”

Jamie smiled faintly.

“Fine.”

He waited anyway.

Izzy picked at loose mortar between the stones.

“She treats me like I'm twelve.”

Jamie looked towards Ashcroft House.

“What happened?”

“I asked her for money.”

“How much?”

Izzy looked at him.

“That’s not the point.”

Jamie thought about the number with six zeros.

“Probably is a little bit.”

She almost smiled.

“Five grand.”

Jamie blinked.

“Five thousand?”

“Yes, Jamie. Five thousand pounds, not five thousand buttons.”

“For what?”

“I want to go travelling after sixth form.”

“Oh.”

“Spain. France. Italy. Maybe Croatia. Just for a few months.”

“That sounds good.”

“She says I can do it if I save towards it and work this summer.”

Jamie frowned.

“That sounds…”

He stopped.

Izzy turned.

“Say it.”

“Reasonable?”

She stared at him.

Jamie held up both hands.

“Sorry.”

“You sound like her.”

“I definitely don't.”

“You do.”

“She has millions.”

“Exactly.”

Jamie understood that part.

“If she has millions, five grand isn't much.”

“That’s what I said.”

“But maybe she wants you to—”

“Earn it?”

Jamie shrugged.

Izzy laughed bitterly.

“Everybody loves that word when it's not their money.”

Jamie looked down at the road.

“She pays for everything already, doesn't she?”

Izzy's head snapped towards him.

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

“Nothing bad.”

“It sounded bad.”

“I just mean school and clothes and stuff.”

“Because my mother is dead.”

Jamie regretted the conversation immediately.

“I know.”

“No, you don't.”

“I know your mum died.”

“You don't know what it's like to have every single thing you need tied to one person deciding you deserve it.”

Jamie thought of Gareth counting money at the kitchen table when bills came in.

He thought of his mother forgetting to send birthday money and his dad saying it didn't matter.

He could not pretend he understood Izzy's life.

But he understood being dependent.

“Maybe she’s scared you'll waste it.”

Izzy stared at him.

Jamie winced.

“That came out wrong.”

“No. It came out honest.”

She stood.

“I thought you'd be on my side.”

“Why do I have to be on a side?”

“Because you're my boyfriend.”

The word caught him off guard.

They had kissed plenty since the storm.

Held hands.

Texted every day.

But neither had called it anything.

Not aloud.

Jamie stared up at her.

“Am I?”

Izzy's expression softened despite herself.

“Idiot.”

Jamie smiled.

The tension cracked slightly.

He stood beside her.

“I am on your side.”

“Doesn't sound like it.”

“I can think you're right about wanting something and still think five grand is loads of money.”

Izzy folded her arms.

“Gran could spend five grand and not notice.”

“That doesn't make it yours.”

The words escaped before Jamie could stop them.

Izzy went completely still.

He knew instantly he had found the centre of the argument.

Not Spain.

Not five thousand pounds.

Ownership.

Izzy looked towards the house.

“One day it is.”

Jamie said nothing.

She continued.

“Mum would have had everything. Then me.”

“Maybe.”

“What do you mean maybe?”

“I mean wills and stuff. People can do what they want.”

Izzy laughed once.

“Listen to you.”

“What?”

“Two months in that house and suddenly you're an expert on everything.”

“I didn't say that.”

“She loves you now.”

Jamie frowned.

“What?”

“Gran.”

“She doesn't love me.”

“Yes, she does.”

Izzy's voice cracked.

“And you love it.”

Jamie stared at her.

“What the hell is that supposed to mean?”

Izzy looked away.

For a moment Jamie thought she might cry again.

Then she wiped her face and shook her head.

“Nothing.”

“It clearly isn't nothing.”

“I said nothing.”

Jamie wanted to push.

Evelyn would have pushed.

Or perhaps Evelyn would have waited.

He wasn't sure.

So he took Izzy's hand.

She let him.

After a while she leaned against him.

“I just want one thing to be mine,” she said.

Jamie squeezed her fingers.

He thought she meant the trip.

He did not yet understand that she meant Ashcroft House.

All of it.
