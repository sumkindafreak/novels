# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-Two — The Cup

Jamie did not return to Ashcroft House for nine days.

Nine days was long enough for the place to become strange again.

The gates were closed when Gareth parked outside.

No police cars.

No ambulance.

No lights in the downstairs windows.

Just the house behind wet trees under a pale December sky.

Jamie sat in the van without moving.

“You don't have to,” Gareth said.

Jamie looked at him.

“Yeah, I do.”

“Your tools can wait.”

“It’s not the tools.”

Gareth nodded.

He knew.

Izzy had returned to the house the previous day.

She hated staying with friends.

She hated people asking whether she was all right.

She hated sleeping in rooms that did not belong to her.

Margaret Pryce had arranged practical things while the estate process began. Bills. Insurance. Security. A temporary allowance for Izzy.

Everyone used careful words around the future.

Nobody said inheritance.

Not yet.

Jamie pushed the gate open.

It gave its usual rusty complaint.

For one second he expected Evelyn's voice from the porch.

Nine fifty-nine.

Instead Izzy opened the door before they reached it.

She hugged Jamie.

Then Gareth.

She looked exhausted.

“Come in.”

Jamie crossed the threshold.

The house smelled exactly the same.

Old books.

Wood polish.

Damp stone.

That was unbearable.

Things were not supposed to smell the same after someone died.

The grandfather clock ticked.

Catherine's stained-glass window coloured the stairs.

The chair Evelyn sometimes used while supervising Jamie stood beside the hall table.

Her walking stick was gone.

Police had taken it.

Jamie looked towards the staircase.

He had tried not to picture it.

Now he could not look anywhere else.

The red runner climbed the centre of the stairs.

Near the top, one edge was loose.

A brass stair rod was missing.

Jamie frowned.

Gareth noticed.

“What?”

Jamie stepped closer.

The loose runner lifted slightly beneath his fingers.

Izzy stood behind him.

“Police think she caught her foot.”

Jamie looked up.

“On this?”

“Maybe.”

He crouched.

Three weeks earlier, he and Gareth had spent an hour dealing with this exact section.

One of the old brass rods had been loose. The bracket at one side pulled away from the wood.

Gareth had shown him how to repair the fixing without splitting the old stair.

Jamie remembered because Evelyn had stood at the bottom announcing that a competent carpenter could have finished in twenty minutes.

He had made a point of testing every rod afterward.

“Dad.”

Gareth came closer.

“I fixed this.”

His father looked.

“When?”

“November.”

Gareth crouched beside him.

“Yeah.”

“You remember?”

“I remember you complaining for an hour.”

Jamie lifted the loose edge.

“This wasn't like this Saturday.”

Izzy folded her arms.

“How do you know?”

“I was up and down these stairs all day.”

“Things break.”

“Not that fast.”

Gareth examined the empty bracket.

“Could've worked loose.”

“The whole rod is gone.”

“Police took things,” Izzy said.

“Did they take the rod?”

“I don't know.”

Jamie looked at her.

She looked back.

Gareth stood.

“Leave it, Jam. They'll have photographed everything.”

Jamie nodded.

He tried to.

But Evelyn's voice was already in his head.

What changed?

---

His tools were in the library.

Jamie had left them beside the repaired window frame on Saturday evening.

Everything was exactly where he remembered.

Toolbox open.

Tape measure on the floor.

Pencil on the sill.

Evelyn's chair angled towards the fireplace.

An unfinished crossword lay on the small table beside it.

Jamie stopped.

“She was in here.”

Izzy stood in the doorway.

“After you left.”

Jamie approached the chair.

The crossword had been filled in halfway.

Evelyn's glasses sat folded on top.

Beside them was a circular tea stain.

But no cup.

Jamie looked around.

“Where’s the mug?”

“What mug?”

“She had tea.”

Izzy shrugged.

“Kitchen probably.”

Jamie went to the kitchen.

Gareth followed more slowly.

Most of the surfaces were clean.

Too clean, somehow.

Then Jamie opened the bin to throw away a dirty cloth from his toolbox.

Something blue caught his eye beneath a newspaper.

He froze.

A broken ceramic handle.

Jamie reached in and lifted the paper.

Pieces of a dark blue mug had been wrapped carefully in kitchen roll.

He recognised it.

Evelyn's mug.

The one with the thin gold line around the rim.

She had used it constantly.

He looked at Izzy.

“When did this break?”

Izzy stared at the pieces.

“I don't know.”

“You live here.”

“So?”

“It was fine Saturday.”

“How do you know?”

“She drank from it when we had food.”

Izzy's eyes narrowed.

“Why are you doing this?”

Jamie stopped.

“Doing what?”

“Looking at everything like it's evidence.”

The word landed between them.

Gareth stood near the sink, silent.

Jamie placed the broken handle on the counter.

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

“I just noticed.”

“Gran dropped a cup. So what?”

“When?”

“I don't know!”

Izzy's voice echoed off the kitchen walls.

Jamie took a step back.

She immediately looked sorry.

“I'm sorry.”

“It’s fine.”

“No. It’s not. I just—”

She rubbed both hands over her face.

“Everything in this house reminds me of her and you're asking me about cups.”

Jamie felt ashamed.

“I’m sorry.”

Izzy came to him and put both arms around his waist.

He held her.

Over her shoulder, he saw the dresser.

The sealed envelope Margaret Pryce had left there on the day of the will meeting was gone.

Maybe Evelyn had moved it.

Maybe Margaret had taken it.

Maybe police had.

There were a hundred explanations.

Jamie closed his eyes.

He hated that his mind was producing explanations at all.

---

Later, Gareth loaded Jamie's tools into the van.

Izzy stayed inside.

Jamie stood beside the passenger door.

“Dad.”

Gareth closed the rear doors.

“What?”

“That stair rod wasn't loose.”

“I know.”

Jamie looked at him.

“You said it could've come loose.”

“It could have.”

“But you don't think it did.”

Gareth leaned against the van.

“I think I don't know.”

Jamie looked towards the house.

“The cup broke.”

“Cups break.”

“The envelope's gone.”

“People move envelopes.”

Jamie became frustrated.

“You sound like you don't believe me.”

“I believe you saw all those things.”

“That isn't what I mean.”

Gareth looked at him carefully.

“Then tell me what you mean.”

Jamie opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

Because saying it would change everything.

He could not say Izzy.

Not yet.

His father waited.

Jamie looked away.

“Nothing.”

Gareth was quiet.

Then he said, “You lied to that detective.”

Jamie turned sharply.

“What?”

“I don't know what about. But I know you.”

Jamie felt the blood drain from his face.

Gareth continued.

“If that lie has anything to do with what you're thinking now, you need to tell me.”

Jamie stared at him.

“Dad—”

“I’m not angry.”

“That makes it worse.”

“Probably.”

Jamie looked towards the upstairs windows.

Izzy stood behind one of them.

Watching.

The same place he had seen her the first night.

She raised one hand.

Jamie raised his.

Gareth followed his gaze.

“Jamie.”

“I can't.”

“Can't what?”

Jamie opened the passenger door.

“I don't know.”

He got into the van.

As Gareth drove away, Jamie looked back at Ashcroft House.

At the restored stained glass.

At the staircase behind it.

At the loose runner he knew had not been loose.

For the first time, he allowed the thought to finish.

What if Evelyn hadn't fallen?
