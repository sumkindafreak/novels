# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Sixteen — The Solicitor

Mrs Pryce arrived in a navy-blue Volvo at eleven twenty on a Saturday morning.

Jamie noticed because Evelyn had taught him to notice cars that did not belong.

The Volvo stopped outside the gate.

A woman in her late fifties climbed out carrying a leather document case and an umbrella she did not open despite the rain.

Jamie was on the first-floor landing sanding the final section of Catherine's window frame.

He looked down through the clear panel beside the front door.

“Visitor.”

Evelyn was below him sorting post on the hall table.

“I am aware.”

“You didn't look.”

“I invited her.”

“Oh.”

Jamie returned to sanding.

The bell clanged.

Evelyn opened the door.

“Margaret.”

“Evelyn.”

The woman stepped inside and shook rain from her coat.

Jamie looked down again.

Evelyn glanced up.

“Jamie.”

“What?”

“You are staring.”

“Observing.”

“There is a difference.”

The visitor looked up and smiled.

“You must be Jamie.”

Jamie frowned.

“How does everyone know me?”

Evelyn closed the door.

“Your reputation precedes you.”

The woman held out her hand when Jamie came downstairs.

“Margaret Pryce.”

“Hi.”

“Solicitor.”

Jamie looked at Evelyn.

“Are you being arrested?”

Margaret laughed.

Evelyn did not.

“Return to the window.”

Jamie grinned and went back upstairs.

---

For the next forty minutes, Evelyn and Margaret were in the study with the door closed.

Jamie tried not to listen.

The house made that easy.

He heard only pieces whenever someone spoke near the door.

“…existing arrangement…”

Then nothing.

“…property itself…”

Nothing.

“…not what Catherine intended…”

Jamie stopped sanding.

He looked towards the study door below.

Then deliberately turned back to the frame.

Private.

Evelyn had been very clear about private.

He put his headphones in.

Ten minutes later Izzy appeared at the top of the stairs.

She looked towards the study.

“Who’s here?”

Jamie removed one earbud.

“Solicitor.”

Izzy's face tightened.

“Pryce?”

“Margaret Pryce.”

“Why?”

Jamie shrugged.

“Didn't ask.”

Izzy stared at the closed study door.

Jamie watched her.

“She comes here loads?”

“Sometimes.”

“For Gran?”

“It’s Gran's solicitor, Jamie.”

“Obviously for Gran.”

Izzy did not smile.

She walked downstairs.

Jamie returned to the frame.

A few minutes later the study door opened.

Evelyn emerged.

Izzy was waiting in the hall.

“What’s she doing here?”

Evelyn stopped.

“Work.”

“What work?”

“Private work.”

Izzy folded her arms.

“Is it about the will?”

Jamie froze upstairs.

He did not turn around.

Evelyn's voice became colder.

“Isabella.”

“What?”

“That is not an appropriate question.”

“Why not?”

“Because my will is none of your concern while I am alive.”

“It is literally my concern.”

“No.”

“You know what I mean.”

“I know exactly what you mean.”

The study went quiet behind them.

Margaret Pryce had obviously heard every word.

Jamie wished he could disappear into the wall.

Izzy lowered her voice.

“Did you change it?”

Evelyn's answer came without hesitation.

“I will not discuss this with you.”

“That means yes.”

“It means I will not discuss this with you.”

Izzy laughed bitterly.

“Fine.”

She turned and saw Jamie on the landing.

Their eyes met.

Jamie looked away too late.

Izzy's face hardened.

“Enjoying the show?”

“No.”

“Could've fooled me.”

She ran upstairs past him and disappeared into the west corridor.

A door slammed.

Evelyn closed her eyes.

Jamie looked down.

“Do you want me to go?”

“No.”

“I can.”

“No.”

Evelyn looked suddenly older than she had ten minutes earlier.

Margaret appeared in the study doorway.

“Perhaps I should finish another day.”

Evelyn shook her head.

“No. We finish today.”

Margaret nodded.

Before returning inside, she looked up at Jamie.

Not curiously.

Carefully.

As though placing him in context.

That unsettled him.

---

At twelve thirty, Margaret left.

Jamie was in the kitchen washing dust from his hands when Evelyn came in.

She placed a sealed envelope on the dresser and started making tea.

Jamie dried his hands.

“Is Izzy all right?”

“No.”

“Are you?”

Evelyn paused.

“An alarmingly perceptive question.”

“You trained me.”

“Yes. Another error.”

Jamie sat.

Evelyn prepared the teapot.

He did not ask about the will.

He wanted to.

Every part of him wanted to.

But he remembered the photograph.

You are not entitled to every answer.

So he said, “I finished the frame.”

Evelyn looked at him.

“Did you?”

“Yeah.”

“Properly?”

“I measured everything twice.”

“Only twice?”

“Three times, actually.”

“Good.”

Jamie smiled.

Evelyn brought the tea to the table.

“You are not going to ask?”

He looked at her.

“About what?”

“You know perfectly well.”

Jamie shrugged.

“You said private.”

For a moment Evelyn said nothing.

Then she sat opposite him.

“It is.”

“Then it's private.”

She studied him.

Jamie took a drink and immediately regretted it.

“Still awful.”

“Yet you continue drinking it.”

“I’m building immunity.”

The corner of her mouth lifted.

Then she looked towards the ceiling, in the direction of Izzy's room.

“People behave strangely around inheritance.”

Jamie waited.

Evelyn tapped one finger against her mug.

“They mistake expectation for ownership.”

Jamie thought of Izzy on the chapel wall.

One day it is.

“She thinks the house is hers.”

Evelyn looked at him sharply.

Jamie realised he had said too much.

“She said her mum would've had everything. Then her.”

Evelyn's face became unreadable.

“Catherine would have inherited a substantial part of my estate.”

“So Izzy's right?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because Catherine is not Isabella.”

Jamie heard the hardness in the sentence.

“That's what Izzy thinks you believe.”

Evelyn's eyes narrowed.

“What?”

“That she's not her mum.”

Silence.

Jamie wanted to take it back.

Then Evelyn looked down at her hands.

“Perhaps I have done a poor job of demonstrating otherwise.”

Jamie said nothing.

Evelyn continued quietly.

“I love my granddaughter.”

“I know.”

“Does she?”

Jamie thought about lying.

Evelyn would know.

“I think sometimes.”

She nodded once.

That answer hurt her.

He could see it.

“Then I have work to do,” Evelyn said.

Jamie looked towards the hallway.

“So does she.”

Evelyn gave him a faint smile.

“Very diplomatic.”

“MI5 training.”

“Do not blame the Service for you.”

Jamie laughed.

The tension eased.

Only slightly.

On the dresser behind Evelyn, Margaret Pryce's sealed envelope remained untouched.

Jamie did not know what was inside.

He did not know that the documents signed that morning would decide who owned Ashcroft House after Evelyn died.

He only knew that, for the first time since he'd met her, Evelyn seemed afraid of something she could not solve by asking the right questions.
