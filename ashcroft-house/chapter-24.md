# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-Four — Impossible

Detective Sergeant Morris did not shout at Jamie.

He almost wished she would.

Shouting would have given him something to push against.

Instead she sat opposite him in a small interview room at the police station and asked him to tell the truth from the beginning.

Gareth sat beside him.

Jamie told her everything.

The argument Izzy admitted having with Evelyn.

The request for twenty minutes.

The lie.

The shop.

The missing stair rod.

The photographs.

Morris took his phone and looked at every image carefully.

At 12:46 the brass rod was there.

At 4:58 it was there.

At 5:41 it was there.

At 6:37 it was there.

Jamie had left at 7:22.

By the time police photographed the staircase on Sunday morning, it was gone.

Morris placed the phone on the table.

“Why didn't you tell me about the argument?”

“Because Izzy asked me not to.”

“I know. Why did you agree?”

Jamie looked at his hands.

“Because I love her.”

The room went quiet.

Saying it aloud here felt horrible.

Morris did not react.

“What was the argument about?”

“A will.”

“Whose?”

“Evelyn's.”

“Did you hear it?”

“No.”

“Izzy told you?”

“After Evelyn died.”

Morris wrote something down.

Jamie hated the sound of the pen.

“Had they argued about inheritance before?”

Jamie looked at Gareth.

His father said nothing.

Jamie answered.

“Yes.”

He told her about Spain.

The money.

The solicitor visit.

Izzy asking whether Evelyn had changed the will.

The cabinet.

The letters.

Everything that had once seemed like family tension now sounded different when arranged in a police interview room.

Motive.

That was the word nobody said.

Jamie could feel it sitting on the table anyway.

Morris closed her notebook.

“There is something else you should know.”

Gareth straightened.

“What?”

“The pathologist found bruising on Mrs Ashcroft's upper arm.”

Jamie stopped breathing.

“What kind?”

“I'm not going to give you forensic detail. At the moment it does not prove she was assaulted. Older people bruise easily. It could have occurred during the fall.”

“But?” Gareth asked.

Morris looked at him.

“But taken with the altered staircase and the change in witness account, we are no longer treating every assumption about the fall as settled.”

Jamie stared at her.

“You think somebody pushed her.”

“I didn't say that.”

“You think Izzy did.”

“I didn't say that either.”

Jamie felt anger rise.

“She loved her.”

Morris remained calm.

“I believe you.”

“No, you don't.”

“I believe you believe that.”

Jamie stood so quickly the chair scraped backwards.

Gareth put a hand on his arm.

“Jam.”

Jamie pulled away.

“No. This is stupid.”

Morris waited.

“She was upset because her mum died. She argued with Evelyn because families argue. That doesn't mean she killed her.”

“No,” Morris said. “It doesn't.”

Jamie looked at her.

“Then stop making it sound like it does.”

“I am not asking you to decide what happened.”

“Everyone already has.”

“No. They haven't.”

Morris leaned forward slightly.

“And Jamie, this is important. Do not try to investigate this yourself.”

Gareth gave him a look.

Jamie sat down.

“I won't.”

Morris studied him.

Jamie hated that everyone could now recognise his lies.

---

Izzy did not answer his messages.

Not that afternoon.

Not that evening.

Not the next morning.

By lunchtime at school, Jamie had sent six.

**JAMIE:** please talk to me

**JAMIE:** i had to tell them

**JAMIE:** the camera showed me alone anyway

**JAMIE:** iz

**JAMIE:** i dont think you did anything

That last one sat on the screen unread.

Callum found him behind the sports hall at break.

“You all right?”

Jamie laughed without humour.

“Everyone keeps asking that.”

“Yeah. Because you look like shit.”

“Thanks.”

Callum leaned against the wall beside him.

He did not know everything.

Nobody at school did.

But enough rumours had spread for him to understand police were asking more questions.

“You think the girl did it?”

Jamie turned sharply.

“Don't call her ‘the girl’.”

“Sorry.”

“No, I don't.”

Callum nodded.

Jamie looked at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Say it.”

Callum hesitated.

“You always answer too fast when you're lying.”

Jamie stared at him.

“Since when are you Evelyn?”

“Who?”

Jamie looked away.

“Never mind.”

---

At three fifteen, Izzy finally replied.

**IZZY:** come over

Jamie stared at the message.

Gareth had told him not to go to Ashcroft House alone.

Morris had told him not to investigate.

Evelyn had spent months teaching him not to let what he wanted distort what was true.

Jamie left school and walked directly to Ashcroft House.

He told himself this was not investigating.

He was going to see his girlfriend.

He was going to hear her side.

He was going to give her the chance everyone else seemed determined not to give her.

The gates were open.

The front door was unlocked.

“Iz?”

No answer.

Jamie entered.

The house felt cold.

“Izzy?”

Somewhere upstairs, a floorboard creaked.

Jamie waited.

Nothing.

He walked into the kitchen.

A mug sat beside the sink.

Her coat hung by the back door.

She was here.

Jamie returned to the hall.

“Izzy?”

Still nothing.

He looked towards the study.

The door stood open.

That was unusual.

Jamie approached.

The room had been disturbed.

Not dramatically.

One desk drawer open.

Books shifted on the lower shelf.

The cabinet panel exposed.

Jamie's heartbeat changed.

He stepped inside.

The little brass key was in the lock.

The hidden compartment stood open.

The old camera and red wallet sat on the desk.

The document boxes had been moved.

Jamie crouched.

Behind the lower box was something wrapped in a folded tea towel.

He reached for it.

His hands began shaking before he opened it.

Metal touched his fingers.

Brass.

Long.

Decorative ends.

Jamie unwrapped the cloth.

The missing stair rod lay across his knees.

For a few seconds his mind simply refused to accept it.

He had fitted this.

Held it.

Polished it with wire wool while Evelyn complained about the smell.

He knew the small dent near one end where somebody decades ago had hit it with something heavy.

There was no mistake.

Jamie stood too quickly and knocked the document box sideways.

Papers spilled across the carpet.

One sheet landed face up.

Letterhead.

**PRYCE & WATKINS SOLICITORS**

Jamie stared at a paragraph halfway down.

He read only fragments.

**…revised testamentary arrangements…**

**…Ashcroft House to be held…**

**…restrictions on sale…**

**…remainder of estate… trust…**

The bottom section was torn away.

Jamie felt cold.

He heard movement behind him.

He turned.

Izzy stood in the study doorway.

She looked at the brass stair rod in his hands.

Neither of them spoke.

Jamie wanted her to laugh.

To call him an idiot.

To explain that police had found the rod and returned it.

That Margaret had left the letter.

That there was some obvious thing he had missed.

Izzy looked at his face.

Then at the open compartment.

Her eyes filled with tears.

Jamie knew before she said anything.

“No,” he whispered.

Izzy closed the study door behind her.

And the impossible became true.
