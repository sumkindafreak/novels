# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Eighteen — The Warning

The argument about the cabinet lasted three days.

Jamie was not present for most of it.

He received updates from Izzy by text.

**IZZY:** she’s ridiculous

Then:

**IZZY:** literally letters from MY MUM

Then, twenty minutes later:

**IZZY:** dont say she has a point

Jamie had not yet replied.

He put his phone face down on the kitchen table.

Gareth looked over from the sink.

“Trouble?”

“No.”

“Girl trouble?”

“No.”

“House trouble?”

Jamie thought about it.

“Yes.”

Gareth nodded as though this made perfect sense.

“Tea?”

“Yeah.”

Jamie waited until his father had put the kettle on.

“Do you think people have a right to read letters their dead mum wrote?”

Gareth stopped.

“That escalated.”

Jamie explained.

His father listened without interrupting.

When Jamie finished, Gareth leaned against the counter.

“Were the letters written to Izzy?”

“No.”

“Then they're Evelyn's.”

Jamie sighed.

“That’s what I said.”

“Then why are you asking me?”

“Because Izzy says it's different because it's her mum.”

“It is different.”

Jamie looked up hopefully.

“Doesn't make them hers.”

“Oh.”

Gareth poured hot water into two mugs.

“You can understand why somebody feels something without agreeing with what they do about it.”

Jamie frowned.

“You've been talking to Evelyn.”

“I’m capable of thoughts independently.”

“Unproven.”

Gareth handed him a mug.

“Careful with Izzy.”

Jamie looked at him.

“You keep saying that.”

“I know.”

“Why?”

Gareth took a moment.

“Because when somebody's lost a lot, sometimes they start holding too tightly to whatever they think is left.”

Jamie stared into his tea.

“That sounds like you don't trust her.”

“I don't know her well enough to trust or distrust her.”

“I do.”

Gareth nodded.

“Then trust what you know. Just don't ignore what you see because you'd rather it wasn't there.”

Jamie looked up.

“That is definitely an Evelyn sentence.”

Gareth smiled.

“Maybe she's rubbing off on me.”

“God help us.”

---

The following Saturday, Izzy was almost aggressively cheerful.

She kissed Evelyn on the cheek when she came into the kitchen.

She made toast for everyone.

She apologised for going into the study.

Evelyn accepted the apology.

Nobody mentioned the letters.

Jamie watched both of them.

Everything looked normal.

That was the problem.

Normal had started becoming something he inspected.

Izzy caught him looking.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Stop analysing me.”

“I wasn't.”

“You've got the spy face.”

“I do not have a spy face.”

Evelyn said, “He does.”

Jamie turned.

“You are meant to be on my side.”

“I have never claimed such a thing.”

Izzy laughed and threw an arm around his shoulders.

“Poor Jamie.”

He relaxed.

Maybe Gareth was right.

Maybe he was looking for trouble because adults had made him suspicious.

Izzy had lost her mother.

She wanted her letters.

That did not make her dangerous.

It made her human.

---

That afternoon Evelyn asked Jamie to help her in the garden.

Not fix anything.

Help her walk.

He noticed the distinction and disliked it.

“You all right?”

“I am eighty-one.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It has answered most medical questions recently.”

She took his arm anyway.

That worried him more.

They walked slowly along the side path towards the old greenhouse.

The actual greenhouse, not the conservatory.

It stood beyond the walled garden beneath a line of yew trees. Half its glass was missing and one door had fallen completely off.

Jamie looked inside.

“No bodies.”

Evelyn frowned.

“What?”

“Local rumour.”

She remembered and rolled her eyes.

“I never buried my husband beneath a greenhouse.”

“What happened to him?”

“Divorce.”

“Oh.”

“Much less interesting.”

“Is he alive?”

“No. Heart attack. 2008.”

Jamie nodded.

They continued along the wall.

Evelyn stopped beside a stone bench.

They sat.

Below them, the valley spread between bare winter trees.

Jamie could see the chapel roof and, farther down, the street where he lived.

Evelyn looked tired.

Not ill.

Just tired in a way Jamie had started recognising.

“You should get someone to do the garden,” he said.

“I have.”

Jamie looked around.

“Who?”

“You.”

He laughed.

“Absolutely not.”

Evelyn smiled.

For a while they watched the town.

Then she said, “Do you love Isabella?”

Jamie nearly fell off the bench.

“What?”

“A straightforward question.”

“No, it isn't.”

“It contains six syllables.”

“That doesn't make it straightforward.”

Evelyn waited.

Jamie looked towards the house.

Izzy was somewhere inside.

His face warmed.

“I don't know.”

“Good.”

Jamie turned.

“Good?”

“You are fifteen. Certainty would be alarming.”

He smiled.

“I really like her.”

“I know.”

“She likes me.”

“I believe so.”

The phrasing bothered him.

“You believe so?”

Evelyn looked at him.

“Izzy has experienced more loss than most people her age. Loss can distort affection.”

Jamie frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“It means people sometimes cling to what makes them feel safe.”

“That’s bad?”

“No.”

Evelyn looked towards Ashcroft House.

“But safety and love are not always the same thing.”

Jamie listened.

“Never confuse somebody loving you with somebody needing something from you.”

The sentence made him uncomfortable.

“Are you saying she's using me?”

“No.”

“Sounds like it.”

“I am saying you should know the difference.”

“How?”

Evelyn smiled faintly.

“There is no convenient test.”

“Helpful.”

“Usually, love permits you to remain yourself. Need is more interested in what you provide.”

Jamie thought about Izzy wanting him on her side.

The argument about money.

The letters.

Then he thought about the storm in the conservatory.

Her laughing.

Her crying about Catherine.

Her hand in his walking down the lane.

“She does love you,” he said suddenly.

Evelyn looked at him.

“Izzy.”

A long silence.

“I know,” Evelyn said.

“Then why are you worried?”

Evelyn's gaze moved towards the house.

“Because love does not automatically make people good.”

Jamie stared at her.

“That is depressing.”

“It is also true.”

She stood slowly.

Jamie offered his arm again.

This time she took it without comment.

They started back towards the house.

Halfway along the path Evelyn stopped.

“And Jamie?”

“Yeah?”

“The same applies to me.”

“What?”

“Do not assume I am correct merely because you care what I think.”

Jamie smiled.

“Trust no one?”

Evelyn sighed.

“Television again.”

He laughed.

When they reached the conservatory, Izzy was waiting in the doorway.

“There you are.”

She came down the steps and took Evelyn's other arm.

“You could've told me you were going outside.”

“I am not under house arrest.”

“You nearly fell yesterday.”

“I did not nearly fall.”

“You grabbed the table.”

“The table was available.”

Izzy rolled her eyes.

Jamie watched them walk together.

One on either side of Evelyn.

For a moment, the warning seemed ridiculous.

All he could see was a granddaughter worried about her grandmother.

And an old woman pretending she did not enjoy being worried about.

Jamie followed them inside.

He would remember Evelyn's words later.

By then, they would mean something entirely different.
