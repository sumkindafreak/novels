# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Seventeen — The Cabinet

The hidden compartment was behind a bookcase.

Jamie was disappointed by this for approximately three seconds.

Then he remembered that hidden compartments behind bookcases were objectively brilliant.

He discovered it while trying to move the lower cabinet in Evelyn's study.

The study was usually off limits.

Not forbidden exactly.

Evelyn did not need signs.

She had a way of saying “my study” that made entering without permission feel equivalent to crossing an international border.

But a damp patch had appeared behind one section of shelving, and Gareth wanted to inspect the wall before it worsened.

Evelyn had agreed.

Reluctantly.

Jamie removed the books, stacked them carefully on the floor and tried to pull the cabinet forward.

It moved an inch.

Then stopped.

He pulled harder.

Nothing.

“Dad?”

Gareth was beneath the desk inspecting a cable.

“What?”

“This is stuck.”

“Don't force it.”

“I’m not.”

“You are literally grunting.”

“That’s effort, not force.”

Gareth crawled out.

They examined the cabinet.

It was built into the wall more cleverly than Jamie had realised. Narrow wooden trim hid screws along both sides.

Gareth ran a finger down one edge.

“This isn't original.”

“How do you know?”

“Different timber. Different fixings.”

Jamie smiled.

“Secret room.”

“No.”

“Could be.”

“It’s a cabinet.”

“Secret cabinet.”

Gareth sighed.

Evelyn appeared behind them.

“What have you broken?”

“Nothing,” Jamie said.

Gareth pointed.

“This has been added later.”

Evelyn looked at the trim.

For half a second, Jamie saw recognition.

Then annoyance.

“Oh.”

Jamie grinned.

“You know.”

“Know what?”

“What’s behind it.”

“Damp, apparently.”

“That isn't what I mean.”

Evelyn looked at Gareth.

“Can the wall be inspected without removing the entire cabinet?”

“Probably, but I need this lower section forward.”

Evelyn considered.

Then sighed.

“Very well.”

She walked to the desk, opened the top drawer and removed the tiny brass key Jamie had found months earlier in the library table.

Jamie stared.

“That key?”

“Yes.”

“I found that.”

“I remember.”

“What does it open?”

Evelyn crouched with difficulty beside the cabinet.

Gareth moved to help.

She waved him away.

“Do not fuss.”

She inserted the key into what Jamie had assumed was a decorative brass screw near the base.

It turned.

There was a soft click.

A narrow panel released from the side of the cabinet.

Jamie stopped breathing.

“No way.”

Gareth laughed.

Evelyn looked deeply unimpressed by both of them.

Jamie pulled the panel open.

Behind it was a shallow compartment built into the wall.

Not a safe.

Not a room.

A space perhaps thirty centimetres wide and a metre high, fitted with three narrow shelves.

On them sat two document boxes, a red leather wallet, a small metal cylinder and an old camera in a black case.

Jamie stared.

“Goodies.”

Gareth looked at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Evelyn sighed.

“Move the items carefully.”

Jamie reached for the metal cylinder.

Evelyn tapped his wrist with her walking stick.

“Ow.”

“Carefully.”

“I was being careful.”

“You were behaving like a Labrador.”

Gareth laughed.

Jamie lifted the cylinder.

It looked like an oversized bolt, dull grey and heavy.

“What is it?”

“A concealment device.”

Jamie's eyes widened.

“For secrets?”

“For small pieces of paper.”

“That is secrets.”

“Sometimes it was shopping lists.”

Jamie stared at her.

“That is the most disappointing thing you've ever said.”

Evelyn smiled.

“It was a training item. Never operational.”

Jamie unscrewed the end.

The inside was empty.

“Dead drop?”

“Similar principle.”

Gareth picked up the camera.

“Minox?”

Evelyn nodded.

Jamie looked between them.

“How do you know that?”

Gareth shrugged.

“I’m old, not dead.”

“You're forty-two.”

“Exactly.”

Jamie opened the red leather wallet.

Inside was an old identity card, several train tickets, a folded map of London and a photograph of Evelyn beside the woman from the 1974 picture.

Margaret Vale.

On the back, in faded handwriting, someone had written:

**You were wrong about the shoes. — M**

Jamie looked up.

“What shoes?”

Evelyn took the photograph from him.

“Private joke.”

“You have too many private jokes.”

“I had a life before you arrived, inconvenient though that may be.”

Jamie smiled.

The first document box contained newspaper clippings, retirement letters and photocopies of papers with thick black redactions.

Nothing looked currently secret.

The second contained personal letters.

Evelyn's expression changed when Jamie touched it.

“That one stays closed.”

He removed his hand immediately.

“Okay.”

She looked slightly surprised that he didn't argue.

Jamie shrugged.

“Private.”

“Yes.”

They moved everything onto the desk.

Gareth loosened the cabinet enough to inspect the wall.

The damp was minor.

A leaking exterior joint rather than structural damage.

Jamie was almost disappointed.

He had hoped for a tunnel.

While Gareth worked, Izzy came into the study.

She stopped when she saw the open compartment.

“What the hell?”

Jamie grinned.

“Secret cabinet.”

Izzy walked straight to the desk.

“Gran.”

“I know.”

“You never showed me this.”

“There was no reason.”

Izzy picked up the metal concealment device.

“This is actual spy stuff.”

“Training material.”

“Still.”

She opened the red wallet.

Jamie noticed the difference immediately.

He had looked at the objects because they were interesting.

Izzy looked through them quickly.

Searching.

Her fingers moved beneath the old tickets.

Checked the inner pocket.

Opened the photograph sleeve.

“What are you looking for?” Jamie asked.

Izzy looked up.

“Nothing.”

“You're definitely looking for something.”

“Money.”

She grinned as though it were a joke.

“Imagine Gran's got fifty grand in old notes back here.”

Evelyn's voice hardened.

“She does not.”

Izzy closed the wallet.

“Shame.”

Jamie smiled uncertainly.

Something about the exchange felt wrong.

Small.

Not important enough to hold onto.

So he let it go.

Then Izzy reached for the closed document box.

Evelyn's walking stick came down across the lid.

“No.”

Izzy frowned.

“What’s in there?”

“Letters.”

“From who?”

“People.”

“Mum?”

Evelyn's face softened.

“Some.”

Izzy's hand stayed on the box.

“Can I read them?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because they were written to me.”

“That’s my mother.”

“Yes.”

“I have a right—”

“No.”

The word stopped her.

Jamie looked down.

There it was again.

Expectation becoming ownership.

Not money this time.

Memory.

Izzy pulled her hand away.

“Fine.”

She left the study.

The door closed behind her.

Gareth pretended to be very interested in the damp wall.

Jamie watched Evelyn.

She rested one hand on the document box.

“Maybe one day,” she said quietly.

Jamie wasn't sure whether she meant Izzy.

Or herself.

Later, when the cabinet was back in place and the little brass panel clicked shut, Jamie stood in front of it for a moment.

A secret hidden inside a secret.

That was Ashcroft House.

Every time he thought he understood one room, another door appeared.
