# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-One — Twenty Minutes

The police spoke to Jamie on Monday afternoon.

Not at a station.

At home.

Detective Sergeant Morris sat at the Morgan kitchen table with a notebook while Gareth made tea nobody drank.

Jamie had spent all morning at school pretending to be normal.

It had not worked.

Everyone knew.

News moved quickly in a valley.

By registration, half his year had heard that the old woman from Ashcroft House was dead.

By lunch, Callum had somehow learned Jamie had been there the night before.

He had not made a joke.

That was how Jamie knew things were serious.

Now Morris sat opposite him beneath the kitchen light.

“Just take your time,” she said.

Jamie nodded.

She asked when he had arrived at Ashcroft House on Saturday.

Around ten.

What he had done.

Window installation. Conservatory. Dinner.

Who had been there.

Peter and Ruth Bowen. Martin. Gareth. Izzy. Evelyn.

When each person left.

Jamie did his best.

Then Morris asked, “What time did you leave?”

Jamie looked at his phone.

“I texted Dad at seven twenty-two saying I was leaving.”

Gareth nodded.

Morris wrote it down.

“And you went straight home?”

Jamie opened his mouth.

Then stopped.

The question should have been easy.

He had gone straight home.

He had walked down the path.

Looked back at the house.

Walked through the chapel lane and down towards the terraces.

He had been home by seven forty-three.

But Sunday afternoon, Izzy had asked him for something.

---

They had been sitting in Gareth's van outside Ashcroft House.

The police had finally allowed Izzy back inside to collect clothes, but she refused to stay in the house alone.

She was coming to sleep at a friend's for a few nights.

Jamie helped carry a bag down the path.

Halfway to the van, Izzy stopped.

“Jamie.”

He turned.

Her face was pale.

“What?”

“They're going to ask you when you left.”

“Yeah.”

“And what I did.”

Jamie frowned.

“I don't know what you did.”

Izzy looked back at the house.

“Gran and I argued after you left.”

Jamie went still.

“About what?”

“The usual.”

“Money?”

“The will.”

Jamie stared at her.

“You told me yesterday you didn't argue.”

“I panicked.”

“Izzy.”

“It wasn't a big argument.”

“Then why lie?”

“Because she died, Jamie!”

Her voice broke.

She lowered it immediately.

“Because she died that night and the last thing I did was scream at her. How do you think that sounds?”

Jamie said nothing.

Izzy stepped closer.

“I went out after.”

“Where?”

“Shop.”

“What shop?”

“The Spar near the bridge. I needed air. I bought cigarettes.”

“You don't smoke.”

“Sometimes I do.”

Jamie stared at her.

Izzy grabbed both of his hands.

“Please don't look at me like that.”

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

She swallowed.

“The police asked if anyone had been angry with Gran. I said no.”

Jamie understood.

“You lied to them.”

“I panicked.”

Again.

The word seemed to do a lot of work.

“What do you want me to do?” Jamie asked.

Izzy looked at him.

“Say I walked with you.”

He stared.

“What?”

“Just down to the chapel. We sat there for a bit. Twenty minutes, maybe.”

“That didn't happen.”

“I know.”

“Then no.”

Izzy's eyes filled.

“Jamie, please.”

“No.”

“They're going to think I did something.”

“She fell.”

“Exactly.”

“Then why does it matter?”

“Because people don't care about exactly once they decide you look guilty.”

Jamie heard Evelyn in his head.

Find out what is true.

He pulled his hands away.

“I don't want to lie to the police.”

Izzy looked as if he had slapped her.

“You think I killed her?”

“No!”

“Then help me.”

“That isn't fair.”

“Nothing is fair.”

Her voice dropped to a whisper.

“My mum's dead. My gran's dead. I have nobody.”

“You've got me.”

“Then act like it.”

Jamie stared at her.

There it was.

Love permits you to remain yourself.

Need is more interested in what you provide.

He pushed the thought away.

This was grief.

That was all.

Izzy leaned her forehead against his chest.

“Twenty minutes,” she whispered. “That’s all. It doesn't change what happened.”

Jamie closed his eyes.

He put his arms around her.

“All right.”

---

Now, at the kitchen table, Detective Sergeant Morris waited.

Jamie looked at his phone.

Then at Gareth.

His father knew nothing about the conversation with Izzy.

Gareth's expression was open.

Trusting.

Jamie looked back at Morris.

“Not straight home,” he said.

Morris's pen moved.

“What did you do?”

“Izzy walked with me.”

The lie sounded enormous in his own kitchen.

“Where to?”

“The chapel.”

“How long were you together?”

Jamie swallowed.

“About twenty minutes.”

Morris wrote it down.

“And then?”

“She went back. I went home.”

“You arrived home at seven forty-three?”

Jamie looked at the message.

“Yeah.”

Morris paused.

The chapel was only a few minutes from Ashcroft House.

If Jamie had sat there for twenty minutes, he would have had to move quickly to be home by seven forty-three.

Not impossible.

Just tight.

Morris looked at him.

“You're sure?”

Jamie heard Evelyn again.

Patterns.

Changes.

The important thing is what people stop doing.

He forced himself to nod.

“Yeah.”

Morris held his gaze for a second longer than Jamie liked.

Then wrote something down.

“All right.”

The interview continued.

When she finally left, Gareth closed the front door and came back into the kitchen.

Jamie stayed at the table.

His father looked at him.

“I thought you came straight home.”

Jamie stared at the grain of the wood.

“I forgot.”

Gareth did not move.

Jamie could feel him noticing.

“You forgot sitting at the chapel with Izzy for twenty minutes?”

“I had a lot going on.”

Silence.

Jamie looked up.

Gareth's expression had changed.

Not angry.

Worried.

“Jamie.”

“I’m tired.”

He stood.

“I'm going upstairs.”

His father let him go.

That somehow made it worse.

In his bedroom, Jamie closed the door and sat on the edge of his bed.

His phone buzzed.

A message from Izzy.

**IZZY:** did they ask?

Jamie stared at it.

Then typed:

**JAMIE:** yeah

Three dots appeared immediately.

**IZZY:** and?

Jamie looked towards the bedroom window.

From here, if he leaned far enough, he could just see the tops of the trees around Ashcroft House.

He typed:

**JAMIE:** i said it

Izzy replied with a heart.

Jamie turned the phone face down.

Twenty minutes.

That was all.

Twenty minutes that had never happened.

He told himself it changed nothing.

The problem was that Evelyn had spent months teaching him exactly why small details mattered.
