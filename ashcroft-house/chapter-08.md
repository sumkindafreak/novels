# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Eight — The Photograph

Jamie found the photograph because he was looking for a screwdriver.

That, he would later decide, was how most important discoveries at Ashcroft House happened.

Nothing announced itself properly.

Secrets were not hidden behind dramatic steel doors.

They sat in drawers beneath receipts, old batteries and things nobody had remembered throwing away.

The screwdriver should have been in the little table beside the library door.

Evelyn insisted it lived there.

Jamie insisted no screwdriver in Ashcroft House had ever lived anywhere for more than three consecutive days.

He pulled open the top drawer.

Pens.

String.

A candle stub.

A tiny brass key.

No screwdriver.

Second drawer.

Old envelopes.

Two dead torch batteries.

A pair of spectacles in a cracked case.

Still no screwdriver.

Jamie pulled the drawer farther.

Something slid behind it.

“Great.”

He crouched and reached into the gap.

His fingers touched card.

He pulled out a black-and-white photograph.

At first he thought it showed a wedding.

Everyone was dressed formally.

Men in suits. Women in dresses and coats. A line of people stood outside an imposing stone building beneath a flag Jamie couldn't quite identify in the faded image.

Then he saw Evelyn.

He almost didn't recognise her.

She could not have been older than thirty.

Her hair was dark, cut to her shoulders. She wore a fitted coat and stood slightly apart from the others, looking directly at the camera with the same expression she used now when Jamie tried to explain why a job had taken twice as long as expected.

Jamie smiled.

“Found you.”

“What?”

Izzy's voice came from the library.

Jamie stood.

She appeared in the doorway holding a mug.

“What’ve you found?”

He held up the photograph.

Izzy's face changed.

Not much.

But enough.

“Oh.”

Jamie looked at her.

“You've seen this.”

“Maybe.”

“That’s a yes.”

Izzy took the photograph from him.

She studied it for a moment.

“Where was it?”

“Behind the drawer.”

“She’ll murder you.”

“I didn't put it there.”

“That won’t matter.”

Jamie took it back.

“Where is this?”

Izzy shrugged.

“No idea.”

“You definitely know something.”

“I definitely know Gran doesn't like people going through her stuff.”

“I was looking for a screwdriver.”

“Classic burglar defence.”

Jamie looked closer at the photograph.

A man beside Evelyn had something written on the back of his hand.

Another wore a small pin on his lapel.

Near the edge of the picture, a uniformed policeman stood half out of frame.

“This isn't a family thing.”

“No.”

“What is it?”

Izzy took a drink.

“You should ask her.”

Jamie stared at her.

“You're enjoying this.”

“A lot.”

---

Evelyn was in the kitchen cleaning mushrooms.

Jamie placed the photograph on the table.

She did not react immediately.

She trimmed the end from one mushroom.

Then another.

Then she wiped the knife carefully on a cloth.

Only then did she look down.

“Where did you find that?”

“Behind the library table drawer.”

“What were you doing behind the library table drawer?”

“Trying to find a screwdriver.”

Evelyn looked at Izzy.

Izzy raised both hands.

“Nothing to do with me.”

Evelyn picked up the photograph.

Jamie sat opposite her.

“When was it?”

“1974.”

“Where?”

“London.”

“What building?”

Evelyn returned to the mushrooms.

Jamie waited.

She looked up.

“You have acquired an irritating habit.”

“What?”

“Silence.”

“I learned it from you.”

“Yes. That was unwise of me.”

Izzy leaned against the counter, smiling.

Jamie tapped the photograph.

“What were you doing in London in 1974?”

“Living.”

“You lived there?”

“For periods.”

“Doing government work?”

Evelyn sighed.

“Still on this?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because you're weird.”

Izzy choked on her tea.

Jamie immediately tried to correct himself.

“I mean, not weird weird.”

“Excellent recovery.”

“You live in a giant house, you know when people are lying, you have photos with men who look like cabinet ministers and you won't tell anyone what your job was.”

Evelyn's eyes narrowed slightly.

“Which man looks like a cabinet minister?”

Jamie pointed.

“Him.”

“That is an accountant.”

“Oh.”

“An exceptionally dull one.”

Jamie pointed at another.

“Him?”

“Diplomat.”

“See!”

“Diplomats are allowed to exist in photographs.”

“And her?”

Evelyn looked.

For the first time, something softer entered her expression.

“Margaret Vale.”

“Who was she?”

“A friend.”

“Government?”

“Yes.”

“Doing what?”

“Work.”

Jamie groaned.

Izzy laughed.

Evelyn put down the knife.

“Jamie.”

He stopped.

“There are some parts of a person's life that belong to them.”

His face fell slightly.

“I know.”

“I am not telling you to stop asking questions.”

“You sort of are.”

“No. I am telling you not to assume you are entitled to every answer.”

Jamie looked at the photograph.

That felt fair.

Annoying.

But fair.

“Okay.”

Evelyn studied him.

Then she sat down.

“In 1969, I joined the Security Service.”

Jamie blinked.

Izzy smiled into her mug.

“The what?”

Evelyn held his gaze.

“MI5.”

Jamie stared at her.

For several seconds his brain refused to connect the words.

Then it did.

“No, you didn't.”

Evelyn's mouth tightened.

“You asked.”

“You worked for MI5?”

“Yes.”

“Like spies?”

“Not like television spies.”

“But spies.”

“Sometimes the people we dealt with were spies.”

Jamie leaned forward so quickly the chair legs scraped the floor.

“What did you do?”

Evelyn lifted one finger.

“No.”

“What?”

“You were given an answer. Do not become greedy.”

“You cannot tell someone you worked for MI5 and then say no more questions.”

“I just did.”

“That’s insane.”

Izzy was openly laughing now.

Jamie looked at her.

“You knew?”

“Since I was about eleven.”

“And you didn't tell me?”

“You never asked me.”

“I asked you about a thousand times what she did.”

“I said government.”

“MI5 is not the same as the council.”

Evelyn stood and returned to the mushrooms.

Jamie stared at the back of her head.

“Were you actually a spy?”

“No.”

“Did you catch spies?”

A pause.

“Occasionally.”

Jamie slapped both hands on the table.

“I knew it!”

“You knew absolutely nothing.”

“Did you have a gun?”

“No.”

“Code name?”

“No.”

“Secret identity?”

“No.”

“Did you follow Russians?”

Evelyn turned slowly.

Jamie grinned.

Her expression gave him nothing.

Then she said, “Once.”

Jamie stared.

Izzy did too.

“Gran.”

Evelyn returned to the chopping board.

“That is enough history for one afternoon.”

Jamie looked down at the photograph again.

Young Evelyn stared back at him from 1974.

Suddenly the house felt different.

The maps in the hallway.

The locked study.

The old passports he had glimpsed on a shelf.

The way she watched people.

The way she seemed to hear the thing behind the thing they were saying.

Mrs Ashcroft was not simply an old woman who lived alone in a collapsing house.

She had been somewhere.

Done things.

Known people.

And for the first time Jamie understood that old age did something cruel to a person's history.

It hid it.

You saw the walking stick before you saw the woman who had once walked quickly enough to follow a Soviet intelligence officer through London.

Jamie picked up the photograph carefully.

“Can I look at it again later?”

Evelyn glanced at him.

“Why?”

“Because I want to know who you were.”

The knife stopped against the chopping board.

Evelyn looked at him for a long time.

Then she said quietly, “I am still here, Jamie.”

He felt heat rise into his face.

“I know.”

Evelyn held his gaze for another second.

Then she nodded towards the photograph.

“Put it in the library. Top drawer. Properly this time.”

Jamie smiled.

“Yes, boss.”

“I was never your boss.”

“No. You’re worse.”

Izzy laughed.

Evelyn threw a mushroom stalk at him.
