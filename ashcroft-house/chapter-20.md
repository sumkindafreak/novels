# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty — The Fall

Gareth woke Jamie at six twelve on Sunday morning.

He did not switch the bedroom light on.

That was the first thing Jamie remembered later.

His father sat on the edge of the bed in darkness, one hand on Jamie's shoulder.

“Jamie.”

Jamie opened his eyes.

“What?”

“Wake up.”

“I am.”

He wasn't.

Not properly.

Then he saw his father's face in the light from the landing.

Something was wrong.

Jamie pushed himself upright.

“What happened?”

Gareth did not answer immediately.

That made everything worse.

“Dad?”

“It’s Evelyn.”

Jamie became completely awake.

“What about her?”

“There’s been an accident.”

The word accident should have helped.

It didn't.

“What accident?”

Gareth looked down.

Jamie felt the answer before he heard it.

“She fell down the stairs.”

Jamie stared at him.

“Is she in hospital?”

His father did not answer.

“Dad.”

“I’m sorry.”

Jamie shook his head.

“No.”

“Jamie—”

“No.”

Gareth reached for him.

Jamie moved away.

Not angrily.

Instinctively.

As if being touched would make the sentence real.

“No, she didn't.”

Gareth's eyes were wet.

That frightened Jamie more than anything.

His father almost never cried.

“Who told you?”

“Izzy.”

Jamie grabbed his phone from beside the bed.

Eight missed calls.

Four from Izzy.

Three from an unknown number.

One from Gareth.

Messages filled the screen.

**IZZY:** Jamie

**IZZY:** answer please

**IZZY:** please

Then, at 5:58:

**IZZY:** grans dead

Jamie stared at the words.

They looked ridiculous.

Grans dead.

Two words on a screen.

Impossible words.

He had seen Evelyn less than eleven hours earlier.

She had stood beneath Catherine's window.

She had told him his work was not dreadful.

She had raised her hand when he looked back from the gate.

People did not go from that to dead while you slept.

That was not how time was supposed to work.

“I need to go there.”

Gareth shook his head.

“Not yet.”

“I need to see Izzy.”

“The police are there.”

“Why?”

“Because she died suddenly at home.”

Jamie threw the duvet aside.

“I’m going.”

“Jamie.”

“She’s on her own.”

Gareth looked at him for a moment.

Then nodded.

“Get dressed.”

---

Ashcroft House looked wrong with blue lights outside it.

A police car stood beyond the gate.

An ambulance remained on the drive even though nobody was being taken anywhere.

Jamie got out of Gareth's van before it had properly stopped.

“Jamie.”

He ignored his father and ran up the path.

A police officer stepped from the porch.

“Whoa. Slow down.”

“I live—”

Jamie stopped.

He did not live there.

“I know them.”

The officer looked past him at Gareth approaching.

Gareth put one hand on Jamie's shoulder.

“He's close to the family.”

The front door opened.

Izzy appeared.

Jamie barely recognised her.

She wore grey jogging bottoms and the same black T-shirt from the previous evening. Her hair was tied badly at the back of her head. Her face looked colourless.

When she saw Jamie, she broke.

She ran down the steps.

Jamie caught her.

Izzy wrapped both arms around him and made a sound he had never heard from another person.

Not crying exactly.

Something lower.

Broken.

Jamie held her.

His own grief disappeared beneath hers for a moment.

“What happened?” he whispered.

Izzy shook her head against his chest.

“I found her.”

Jamie closed his eyes.

“No.”

“She was just there.”

He held her tighter.

“I’m sorry.”

“I went downstairs and she was there.”

Her whole body shook.

“I thought she was sleeping. I thought—”

“Don't.”

“I touched her hand.”

“Iz.”

“She was cold.”

Jamie felt sick.

Behind them Gareth spoke quietly to the police officer.

Jamie looked towards the open door.

He could see part of the entrance hall.

Only part.

The bottom of the main staircase.

A section of the red rug.

A white forensic suit moving in the distance.

Then the officer closed the door.

Jamie was grateful.

He was also furious.

That was Evelyn's house.

His house too, in some strange way.

People in white suits should not be walking through it.

The thought made no sense.

None of the morning did.

---

They sat in Gareth's van for nearly an hour.

The heater ran.

Nobody was warm.

Izzy sat between Jamie and the passenger door wrapped in Gareth's coat.

She told them what she knew in pieces.

She had gone to bed late.

At some point in the early morning, she woke and realised the hall light was still on.

She went downstairs.

Evelyn was at the bottom of the staircase.

Her walking stick was beside her.

She called an ambulance.

They came quickly.

Too late.

“Did she say anything last night?” Jamie asked.

Izzy stared at the dashboard.

“No.”

“You didn't argue?”

Her eyes flicked towards him.

“Why would you ask that?”

“I don't know.”

Izzy looked hurt.

Jamie hated himself.

“Sorry.”

“We had dinner. She went to the library. I went upstairs.”

Jamie nodded.

“What time?” Gareth asked gently.

Izzy rubbed her hands together.

“I don't know. Eight? Half eight?”

Gareth nodded.

“Did she usually go upstairs herself?”

“Yeah.”

Jamie frowned.

Evelyn had been tired recently.

But she still used the stairs.

Slowly.

Carefully.

One hand on the banister.

Walking stick first.

Step.

Stick.

Step.

He could picture it perfectly.

That made the idea of her falling both possible and impossible at once.

Izzy leaned into him.

Jamie put his arm around her.

He did not want to think.

Not yet.

---

At nine thirty, a detective spoke to them.

Detective Sergeant Helen Morris.

She was in her forties, dark hair pulled back, rain on the shoulders of her coat.

She explained that because Evelyn had died unexpectedly, there would be questions.

Routine.

They would speak to Izzy formally later.

They might need to speak to Jamie because he had been at the house the previous evening.

Jamie nodded.

Words passed around him without settling.

Coroner.

Post-mortem.

Statements.

Next of kin.

Evelyn reduced to procedures.

Jamie hated every word.

“Can I go in?” he asked.

Morris looked at him.

“Not today.”

“My tools are there.”

It was a stupid thing to say.

He did not care about the tools.

He wanted to walk into the kitchen and discover everyone had made a terrible mistake.

He wanted Evelyn to complain about muddy shoes.

He wanted the tea to be awful.

He wanted her to say wrong.

Morris's voice softened.

“Another day.”

Jamie nodded.

He looked up at the house.

Catherine's window caught the grey morning light.

Blue.

Amber.

Red.

Perfect again.

Evelyn would never see it in sunlight.

That was the thought that finally did it.

Jamie bent forward and covered his face.

He cried so hard he could not breathe.

Gareth held him.

Izzy held his hand.

And above them, Ashcroft House stood silent.
