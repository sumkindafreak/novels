# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Thirteen — Catherine

Jamie learned about Catherine on a Wednesday.

Not a Saturday.

That mattered somehow.

Saturdays had rules.

Work clothes. Tools. Lunch. Evelyn pretending not to be pleased with whatever he had fixed. Izzy appearing and disappearing. Gareth collecting him sometime after one.

Wednesday was different.

Wednesday happened because Gareth's van broke down forty miles away and Jamie had nowhere sensible to go after school.

He texted Izzy.

**JAMIE:** your gran mind if i come up for a bit

The reply came almost immediately.

**IZZY:** she’ll pretend she does

Then:

**IZZY:** come

Jamie arrived at Ashcroft House at four fifteen wearing his school uniform and carrying a rucksack full of homework he had no intention of doing.

Evelyn opened the door.

She looked him up and down.

“This is Wednesday.”

“I checked.”

“Your uniform suggests you have not come to repair anything.”

“My dad's stuck in Newport.”

“And your house?”

“Locked.”

“You have a key.”

Jamie stared at her.

Evelyn stared back.

Then stepped aside.

“Tea?”

“Please.”

“Homework first.”

Jamie groaned.

“That wasn't part of the deal.”

“There is no deal. This is Wednesday.”

---

Izzy was out.

That disappointed him more than he wanted Evelyn to notice.

She noticed.

“Isabella has gone into town.”

“I didn't ask.”

“No.”

Evelyn placed a mug beside his exercise book.

Jamie looked suspiciously at it.

“Did you make this?”

“Yes.”

“Can I have water?”

“No.”

He drank anyway.

They sat at the kitchen table while darkness gathered outside.

Jamie worked through science questions.

Evelyn read a book.

It was surprisingly comfortable.

No conversation for twenty minutes.

No need for one.

Then Jamie looked towards the photograph on the dresser.

Catherine and Izzy.

The same photograph he'd seen in the damaged bedroom upstairs had a copy here.

“How old was she?”

Evelyn did not look up.

“Forty-one.”

Jamie waited.

Evelyn turned a page.

“Cancer?”

“Yes.”

“Did she live here?”

“Sometimes.”

Jamie put down his pen.

“What does that mean?”

Evelyn closed the book.

It was the sort of movement that told him she had decided whether or not to answer.

“Catherine and I had a complicated relationship.”

“So does everyone in this house.”

A small smile.

“Yes.”

Evelyn looked at the photograph.

“When she was young, I was away too often.”

“Work?”

“Yes.”

“MI5?”

“Yes.”

Jamie listened.

“I told myself that children adapt. That Catherine was well cared for. That her father was present when I was not. All of those things were true.”

She paused.

“They were also excuses.”

Jamie had never heard Evelyn speak about herself that way.

Not critically.

Not without immediately turning it into a joke.

“What did she think?”

“She thought I chose work over her.”

“Did you?”

Evelyn looked at him.

Jamie nearly apologised.

Then remembered she had told him to ask better questions.

He held her gaze.

“Sometimes,” Evelyn said.

The honesty surprised him.

“Why?”

“Because work rewarded competence. Family does not operate so cleanly.”

Jamie frowned.

Evelyn continued.

“At work, if I solved a problem, the problem was solved. At home, a child could be angry because I had missed a school concert six months earlier and no amount of competence could make that evening happen again.”

Jamie thought about his mother promising weekends and cancelling them.

“You missed loads?”

“Too many.”

“Did she forgive you?”

“Sometimes.”

“That sounds like not really.”

“It means forgiveness is not a permanent condition.”

Jamie looked down at his science book.

“Did you stop working?”

“Eventually.”

“For her?”

“Partly. By then she was an adult and perfectly capable of informing me that my timing was appalling.”

Jamie smiled.

“What was she like?”

Evelyn looked towards the photograph again.

For a moment she seemed to forget Jamie was there.

“Loud.”

Jamie laughed.

“Really?”

“Exceptionally. She sang badly. She drove too fast. She believed any meal could be improved by adding garlic.”

“Sounds all right.”

“She was impossible.”

The words were affectionate.

“She trained as an architect for two years, decided she hated architects, then became an illustrator.”

“You told me she wasn't an artist.”

“She wasn't when she designed the window. Later she became one.”

Jamie pointed at her.

“You withheld information.”

“I refined the timeline.”

“That’s lying.”

“It is absolutely not.”

Jamie smiled.

“What did she draw?”

“Children's books. Advertising. Botanical work for a few publications. Whatever paid.”

“That’s why the window looks like that.”

“Yes.”

“The plants.”

“Yes.”

“And the bird?”

Evelyn looked at him.

Jamie grinned.

“Still private?”

“Still private.”

He returned to his homework.

After another few minutes he asked, “Was Izzy here when Catherine died?”

Evelyn's expression changed.

“Yes.”

“How old?”

“Twelve.”

“Did she know it was going to happen?”

“No.”

Jamie looked up.

“Cancer was fast?”

“Very.”

Evelyn folded her hands on the table.

“Catherine was diagnosed in February. She died in July.”

Jamie swallowed.

That was barely five months.

And it had been almost five years ago.

“She came back here after the diagnosis. Isabella with her.”

“So Izzy's lived here since then?”

“Yes.”

“What about her dad?”

Evelyn's mouth hardened.

“Unreliable.”

Jamie smiled faintly.

“She said basically the same.”

“She was kinder.”

Evelyn stood and moved towards the kettle.

Jamie watched her.

“You looked after both of them.”

“I attempted to.”

“Did Catherine forgive you then?”

Evelyn stopped.

The kettle clicked on.

For several seconds, the only sound was the water beginning to heat.

“She told me to stop asking.”

Jamie frowned.

“What?”

“Whether she forgave me.”

Evelyn turned around.

“She said I was making her final months about my guilt.”

Jamie had no answer.

“That sounds harsh.”

“It was correct.”

The kettle grew louder.

Evelyn's voice remained calm.

“She said if I wanted forgiveness, I should do something useful with the time we had rather than asking her to make me feel better about the time we'd lost.”

Jamie looked at Catherine's photograph.

“She sounds like you.”

Evelyn laughed unexpectedly.

A proper laugh.

“She would have despised that observation.”

Jamie smiled.

“What did you do?”

“With the time?”

He nodded.

“We sat in the conservatory. She drew. Isabella complained about everything. I cooked very poor meals. Catherine criticised them. We watched appalling television. Ordinary things.”

Evelyn looked towards the dark window.

“They were the best months we had had in years.”

Jamie felt his throat tighten.

He looked down quickly.

Evelyn noticed, because Evelyn noticed everything.

She did him the kindness of not mentioning it.

The front door opened somewhere in the hall.

“Gran?” Izzy shouted.

Evelyn called back, “Kitchen.”

Izzy entered carrying a shopping bag.

She stopped when she saw Jamie.

Her face lit up.

“What are you doing here?”

Jamie closed his science book.

“Being held hostage.”

Evelyn picked up her own book.

“He has completed nine questions in forty minutes. The rescue operation may stand down.”

Izzy came behind Jamie and kissed the top of his head.

“You're such a nerd.”

Jamie looked up at her.

“You live with an MI5 pensioner.”

“Former intelligence officer,” Evelyn corrected.

Izzy put the shopping down.

Jamie looked from her to Evelyn.

For a split second he saw Catherine between them.

Not literally.

In Izzy's eyes.

In Evelyn's expression.

In all the missing years neither of them knew what to do with.

Then Izzy stole his pencil and the moment disappeared.

“Give that back.”

“No.”

“Gran.”

Evelyn opened her book.

“This is Wednesday.”

Apparently that explained everything.
