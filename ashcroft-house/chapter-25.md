# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-Five — I Know

Jamie could hear the clock in the hall.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

He had heard that clock hundreds of times.

While sanding.

While eating lunch.

While Evelyn corrected his homework.

Now every second sounded enormous.

Izzy stood with her back against the study door.

Jamie held the missing stair rod across both hands.

“Tell me,” he said.

Izzy shook her head.

“Tell me I'm wrong.”

Tears ran down her face.

Jamie stepped towards her.

“Say the police put this here.”

Nothing.

“Say Gran—”

His voice broke on the word.

He had never called Evelyn that.

Not really.

“Say Evelyn moved it. Say your solicitor did. Say anything.”

Izzy covered her mouth.

Jamie stared at her.

“Please.”

She closed her eyes.

“I didn't plan it.”

The room tilted.

Jamie gripped the brass rod tighter.

“No.”

“I didn't.”

“No.”

“You have to listen.”

“I don't.”

“Jamie—”

“You killed her.”

Izzy flinched.

“I didn't mean to.”

“You hid this.”

“I panicked.”

The same word.

The lie about the argument.

The false alibi.

The shop.

Everything had been panic.

Jamie suddenly hated the word.

“What happened?”

Izzy looked at the floor.

“After you left, she went into the library.”

Jamie said nothing.

“I followed her.”

“The cup.”

Izzy nodded.

“I asked her about the will again.”

“Why?”

“Because I found the letter.”

She looked towards the papers on the carpet.

“Pryce sent a copy of the changes. Gran left it in the study. I opened it.”

Jamie felt sick.

“What changes?”

“The house.”

Izzy wiped her cheeks angrily.

“She was putting everything into stupid arrangements. Trusts. Conditions. I wouldn't be able to sell Ashcroft. I wouldn't control most of the money until I was older.”

“You're seventeen.”

“Don't.”

“You are.”

“Don't talk to me like her.”

Jamie stared.

“That’s what this was about?”

“It wasn't just money.”

“You killed her over money.”

“No!”

Izzy's shout filled the study.

“It was everything.”

Jamie stepped back.

Izzy pressed both hands against her head.

“You don't understand what it was like.”

“Then explain it.”

“She controlled everything. Every single thing. What school I went to. What I spent. Where I went. Whether I could travel. Whether I could touch Mum's letters. She treated me like I was waiting to become a bad person.”

Jamie looked at the stair rod.

“Maybe she was scared.”

“Of what?”

“This.”

Izzy's face collapsed.

“That’s not fair.”

“Fair?”

Jamie laughed once, a horrible sound.

“You want to talk about fair?”

“I loved her.”

“Then why is she dead?”

Izzy turned away.

Jamie wanted to stop.

He wanted to undo the last ten minutes.

He wanted to be back on the conservatory floor while rain hit the roof.

He wanted Izzy to be the girl who kissed him because thunder made her jump.

He wanted Evelyn alive.

None of those things were available.

“What happened on the stairs?” he asked.

Izzy's shoulders shook.

“She told me I was proving her right.”

“About what?”

“The will. She said the way I was talking about the house made her certain she needed to protect it.”

Jamie listened.

“She said Mum never cared about the money.”

“Did she?”

“No. Mum cared about plenty of things. She just wasn't around long enough to need it.”

Jamie recoiled.

Izzy looked horrified by her own sentence.

“I didn't mean that.”

“But you said it to Evelyn.”

Izzy nodded.

“What did she say?”

“Nothing.”

The word came out small.

“She just looked at me.”

Jamie knew that look.

Evelyn saying nothing was often worse than anger.

Izzy continued.

“She left the library. I followed her. We argued in the hall. I told her she loved Mum more than me.”

Jamie's throat tightened.

“What did she say?”

“She said I was wrong.”

“Then?”

“I asked if she'd put you in the will.”

Jamie froze.

“What?”

Izzy looked at him.

“I knew she had.”

“You didn't.”

“I could tell.”

“Did the letter say my name?”

“No.”

“Then you didn't know.”

“She changed after you came here.”

Jamie stared at her.

“The house changed. She changed. Suddenly she cared about everything again, and it was because of you.”

“That isn't my fault.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

Izzy looked down.

“She said you had nothing to do with what she was leaving me.”

Jamie felt his eyes burn.

“And then she said something about Mum. That Mum would've been ashamed of the way I was talking.”

Izzy swallowed.

“I told her I hated her.”

Jamie closed his eyes.

“She turned away.”

“Where?”

“Top of the stairs.”

Jamie opened his eyes again.

Izzy was crying silently now.

“I grabbed her arm.”

The bruise.

“She told me to let go.”

Jamie could barely breathe.

“I did. Then she said she wasn't going to spend the rest of her life being frightened of what I might do when she died.”

Izzy's voice became a whisper.

“And I pushed her.”

Silence.

Jamie looked towards the study door.

Beyond it waited the staircase.

He pictured Evelyn at the top.

Small.

Silver hair.

Walking stick.

The fall.

He stopped himself.

“Was she alive?”

Izzy covered her face.

“Was she alive after?”

“I don't know.”

“You found her.”

“I couldn't tell.”

“Did you call an ambulance?”

“No.”

Jamie's knees weakened.

“Why?”

“I was scared.”

“You left her there.”

“I thought she was dead.”

“You didn't know.”

“I couldn't feel anything.”

“So you called someone?”

Izzy shook her head.

“You called me?”

“No.”

“The police?”

“No.”

“What did you do?”

Izzy looked at the brass rod.

Jamie understood.

“No.”

“I thought if it looked like she tripped—”

“No.”

“I pulled the runner loose.”

Jamie dropped the rod.

It struck the carpet with a dull metallic thud.

Izzy flinched.

“I put her stick beside her. The cup had broken when we argued, so I cleaned it up. I went to the shop because I needed—”

“An alibi.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“I needed to get out.”

“You came back.”

“Yes.”

“And waited until morning.”

Izzy said nothing.

Jamie felt physically sick.

She had walked through the house while Evelyn lay at the bottom of the stairs.

She had gone to her room.

She had waited.

Then called for help when help could no longer help.

“I hate you,” Jamie whispered.

Izzy broke.

She crossed the room and grabbed him.

Jamie pushed her away.

“Don't touch me.”

“Please.”

“Don't.”

“I love you.”

“Stop saying that.”

“It’s true.”

Jamie looked at her.

He remembered Evelyn's warning.

Love does not automatically make people good.

That was the cruelest part.

He believed Izzy.

He believed she loved him.

And now he understood that it changed nothing.

“I have to tell them.”

Izzy went still.

Jamie reached for his phone.

“Don't.”

“I have to.”

“Jamie.”

He unlocked the screen.

Izzy stepped between him and the door.

“Move.”

“Just think.”

“I've thought enough.”

“If you tell them, I'm finished.”

Jamie laughed bitterly.

“Evelyn is dead.”

“I know.”

“Because of you.”

“I know!”

Her voice cracked.

She wiped her face.

“I'm not asking you to pretend it didn't happen.”

“Yes, you are.”

“No. I'm asking you to understand.”

“I understand exactly.”

“You don't.”

Izzy took a step closer.

“Everything can still be okay.”

Jamie stared at her.

She gestured around the room.

“This house. The money. Gran was going to leave most of it to me anyway.”

Jamie's stomach turned.

“You're talking about the money now?”

“No. I'm talking about us.”

“There is no us.”

Izzy flinched.

Jamie continued.

“Not after this.”

“You don't mean that.”

“I do.”

“We could stay here.”

Jamie stared in disbelief.

“What?”

“You love Ashcroft.”

“Evelyn loved Ashcroft.”

“So did you.”

“I loved being here with her.”

“With me.”

“Yes. Before I knew you killed her.”

Izzy shook her head frantically.

“You said you loved me.”

“I did.”

“Did?”

Jamie looked at her.

He hated that he still knew exactly how her face changed before she cried.

He hated that part of him wanted to comfort her.

“I don't know what I feel.”

“Then don't decide now.”

“I know what I have to do.”

He moved towards the door.

Izzy did not move.

“Please.”

“Izzy.”

“If you walk out, you'll never see me again.”

Jamie swallowed.

“I know.”

She stared at him.

Jamie reached for the handle.

Izzy turned the key in the study door.

Click.

Jamie looked down.

Then slowly back at her.

“Izzy.”

She pulled the key free.

“I can't let you leave.”

And for the first time since he'd met her, Jamie was afraid of the girl he loved.
