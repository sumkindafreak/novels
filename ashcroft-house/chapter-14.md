# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Fourteen — Family

The first time Gareth ate dinner at Ashcroft House, nobody planned it.

He arrived at six expecting Jamie to be ready.

Jamie was not ready.

Jamie was halfway up a ladder in the conservatory holding a length of timber while a glazier named Martin shouted measurements from outside.

Izzy was carrying panes of replacement glass from the kitchen.

Evelyn was supervising all of them from a chair with the expression of a woman who believed the project would collapse without her continued disappointment.

Gareth stepped through the conservatory doors.

“What’s going on?”

Jamie looked down.

“Roof.”

“I can see that.”

“Martin had a cancellation.”

Gareth looked at the exposed section above Jamie.

“You're not touching the glazing, are you?”

“No.”

“Or climbing outside?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Evelyn said, “I have already threatened him.”

Gareth nodded.

“Appreciate it.”

Jamie climbed down.

“You two coordinating punishments now?”

“Only the effective ones,” Evelyn said.

Izzy handed Gareth a piece of timber.

“Since you're here.”

Gareth took it automatically.

Jamie laughed.

“Welcome to the cult.”

---

The work ran late.

By seven, the temporary covering was secure and the worst section of the conservatory roof no longer admitted weather directly into the building.

Martin packed his tools.

Gareth helped him carry a ladder to the van.

Jamie swept broken sealant and sawdust from the tiles.

Izzy sat on the table eating crisps.

“Why are you never working?” Jamie asked.

“I carried glass.”

“Three pieces.”

“They were emotionally heavy.”

Jamie threw the broom at her feet.

“Do something.”

Izzy picked it up.

Then leaned it against the wall.

“Done.”

Jamie stared.

Evelyn called from the kitchen.

“Food.”

Jamie looked at the clock.

“Dad'll want to go.”

Gareth entered behind him.

“Actually, she threatened me.”

Jamie smiled.

“With what?”

“Stew.”

Evelyn appeared in the doorway.

“It is not a threat.”

Gareth removed his coat.

“Debatable.”

Jamie looked at him.

“You're staying?”

“Apparently.”

Izzy grinned.

“Family dinner.”

The words came easily.

Too easily.

Nobody reacted immediately.

Then Izzy's expression shifted, as though she wished she could pull them back.

Evelyn looked at her granddaughter.

Jamie looked at Gareth.

Gareth broke the silence.

“Does family dinner include beer?”

Evelyn said, “One.”

“Then I'm family.”

Everyone laughed.

The moment passed.

Almost.

---

They ate in the smaller dining room because Evelyn claimed the main dining room was “designed for people who require distance from each other.”

The smaller room was still bigger than Jamie's entire downstairs.

A fire burned in the grate.

Rain tapped gently against the windows.

The stew was better than Jamie expected.

He told Evelyn so.

She looked insulted.

“What did you expect?”

“Your tea.”

Izzy laughed into her drink.

Gareth nodded seriously.

“He’s got a point.”

Evelyn looked at him.

“You drank two cups.”

“I was cold.”

“Coward.”

Jamie stared.

“Did Evelyn Ashcroft just call my dad a coward?”

“I believe she did,” Gareth said.

“Historic evening.”

Dinner became one of those rare nights that seemed to assemble itself without effort.

Gareth told the story of Jamie trying to fix the garden gate when he was nine using cable ties and a spoon.

Jamie strongly disputed several details.

Izzy demanded photographic evidence.

Evelyn sided with Gareth on principle.

Then Izzy told Gareth about Jamie nearly falling into a compost bag because he had been staring at her instead of walking.

Jamie disputed this even more strongly.

“You absolutely were,” Izzy said.

“I tripped.”

“Because you were staring.”

“Where else was I supposed to look?”

“Forward?”

Evelyn took a drink of wine.

“I feel the education system has failed him.”

Gareth raised his bottle.

“To that.”

Jamie threw a bread roll at him.

For a while, Evelyn laughed more than Jamie had ever heard her laugh.

Not loudly.

But often.

He noticed Izzy watching her.

At first Izzy smiled too.

Then something changed.

The smile remained, but it became fixed.

Jamie recognised it only because Evelyn had taught him the difference between looking and noticing.

He wondered what Izzy saw.

Her grandmother laughing with Jamie's father?

Jamie sitting in a chair Catherine had once used?

A version of Ashcroft House that had begun moving again without asking whether Izzy was ready?

He did not know.

He almost asked later.

He didn't.

---

After dinner, Gareth offered to help clear the table.

Evelyn refused.

Gareth did it anyway.

Jamie and Izzy escaped into the hall.

The restored stained-glass window had returned two days earlier.

Not fully installed yet. Peter and Ruth had set it temporarily in a protective frame while the last work on the original surround dried.

It stood propped beside the staircase, covered with fabric.

Jamie lifted one corner.

Izzy slapped his hand.

“Don't.”

“I just want to look.”

“You've looked about fifty times.”

“I broke it. I'm allowed.”

“That logic is insane.”

Jamie smiled.

Izzy leaned against the banister.

“You like this, don't you?”

“What?”

“All of it.”

Jamie looked around.

The hall still had cracks.

Still had damp.

Still had a bucket under one leak.

But the morning room door closed.

The library window no longer let in rain.

Fresh plaster dried on one section of wall.

The conservatory roof was finally beginning to resemble a roof.

“Yeah,” he said.

Izzy's face was hard to read.

“You're weird.”

“You live here.”

“I didn't choose it.”

Jamie frowned.

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

Izzy shrugged.

“It means everybody looks at this place and thinks it's amazing.”

“It is amazing.”

“Because you get to leave.”

Jamie leaned against the opposite banister.

“You could leave.”

“With what money?”

He hesitated.

“You said your gran pays for stuff.”

“Exactly.”

“What's wrong with that?”

Izzy laughed without humour.

“Everything here belongs to her. House. Money. Car. My school fees. My phone. My clothes. If she decides no, then no.”

Jamie thought of the bank papers.

“She's not exactly mean with money.”

“That’s not the point.”

“What is?”

Izzy looked towards the dining room.

“She gets to decide.”

Jamie didn't immediately understand why the words felt so sharp.

Before he could answer, Gareth called his name.

“Coming.”

Jamie grabbed his coat.

Izzy kissed him quickly beside the front door.

“Text me.”

“I will.”

He smiled and started outside.

Then looked back.

Izzy stood beneath the staircase.

Behind her, Catherine's covered stained glass waited to be put back where it belonged.

Evelyn's laughter carried faintly from the dining room.

Izzy was no longer smiling.

Jamie almost asked why.

Izzy reached for the door.

“Text me,” she said again.

Then it closed.
