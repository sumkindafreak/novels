# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-Seven — The Truth

Jamie spent four hours in hospital and seventeen minutes being examined.

The rest was waiting.

His ankle was sprained.

His shoulder was badly bruised.

There were scratches across his hand and a dark mark forming along his ribs where the banister had caught him.

Nothing was broken.

Gareth repeated this several times as if each repetition might make the afternoon less terrible.

“Nothing broken.”

Jamie sat on the edge of the hospital bed.

“I heard you.”

“Good.”

“You've said it four times.”

“Have I?”

“Yes.”

Gareth nodded.

“Nothing broken.”

Jamie almost laughed.

Then he saw his father's hands.

They were shaking.

Jamie stopped smiling.

“Dad.”

Gareth looked away.

“You scared the life out of me.”

“I know.”

“No. I mean it.”

Jamie swallowed.

“I’m sorry.”

Gareth pressed his lips together.

Then stepped forward and pulled Jamie against him.

Jamie winced as his shoulder protested.

“Sorry,” Gareth said.

“It’s fine.”

“Don't ever do that again.”

“Get chased through a Victorian house by my murderous girlfriend?”

Gareth let out a laugh that broke halfway through.

“Preferably.”

Jamie held onto him.

For a moment he was five years old again.

Then twelve.

Then fifteen.

All the ages a child became when his father was frightened enough.

---

Detective Sergeant Morris came to the hospital that evening.

She did not say I told you so.

Jamie respected her for that.

She took a full account with Gareth present.

The study.

The stair rod.

Izzy's confession.

The struggle.

The first-floor landing.

The push.

The escape.

When Jamie finished, Morris closed her notebook.

“We recovered the stair rod where you described.”

Jamie nodded.

“And the solicitor's correspondence.”

“Okay.”

“We also recovered your phone.”

“Is it dead?”

“The screen has seen better days.”

“Typical.”

Morris almost smiled.

Then became serious again.

“Isabella has been arrested on suspicion of Evelyn's murder and the attempted murder of you.”

The words entered the room slowly.

Jamie stared at the floor.

Murder.

Attempted murder.

Izzy.

He still could not make the words belong together.

“Did she tell you?”

“I can't discuss her interview.”

“Did she admit it?”

“Jamie.”

“Please.”

Morris looked at Gareth.

Then back at Jamie.

“She has made significant admissions. That's all I can tell you.”

Jamie nodded.

It should have felt like relief.

He had been right.

The truth was out.

Instead he felt hollow.

Morris stood.

“One more thing.”

Jamie looked up.

“The false statement you gave earlier.”

Gareth's jaw tightened.

Jamie waited.

“What you did was serious,” Morris said. “But you corrected it yourself, you are fifteen, and you were being manipulated by someone you trusted while grieving a sudden death. I cannot promise how every procedural decision is made, but our priority here is the investigation into Evelyn's death and what happened to you.”

Jamie nodded.

“I’m sorry.”

“I believe you.”

She paused at the door.

“Mrs Ashcroft taught you to notice things, didn't she?”

Jamie frowned.

“How do you know?”

“You told me about the photographs and the stair runner. Most people wouldn't have looked twice.”

Jamie looked down.

“She would've noticed sooner.”

Morris considered that.

“Maybe.”

“No. She would.”

“Perhaps.”

Morris opened the door.

“But she also trusted her granddaughter.”

Jamie looked at her.

“Being observant doesn't make you invulnerable.”

Then she left.

Jamie sat in silence.

Gareth handed him his coat.

“Ready?”

Jamie nodded.

He was not.

---

Evelyn's funeral took place twelve days later.

The chapel was fuller than Jamie expected.

He had imagined a handful of people.

Evelyn had always seemed alone.

Instead old colleagues came from London.

Neighbours Jamie had never seen inside Ashcroft House sat in the back rows.

Peter and Ruth Bowen came.

Martin the glazier came.

Margaret Pryce sat near the front.

There were people with silver hair and careful voices who introduced themselves to Jamie by first name only and seemed to know far more about Evelyn than they were willing to say.

One elderly man shook Jamie's hand.

“Michael Webb.”

“Hi.”

“Evelyn mentioned you.”

Jamie blinked.

“She did?”

“Repeatedly.”

“What did she say?”

Michael smiled.

“That you were a vandal.”

Jamie laughed before he could stop himself.

The laugh turned into a sob.

Michael squeezed his shoulder.

“And that you were learning.”

Jamie nodded.

“Yeah.”

He looked towards Evelyn's coffin.

“I was.”

---

Izzy was not at the funeral.

Nobody said her name during the service.

That felt wrong too.

Whatever she had done, she had been part of Evelyn's life.

Catherine's daughter.

The little girl in the photograph.

The teenager who had sat beside Evelyn beneath the restored window.

Jamie hated her.

He missed her.

Both were true.

That was perhaps the hardest thing Evelyn had ever taught him without meaning to.

Contradictory things could both be true.

Love did not cancel harm.

Grief did not cancel anger.

Understanding did not require forgiveness.

After the service, Gareth found Jamie standing alone beside a low stone wall behind the chapel.

The same chapel wall where Jamie and Izzy had once argued about five thousand pounds.

“Ready to go?” Gareth asked.

“In a minute.”

Gareth stood beside him.

Across the lane, Ashcroft House was visible through bare winter trees.

“What happens to it?” Jamie asked.

“The house?”

“Yeah.”

“No idea.”

“Margaret knows.”

“Probably.”

Jamie looked at his father.

“Do you think they'll sell it?”

Gareth shrugged.

“Depends on the will, I suppose.”

Jamie looked back at the house.

The thought of strangers buying it hurt unexpectedly.

They would paint the walls.

Rip out the old kitchen.

Call the conservatory dated.

Remove the servants' bells.

Maybe replace Catherine's window because it wasn't energy efficient.

Jamie hated all of them and they did not even exist.

“Don't start buying it in your head,” Gareth said.

Jamie smiled faintly.

“We couldn't afford the gate.”

“Probably not.”

They stood in silence.

Margaret Pryce approached from the chapel.

“Jamie?”

He turned.

She carried a black folder beneath one arm.

“I need to speak to you and your father at some point.”

Gareth frowned.

“About the investigation?”

“No.”

Margaret looked towards Ashcroft House.

“About Evelyn's estate.”

Jamie felt something shift in his stomach.

“Why me?”

Margaret looked at him.

There was the same careful expression she had worn the first day in the study.

“Not today.”

Jamie stared.

“What does that mean?”

“It means today is for saying goodbye.”

She handed Gareth a card.

“Call my office after Christmas.”

Then she walked away.

Jamie looked at his father.

Gareth looked at the card.

Neither said anything.

Across the lane, winter sun struck Catherine's stained-glass window.

A tiny red shape caught the light.

The bird.

For the first time since Evelyn died, Jamie wondered whether she had left one more secret behind.
