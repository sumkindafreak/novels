# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Eleven — Debt Paid

Jamie spent the following week discovering that first kisses created practical problems nobody mentioned.

The main one was school.

School required him to sit through lessons while his brain replayed Saturday every seven seconds.

Rain on glass.

Izzy laughing.

Her forehead against his.

The lights coming back on.

He wrote the date wrong twice in science.

He walked into a chair in English.

He answered “yeah” when Mr Walters asked him to simplify an equation.

Callum noticed by Tuesday.

“Right.”

Jamie looked up from his lunch.

“Right what?”

“You've kissed her.”

Jamie nearly inhaled a chip.

Leon slapped the table.

“I knew it.”

Jamie coughed.

“What are you talking about?”

Callum pointed at him.

“That face.”

“What face?”

“The one where you're pretending nothing happened so hard that something definitely happened.”

Jamie wiped his mouth.

“You're both mental.”

Leon leaned closer.

“Was it the granddaughter?”

Jamie stood with his tray.

Callum shouted after him.

“THAT’S A YES.”

Half the dining hall turned.

Jamie kept walking.

He would have killed both of them if he had not needed witnesses later.

---

Saturday came slowly.

Jamie was awake before his alarm.

He hated himself for this.

He spent too long choosing a hoodie, realised what he was doing, changed back into the first one and went downstairs annoyed with everyone despite being alone.

Gareth was already in the kitchen.

“You're cheerful.”

“I'm fine.”

“You’re wearing aftershave.”

Jamie froze.

“No, I'm not.”

Gareth looked at him.

Jamie looked at the kettle.

His father smiled.

“Oh, dear.”

“Shut up.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I really wasn't.”

“You had the face.”

“What face?”

Jamie glared.

Gareth laughed and slid a plate of toast towards him.

“You seeing Izzy?”

Jamie stared at the toast.

“Maybe.”

“That means yes.”

“It’s not a big thing.”

“Didn't say it was.”

“We just kissed.”

Gareth raised both eyebrows.

Jamie regretted speaking.

“Once.”

“Still didn't ask.”

“You were thinking it.”

“I was thinking you're fifteen and suddenly interested in smelling less like a changing room.”

Jamie threw a tea towel at him.

Gareth caught it.

Then his expression softened.

“She seems nice.”

Jamie looked at him.

“You've barely spoken to her.”

“I've spoken to her enough to know she can hold a conversation.”

“That’s a low bar.”

“You'd be surprised.”

Gareth poured coffee.

“Just remember she's seventeen.”

“I know.”

“And living in a complicated situation.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

Jamie frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“It means exactly what I said.”

“You don't like her.”

“I didn't say that either.”

“You’re doing the dad thing.”

“What dad thing?”

“Where you say something normal but it means another thing.”

Gareth smiled.

“You've been spending too much time with Evelyn.”

Jamie hated that everyone had started saying this.

---

At Ashcroft House, Evelyn did not mention the kiss.

This was worse.

Jamie was convinced she knew.

She seemed to know everything else.

Izzy did not appear for the first hour.

Jamie worked in the hallway fitting a new length of moulding where water damage had ruined the old piece.

Evelyn watched him measure.

“Again.”

“I measured it.”

“Again.”

Jamie sighed and measured a second time.

The number was different.

Evelyn raised an eyebrow.

“Say nothing.”

“I wasn't going to.”

“You were absolutely going to.”

Jamie marked the timber.

From the staircase came Izzy's voice.

“Morning, vandal.”

Jamie turned too quickly.

Izzy was halfway down the stairs wearing an old denim jacket over a red jumper.

She smiled.

Jamie smiled back.

Evelyn looked between them.

There it was.

The look.

Jamie returned to the timber.

“Morning.”

Izzy came closer.

“Just morning?”

“Gran is literally there.”

Evelyn said, “I remain capable of hearing.”

Izzy kissed Jamie on the cheek.

He nearly dropped the tape measure.

Evelyn closed her eyes.

“Good grief.”

Izzy laughed and walked towards the kitchen.

Jamie stared after her.

Evelyn tapped his boot with her stick.

“Measure.”

“Yes, boss.”

---

At lunch, Gareth arrived unexpectedly.

Jamie was in the kitchen with Izzy when the front bell clanged.

Evelyn went to answer it.

A moment later Gareth appeared.

“Everything all right?” Jamie asked.

“Yeah.”

His father looked strangely formal.

“Evelyn asked me to come up.”

Jamie looked at her.

“Why?”

Evelyn sat at the head of the table.

“Because your indenture has concluded.”

Jamie frowned.

“My what?”

“Your punishment.” Gareth translated.

Jamie looked between them.

Evelyn folded her hands.

“The value of the work you've completed now exceeds any reasonable contribution towards the damage. The window restoration is paid for. Your father owes me nothing. Neither do you.”

Jamie sat very still.

“Oh.”

He had imagined this moment for weeks.

Back in September, he had pictured fireworks.

Sleeping until noon.

Sending Callum a photograph of himself doing absolutely nothing on Saturday morning.

Instead the kitchen suddenly felt strange.

Izzy watched him.

Gareth took a drink of tea.

Evelyn said, “You are free.”

Jamie nodded.

“Right.”

Nobody spoke.

“Well,” Gareth said eventually. “There we are.”

“Yeah.”

“You've done what you were asked.”

“Yeah.”

Jamie looked towards the window.

The conservatory was visible through the rain-spotted glass.

There were still two benches to repair.

The pantry shelves needed another coat.

The library window frame had a soft section Gareth wanted to inspect.

The west gutter still sagged.

Catherine's stained glass had not yet returned.

Evelyn stood.

“I assume you would like your Saturday mornings back.”

Jamie heard himself answer.

“Not next week.”

Everyone looked at him.

Jamie felt his face warm.

“I mean, I've already started the library window.”

Gareth's mouth twitched.

“And?”

“And I should finish it.”

Evelyn studied him.

“There is no obligation.”

“I know.”

“And I will not pay you.”

“Wouldn't expect you to.”

Izzy smiled into her mug.

Jamie pointed at her.

“Don't.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were going to.”

“Had the face?”

Jamie rolled his eyes.

Gareth stood and put his coat on.

“Well, I'll leave you to your entirely voluntary unpaid labour.”

“Dad.”

“Ten o’clock next Saturday?”

Jamie looked at Evelyn.

Evelyn looked back.

There was something different in her expression.

Not triumph.

Not amusement.

Something quieter.

“If you wish,” she said.

Jamie shrugged as though it meant nothing.

“Yeah. Probably.”

Gareth waited until they were outside before speaking.

They stood beside the van beneath a grey sky.

“You know the library window isn't why you're coming back.”

Jamie shoved his hands into his pockets.

“I know.”

Gareth looked towards an upstairs window.

Izzy was visible behind the glass.

Jamie followed his gaze.

His father said, “Just be careful.”

Jamie frowned.

“With what?”

Gareth considered the question.

“People who feel like home very quickly.”

Jamie looked at him.

“That’s weirdly deep for you.”

“Get in the van.”

Jamie laughed.

But as they drove away, he looked back at Ashcroft House until the trees hid it from view.
