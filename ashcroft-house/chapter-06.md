# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Six — The Conservatory

The conservatory had been trying to collapse for years.

Izzy told Jamie this as if it were a personality trait.

“It’s dramatic,” she said.

Jamie stood outside the warped glass doors and looked up.

Two roof panels were missing entirely. Three others had been covered with cloudy plastic. Ivy had climbed through a broken side vent and was now growing inside with such confidence that it seemed to believe it owned the place.

A rusted metal table leaned against one wall.

Dead plant pots were stacked beneath it.

The tiled floor had disappeared beneath leaves, soil and years of whatever Ashcroft House had dropped through the holes above.

Jamie folded his arms.

“And your gran wants this cleared?”

“No.”

He looked at Izzy.

“She doesn't know we're doing it.”

Jamie stared at her.

“That sentence has never ended well for me.”

Izzy pushed the door.

It moved three inches and stuck.

She pushed harder.

Nothing.

Jamie stepped beside her.

“Move.”

“I can open a door.”

“Clearly.”

Together they shoved.

The frame groaned, something scraped across the floor inside and the door finally gave way with a shower of dust from above.

Izzy coughed.

Jamie waved the air in front of his face.

“Excellent. We’ll die before she catches us.”

“Efficient.”

They stepped inside.

The conservatory smelled of wet earth, moss and old timber.

Sunlight came through the remaining glass in fractured shapes. In places, green algae had turned the panes almost opaque.

Jamie walked slowly between the debris.

“This was nice once.”

Izzy kicked an empty terracotta pot aside.

“Mum loved it.”

Jamie looked at her.

He had learned not to ask immediately whenever Catherine came up.

Sometimes Izzy spoke.

Sometimes she shut down.

Today she continued.

“She used to grow tomatoes over there.”

She pointed towards a long raised bed against the wall.

“And strawberries. Gran pretended not to care about the strawberries, but she used to come out here and steal them.”

Jamie smiled.

“Your gran stole fruit from her own greenhouse?”

“Conservatory.”

“Same thing.”

“Say that to Gran and see what happens.”

Jamie looked around.

“What happened to it?”

Izzy shrugged.

“Nothing.”

“That’s sort of the problem.”

“Exactly.”

She crouched and began gathering broken pots into a crate.

Jamie joined her.

“You could just ask her to pay someone.”

“She won't.”

“Why?”

Izzy's hands stopped for a moment.

“She doesn't like people doing things for her.”

“She lets me.”

“You’re punishment.”

“Thanks.”

Izzy grinned.

“Also, you don't count.”

Jamie threw a dead leaf at her.

It hit her in the hair.

She stared at him.

“Oh.”

Jamie backed away.

“Don't.”

Izzy picked up a handful of leaves.

“Don't.”

She threw them.

Jamie ducked.

Most missed.

One damp clump landed down the back of his jumper.

He yelled.

Izzy doubled over laughing.

“You absolute—”

Jamie grabbed a broom and pushed a pile of leaves towards her feet.

She jumped onto the old metal bench.

“Coward.”

“You've got a weapon.”

“It’s a broom.”

“You're emotionally unstable.”

“Fair.”

They were both laughing when a sharp tap sounded against the glass behind them.

Jamie froze.

Evelyn stood outside.

Walking stick raised.

Expression unreadable.

Izzy whispered, “Run.”

“There is one door.”

“Then accept death.”

Evelyn opened the door.

Or tried to.

It stuck.

Jamie and Izzy looked at each other.

Neither moved.

Evelyn pushed again.

The door remained stubbornly closed.

Jamie bit the inside of his cheek.

Izzy looked at the ceiling.

Evelyn stepped back.

Then brought the silver handle of her walking stick sharply against the frame.

The vibration shifted whatever was blocking it.

The door opened.

Jamie stopped smiling immediately.

“Hello.”

Evelyn looked around the conservatory.

At the crates.

At the cleared section of floor.

At the broom in Jamie's hand.

At the leaves in Izzy's hair.

“What are you doing?”

“Tidying,” Jamie said.

“I can see that.”

Izzy jumped down from the bench.

“It was disgusting.”

“It remains disgusting.”

“Less disgusting.”

Evelyn looked towards the raised bed Catherine had once used.

Something changed in her face.

Jamie saw it this time.

The same stillness as in Catherine's old bedroom.

He lowered the broom.

“We can stop.”

Evelyn looked at him.

“Why?”

“I don't know. If you don't want us in here.”

She stepped farther inside.

Her stick clicked against the tiles.

She stopped beside the raised bed.

A single green shoot had pushed through the old soil among the weeds.

Evelyn touched one leaf with the tip of her finger.

“Catherine planted lavender there,” she said.

Izzy came beside her.

“I remember.”

“No, you don't.”

“I do.”

“You were five.”

“I remember the bees.”

Evelyn looked at her granddaughter.

Izzy smiled faintly.

For a moment they seemed completely different from the two women Jamie usually saw trading insults across the kitchen.

Not Gran and granddaughter.

Mother and child, almost.

Or two people connected by the same missing person.

Jamie looked away.

“Can we fix it?” he asked.

Evelyn turned.

“The conservatory?”

“Eventually.”

“With what expertise?”

“I fixed a door.”

“A magnificent qualification.”

“I can learn.”

Evelyn glanced up at the damaged roof.

“That would require a glazier. Some of the lower timber is rotten. The drainage is inadequate. The roof structure may need strengthening.”

Jamie blinked.

“So you have thought about it.”

Evelyn looked at him.

He smiled.

“You know exactly what’s wrong with everything in this house.”

“That is not difficult.”

“Then why don't you fix it?”

Izzy went still.

Jamie knew immediately he had stepped into something.

Evelyn's expression closed.

“I have other priorities.”

“You live here.”

“Yes.”

“So does Izzy.”

“Jamie,” Izzy said quietly.

He ignored her.

“The gutter’s hanging off. The roof leaks. Half the rooms are shut. You've got buckets catching rain indoors.”

Evelyn stared at him.

Jamie suddenly wished his mouth had an emergency stop button.

He lowered his eyes.

“Sorry.”

Silence filled the conservatory.

Then Evelyn said, “Do you imagine I haven't noticed?”

Jamie looked up.

“No.”

“I spent the greater part of my adult life maintaining things that mattered to other people.”

Her voice was controlled.

Not angry.

That made it heavier.

“When Catherine died, I found I had rather less enthusiasm for maintaining anything at all.”

Izzy looked at the floor.

Evelyn looked towards the dead raised bed.

“For a while, deterioration felt appropriately honest.”

Jamie didn't completely understand what she meant.

But he understood enough.

“You could start again,” he said.

Evelyn gave him a long look.

“Could I?”

Jamie shrugged.

“We already started.”

He pointed at the crates of rubbish.

Izzy glanced at him.

Evelyn looked around the cleared patch of tile.

A reluctant smile appeared.

“Barely.”

“Still counts.”

Evelyn nodded once.

“Very well.”

Jamie brightened.

“We can fix it?”

“I said you may clear it.”

“Oh.”

“And Jamie?”

“Yeah?”

“If you climb onto that roof, I will personally tell your father.”

He laughed.

“Deal.”

Evelyn left them to it.

When she had gone, Izzy nudged Jamie with her shoulder.

“You're weird.”

“What?”

“Nobody talks to her like that.”

“You do.”

“No. I argue with her.”

“What’s the difference?”

Izzy thought about it.

“I’m trying to win.”

Jamie picked up the broom again.

“And me?”

She watched him.

“You're trying to make her care.”

Jamie looked towards the door Evelyn had disappeared through.

He didn't have an answer.

So he swept.
