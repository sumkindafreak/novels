# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Twenty-Three — Evelyn's Lessons

Jamie made the timeline at two fourteen in the morning.

He used the back page of his maths book because it was the only paper within reach and because ruining maths felt appropriate.

He wrote:

**SATURDAY**

Then underneath:

**10:00 — Ashcroft**

**Window fitted**

**Dad leaves afternoon**

**Dinner**

**7:22 — text Dad, leaving**

**7:43 — home**

**7:45 — Izzy: love you x**

He stared at the final line.

Then added:

**SUNDAY 5:58 — grans dead**

Jamie crossed out *grans* and wrote *Evelyn*.

He sat against the headboard with the notebook on his knees.

Outside, rain moved against the window.

His room was dark except for the lamp beside his bed.

People spend their lives looking.

Very few notice.

“All right,” Jamie whispered.

He tried to imagine Evelyn sitting opposite him.

Not comforting him.

She would be terrible at comforting him.

Questioning him.

**What do you know?**

Jamie wrote:

**Stair runner was fixed.**

**Rod missing.**

**Cup broken.**

**Solicitor envelope gone.**

Then:

**Izzy lied about argument.**

He stopped.

The pen hovered.

He added:

**I lied about 20 mins.**

Seeing it written made him feel sick.

He turned the page over.

Then turned it back.

Evelyn would not let him hide a fact because he disliked it.

He picked up his phone.

His camera roll was full of Ashcroft House.

He had never realised how many photographs he'd taken.

Doors he'd repaired.

The conservatory.

Izzy covered in paint.

Evelyn asleep in a chair with a newspaper over her lap.

Jamie smiled despite himself.

He kept scrolling.

Saturday.

Catherine's window being lifted into position.

Peter on a ladder.

Gareth pretending not to pose while holding a drill.

Izzy making a face behind Martin's back.

Jamie opened one photograph and zoomed in.

The staircase filled the background.

Red runner.

Brass rods.

All of them.

Including the one now missing.

He checked the time stamp.

**12:46.**

Jamie sat straighter.

That proved the rod was there at lunchtime.

Not enough.

A lot could happen in seven hours.

He kept scrolling.

At 4:58, Izzy had taken his phone and photographed him carrying boxes of Christmas lights through the hall.

The staircase was behind him.

Jamie zoomed.

Rod present.

5:41.

A photo of the little fir tree beneath the window.

Rod present.

6:37.

Izzy had taken a photograph of the coloured light from the stained glass falling across the stairs.

Jamie zoomed until the image broke into pixels.

The rod was there.

Secure.

Forty-five minutes before he left.

His skin prickled.

He got out of bed.

His first instinct was to wake Gareth.

He reached the bedroom door.

Then stopped.

If he showed Gareth, Gareth would call the police.

If the police looked closely, the twenty-minute lie would collapse.

Jamie closed his eyes.

Evelyn again.

Find out what is true.

Not what is convenient.

He opened the door.

---

Gareth was asleep on the sofa.

The television flickered silently in front of him.

Jamie stood in the doorway.

“Dad.”

Nothing.

“Dad.”

Gareth woke with a start.

“What?”

“Sorry.”

He sat up and rubbed his face.

“What time is it?”

“Two thirty.”

Gareth stared at him.

“This had better be excellent.”

Jamie held out the phone.

“Look.”

His father took it.

Jamie showed him the photographs.

One by one.

The staircase.

The brass rod.

The time stamps.

Gareth's tiredness disappeared.

“That’s the one missing now?”

“Yes.”

“You sure?”

“I fitted it with you.”

Gareth zoomed into the 6:37 photograph.

“It could've come away later.”

“In forty-five minutes?”

“Could have.”

“But?”

Gareth looked at him.

“But it makes me uncomfortable.”

Jamie sat on the armchair opposite.

“What do I do?”

“You show Morris.”

Jamie looked down.

Gareth waited.

There was no way around it now.

“I lied.”

His father did not react immediately.

Jamie continued before he lost his nerve.

“Izzy didn't walk with me.”

Gareth's face became completely still.

“The twenty minutes.”

“Didn't happen.”

“Why?”

“She asked me to say it.”

Gareth inhaled slowly.

“Why?”

“Because they argued after I left. Izzy went to the shop. She thought police would think she did something if they knew they argued.”

Gareth stood.

Jamie flinched.

His father saw it and stopped.

He pressed both hands against his face.

“Jesus Christ, Jamie.”

“I know.”

“No, I don't think you do.”

“I know I shouldn't have.”

“You lied to a detective investigating a death.”

“I thought it was an accident.”

“So did everyone.”

Jamie felt tears burn behind his eyes.

“She has nobody.”

Gareth lowered his hands.

“That does not mean you become her alibi.”

“I know.”

“Does she know about the stair rod photo?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Jamie looked at him sharply.

His father was scared.

Jamie had never seen that expression directed at anything involving one of his friends.

“Dad.”

“From now on, you do not go to that house alone.”

Jamie stood.

“You think she did something.”

“I think I don't know.”

“That's what you said before.”

“It’s still true.”

“But now?”

Gareth looked at the phone in his hand.

“Now I think the police need to know everything.”

Jamie's own phone rang.

Both of them jumped.

The screen showed an unknown number.

Jamie stared.

“Answer it,” Gareth said.

Jamie did.

“Hello?”

“Jamie? Detective Sergeant Morris.”

His stomach dropped.

“Hi.”

“Sorry to call late. I tried your father first.”

Gareth checked his phone on the sofa.

Two missed calls.

Morris continued.

“I need to speak to you both tomorrow.”

Jamie swallowed.

“Okay.”

“There’s something from your original statement we need to clarify.”

Jamie looked at Gareth.

“What?”

“The walk from Ashcroft House.”

Silence.

Morris's voice remained calm.

“A neighbour's doorbell camera covers part of Chapel Lane. It shows you passing at seven twenty-seven.”

Jamie closed his eyes.

Alone.

He knew the next word before she said it.

“Alone.”

Gareth watched him.

Morris continued.

“So I need you to think very carefully about what you told me, Jamie.”

He tightened his grip on the phone.

“I don't need to think.”

A pause.

“What do you mean?”

Jamie looked at the timeline on his maths book.

At the line he had finally written.

**I lied about 20 mins.**

He took a breath.

“I lied.”

And for the first time since Evelyn died, Jamie chose the truth even though it frightened him.
