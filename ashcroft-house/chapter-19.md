# Ashcroft House

## Chapter Nineteen — Not Dreadful, Morgan

Catherine's window went back into Ashcroft House on the first Saturday in December.

Everybody came to watch.

Peter and Ruth arrived before nine with crates of tools, lead, sealants and the careful expressions of people about to handle something irreplaceable.

Catherine's restored glass was already waiting in the hall beneath its protective covering, exactly where it had been since Peter and Ruth brought it back for the final frame work.

Martin the glazier came half an hour later.

Gareth brought a box of tools nobody asked for but everyone eventually used.

Izzy made coffee.

Evelyn made tea.

Jamie warned everyone about the tea.

Evelyn threatened to remove him from the premises.

By ten fifteen, the entrance hall had become a workshop.

Protective sheets covered the floor.

Ladders rose to the first-floor landing.

The temporary board came away from the window opening, letting a blade of cold December air into the house.

Jamie stood below staring up at the empty frame.

It looked strange now.

For months he had known it damaged.

First jagged and broken.

Then boarded.

Then surrounded by stripped timber and tools.

He had almost forgotten what the hall looked like before he threw the stone.

Ruth came beside him.

“Nervous?”

Jamie laughed.

“Why would I be nervous?”

“Because you've checked the frame six times.”

“I did not.”

“Peter counted.”

Jamie looked across the hall.

Peter raised six fingers.

“Traitor.”

Ruth smiled.

“You did good work.”

Jamie looked up again.

“Evelyn says it's not dreadful.”

“High praise?”

“Apparently.”

---

The installation took almost three hours.

Jamie was not allowed on the upper ladder.

He protested.

Four adults said no.

Even Izzy sided against him.

So he stayed on the landing and passed tools, watched the restored panel rise into place and tried not to interfere.

The red bird appeared first.

Then the blue flowers.

Then the amber leaves.

Piece by piece, Catherine's design returned to the hall.

Some sections were original.

Some were new.

Ruth had been right: the new glass was not made to look perfect. Tiny differences remained. Slight shifts of texture. Colours that did not match exactly until light passed through them.

The damage had not vanished.

It had become part of the window's history.

Jamie liked that.

At one thirty, Martin climbed down.

“Done.”

Nobody spoke immediately.

The sky outside had brightened.

Winter sunlight struck the glass.

Blue, amber and red light spilled across the staircase.

One small patch landed on Evelyn's cardigan.

She looked down at it.

Then up at the window.

Jamie watched her.

He expected tears.

There were none.

Evelyn rarely did what people expected.

She simply stood very still.

Izzy moved beside her.

Their shoulders touched.

Evelyn reached for Izzy's hand.

Izzy took it.

Jamie looked away.

Some moments did not need him looking.

Peter began packing tools.

Gareth came beside Jamie.

“You all right?”

“Yeah.”

“Proud?”

Jamie shrugged.

“A bit.”

Gareth looked up at the frame his son had helped prepare.

“You should be.”

Jamie smiled.

Across the hall, Evelyn looked at him.

Their eyes met.

She tapped her stick once against the floor.

“Jamie.”

He walked over.

Evelyn looked towards the window.

“Catherine would have been furious.”

His smile disappeared.

“What?”

“She hated people fussing over her work.”

Izzy laughed through something dangerously close to tears.

“She would've said the new blue was wrong.”

“It is slightly wrong,” Evelyn said.

Ruth called from the doorway, “I can still hear you.”

Evelyn raised her voice.

“IT IS BEAUTIFUL.”

Everyone laughed.

Jamie looked up at the tiny red bird.

“You still haven't told me the joke.”

Evelyn looked at it too.

“No.”

“Window's back now.”

“That changes nothing.”

“I'll find out eventually.”

“I admire your optimism.”

---

The others left gradually.

Peter and Ruth first.

Then Martin.

Gareth had to work an afternoon call-out and told Jamie he could walk home later if Evelyn was happy for him to stay.

Jamie stayed.

He and Izzy spent the afternoon in the conservatory wrapping small strings of warm white lights around the newly repaired iron shelves.

Evelyn claimed Christmas decorations before the fifteenth of December were vulgar.

Izzy pointed out it was the seventh.

Evelyn said exactly.

They decorated anyway.

By five, Ashcroft House looked almost alive.

Not transformed.

It was still old.

Still crooked.

Still damp in places.

But light now glowed through windows that had been dark for years.

The repaired conservatory roof held back a sudden shower.

A small fir tree stood in the hall beneath Catherine's window.

Jamie sat on the staircase with Izzy.

“Your gran is going to kill us when she sees the lights.”

“She bought them.”

Jamie looked at her.

“No.”

“Found the receipt.”

He laughed.

“Knew it.”

Izzy leaned against him.

“She likes you more than me.”

Jamie felt the old unease return.

“Don't start.”

“I’m joking.”

“Are you?”

Izzy lifted her head.

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

Jamie sighed.

“Nothing.”

“No, say it.”

“We've had this conversation.”

Izzy looked towards the kitchen.

Then back at him.

“I said I'm joking.”

Jamie studied her.

She smiled and kissed him.

The tension dissolved almost immediately.

That was another thing about Izzy.

She could make Jamie forget what he had been thinking with frightening ease.

They stayed on the stairs until Evelyn called them for food.

---

It was after seven when Jamie finally put his coat on.

The rain had stopped.

His phone showed three messages from Gareth asking whether he had started walking home yet.

**JAMIE:** leaving now

He shoved the phone into his pocket.

Izzy followed him into the hall.

“You could stay.”

“My dad will send a search party.”

“Tell him Gran kidnapped you.”

“He'd believe it.”

Izzy smiled and pulled him closer by the front of his coat.

They kissed.

Long enough for Evelyn to clear her throat from the staircase.

Jamie jumped apart.

Izzy groaned.

“Gran.”

“Front doors are for leaving through.”

Jamie laughed.

Evelyn stood halfway up the stairs beneath Catherine's window.

Coloured light from the hall lamp caught the glass behind her.

Jamie looked up.

“Looks good.”

Evelyn followed his gaze.

“Yes.”

“Worth all the hassle?”

She looked at him.

“Most worthwhile things are.”

Jamie smiled.

He opened the door.

Cold air entered the hall.

Evelyn called after him.

“Jamie.”

He turned.

She nodded towards the restored timber around the window.

“Not dreadful, Morgan.”

Jamie grinned.

“That’s basically a medal.”

“Do not become unbearable.”

“Too late.”

He stepped outside.

Izzy came onto the porch with him.

“Text me when you're home.”

“Yeah.”

She kissed his cheek.

Jamie started down the path.

At the gate he looked back.

Izzy was still standing beneath the porch light.

Behind her, through the open front door, Evelyn crossed the hall beneath Catherine's restored window.

Jamie raised a hand.

Evelyn saw him and raised hers.

Then Izzy closed the door.

Jamie walked home.

At seven forty-three, he texted Izzy.

**JAMIE:** home

At seven forty-five she replied.

**IZZY:** love you x

Jamie stared at the message for a long time.

Then smiled.

He typed:

**JAMIE:** love you too x

It was the first time either of them had written it.

It was also the last message Evelyn Ashcroft was alive to know nothing about.
