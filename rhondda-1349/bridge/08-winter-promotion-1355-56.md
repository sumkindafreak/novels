# Pass Three: Winter 1355–56 — Promotion Becomes Real

This file defines the winter period in which Ieuan's years of service and his 1355 campaign performance finally become **man-at-arms / esquire status**.

The promotion must change his daily life before Poitiers. Otherwise the title is only decoration.

---

# 1. Historical setting

The grande chevauchee ended with the army reaching La Reole on **2 December 1355**.

Between then and the 1356 campaign, the Prince's force did not simply dissolve.

Modern logistical studies document:

- winter quarters/garrisons;
- repairs;
- horse shoeing and replacement;
- wage and reimbursement administration;
- equipment/supply demands sent back to England;
- frontier defence;
- small-scale offensive operations;
- reinforcements arriving before the next major campaign.

The wider army was distributed through places including La Reole, Saint-Emilion, Sainte-Foy, Libourne and Bordeaux, with troops also operating on the Gascon frontier.

Early 1356 saw further local advances and the capture/surrender of fortified places.

This gives Ieuan **months of structured military life** between his first great raid and Poitiers.

---

# 2. The promotion is administrative/social, not theatrical

Do not stage a fantasy ceremony where Matthew dramatically touches Ieuan with a sword and declares him a squire.

Instead, the change becomes undeniable through practical decisions.

## Working sequence

### Early December

Ieuan returns exhausted with Warwick's company.

He expects ordinary valet/groom duties.

Instead, senior men begin asking him to:

- sit in on armed watches;
- supervise lower horse servants rather than merely work beside them;
- carry a weapon as duty rather than emergency protection;
- take responsibility for specific equipment/animals.

He notices the change before anybody explains it.

### December / Christmas period

Matthew de Soor's sponsorship and reports from other witnesses establish that Ieuan's late-campaign performance was not a one-off accident.

Because Matthew's own 1356 movements are not securely documented, the story should frame this as **Matthew recommending/advancing Ieuan within Warwick's broader service network**, not Matthew personally controlling every later appointment.

### Formal recognition

A Warwick-affinity officer/knight makes the practical regrading:

- Ieuan will serve at man-at-arms level;
- he is assigned/advanced a suitable horse;
- armour is upgraded from household stores/purchases;
- his military wage/status changes accordingly;
- he is now expected to stand with armed retainers when battle is ordered.

The exact bookkeeping language remains fictional reconstruction unless a matching household account form is found.

---

# 3. His reaction should not be simple joy

For Ieuan this means:

**More money.**  
**Better equipment.**  
**Higher status.**  
**And a much greater chance of being killed.**

He understands the promotion more clearly than a modern reader might expect because medieval work/status is concrete.

The new horse alone may represent a level of wealth that once would have dwarfed his family's household resources.

He becomes anxious about damaging equipment that technically makes him look successful.

---

# 4. Armour transition

Winter is where the equipment arc becomes visible.

Ieuan receives/acquires a practical mid-fourteenth-century man-at-arms package, assembled rather than magically matched:

- padded/arming layers;
- useful mail protection;
- bascinet/helmet and neck protection;
- plate reinforcement where resources permit;
- gauntlets;
- sword;
- lance/spear;
- serviceable campaign horse and tack.

Some pieces may be:

- household issue;
- bought from campaign proceeds;
- older hand-me-down equipment;
- altered to fit him.

Nothing needs to gleam.

A scene where an armour piece hurts because it was made for somebody else is more truthful than an instant perfect harness.

---

# 5. His first responsibility for another servant

Ieuan should be assigned a younger/lower servant or groom for at least some duties.

This is psychologically important.

At fourteen, nobody told him what job came next after his family died.

At twenty, **he is now the one telling somebody else what comes next**.

He discovers Dafydd in his own voice:

- impatience when a job is half-done;
- checking a strap after saying it is fine;
- pretending not to praise good work directly.

This inheritance should unsettle him.

---

# 6. The bed-wetting becomes socially more dangerous

This must remain part of Ieuan's continuity.

His condition may be less frequent by twenty, but it has **not magically disappeared**.

Promotion creates new difficulty:

- he sleeps among higher-status armed servants/esquires more often;
- he has fewer excuses to sleep near animals/lower stable staff;
- better bedding/blankets are more valuable and harder to conceal when wet;
- a rumour could undermine the masculine status he has spent years building.

## Working winter scene

After several dry weeks, Ieuan has an accident in shared winter quarters.

He wakes before the others.

For several seconds he is fourteen again.

He considers:

- lying;
- hiding the bedding;
- sleeping outside;
- abandoning the new quarters altogether.

A trusted immediate superior or servant discovers what happened.

The response is not a modern therapeutic conversation.

Something closer to:

> 'Change it before it freezes.'

Then the man carries on discussing horses/weapons/duty.

This is devastatingly important to Ieuan because **nothing happens**.

The secret is known by one more person and his world does not end.

That person should later become the wounded superior he protects at Poitiers, creating a relationship deeper than mere chain of command.

---

# 7. Create the immediate superior

Matthew de Soor is historically excellent as the bridge into Warwick's retinue, but his Poitiers presence is not secured.

Therefore Pass Three needs one **fictional immediate Warwick-affinity knight/esquire** who is safely available for the 1355–56 story.

This man should:

- already belong to the armed household world;
- be above Ieuan but not a great lord;
- serve alongside Matthew's circle in 1355;
- witness or hear of Ieuan's late-campaign performance;
- become responsible for Ieuan after the winter promotion;
- discover Ieuan's wetting without humiliating him;
- fight in Warwick's vanguard at Poitiers;
- be the wounded man Ieuan protects during the climax.

He is not another father replacement.

Their bond is built on **professional trust**.

Exact name/personality remains to be designed.

---

# 8. Early 1356 operations

The winter/spring forces conducted smaller frontier operations rather than waiting inertly.

Ieuan should therefore receive genuine practice at his new status before Poitiers:

- escort;
- garrison/watch;
- riding patrols;
- local raids;
- accepting surrender of minor positions;
- handling prisoners;
- learning that taking a fortified place can involve negotiation as well as assault;
- managing a horse over repeated short deployments.

This is critical.

He must arrive at September 1356 feeling like an **experienced junior man-at-arms**, not someone wearing new armour for the first time.

---

# 9. Language/social development

By winter 1355–56 Ieuan's linguistic world has broadened substantially.

Welsh remains emotional/home language.

He has acquired practical competence in the mixed speech of military service:

- enough English for household command and ordinary conversation;
- military/elite French vocabulary and phrases through exposure;
- no claim of elegant courtly French unless later character development earns it.

He still occasionally thinks a phrase in Welsh before answering in another language.

Class insecurity remains stronger than language insecurity.

---

# 10. Money

Ieuan begins to accumulate something he has never possessed before:

**capital.**

Not modern savings-account wealth, but:

- wages;
- shares of permitted booty;
- equipment of his own;
- a horse/economic stake in service;
- patronage that itself has material value.

He still measures wealth against childhood categories.

He may look at the value of one piece of equipment and involuntarily think:

> That was two cows.

Do this sparingly in prose, but keep the mental habit.

---

# 11. End of winter/spring

By summer 1356 Ieuan no longer asks whether he belongs among the men-at-arms every morning.

He still notices when other men think he does not.

That distinction matters.

He has become socially functional inside the armed household world **without forgetting where he came from**.

---

# Pass Three locks

**LOCKED:** winter 1355–56 is the period in which Ieuan becomes formally/functionally an esquire/man-at-arms.

**LOCKED:** the promotion is made real through horse, armour, pay, duties, social placement and fighting expectation rather than a decorative title.

**LOCKED:** he receives months of smaller-scale armed service before Poitiers.

**WORKING LOCK:** a fictional immediate Warwick-affinity superior discovers Ieuan's ongoing bed-wetting during winter quarters and treats it as a practical problem rather than a moral failure. That same man becomes the superior Ieuan protects at Poitiers.
