# Return, Patronage and Knighthood — 1357

This file solves the final economic/social mechanism and deliberately extends the novel's end a few months beyond Poitiers.

## Core decision

**Poitiers makes Ieuan worthy of knighthood. Spring 1357 makes knighthood sustainable.**

The accolade therefore does **not** happen immediately on 19 September 1356.

The stronger ending is:

- Poitiers, September 1356 — Ieuan's battlefield reputation is made;
- winter 1356–57 — prisoner/ransom, recovery and recommendation;
- spring 1357 — return to England/Wales and Edward le Despenser's assumption of his inheritance;
- spring/summer 1357 — Ieuan receives patronage/income and is dubbed a knight.

This keeps the climax and reward separate.

---

# 1. Edward le Despenser is the ideal final patron

Edward le Despenser was born **24 March 1336**, making him only about a year younger than Ieuan.

Historical evidence places him:

- as heir to Glamorgan after Hugh le Despenser's death in 1349;
- in minority/wardship until 1357;
- as **Edward Despenser, knight** in the Prince of Wales's 1355 army;
- at Poitiers in 1356;
- returning after his minority and doing homage for the family inheritance;
- summoned to Parliament later in 1357.

This produces an unusually strong thematic circle.

## 1349

Edward is a boy whose great inheritance is being administered for him.

Ieuan is a boy whose tiny household collapses and leaves him with nobody.

## 1357

Edward finally takes effective control of Glamorgan.

Ieuan returns from the same French war as an accepted man-at-arms with witnesses, ransom rights and a reputation.

Their worlds can now meet **without inventing a childhood relationship**.

---

# 2. Why delay the knighting until 1357?

If Ieuan is dubbed immediately after Poitiers, three problems remain:

1. his ransom is not yet necessarily realised;
2. he has no secure recurring income;
3. Edward le Despenser is still technically within his minority until March 1357.

Waiting fixes all three.

It also avoids the cliché of a battlefield commander handing out instant fairy-tale titles while corpses are still warm.

---

# 3. Edward can physically dub him

Madden's reconstruction of the 1355 army explicitly lists **Edward Despenser, knight**.

Medieval knighting did not require the king personally to perform every accolade; wartime/household dubbing could be performed by an established knight or great lord.

Therefore Edward can plausibly dub Ieuan in 1357.

### Novel rule

Do not use modern investiture language.

The scene may include:

- religious preparation/vigil if circumstances and pacing support it;
- girding with sword/spurs;
- the accolade/dubbing;
- witnesses from Ieuan's military and Glamorgan worlds.

The exact ceremony should be researched in Pass Four before prose.

---

# 4. Recurring income: the missing piece

A later-medieval benchmark often associated with sustaining knightly status is around **£40 annual landed income**, although this is not a rigid 1357 statutory test for Ieuan and must not be written as one.

The important point is simpler:

> Ieuan needs recurring resources, not merely one lucky ransom.

Military pay also helps explain the gap:

- man-at-arms: around £18 5s for a full year at 1s/day;
- knight: around £36 10s for a full year at 2s/day.

A knight can therefore earn significant wartime wages, but peacetime status still requires patronage/property/fees.

---

# 5. Working Despenser reward package

This is fictional patronage built from real fourteenth-century retaining practice.

## Proposed package

Edward le Despenser offers Ieuan:

### A. A **£20 annual life fee/annuity**

Purpose:

- retains Ieuan to Despenser service;
- provides dependable income;
- recognises Poitiers service;
- makes continued military attendance likely.

Comparable fourteenth-century magnate retaining later commonly uses fees in this broad range for knights, with lower figures for esquires.

Evidence grade: **B/C** for the exact £20 figure in Edward's 1357 household.

### B. A profitable **estate responsibility/lease in Glamorgan**

Working idea:

Ieuan receives favourable tenure/lease or management rights over a modest group of post-plague/vacant holdings and pasture in the wider Llantrisant–Glynrhondda administrative world.

This is not a barony and not necessarily ownership in fee simple.

The arrangement can provide:

- roughly another £15–£25 annual value in good years;
- horses/cattle responsibilities that suit his entire life story;
- an actual local role rather than a decorative title.

Exact legal form stays unlocked until Pass Four wording.

### C. Ieuan's ransom capital

His 40–50 marks of effective ransom value can be used for:

- armour/horse obligations;
- entry costs/deposits connected to land/lease arrangements;
- household establishment;
- maintaining servants/equipment;
- reserve against delayed income.

## Result

Ieuan is not suddenly wealthy.

But the combination of:

- **£20 annual patron fee**;
- **productive Glamorgan holding/office/lease**;
- **war wages when campaigning**;
- **ransom capital**

makes new knightly status defensible rather than ridiculous.

---

# 6. Hendre Gelli and the temptation to make the ending too neat

Do **not** simply give Ieuan his childhood home back as a sentimental prize.

That risks turning lordship property and plague succession into a fairy tale.

Better:

- Hendre Gelli may still exist, altered, occupied, ruined or absorbed into another arrangement;
- Ieuan may choose to visit it;
- Edward's reward can be elsewhere in the same administrative landscape;
- Ieuan's status does not depend on reclaiming his exact childhood bed.

The Rhondda remains emotionally home without becoming a prize parcel.

---

# 7. Who recommends Ieuan?

The recommendation should come through the military chain already built.

Working sequence:

1. **Iorwerth ap Morgan** survives his Poitiers wound and testifies to Ieuan's conduct.
2. Warwick-affinity men confirm the prisoner and battlefield service.
3. Sir Matthew de Soor's earlier patronage record explains how Ieuan entered this military network, whether or not Matthew was personally at Poitiers.
4. Edward le Despenser already knows of Ieuan or is introduced to his case within the post-campaign military/Glamorgan network.
5. Edward's decision therefore recognises a **seven-year record**, not one dramatic afternoon.

---

# 8. The knighting scene — working emotional design

The scene should be surprisingly quiet.

Ieuan has spent the entire novel imagining high status as something loud:

- armour;
- horses;
- banners;
- men shouting orders;
- battle.

The actual accolade can be contained and formal.

Ieuan kneels.

He has:

- his father's ordinary work knife somewhere on him or among his effects;
- memories of Gwenllian's unconsecrated grave;
- scars and habits nobody in the room fully understands;
- perhaps a fear, even that morning, that he might wake with wet bedding.

The title does not erase any of it.

### Important wording rule

Do **not** automatically use the modern/popular line **'Rise, Sir Ieuan'** unless a period source supports that exact phrasing.

The emotional fact matters more than a Hollywood catchphrase.

---

# 9. Final visual/thematic beat

After the ceremony, Ieuan should eventually return to the Rhondda landscape.

Not necessarily straight to Hendre Gelli.

A possible final image:

- he rides a good horse along a ridge he once walked barefoot/poorly shod;
- sees Ystradyfodwg and the valley below;
- recognises where hearth smoke used to vanish during the mortality;
- carries a sword worth more than everything his family owned;
- still has Dafydd's small knife.

He has become **Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd**.

But the final emotional truth is not that the plague made him great.

It is that the boy who survived it is still inside the knight.

# 10. Ending chronology lock

## 1356

- 19 September — Poitiers
- autumn/winter — Guillaume ransom claim, Iorwerth recovery, Ieuan remains in military service

## 1357

- Edward le Despenser reaches full age in **March 1357** after a seven-year minority and assumes his inheritance following return/homage
- spring/summer — Ieuan's Glamorgan retention/reward arranged
- **knighthood: spring/summer 1357**

This extends the novel roughly six to nine months beyond the battle while preserving Poitiers as the action climax.
