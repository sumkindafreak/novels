## Historical bridge

### Sir Matthew de Soor

Real Glamorgan knight/administrator and the strongest documentary bridge into the 1355 army.

Evidence places him gathering supplies in Wales on **26 April 1355** with the historical Welshman **Ieuan ap Ruyn** for Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick's Gascony retinue; modern army reconstruction also places Matthew in Warwick's 1355 vanguard.

**Caution:** Matthew's personal presence at Poitiers in 1356 has not been securely proven. The novel therefore uses Warwick's wider company to carry Ieuan forward after 1355.

### Edward le Despenser

Real lord of Glamorgan, born 24 March 1336.

He is explicitly listed as **Edward Despenser, knight** in the 1355 Prince of Wales army and is historically present at Poitiers.

After his seven-year minority he returns and does homage for the Despenser inheritance in 1357.

This makes him the final patron/dubber without inventing a childhood friendship.

## Fictional military relationship

### Iorwerth ap Morgan

Welsh uchelwr/esquire in Warwick's armed world.

He is Ieuan's immediate superior, discovers the lingering bed-wetting problem during winter quarters without humiliating him, is wounded at Poitiers, and becomes the key witness to Ieuan's battlefield conduct.

## Poitiers lock

Battle: **19 September 1356**.

Ieuan fights **dismounted as an esquire/man-at-arms in Warwick's wider vanguard**.

Climax:

1. Iorwerth is wounded in close fighting.
2. Ieuan acts when formation/order permits rather than breaking discipline for heroics.
3. He helps recover Iorwerth.
4. He fights fictional minor French knight **Sir Guillaume de la Motte**.
5. Guillaume yields; Ieuan accepts the surrender.
6. Ieuan gets both wounded superior and prisoner clear.

The battle produces witnesses, loyalty, chivalric credibility and ransom — not a superhero kill count.

## Ransom lock

Guillaume's working ransom: **100 marks (£66 13s 4d)**.

Ieuan's eventual effective benefit after retinue/superior claims, costs and obligations: roughly **40–50 marks**.

The ransom is capital, not permanent knightly income.

## Final economic mechanism

Spring/summer 1357:

- Edward le Despenser retains/rewards Ieuan;
- working patron fee: **£20 annually**;
- additional productive Glamorgan estate role/lease supplies recurring value;
- ransom capital supports horse/armour/household costs;
- knight-level campaign wages remain available in future service.

The exact legal form of the Glamorgan estate reward remains a Pass Four wording/detail question.

## Final accolade lock

**Dubber/patron:** Edward le Despenser  
**Timing:** spring/summer 1357  
**Result:** **Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd**

Poitiers makes the case for knighthood; 1357 makes it financially/socially sustainable.

## 1356 campaign route anchors

**Bordeaux → La Réole → Bergerac → Vienne/Manot → Romorantin → Loire/Tours theatre → Chauvigny → Nouaillé-Maupertuis/Poitiers → Bordeaux**.

Exact minor routes remain intentionally uncertain where the historical itinerary is uncertain.
