# Historical bridge: Sir Matthew de Soor

Sir Matthew is the strongest real link found between Glamorgan and the 1355 army.

Evidence places him:

- in Hugh le Despenser's Glamorgan administration in the 1340s;
- as Sheriff of Glamorgan;
- on Welsh administrative business in the early 1350s;
- on **26 April 1355** gathering supplies in Wales with **Ieuan ap Ruyn** for Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick's Gascony retinue;
- in modern reconstruction of **Warwick's 1355 vanguard**, under the variant spelling Matthew de Sooz.

The historical Ieuan ap Ruyn is not the fictional Ieuan ap Dafydd; the coincidence simply demonstrates that Welsh personnel genuinely moved through this exact military network.

## Matthew caution

Matthew's secure presence at **Poitiers in 1356 has not been proven**.

Therefore:

- Rhys gets Ieuan into estate/transport service;
- Matthew becomes the knightly patron who turns that work into a military route;
- Warwick's broader company carries Ieuan forward after 1355;
- the novel does not falsely state that Matthew fought at Poitiers.

# 1355 campaign lock

The grande chevauchée is primarily an **endurance, logistics and moral trial**, not a sequence of glamorous battles.

Historically the army:

- left Bordeaux in October;
- marched roughly 300 miles to Narbonne;
- averaged about 13 miles per marching day outward;
- returned faster at roughly 15 miles per marching day with fewer rests;
- depended heavily on horses, pack animals, fodder and water;
- deliberately burned/plundered enemy settlements and agricultural resources;
- faced pursuit and limited skirmishing on the return.

## Ieuan's promotion-defining deed

During the difficult late-November return, a fictional small skirmish/alarm threatens a Warwick-affinity baggage/remount group.

Ieuan:

- recognises a horse/route blockage before others do;
- gets animals and men moving in the correct order;
- obeys an order to hold rather than fleeing with the horses;
- fights when the escort is pressed;
- returns to the animals instead of chasing glory;
- preserves valuable mounts/equipment and helps keep men alive.

The campaign supplies the **evidence** for promotion. Winter quarters supply the **decision**.

# Winter 1355–56 lock

The army establishes winter quarters and undertakes repairs, horse shoeing/replacement, wages/accounts, resupply and smaller frontier operations.

Ieuan's promotion becomes real through:

- man-at-arms duties;
- improved horse;
- upgraded armour/weapons;
- changed pay/status;
- responsibility for lower servants;
- expectation that he will stand in the fighting line.

He receives months of smaller-scale armed experience before Poitiers.

## Bed-wetting continuity

Promotion makes Ieuan's remaining nocturnal wetting **more socially dangerous**, because he now sleeps among higher-status armed men.

A winter accident is discovered by his immediate superior, who treats it as an inconvenient practical problem rather than a moral failing. For Ieuan this is the first time an outsider knows his deepest shame and his life does not collapse.

# Iorwerth ap Morgan

**Fictional.** About thirty in 1355–56, a Welsh uchelwr/esquire already established in Warwick's armed household world.

Iorwerth becomes Ieuan's day-to-day superior after Matthew opens the military network.

He is:

- Welsh-speaking but socially far above Ieuan's original tenant world;
- bilingual/military-culture fluent;
- controlled, dry and difficult to impress;
- uninterested in heroic showing-off;
- the man who discovers Ieuan's wetting and simply tells him to change the bedding before it freezes;
- the wounded superior Ieuan later refuses to abandon at Poitiers.

Their bond is professional trust rather than substitute fatherhood.

# Equipment and embarkation

By Poitiers Ieuan must possess the functional package of a man-at-arms through wages/patronage/household resources:

- suitable campaign horse;
- substantial mid-fourteenth-century armour;
- lance/spear;
- sword/sidearm;
- recognised position in an armed retinue.

The Gascon Rolls document huge preparations for transporting horses, including wooden shipboard hurdles and gangplanks. Warwick/Suffolk horse shipping was specifically provisioned at **Southampton**, while the Prince's own transport also used Plymouth, so Ieuan's exact embarkation route remains tied to Warwick's retinue evidence rather than automatically following the Prince's ship.

# Poitiers lock

Battle: **19 September 1356**.

Secure framework:

- strong hedged/difficult Anglo-Gascon position;
- archers help break the initial French cavalry assault;
- repeated attacks develop into close hand-to-hand fighting;
- **Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick, commands the vanguard/first battle**;
- King John II is captured.

Exact map-level deployment is disputed, so the novel will not invent false precision.

## Ieuan's role

**LOCKED:** Ieuan fights **dismounted as an esquire/man-at-arms in Warwick's wider vanguard**.

Working climactic deed:

1. Iorwerth is badly wounded during close fighting.
2. Ieuan does not instantly abandon formation; he acts when an opening/order permits.
3. He and others protect/recover Iorwerth.
4. A respectable but non-famous French knight presses the group.
5. Ieuan fights him and accepts his surrender rather than killing him once surrender is possible.
6. He gets Iorwerth and the prisoner clear.

This produces witnesses, loyalty, combat merit and a plausible prisoner/ransom mechanism without turning Ieuan into a superhero or giving him a famous captive.

---

# Knighthood constraints

Ieuan may only become a knight after the story has shown:

1. years of household service;
2. real horse competence;
3. armed service before Poitiers;
4. recognised patronage;
5. esquire/man-at-arms status before the battle;
6. conspicuous witnessed Poitiers service;
7. credible economic support through wages, reward, ransom, annuity or patronage.

The accolade should occur **after the battle**, not as a romantic mid-melee dubbing.

Edward le Despenser remains a strong possible high-level participant because he is lord of Glamorgan and is independently recorded at Poitiers. The exact person who performs the knighting remains unlocked.
