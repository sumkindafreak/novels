# Final locked ladder

## 1349 — age 14

Plague survivor → temporary stock/route work with **Rhys ap Llywelyn**.

## 1350 — 15

Stable, pack and livestock servant in the Llantrisant orbit.

## 1351–52 — 16–17

Groom/valet-level servant moving through the Glamorgan administrative/horse network.

**Sir Matthew de Soor** becomes the historically grounded knightly bridge.

## 1353–54 — 18–19

Experienced mounted valet and increasingly armed household servant.

Works beside higher-status Welsh armed retainers and learns military household culture.

## 1355 — 20

Welsh mobilisation/supply work leads into **Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick's Gascony retinue**.

Matthew de Soor is historically documented gathering Welsh supplies for Warwick and appears in Warwick's 1355 vanguard.

Ieuan sails/campaigns as an armed mounted servant rather than a knight.

The grande chevauchée becomes his endurance/moral trial.

A late-November alarm/skirmish proves he can handle animals, obey orders and fight without chasing glory.

## Winter 1355–56

Ieuan is regraded into the functional status of **esquire/man-at-arms**.

He receives:

- better horse;
- better armour;
- man-at-arms duties/pay;
- responsibility for lower servants;
- recognised place among armed retainers.

**Iorwerth ap Morgan** (fictional uchelwr/esquire) becomes his immediate superior.

## 1356 — 21

Ieuan campaigns north with Warwick's wider company.

Major campaign anchors:

**Bordeaux → La Réole → Bergerac → Vienne/Manot → Romorantin → Loire/Tours pressure → Chauvigny → Nouaillé-Maupertuis/Poitiers.**

### 19 September 1356

Ieuan fights dismounted in **Warwick's wider vanguard**.

Iorwerth is wounded.

Ieuan helps recover him, fights a fictional minor French knight **Sir Guillaume de la Motte**, accepts Guillaume's surrender, and gets both wounded superior and prisoner clear.

This deed produces:

- witnesses;
- proof of loyalty and discipline;
- chivalric/social credibility;
- a ransom asset.

## 1356–57 aftermath

Working Guillaume ransom: **100 marks (£66 13s 4d)**.

Ieuan's eventual effective benefit after superior/retinue claims, costs and obligations: roughly **40–50 marks**.

The ransom supplies capital but not enough recurring income to sustain knighthood alone.

## Spring/summer 1357 — age 22

**Edward le Despenser**, already documented as a knight in the 1355 army and historically at Poitiers, reaches the end of his minority and assumes effective control of his Glamorgan inheritance after returning and doing homage.

Ieuan's Poitiers service is recommended through the Warwick/Glamorgan military network.

Working reward package:

- **£20 annual Despenser life fee/annuity**;
- profitable Glamorgan estate role/lease/tenurial opportunity worth additional recurring income;
- Ieuan's ransom capital;
- knight-level military wages when campaigning.

Edward le Despenser performs the final accolade.

Ieuan becomes:

> **Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd**

The exact legal form of the estate reward and exact ceremonial wording remain Pass Four/prose research details, not structural uncertainties.

# Final financial logic

Knighthood is sustainable through a **portfolio**, not a treasure chest:

1. war wages;
2. ransom capital;
3. Despenser annual fee;
4. productive Glamorgan estate role/lease;
5. future military service.

The later-medieval £40 annual-income benchmark for supporting knightly status is used only as a comparative warning, not a rigid 1357 rule.

# Final thematic logic

The plague does not 'make Ieuan a knight'.

It destroys his first life.

Everything afterwards is work.

His greatest qualities at Poitiers are all distorted versions of things learned before battle:

- watch animals before forcing them;
- notice routes and weather;
- stay still when afraid;
- do the next job;
- protect somebody more vulnerable;
- do not waste what can still be saved.

At the end he carries a knight's sword but keeps **Dafydd's working knife**.

The title changes his social position.

It does not erase Glynrhondda, Gwenllian, the little end, or the boy who waited to see whether plague would kill him.
