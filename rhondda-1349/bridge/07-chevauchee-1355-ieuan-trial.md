# Pass Three: The 1355 Chevauchée — Ieuan's Trial

The 1355 campaign must be the experience that changes Ieuan from a trusted armed household servant into somebody his superiors can credibly promote to man-at-arms during the following winter.

It should **not** be written as one continuous heroic battle. Historically the expedition's defining features were speed, logistics, destruction, exhaustion and psychological/economic warfare, with relatively limited large-scale fighting.

---

# 1. Campaign chronology

## September 1355 — arrival in Gascony

The English force crosses to Gascony in September.

Ieuan's first responsibility remains horses and equipment.

The voyage has already shown him that:

- expensive military horses can panic and die as easily as cattle;
- rank does not protect anyone from seasickness;
- armies depend on men doing filthy work.

The horses require recovery and preparation after landing.

---

## 5 October — army leaves Bordeaux

The grande chevauchee begins.

The army moves through the Gascon frontier before entering enemy territory.

By the time banners are formally unfurled/divisions established, Ieuan understands that this is not an escort journey.

It is an organised raid.

---

# 2. Marching reality

Mollie Madden calculates that the army reached Narbonne after roughly **300 miles / 482 km** in 35 days.

Because twelve of those were halt/rest days, the actual marching-day average was around:

> **13 miles / 21 km per marching day**

The return was faster and harsher:

> roughly **15 miles / 24 km per marching day**, with far fewer rest days.

By late November daylight was short and French forces were pursuing/shadowing the army.

### Story implication

Ieuan's achievement cannot be 'he rode 15 miles once'.

He must do it repeatedly while:

- feeding/watering animals;
- finding replacement mounts;
- carrying armour and personal gear;
- sleeping badly;
- eating irregularly;
- guarding camp;
- responding to alarms;
- dealing with lame/injured horses;
- helping burn/plunder settlements when ordered;
- functioning again the next morning.

This is where his Rhondda endurance becomes militarily valuable.

---

# 3. Horse pressure

The campaign used an enormous number of horses and pack animals.

The logistical literature repeatedly stresses:

- fodder;
- water;
- replacement mounts;
- horse exhaustion;
- repair/resupply.

The return from Narbonne became particularly demanding because the army moved more quickly with fewer halts.

### Ieuan's role

He becomes known for noticing trouble early:

- heat/swelling in a leg;
- damaged hoof/shoe;
- rubbed tack;
- horse refusing water;
- animal going dull before obvious collapse;
- overloading a pack animal.

His father's rule returns in a new form:

> **Watch the beast before you move it.**

Other men think he is being overcautious until his judgement repeatedly saves valuable animals.

---

# 4. The moral rupture: this army creates empty houses

The 1355 chevauchee deliberately devastated enemy territory.

Villages, agricultural resources, mills, property and undefended settlements were targeted because damaging the French king's economic and political authority was part of the strategy.

This must affect Ieuan profoundly.

In 1349 he survived by entering homes made empty by pestilence.

In 1355 he rides with men **making homes empty on purpose**.

## First burning scene

Do not have Ieuan suddenly give a modern anti-war speech.

His reaction is practical first:

- he sees grain destroyed that could feed people;
- livestock driven away;
- household vessels smashed or taken;
- winter stores burned;
- a child watching from somewhere he cannot reach.

The cognitive blow is simple:

> He knows what an empty house means from the inside.

He obeys anyway.

That moral compromise is part of his military adulthood.

---

# 5. Toulouse / river-crossing lesson

The army avoids a costly assault on Toulouse and crosses difficult river country to the south.

The French had regarded some crossings as serious obstacles to a large army.

### Story use

Ieuan learns that military courage often looks like:

- finding a crossing;
- keeping baggage from jamming;
- getting animals out of deep mud/water;
- maintaining order while everybody is wet and angry.

This should be one of the first times Matthew/other senior men praise him publicly for something that would have looked like ordinary cattle work at home.

---

# 6. Carcassonne and Narbonne

The army reaches the wealthy Languedoc corridor and attacks/sacks vulnerable urban suburbs and settlements rather than conducting prolonged formal sieges of every fortified core.

Ieuan should experience:

- narrow streets/suburbs;
- smoke behaving differently among buildings than on open farmland;
- civilians fleeing with possessions;
- troops becoming harder to control once plunder is available;
- the dangerous confusion between military order and looting.

He does **not** become enthusiastic about pillage merely because others are.

But he also takes useful equipment/food when permitted.

That contradiction is intentional.

---

# 7. Narbonne departure and the harder return

The return begins in November.

The army cannot remain indefinitely because enormous numbers of animals rapidly consume local fodder/grain.

The return route is faster, with:

- shorter days;
- fewer halts;
- accumulated booty/baggage;
- exhausted horses;
- French forces increasingly close;
- limited skirmishing and harassment.

This is the best place for Ieuan's **promotion-defining incident**.

---

# 8. Promotion-defining incident — working lock

## Setting

Late November 1355 during the difficult return toward friendly Gascon territory.

Use a **fictional local skirmish/alarm within the historically documented period of pursuit and limited fighting**, rather than claiming Ieuan changed a named battle that never occurred.

## Situation

A small Warwick-affinity column is moving:

- exhausted replacement/remount horses;
- pack animals;
- wounded/sick men;
- valuable equipment and accumulated supplies/booty.

French/local mounted troops or armed pursuers strike at stragglers / threaten the column during confused movement.

## Ieuan's test

A better-born armed retainer's horse goes down or panics, blocking movement.

Ieuan:

1. recognises immediately that trying to drag the animal straight forward will jam the route;
2. gets animals/men moved around or off the narrow track in the correct order;
3. obeys an order to hold rather than instinctively flee with the horses;
4. uses spear/sword in real close violence when the escort is pressed;
5. returns to the animals after the immediate threat instead of pursuing for glory;
6. gets the valuable remount/pack train moving before the main column leaves them behind.

### Why it matters

Ieuan's success is not that he kills the most enemies.

It is that for the first time his superiors see **all his identities working together**:

- cattle boy;
- horse servant;
- survivor under panic;
- trained armed servant;
- disciplined household man.

He preserves military wealth and keeps other men alive.

That is a better reason to promote him than a flashy duel.

---

# 9. His first confirmed kill

If the finished outline needs a clear first killing by Ieuan, this late-1355 incident is the strongest location.

It should be:

- fast;
- ugly;
- not skill-display choreography;
- done because somebody is coming through him or his charge;
- remembered afterwards in physical fragments rather than heroic lines.

The plague taught Ieuan to live beside death.

It did not teach him how it feels to **cause** it.

That distinction matters.

---

# 10. Why this earns promotion but does not cause it instantly

Nobody stops on the road and says:

> 'You have fought bravely, therefore you are now an esquire.'

Instead:

- witnesses remember the incident;
- a senior servant/retainer reports it;
- Matthew/Warwick-affinity officers already know Ieuan's years of service;
- the winter return to garrison/quarters creates the administrative/social moment for promotion.

Ieuan may not even know anyone is discussing his status.

The campaign is **evidence**.

Winter is the **decision**.

---

# 11. Return to Bordeaux

The army reaches La Reole/Bordeaux-region security in early December after the accelerated return.

Ieuan is:

- thinner;
- better on horseback;
- more useful militarily;
- less impressed by armour;
- more disturbed by fire;
- no longer able to tell himself that war merely means defending somebody.

The boy who survived destroyed homes has now helped an army destroy homes.

That contradiction should stay alive through Poitiers.

---

# Pass Three lock

**LOCKED:** Ieuan's 1355 chevauchee is primarily an endurance/logistics/moral trial, not a continuous battle adventure.

**WORKING LOCK:** his promotion-defining deed occurs during the difficult late-November return in a fictional small skirmish/alarm consistent with documented pursuit and limited fighting. He preserves men/horses/equipment and fights when required.

**LOCKED:** the formal promotion itself occurs later in winter quarters, after witnesses and household patronage have time to operate.
