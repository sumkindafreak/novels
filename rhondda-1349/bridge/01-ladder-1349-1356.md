# Ieuan's Working Ladder — 1349 to 1356

This is the first complete year-by-year progression from plague survivor to Poitiers.

It is a **planning model**, not yet the final chapter structure.

---

# 1349 — age 14

## Position

Plague orphan / temporary stock and route helper.

## Story progression

- June: completely alone after Gwenllian's death.
- July: meets Rhys ap Llywelyn and eventually accepts temporary work.
- Late summer/autumn: helps locate, gather and move stock around damaged holdings.
- Begins travelling more regularly toward Llantrisant with adults.

## Skills gained

- working beside unfamiliar adults;
- handling livestock in larger/less familiar groups;
- basic pack-animal exposure;
- following orders from somebody other than family;
- sleeping in shared/service spaces while hiding bed-wetting.

## Social position

Still simply a working boy.

No military status.

---

# 1350 — age 15

## Position

Stable/pack/stock servant in the Llantrisant orbit.

## Work

Ieuan becomes useful for:

- grooming ordinary riding/pack animals;
- feeding and watering;
- carrying tack and loads;
- leading animals;
- route work;
- basic stable cleaning;
- cattle movements;
- messages/tasks too minor for higher servants.

## Horse development

Rhys begins teaching him that horse handling differs from cattle.

By the end of 1350 Ieuan can:

- catch/lead/groom calm horses;
- saddle simple riding/pack animals under supervision;
- recognise basic lameness/injury signs;
- ride competently enough for work, not warfare.

## Language/social change

Ieuan hears more English and Anglo-Norman/French administrative vocabulary around officials and higher households.

Welsh remains his natural language.

---

# 1351 — age 16

## Position

Trusted working servant with enough competence to be recommended upward.

Sir Roger de Berkerolles, one of the real 1349 custodial figures in Glamorgan, dies in 1351. The exact effect on Ieuan is fictional, but the changing personnel of the lordship provides a natural moment for servants and connections to shift.

## Working story move

Rhys places Ieuan in or recommends him to a **fictional knightly/upper-servant household connected to the Glamorgan affinity**.

The immediate patron is not yet named; Pass Three research should try to anchor this role to a defensible real network before prose drafting.

## New tasks

- stable work for better horses;
- travelling with a household party;
- maintaining equipment;
- serving armed men;
- observing drills/training;
- carrying messages or gear.

Still not a squire.

---

# 1352 — age 17

## Position

Household servant increasingly attached to mounted/armed service.

## Skills

- competent everyday riding;
- tack maintenance;
- helping arm/disarm a fighting man;
- cleaning mail/helmets/weapons;
- understanding how expensive military equipment is maintained;
- basic spear/staff/sword familiarity under supervision;
- possibly bow practice if later research and character needs support it.

Ieuan's value remains largely logistical and animal-based.

## Social education

He learns:

- who eats where;
- who may speak first;
- how rank changes behaviour;
- how servants protect their masters' reputation;
- that courage and status are not the same thing.

---

# 1353 — age 18

## Position

Mounted household servant / beginning armed retainer trajectory.

Ieuan is now physically adult by medieval expectations and has several years of reliable service behind him.

## Working promotion

He receives or is allowed to use:

- protective padded clothing;
- helmet;
- spear or other practical weapon;
- perhaps borrowed/hand-me-down mail elements later.

He accompanies household men on duties where violence is possible.

He is **not yet a knightly tournament fighter**.

## Key story test

This is where Ieuan must prove he can obey under danger rather than simply survive independently.

His biggest weakness is still pride: he hates being ordered away from danger if somebody thinks he is only a servant.

---

# 1354 — age 19

## Position

Armed household retainer approaching esquire-level recognition.

Edward le Despenser is married by 1354 and increasingly visible as an adult aristocratic figure even though inheritance/custody issues remain legally significant until his majority.

## Working route

Ieuan's patron/household becomes connected more directly to the wider Despenser/Prince of Wales military affinity.

Possible mechanism:

- recommendation through Glamorgan service networks;
- transfer as a horse/armed servant to a retainer preparing for overseas service;
- inclusion because competent mounted manpower is valuable after the mortality.

## Critical rule

Do **not** simply write 'Ieuan became a squire' in 1354.

We need a scene/mechanism in which a knightly patron begins treating him as an armed household man of higher standing.

His low birth remains socially noticeable.

---

# 1355 — age 20

## Position

Provisional target: **armed household man / esquire-level retainer** attached to a company serving with the Prince of Wales in Gascony.

## Historical campaign anchor

The Prince of Wales sailed from Plymouth in September 1355 with a substantial army organised through household retinues, indentures and array.

The force included:

- men-at-arms;
- mounted archers;
- foot archers;
- Welsh soldiers;
- grooms/valets/household support;
- magnate companies.

Edward le Despenser is listed among the prince's military personnel.

## Ieuan's role

Current best option:

Ieuan goes **not as a glamorous independent knight**, but as a mounted armed servant/esquire within a larger retinue.

He spends the 1355 chevauchée doing far more than fighting:

- horse care;
- marches;
- camp work;
- guard duty;
- foraging/purveyance realities;
- equipment repair;
- keeping up with a brutally fast-moving army;
- occasional violent contact.

## Emotional shift

This campaign confronts him with deliberate destruction of civilian resources.

The plague destroyed homes without intention.

The army burns them on purpose.

That distinction becomes central to the later novel.

---

# Winter 1355–56 — age 20

## Position

Experienced campaign servant / probable esquire candidate.

The prince's army winters and resupplies in Gascony.

Modern scholarship documents:

- repairs;
- payment/accounting;
- replacement supplies;
- horses needing shoeing/replacement;
- additional archers recruited from England.

This period is perfect for Ieuan's **social promotion** because spectacular battle is absent and people have months to observe who is dependable.

## Working promotion mechanism

Ieuan's immediate patron formally accepts him into a higher armed-household role after the 1355 campaign.

Whether the prose calls him **esquire** must depend on the final social-history test.

The label cannot be decorative.

---

# 1356 — age 21

## Historical anchors

- English/Gascon reinforcements arrive.
- Edward le Despenser travels/serves in the prince's circle.
- the Prince's army begins the campaign that culminates at Poitiers.
- Edward le Despenser is historically present at the battle.
- Poitiers: **19 September 1356**.

## Ieuan's target status before the battle

To make the ending plausible, by September Ieuan must be functioning as one of:

- an esquire;
- an armed household man effectively equivalent to a lower man-at-arms;
- a trusted attendant fighting alongside a knightly patron.

He cannot still be merely a groom who picks up a sword at the climax.

## Battlefield role — not yet locked

Possible directions:

1. fights on foot beside his patron among men-at-arms;
2. carries/guards horses and is drawn into the crucial remount/counterattack phase;
3. protects a wounded patron or banner;
4. captures a prisoner in close combat;
5. combines practical horse skill with fighting at the moment mounted mobility becomes important.

The final choice must fit the best reconstruction of Poitiers tactics.

---

# After Poitiers

Knighthood is **not automatic**.

The current strongest ending mechanism requires a combination of:

- several years of household military service;
- recognised esquire/armed-retainer standing before the battle;
- conspicuous service at Poitiers;
- a patron willing to advance him socially;
- enough reward/booty/ransom or granted income to make the status sustainable.

Froissart's narrative includes a Poitiers squire who later became a knight because the ransom profit from a prisoner transformed his means. That is not proof that a tenant's son like Ieuan would normally be knighted, but it demonstrates the relationship between **war profit, patronage and advancement**.

## Working end

Ieuan is knighted **after** the victory, not because he happens to kill many men, but because years of service have already brought him to the edge of the knightly world and Poitiers provides the final exceptional reason to cross it.

Who knights him is not yet locked.

Best candidates:

- his immediate knightly patron;
- Edward le Despenser, if Ieuan's service is brought close enough to Despenser to make it personal and credible;
- another senior noble in the same affinity.

Avoid having the Prince of Wales personally knight an obscure Ieuan unless later evidence/story architecture earns that degree of direct contact.
