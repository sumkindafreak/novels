# Pass Three: Sir Matthew de Soor — The Glamorgan-to-Gascony Bridge

This file identifies the strongest historical patronage route found so far for Ieuan's middle years.

## Working conclusion

**Sir Matthew de Soor is the best historical bridge currently available between Ieuan's Glamorgan service world and the Prince of Wales's 1355 Gascony army.**

He is not invented.

Surviving records place him:

1. inside the Despenser administration of Glamorgan in the 1340s;
2. as sheriff of Glamorgan;
3. on crown/estate business in Wales in the early 1350s;
4. gathering supplies in Wales in 1355 for the Earl of Warwick's Gascony retinue;
5. in modern reconstruction of the Prince's 1355 army under Warwick's vanguard.

This is almost exactly the chain the novel requires.

---

# 1. Matthew in Hugh le Despenser's Glamorgan administration

National Library of Wales estate records preserve **Sir Matthew le Soor** as a witness in the Cardiff chancery world of Hugh le Despenser.

A grant dated 16 February 1344/5 identifies:

> Sir Matthew Le Soor, Sheriff of Glamorgan

among the witnesses.

Another Despenser confirmation dated broadly 1344–47 again identifies Sir Matthew in the Cardiff court/administrative setting.

### Story meaning

Matthew is not a random English knight imported for our plot.

He belongs to the exact political-administrative world sitting above Ieuan's Glynrhondda tenant community.

Evidence grade: **A.**

---

# 2. Matthew remains useful to the Crown in the early 1350s

Calendar-roll material from 1353 refers to **Matthew Soor, chivaler**, being sent with others to Wales in connection with auditing the accounts of bailiffs and ministers in lands under royal control/custody.

The surviving search extract is incomplete and the precise estate wording needs final archival checking before prose uses a detailed mission.

### Safe story use

By 1352–53 Matthew can credibly be treated as a knight involved in:

- estate administration;
- movement between Cardiff, local officers and Welsh districts;
- horses/transport;
- armed escort;
- household servants and messengers.

This creates a believable mechanism by which Rhys ap Llywelyn's useful young helper could enter Matthew's wider service network.

Evidence grade: **A/B pending full roll wording.**

---

# 3. The April 1355 Gascon Roll — the breakthrough

The Gascon Rolls record that on **26 April 1355** letters of protection were granted to:

- **Matthew de Soor, knight**;
- **Ieuan ap Ruyn**;

who were tasked with going through Wales to obtain supplies for the sustenance of **Thomas de Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick**, who was going to Gascony in royal service, and for Warwick's retinue.

This is extremely important.

It proves a real chain:

**Glamorgan/Welsh administrative knight**  
→ **Welsh supply gathering**  
→ **Warwick's retinue**  
→ **Gascony 1355**.

It also proves that a Welshman with the name **Ieuan** was literally working beside Matthew on this exact mobilisation task.

That historical Ieuan ap Ruyn is **not our fictional Ieuan ap Dafydd**. The coincidence is valuable evidence, not permission to merge the two men.

Evidence grade: **A.**

---

# 4. Matthew appears in the 1355 army

Mollie Madden's reconstruction of Edward of Woodstock's 1355 army places **Matthew de Sooz, knight** in the **vanguard commanded by Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick**.

The same reconstructed list also includes **John/Ieuan ap Ruyn** in Warwick's vanguard.

Given the April Gascon Roll pairing of Matthew de Soor and Ieuan ap Ruyn on Warwick supply business, the identification of `Matthew de Sooz` with the Glamorgan `Matthew de Soor` is extremely strong, though medieval spelling/name normalisation must be kept visible in research notes.

### Story meaning

Matthew does not merely send food to a war he never sees.

The current evidence lets us place his service network **inside Warwick's vanguard on the 1355 chevauchee**.

That solves our embarkation problem.

Evidence grade: **A/B identification across spelling variants; army listing itself A/B modern reconstruction from primary records.**

---

# 5. Recommended relationship with Ieuan

Do not make Matthew a substitute father.

He should be a **real knight around whom fictional household detail is cautiously built**.

We know enough to use his historical roles.

We do **not** know enough to invent intimate personality traits and present them as biography.

### Working fictional relationship

## 1351–52

Rhys's work around Llantrisant/Cardiff transport puts Ieuan into occasional contact with men serving Matthew's administrative/stock movements.

Ieuan begins as a groom/pack/horse servant.

## 1353

Matthew's documented estate-account activity gives a strong background for Ieuan travelling in a knight's service through lordship territory.

Ieuan becomes a reliable valet-level horse/transport servant.

## 1354

Ieuan is trusted with:

- better horses;
- equipment;
- messages;
- travelling money/supplies under supervision;
- armed escort work.

Matthew learns his usefulness over **years**, not in one dramatic scene.

## Spring 1355

Matthew's real Welsh supply mission for Warwick becomes Ieuan's gateway to full military mobilisation.

Ieuan helps move/gather:

- food;
- pack animals;
- horse-related supplies;
- equipment and loads.

We should not specify particular commodities unless supported by the mobilisation records.

## Gascony 1355

Ieuan accompanies Matthew's service network into Warwick's vanguard as a trusted mounted valet/armed servant.

This is his first great campaign.

## Winter 1355–56

Matthew becomes the strongest current candidate to **sponsor Ieuan's regrading to esquire/man-at-arms** after the campaign.

The exact administrative form of that fictional promotion can be built around real household practice.

---

# 6. Why Matthew is better than Edward le Despenser as the *first* patron

Edward le Despenser remains thematically important, but he is too high-status to solve Ieuan's early transition elegantly.

Matthew is better because:

- he is an established adult knight when Ieuan is a teenager;
- he has direct Glamorgan administrative experience;
- he has reason to employ servants, messengers, horsemen and local Welsh helpers;
- he is directly connected to the 1355 Welsh supply mobilisation;
- he appears in Warwick's vanguard.

Edward can enter Ieuan's personal orbit **later**, once Ieuan has already climbed enough to be visible among armed retainers.

This avoids the 'great lord spots special peasant boy' cliché.

---

# 7. Edward le Despenser can still close the circle

Madden's army reconstruction places **Edward Despenser, knight** in the Prince's company in the same 1355 army, while Matthew is in Warwick's vanguard.

Therefore Ieuan can know of / occasionally encounter his young regional lord on campaign without serving him directly at first.

Over 1355–56:

- Matthew can remain Ieuan's actual sponsor;
- Edward can gradually become aware of a Glamorgan Welsh esquire who rose through Matthew's service;
- Poitiers can bring the two military divisions/household networks into the same crisis.

If Edward ultimately participates in Ieuan's knighting, it will then rest on **prior campaign familiarity and Matthew's recommendation**, not instant recognition.

---

# 8. Critical historical restraint

Because Matthew is real, finished prose must avoid invented factual claims such as:

- exact birth date unless sourced;
- exact family relationships unless sourced;
- invented coat of arms unless sourced;
- claiming he personally owned a specific castle/estate without evidence;
- assigning him a documented death at Poitiers without evidence;
- attributing private opinions or major historical decisions to him as though recorded.

Dialogue, household interactions and his mentorship of Ieuan are **C-level historical fiction**.

His offices, mobilisation and army presence remain evidence-anchored.

---

# 9. Pass Three lock

**WORKING LOCK:** Sir Matthew de Soor becomes Ieuan's principal knightly patron from approximately **1352 through the 1355–56 campaigns**.

Rhys gets Ieuan into the service network; Matthew turns that service into a military career.

Edward le Despenser remains the later, higher-level Glamorgan connection rather than the boyhood patron.
