# Chapters 1–9 — Day-by-Day Continuity Spine

This file is the **date and continuity authority** for the plague/survival half of the novel. Where the earlier 18-chapter architecture gives a broader range, these dates control unless later evidence forces a revision.

Calendar: **Julian**.

Evidence convention:
- **A** — documented/direct historical or modern biological evidence
- **B** — strong period/regional reconstruction
- **C** — story invention constrained by A/B evidence

The exact first plague case in Ystradyfodwg is not documented. May 1349 remains a **C-level story lock inside an A/B-supported Glamorgan 1349 outbreak**.

---

## Core biological continuity rule

Modern plague guidance gives bubonic plague a typical incubation period of roughly **2–8 days**. Pneumonic plague can develop faster after respiratory exposure, commonly about **1–3 days**. The family chronology is built so nobody needs impossible same-day infection and nobody is assumed to catch ordinary bubonic plague merely by touching a sick relative.

The story never states exactly which exposure infected each victim.

Ieuan's different sleeping position and frequent bedding turnover may reduce one route of exposure, but his survival remains partly luck.

# APRIL 1349 — THE LAST ORDINARY MONTH

## Thursday 2 April — Chapter 1 opens

**Location:** Hendre Gelli.

- Ieuan wakes wet in **the little end**.
- He tries to clear straw/bedding before Gwenllian notices.
- Angharad quietly manages the practical mess.
- Dafydd is frustrated by wasted straw/cloth/drying labour but does not shame him publicly.
- Ordinary cattle work follows.
- Dafydd's knife and the lesson **"Watch the beast before you move it"** appear naturally.
- First fragmentary plague rumour is heard, probably second- or third-hand.

### Seasonal state

- winter stores are reduced but the household has survived the leanest part of the year;
- spring grazing is improving;
- nights can still be cold/damp in the uplands;
- no new cereal harvest exists yet.

### Continuity lock

Ieuan's private fear is still social humiliation, not disease.

---

## Sunday 5 April — Palm Sunday

**Chapter 2 begins its Holy Week movement.**

- family/community church life is intact;
- Meurig ap Cynan is seen functioning normally as the familiar local cleric;
- Gruffudd, Elen, Madog, Nest and other ordinary social relationships are visible;
- rumours are discussed but not yet treated as a local emergency.

Do not give Ystradyfodwg an elaborate ceremony that research has not established. The day is a real calendar anchor, while the exact local observance remains reconstruction.

---

## Friday 10 April — Good Friday

- fasting/solemn religious rhythm;
- plague rumours may make sermons/prayer feel more urgent without anyone possessing germ theory;
- the family remains complete.

---

## Sunday 12 April — Easter Sunday

**Final Easter with all four family members alive.**

- warm domestic/community material is essential;
- Ieuan has recently had a dry night and privately treats it as a hopeful sign/prayer answered;
- Gwenllian remains funny and irritating;
- Dafydd remains physically capable;
- Angharad remains practical and busy;
- a traveller on or near the Penrhys route gives the clearest warning yet that mortality is moving closer.

### Chapter 2 end

A departing traveller says, in some form:

> It is coming this way.

No character knows how soon.

---

# 13 APRIL–14 MAY — THE DELIBERATE GAP

This month does **not** need another chapter.

The gap does useful work:

- spring labour continues;
- cattle and upland work expand;
- rumours become less exotic and more specific;
- travellers/markets/church conversations become increasingly uneasy;
- nobody in the family falls ill yet;
- the reader experiences the horrible delay between warning and catastrophe.

Ieuan may have additional wet and dry nights during this period. Do not make the condition behave according to plot convenience.

---

# MAY 1349 — THE HOUSEHOLD COLLAPSES

## Friday 15 May — Chapter 3 opening window

**The pestilence becomes local enough to alter behaviour.**

- one familiar holding is unusually quiet;
- rumours include named places/people rather than distant cities;
- work continues because animals still require care.

Do not claim this is the historically documented first Ystradyfodwg case.

---

## Monday 18 May–Wednesday 20 May — Rogation period

These are real liturgical dates in 1349.

Possible story texture:

- prayer for crops/community carries a frightening new meaning;
- normal religious rhythms are increasingly disrupted by fear and sickness;
- Madog ap Rhys is visibly ill by this window.

Do not invent a large documented Ystradyfodwg Rogation procession.

---

## Thursday 21 May — Ascension / Madog dies

**Working death lock for Madog ap Rhys: 21 May.**

- Madog has fever, weakness and a painful swelling before death;
- Nest's certainty breaks;
- Meurig attempts to maintain confession/prayer/burial obligations;
- community attendance is already thinner than it would have been in ordinary times.

This provides Ieuan with his first death of a familiar adult from the mortality.

---

## Friday 22 May — Madog's reduced burial

- still recognisably Christian;
- organised rather than Hollywood chaos;
- fewer helpers/mourners;
- people are afraid of one another and of the dead without understanding transmission correctly.

**Important:** Dafydd may have shared the same local environment/exposure before or around this point. The novel does not need to specify a single infection event.

---

## Saturday 23 May — Dafydd first clearly ill

**Chapter 4 begins.**

- fever/weakness emerge;
- Dafydd initially insists on working;
- Ieuan assumes strength and stubbornness can defeat illness.

The timing fits a plausible bubonic incubation after exposure during the preceding week.

---

## Sunday 24 May

- swelling under arm/groin/neck becomes undeniable according to final symptom choice;
- Angharad begins household remedies/prayer/help-seeking;
- Ieuan takes over more cattle/water/fire work.

No modern diagnostic vocabulary.

---

## Monday 25–Tuesday 26 May

Dafydd declines.

- alternating delirium/lucidity;
- Ieuan performs adult work badly at first, then better;
- one wet night occurs during the crisis and Ieuan has to deal with it himself because Angharad is occupied;
- Dafydd's clearest final interaction should concern work, not a polished death speech.

Possible final useful correction from Dafydd: a knot, a gate, an animal, a tool position, or another ordinary action.

---

## Wednesday 27 May — Dafydd dies

- death is at home;
- Ieuan does not immediately become emotionally expressive;
- Dafydd's working knife remains where he normally kept it until Ieuan later needs it.

### Continuity lock

The knife is **taken for use**, not ceremonially gifted.

---

## Thursday 28 May — Dafydd's improvised burial

**NEW HARD CONTINUITY LOCK.**

Dafydd does not remain unburied in Hendre Gelli until June.

- Ieuan and whatever limited surviving help can be obtained get him buried;
- rites/attendance are abbreviated by crisis;
- physical grave work exhausts Ieuan;
- the experience teaches him exactly how hard it is to dispose of a body with dignity.

This makes Gwenllian's later burial much more painful: Ieuan knows what proper burial should involve and cannot provide it.

---

## Friday 29 May — Angharad develops fever

**Chapter 5 begins.**

The likely exposure is deliberately left unresolved.

Plausible mechanisms include:

- the same household flea/rodent ecology that affected Dafydd;
- later infected flea exposure during the household outbreak;
- if Dafydd developed secondary pneumonic disease, respiratory exposure is possible, but the prose should not require this.

The date remains biologically plausible without saying she simply "caught it by nursing him."

---

## Saturday 30 May

- Angharad clearly deteriorates;
- Ieuan is now responsible for Mam, Gwenllian, fire, food, water and animals;
- he cannot successfully do every job.

This is overload, not incompetence.

---

## Sunday 31 May — Whitsunday / Pentecost

The family cannot participate normally.

- Angharad is too ill;
- church/parish support is unreliable;
- Meurig's whereabouts may now be unknown;
- Ieuan hears or imagines bells but cannot rely on them to mean what they once meant.

The contrast with Easter is deliberate: seven weeks earlier all four family members were together at the centre of a functioning Christian year.

---

# JUNE 1349 — THE CHILDREN

## Monday 1 June

Angharad has intermittent lucidity.

Her final useful instructions should be practical rather than theatrical:

- where food is stored;
- what Ieuan can carry;
- which route uphill is safer;
- protect Gwenllian;
- perhaps what must be left behind.

She does not know she is delivering a final monologue.

---

## Tuesday 2 June — Angharad dies

- Ieuan's instinct is to turn her death into another task because grief is too large;
- unlike Dafydd's death, there is no functioning adult network left to help him organise burial immediately;
- keeping Gwenllian in the infected/terrifying house becomes intolerable.

### HARD CORRECTION TO EARLIER OUTLINE WORDING

Dafydd is already buried.

Ieuan is **not leaving two parental bodies in the house**.

Angharad's body is the immediate unresolved burial crisis.

Ieuan wraps/covers her as best he can but prioritises getting Gwenllian away.

Whether surviving parish/community people later bury Angharad can remain unknown to Ieuan.

---

## Wednesday 3 June — Ieuan and Gwenllian leave at/near first useful light

**Chapter 6.**

- they carry only what Ieuan can actually manage;
- Dafydd's knife is among the essential items;
- route is familiar upland ground, not wilderness discovery;
- the destination is the known seasonal cattle/hafod refuge toward Mynydd Gelli;
- Ieuan knows water, fuel, leaks and animal space.

Gwenllian is still apparently healthy.

This is crucial.

Ieuan begins believing he may have saved her.

---

## Thursday 4 June

A deceptive day of relative safety.

- they eat;
- Ieuan repairs/organises the shelter;
- Gwenllian irritates him in ordinary little-sister ways;
- he begins constructing a future of several days rather than hours.

If a wet night occurs here, Gwenllian sees/knows and **does not tease him**. The altered behaviour tells Ieuan she is frightened before either child says so.

---

## Friday 5 June — Gwenllian becomes feverish

This timing is deliberately chosen because it remains plausible for exposure in the final days at Hendre Gelli.

Ieuan has now seen the sequence enough times to react before she understands why he is frightened.

- fever;
- pain/weakness;
- eventual discovery of swelling.

He knows what it means.

---

## Saturday 6 June

Ieuan tries everything emotionally available to him:

- water;
- warmth/cooling attempts according to household custom;
- prayer;
- remembered remedies;
- bargaining with God;
- perhaps thoughts of Penrhys/Ffynnon Fair, though carrying Gwenllian there may be impossible or obviously futile from his location/state.

Do not create a miraculous near-recovery.

---

## Sunday 7 June — Gwenllian dies

**Working locked death date.**

The fact that it is Sunday matters psychologically more than administratively: the familiar weekly sacred rhythm continues while his family no longer does.

Ieuan is now completely alone.

### Chapter 6 end

There is nobody to tell him where to put his sister.

---

# CHAPTER 7 — THE WAITING

## Monday 8 June — Gwenllian's burial

Ieuan buries her near the upland refuge.

Why not Ystradyfodwg churchyard?

- one fourteen-year-old cannot realistically carry an eight-year-old corpse safely down/across dangerous plague-struck country while exhausted;
- he fears returning to the infected lower world;
- normal burial support has collapsed around him.

The grave is as respectful as he can manage but still feels inadequate.

This becomes lifelong guilt.

---

## 8–10 June — first waiting period

Ieuan assumes he is infected.

- repeatedly checks groin, armpits and neck;
- misreads ordinary aches as symptoms;
- eats little;
- measures time through daylight, thirst, fire and prayer rather than clocks;
- every warm feeling becomes "fever" in his mind.

---

## 11–13 June — bodily life refuses to stop

- he sleeps more deeply from exhaustion;
- another wet night occurs;
- initial response is rage/disgust;
- then the contradiction lands: his body is still doing the humiliating ordinary thing it always did.

The scene must **not** imply medical proof that wetting saved him.

It functions psychologically:

> the thing he associates with weakness is occurring inside a body that has, so far, refused to die.

---

## Sunday 14 June — hunger wins

One full week has passed since Gwenllian's death.

Ieuan has no guarantee that plague cannot still strike, but psychologically he can no longer sit still waiting.

He goes downhill because he needs food and because continued waiting has become another form of dying.

### Chapter 7 end

He walks toward the houses.

---

# CHAPTER 8 — EMPTY HOUSES

## Monday 15 June — return toward Hendre Gelli

- Ieuan sees the valley from above before re-entering it;
- hearth smoke, missing smoke and unattended animals become information;
- home is physically familiar but emotionally intolerable.

### Angharad continuity option — preferred

Her body is no longer where Ieuan left it.

He cannot know with certainty who moved/buried her or exactly where.

This avoids leaving a decomposing corpse in the house for two weeks and creates a quieter form of grief: **someone performed his last duty for him, but he cannot thank them or even identify the grave with certainty.**

This is C-level fiction and should remain understated.

---

## 16–18 June — own stores first

Ieuan initially uses what belongs to his household:

- grain/pulses;
- vessels;
- cloth/blanket;
- rope;
- tools;
- preserved food where still usable.

He does not instantly rob neighbours because the moral boundary still matters.

He avoids sleeping in the main family area and uses the little end/yard/upland refuge depending on scene needs.

---

## 19–22 June — necessity crosses the property line

Ieuan enters another dead/abandoned household, most powerfully **Madog's**.

He finds useful things, not treasure:

- dry grain/pulses;
- a usable pot/container;
- tools;
- salt or preserved food if justified;
- livestock nearby.

The act feels like theft because in his moral world it **is** theft even if nobody living appears to claim the goods.

Welsh plague-era court records support people entering houses after deaths and taking goods/cattle; our exact incident remains fictional.

---

## 22–24 June — the cow

Ieuan chooses **one manageable useful cow** rather than attempting to inherit a herd.

This is practical because:

- milk can provide repeated calories if the animal is lactating;
- one animal can be watered/moved/contained by a competent teenager;
- several cattle would overwhelm him once hay/fodder work is considered.

The cow is work, not a magical food dispenser.

---

## 24–27 June — human danger returns

Ieuan encounters **Goronwy ap Tudur**.

- neither knows whether the other is safe;
- both have claims/needs around abandoned goods;
- conflict should be tense and materially specific rather than villain melodrama;
- Ieuan learns that "survivor" does not mean "friend".

---

## 28–30 June — winter enters summer

Ieuan sees the real long-term problem:

**hay/fodder.**

The landscape looks abundant because it is summer, but an animal kept through winter consumes stored work.

Ieuan understands he cannot simultaneously:

- preserve Hendre Gelli;
- cut/manage enough hay;
- guard livestock;
- maintain crops/stores;
- scavenge safely;
- perform every household task alone.

### Chapter 8 end

The question changes from:

> Can I survive today?

To:

> How do I survive winter?

---

# CHAPTER 9 — ONLY TO WORK

## Early July — Rhys enters

**Preferred first encounter window: 4–8 July.**

Rhys ap Llywelyn is involved in practical stock/transport recovery in the damaged lordship economy.

He finds Ieuan because:

- animals and holdings need assessing/recovering;
- local labour has collapsed;
- Ieuan is visibly managing work beyond what most surviving fourteen-year-olds could manage alone.

Rhys does **not** hear a prophecy/story about the plague-proof boy and seek him out.

---

## First offer

Rhys offers:

- food;
- temporary paid/maintained work;
- stock/route tasks;
- adult company and access to wider recovery systems.

He does not offer adoption, destiny or instant military training.

Ieuan refuses or evades at first because leaving Hendre Gelli feels like abandoning:

- Dafydd;
- Angharad;
- Gwenllian;
- the holding itself;
- the boy he used to be.

---

## July–August — temporary means temporary

Ieuan begins taking work while repeatedly returning mentally/physically toward home.

Continuity points:

- cattle competence gives immediate value;
- horse/pack work begins but he is not yet a horse expert;
- shared sleeping arrangements revive his wetting anxiety;
- he lies badly when explaining why he wants edge-space/extra straw;
- Rhys initially notices only that Ieuan is excessively private.

---

## Late summer/autumn — chapter endpoint

Ieuan spends a night away from Glynrhondda **by choice rather than emergency**.

That is the true end of the plague-survival movement.

He tells himself:

> Only to work.

The reader understands this is the beginning of leaving home.

# Infection-window audit

## Dafydd

- symptoms: **23 May**
- plausible exposure window under 2–8 day bubonic incubation: roughly **15–21 May**
- fits local Madog/community outbreak period.

## Angharad

- symptoms: **29 May**
- plausible exposure window: roughly **21–27 May**
- fits intense household/community exposure while Dafydd is ill.
- does not require simple touch-based transmission.

## Gwenllian

- symptoms: **5 June**
- plausible exposure window: roughly **28 May–3 June**
- fits final days in the plague-struck household before/around escape.

## Ieuan

- shares broad environmental risk but sleeps differently and has frequently disturbed/replaced bedding;
- no disease onset is ever guaranteed or medically explained;
- several symptom-free weeks eventually make survival emotionally believable to him, but he never receives proof of "immunity."

---

# Burial audit

- **Madog:** church/parish burial, reduced attendance — 22 May.
- **Dafydd:** improvised/reduced Christian burial — 28 May.
- **Angharad:** Ieuan leaves before he can organise proper burial; body later removed/buried by unknown survivors/community workers — exact grave unknown to him.
- **Gwenllian:** Ieuan buries her near upland refuge — 8 June, outside consecrated ground.

This creates four different burial experiences and prevents repetitive death scenes.

---

# Food and labour audit

## April

- leaner stored foods still matter;
- new grazing begins improving animal condition;
- no summer/harvest abundance yet.

## May

- livestock work is expanding;
- dairy may improve depending on individual animals;
- hay season is approaching but not yet safely banked;
- household disruption therefore threatens the coming winter before Ieuan understands it.

## June

- growing landscape creates misleading visual abundance;
- stored grain/pulses and abandoned domestic supplies remain more reliable than wild foraging;
- milk from one manageable cow can help but creates daily labour;
- haymaking/fodder becomes the key long-range survival problem.

No chapter should solve survival with unlimited berries, hunting skill or conveniently preserved meat.

---

# Travel audit

- Hendre Gelli ↔ Ystradyfodwg: ordinary local movement, not an expedition.
- Hendre Gelli ↔ upland refuge: familiar work route Ieuan has travelled before.
- Penrhys: known sacred/travel ridge location; not treated as next door.
- valley travel relies on tracks, slopes, ridge routes and fords rather than modern roads/bridges.
- Ieuan's first genuinely expanded travel world begins with Rhys and movement toward Llantrisant.

Heneb's Rhondda research supports medieval ridgeway communication, Penrhys's route/hostelry role and the persistence of upland seasonal cattle settlement.

---

# Final continuity locks for drafting Chapters 1–9

1. **Chapter 1 opens Thursday 2 April 1349.**
2. **Final family Easter: Sunday 12 April.**
3. **Madog dies Thursday 21 May.**
4. **Dafydd symptoms begin Saturday 23 May; dies Wednesday 27 May; buried Thursday 28 May.**
5. **Angharad symptoms begin Friday 29 May; dies Tuesday 2 June.**
6. **Ieuan and Gwenllian leave Wednesday 3 June.**
7. **Gwenllian symptoms begin Friday 5 June; dies Sunday 7 June; Ieuan buries her Monday 8 June.**
8. **Ieuan waits through Sunday 14 June before returning downhill.**
9. **Chapter 8 occupies roughly 15–30 June.**
10. **Rhys first appears in early July; Chapter 9 can extend into autumn.**
11. Dafydd is already buried when Angharad dies.
12. Angharad's exact grave becomes unknown to Ieuan after surviving people remove/bury her body.
13. No family infection is explained by a single scientifically certain exposure.
14. Ieuan's bed-wetting is never biological immunity.
15. The plague story remains primarily about people, work, faith and loss; research should stay underneath the prose rather than turning dialogue into lectures.
