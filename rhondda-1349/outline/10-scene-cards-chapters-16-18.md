# Scene Cards — Chapters 16–18

These chapters complete the novel.

The ending must preserve two different climaxes:

- **action climax:** Poitiers, 19 September 1356;
- **life/social climax:** Ieuan's knighthood in Glamorgan, spring/summer 1357.

The accolade is intentionally delayed. Poitiers proves what Ieuan has become; 1357 gives that identity a sustainable place in the world.

# CHAPTER 16 — THE ROAD NORTH

**Dates:** **6 July–18 September 1356**  
**Ieuan:** 21  
**Locations:** Bordeaux/La Réole → campaigning north through central France → Poitiers region

## Chapter job

Show Ieuan operating as an actual man-at-arms for an entire campaign before the set-piece battle.

The chapter should build pressure through **distance, pursuit and shrinking options**, not announce Poitiers from page one as destiny.

---

## SCENE 1 — 6 JULY: ANOTHER CAMPAIGN

### Historical anchor

The Prince leaves Bordeaux to join the army at La Réole as the 1356 campaign begins.

### Ieuan's changed role

Compare directly with Chapter 13 without explaining the comparison:

1355:
- checked other men's horses;
- carried/maintained gear;
- fought only when pressed.

1356:
- has his own fighting obligation;
- lower servant assists with horse/equipment;
- Ieuan checks the work anyway;
- rides with the man-at-arms layer.

### Ieuan wants

To perform his new position without giving anyone cause to say the promotion was a mistake.

### Iorwerth

Treats him as a junior colleague/subordinate, not a promoted groom being indulged.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE NEW ROUTINE

**July/August**

### Purpose

Show that man-at-arms status does not exempt Ieuan from logistics.

He still thinks about:

- horse condition;
- water;
- food;
- routes;
- armour maintenance;
- where baggage is;
- where the lower servant has gone.

### Armed responsibility

Add:

- watches;
- patrol;
- escort;
- prisoner/negotiation awareness;
- holding formation with other men-at-arms.

### Emotional change

War has become work enough to be frightening in a different way.

Ieuan can now be bored inside danger.

---

## SCENE 3 — A SURRENDER / PRISONER LESSON

### Purpose

Seed the law/custom/economics of surrender before Guillaume.

During a minor action or negotiated submission, Ieuan sees an enemy man-at-arms or local defender surrender rather than die.

### What he learns

A captive can mean:

- information;
- status;
- ransom;
- responsibility;
- paperwork/obligation;
- superior shares.

### Iorwerth

Explains only what Ieuan needs for work.

No lecture on medieval ransom law.

### Payoff

When Guillaume yields in Chapter 17, Ieuan already understands **yielding changes the rules**.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE ROAD TIGHTENS

**Early September**

### Historical frame

The campaign moves into an increasingly dangerous strategic position as French forces converge.

Use broad secure route anchors, not fake GPS precision:

- northern campaigning;
- Loire/Tours theatre;
- movement toward Chauvigny/Poitiers;
- repeated intelligence about King John's larger army.

### Ieuan perspective

He receives information as a junior retainer would:

- rumours;
- orders changing;
- scouts coming in;
- faster packing;
- arguments among men who know more than he does.

### Plague echo

This resembles spring 1349:

bad news comes in fragments, adults disagree, then suddenly the danger is local.

Ieuan recognises the feeling.

---

## SCENE 5 — 17 SEPTEMBER: THEY ARE THERE

### External problem

The possibility of simply riding away narrows.

### Army atmosphere

- tired horses;
- men looking toward roads/approaches;
- archers preparing;
- armour checked repeatedly;
- prayers/confession;
- arguments about water/food/ground.

### Ieuan

He checks himself for fear the same way he once checked himself for plague symptoms.

Not literally lymph nodes — the behavioural pattern is the echo:

> Is it here yet?

### Iorwerth

Reminds him of an ordinary task.

This helps because work still exists.

---

## SCENE 6 — 18 SEPTEMBER: THE DAY BEFORE

### Historical frame

Negotiation/manoeuvre immediately precedes the battle.

Avoid giving Ieuan privileged knowledge of high command discussions.

He sees:

- messengers;
- pauses;
- orders to prepare then wait;
- uncertainty whether they will fight, withdraw or negotiate.

### Religious preparation

Ieuan should seek confession/prayer if available and appropriate.

This is not because he has become especially pious.

He thinks he may die tomorrow.

### Dafydd's knife

He checks it almost automatically with his other equipment.

It is not his battlefield weapon.

It is still simply there.

### Bed-wetting possibility

The night before Poitiers does **not** need an accident; that is too neatly symbolic.

He barely sleeps.

### Chapter end

At first light/order, Ieuan prepares to dismount and stand with Warwick's men.

For once, there is no horse problem he can solve to avoid what comes next.

# CHAPTER 17 — MAUPERTUIS

**Date:** **19 September 1356**  
**Ieuan:** 21  
**Location:** Poitiers/Nouaillé-Maupertuis battlefield zone  
**Formation:** Warwick's wider vanguard / first battle

## Chapter job

Pay off every skill and psychological pattern in the novel **without turning Ieuan into the man who won Poitiers**.

He is one junior man-at-arms inside a huge battle.

---

## SCENE 1 — DISMOUNT

### Practical action

Ieuan helps/ensures horses are positioned/secured as men-at-arms prepare to fight on foot.

### Horse continuity

He notices animal fear:

- sweating;
- whites of eyes;
- refusal;
- noise from unfamiliar masses.

His competence is useful but not decisive.

### Transition

Once he leaves the horse, his old safe professional identity is physically behind him.

He joins the line.

---

## SCENE 2 — WAITING IN ARMOUR

### Purpose

Battle should not begin instantly.

### Sensory detail

- weight;
- heat/cold depending final weather reconstruction;
- restricted vision/hearing;
- men praying/joking/vomiting/silent;
- archers moving;
- hedge/ditch/difficult ground as visible local facts.

### Ieuan

Becomes very still when afraid.

Iorwerth recognises it by now.

### Dialogue

Keep it ordinary.

A mundane complaint can be stronger than a heroic speech.

---

## SCENE 3 — THE HORSES COME

### Secure historical framework

French mounted attacks press the difficult approach and archers target horses/vulnerable angles.

### Ieuan's experience

This is horrifying precisely because he understands horses.

He sees:

- animals hurt before riders;
- panic spreading;
- falls creating obstacles;
- the difference between a trained warhorse and an animal that has reached terror.

### Moral/physical conflict

He knows how to help a horse and cannot.

He must stay where ordered.

This is the inverse of his entire childhood skill set.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE PRESS

### Transition

The battle becomes close hand-to-hand fighting.

### Writing rule

Reduce Ieuan's world.

Do not narrate the whole Battle of Poitiers from above.

He knows:

- man in front;
- man beside him;
- hedge/ditch/mud/ground nearby;
- shouted command half heard;
- impact;
- exhaustion;
- whether Iorwerth is still upright.

### Ieuan behaviour

He uses trained discipline rather than flashy individual skill.

---

## SCENE 5 — IORWERTH DOWN

### Event

Iorwerth is badly wounded during close fighting.

### Crucial rule

Ieuan does **not** immediately abandon the line.

He sees/knows Iorwerth is down and continues doing what the formation requires until an opening/order allows action.

### Character payoff

This is the lesson from:

- dangerous household duty;
- 1355 skirmish;
- years of Iorwerth corrections.

Courage now means discipline, not impulsive rescue.

---

## SCENE 6 — RECOVERY

### Action

When movement/order permits, Ieuan and perhaps one other fictional retainer reach Iorwerth.

### Problem

Iorwerth cannot simply walk away.

Ieuan must manage:

- body weight;
- armour;
- ground;
- continuing threat;
- formation movement.

### Plague echo

He has moved sick/dead family before.

Do not state the comparison bluntly.

His body remembers how heavy people are.

---

## SCENE 7 — SIR GUILLAUME DE LA MOTTE

### Character

Fictional minor French knight, respectable, early/mid-thirties.

### Encounter

Guillaume presses the small group around wounded Iorwerth during the chaos.

### Fight

Ieuan's hardest individual combat in the book.

Rules:

- tired men;
- armour matters;
- no elegant choreography;
- environment/other fighters create interruptions;
- Ieuan survives through training, stubbornness and opportunity, not superior destiny.

### Turning point

Guillaume is beaten/disadvantaged enough to yield.

He communicates surrender in a way Ieuan understands through military language/custom.

### Ieuan choice

There is a fraction of a moment where killing him would be easier.

Ieuan accepts the surrender.

Once he accepts it, Guillaume becomes **his responsibility**.

---

## SCENE 8 — TWO MEN TO GET OUT

### The actual feat

Ieuan now has:

- wounded Iorwerth;
- surrendered Guillaume;
- continuing battle chaos.

### Why it matters

He has to solve a practical extraction problem rather than pose over victory.

Possible horse skill callback later:

- secure/get access to a mount after immediate close fighting;
- organise another servant/man to help;
- move through restricted ground without creating a blockage.

### Witnesses

Other Warwick-affinity men see enough to later confirm:

- he stayed disciplined;
- protected Iorwerth;
- personally accepted Guillaume's surrender;
- did not abandon his duty for plunder.

---

## SCENE 9 — AFTER THE BATTLE

### Historical reality

Ieuan learns in fragments that the French king has been captured and the battle is an extraordinary victory.

### Ieuan's immediate priorities

- Iorwerth alive?
- Guillaume secure?
- his horse/equipment?
- water;
- removing/loosening armour;
- exhaustion.

### Guillaume

Now that they are not actively trying to kill each other, Ieuan sees:

- fear;
- injury/fatigue;
- concern about status/family/ransom.

He is not a monster.

### Emotional effect

Ieuan has just created a piece of war wealth out of another human being.

That should disturb him.

### Chapter end

Someone tells Ieuan, perhaps with disbelief or dry humour, that Guillaume is worth money.

Ieuan looks at the French knight and understands that survival has acquired another price.

# CHAPTER 18 — SIR IEUAN

**Dates:** 20 September 1356 → spring/summer **1357**  
**Ieuan:** 21→22  
**Locations:** return toward Gascony → winter aftermath → England/Wales/Glamorgan → Rhondda

## Chapter job

Turn battlefield achievement into a believable social future without making the ending a fairy tale.

The chapter should feel **quieter after Poitiers**, but not empty.

---

## SCENE 1 — THE DAY AFTER

**20 September 1356**

### Ieuan

Wakes/continues in a world where he is still the same person despite surviving the battle.

### Iorwerth

Alive but seriously wounded.

Do not recover him unrealistically quickly.

### Guillaume

His identity/status is established more clearly.

### Ransom concept

Ieuan learns that accepting the surrender has given him a recognised claim — subject to:

- company/superior claims;
- costs;
- negotiation;
- proof/witness.

### Ieuan's first reaction

He is less excited by money than confused by the idea that one man's life can be converted into several years of wages.

---

## SCENE 2 — SOUTH AGAIN

**Late September–October 1356**

### Historical frame

The army returns south toward Gascony.

### Role reversal

Ieuan is now responsible for:

- prisoner;
- wounded superior relationship;
- his own man-at-arms equipment;
- lower servant duties beneath him.

### Guillaume relationship

Keep restrained.

They do not become modern friends.

But repeated practical contact can establish:

- Guillaume has family/lands;
- ransom will hurt his household;
- he fought because Ieuan's army invaded his country;
- both men understand the other as a professional enemy rather than demon.

### Moral echo

Ieuan remembers the civilians of 1355.

Victory does not resolve that unease.

---

## SCENE 3 — ONE HUNDRED MARKS

**Autumn/winter 1356**

### Working ransom lock

Guillaume's ransom: **100 marks (£66 13s 4d)**.

### How it appears in prose

Do not have a chest of cash arrive.

Show:

- negotiation;
- identity/means being assessed;
- guarantees/obligation;
- superior shares;
- possible staged payment;
- costs of maintaining/handling the prisoner.

### Ieuan's effective benefit

Planning value: roughly **40–50 marks** after obligations/costs.

### Ieuan understands scale

Compare once to ordinary man-at-arms wages / childhood household value.

He now possesses real capital.

He still does not possess a permanent knight's income.

---

## SCENE 4 — IORWERTH'S TESTIMONY

### Iorwerth recovery

Still recovering enough that the wound has consequences.

### Purpose

Make the recommendation chain visible.

Iorwerth confirms Ieuan's conduct to Warwick-affinity/senior men:

- stayed in formation;
- came when able;
- protected him;
- took Guillaume properly;
- completed the task.

### Ieuan

Hates hearing the story retold because other men make it sound cleaner than it felt.

### Matthew connection

Ieuan's prior years under Matthew de Soor's network form part of the record of who this man is.

No need to bring Matthew physically back if chronology/evidence does not support it.

---

## SCENE 5 — EDWARD LE DESPENSER

**Late 1356 / early 1357 transition**

### Historical role

Edward is:

- already a knight;
- lord/heir of Glamorgan;
- almost Ieuan's age;
- historically part of the Poitiers military world;
- approaching the end of his minority.

### First meaningful meeting

This should be the first time Ieuan and Edward actually speak at length.

Edward has heard:

- Glamorgan origin;
- Warwick service;
- Poitiers conduct;
- prisoner claim;
- military recommendations.

### Critical rule

Edward does not marvel that a peasant has feelings or deliver a destiny speech.

He evaluates a useful man whose origin gives him a particularly useful Glamorgan connection.

Patronage is emotional **and political/economic**.

---

## SCENE 6 — BACK ACROSS THE WATER

**1357**

### Mirror Chapter 12

Sea crossing home.

Ieuan is now:

- experienced man-at-arms;
- ransom claimant;
- carrying better gear;
- riding/returning under patronage.

The sea remains unpleasant.

### Emotional change

On the outbound journey, water severed him from Gwenllian's grave.

Now it brings him back toward it.

### Wetting continuity

No need for symbolic accident.

The condition still exists as part of him; the ending does not need to prove whether it has stopped.

---

## SCENE 7 — GLAMORGAN, 1357

### Historical backdrop

Edward le Despenser reaches full age in **March 1357** after his minority and assumes his inheritance following return/homage.

### Working reward package

Edward retains Ieuan through a fictional but historically grounded package:

- **£20 annual life fee/annuity**;
- productive estate responsibility/lease in Glamorgan;
- continuing military service expectation;
- Ieuan's own ransom capital.

### Important rule

Do not give Ieuan Hendre Gelli as a fairy-tale property prize.

His new economic place may be elsewhere in the same wider administrative landscape.

### Ieuan's reaction

The money/land responsibility scares him more than the title possibility because he knows exactly how easy it is for a holding to fail.

---

## SCENE 8 — PREPARATION FOR KNIGHTHOOD

**Spring/summer 1357**

### Ceremony research lock

Fourteenth-century dubbing could range from elaborate religious/ceremonial preparation to simpler forms. The novel should **not automatically impose the full later 'Knight of the Bath' programme** on an ordinary knight bachelor.

Secure/useful elements include:

- religious preparation/confession/Mass if chosen;
- knightly spurs as an important status sign;
- girding with a sword as a central investiture element;
- the actual dubbing/accolade by an established knight;
- witnesses.

### Working ceremony choice

Keep Ieuan's ceremony restrained rather than royal/coronation-scale:

1. confession/religious preparation;
2. Mass/prayer in an appropriate chapel/church setting;
3. spurs and sword formally associated with the new status;
4. Edward performs the dubbing/accolade;
5. witnesses from Despenser/Glamorgan and some military connection.

No mandatory ritual bath, elaborate colour-coded costume or overnight vigil unless later source-specific drafting deliberately chooses them.

### Why restrained works

Ieuan's entire rise has been practical.

The ceremony should be solemn without becoming pageant fantasy.

---

## SCENE 9 — THE ACCOLADE

### Ieuan kneels

What he physically carries/remembers matters more than grand dialogue.

He is aware of:

- new sword/spurs/status;
- old scars;
- Dafydd's small work knife among his personal effects;
- Gwenllian's grave;
- the little end;
- Iorwerth's wound;
- Guillaume alive because Ieuan accepted surrender.

### Edward

Performs the accolade.

### Wording rule

Do **not** default to Hollywood wording like:

> Rise, Sir Ieuan.

unless final prose research finds a defensible period formula.

The narrator can make the status clear without invented ceremonial quotation.

### Result

**Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd.**

### Emotional truth

He expects transformation.

He feels mostly like himself.

That is the point.

---

## SCENE 10 — THE RHONDDA

### Final movement

Ieuan returns/visits the Rhondda landscape after the accolade.

He rides a good horse along ground he once walked as a tenant boy.

### Do not make the valley frozen in grief

There should be:

- smoke;
- people;
- animals;
- repaired/new households;
- children who were born after the mortality;
- evidence life did not wait for him.

### Hendre Gelli

May be:

- altered;
- partly occupied;
- absorbed into another arrangement;
- physically smaller than memory.

Ieuan does not seize it because he is now a knight.

### Gwenllian's grave

He goes there.

He can finally afford/help arrange something more durable or proper if we choose, but **do not erase the original unconsecrated burial** by magically relocating her without ecclesiastical/legal thought.

The guilt is part of him.

### Final object

Dafydd's work knife beside/under the equipment of a knight.

### Possible final image

Ieuan looks over the Rhondda and sees hearth smoke.

In 1349 he watched smoke disappear house by house.

In 1357 there is smoke again.

The final emotional meaning is not:

> The plague made him a knight.

It is:

> He survived long enough to become something nobody in the little end could have imagined — and none of it brought the dead back.

End.

# CROSS-CHAPTER CONTINUITY AUDIT — 16–18

## Age

- July–September 1356: 21;
- spring/summer 1357: about 22.

## Poitiers status

Ieuan is already a genuine junior esquire/man-at-arms **before** 19 September.

Poitiers is not his first fight and does not manufacture his social identity from nothing.

## Battlefield certainty

Use secure structural elements:

- difficult hedged/ditched position;
- opening mounted crisis;
- archers attacking horses;
- repeated close assaults;
- Warwick commanding vanguard/first battle;
- King John captured.

Avoid false exact tactical coordinates or making Ieuan witness every famous event.

## Guillaume

- fictional minor French knight;
- yields personally to Ieuan;
- working ransom: 100 marks;
- effective benefit to Ieuan: about 40–50 marks after obligations/costs;
- ransom is delayed/structured, not instant cash;
- Guillaume remains human, not comic enemy or new best friend.

## Iorwerth

- wounded at Poitiers;
- survives;
- recovery has real duration/consequence;
- testimony carries weight because relationship was built over years;
- is still not a father substitute.

## Edward le Despenser

- does not know Ieuan as a child;
- personal patronage becomes plausible only after the military record exists;
- reaches full age in 1357;
- performs fictional accolade within a real social/political context.

## Knighting ceremony

Do not confuse ordinary knight-bachelor dubbing with the most elaborate royal/coronation 'Bath' ceremonies.

Sword/spurs/dubbing are safe central elements; religious preparation can be used cautiously.

## Bed-wetting ending

The novel does **not** require:

- a cure;
- a diagnosis;
- a final symbolic accident;
- a statement that it definitely stopped.

Its narrative function has changed from catastrophic shame to one private part of Ieuan's body/life that status cannot redefine.

## Final home rule

Knighthood does not restore 1349.

No secret ancestry.
No inherited castle.
No magical return of Hendre Gelli.
No implication that suffering was worthwhile because it produced status.

# END STATE

The novel ends with **Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd**, about twenty-two.

He has:

- recurring patronage/income sufficient to make the title defensible;
- ransom capital;
- a military future if he chooses/owes further service;
- a place in Glamorgan's armed/gentry-adjacent world that would have been extraordinary for his birth;
- unresolved grief;
- unresolved moral unease about war;
- his father's knife;
- his sister's grave;
- the habits of the boy who survived.

The knight does not replace the boy.

He contains him.
