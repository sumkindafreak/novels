# Scene Cards — Chapters 4–6

These chapters form one continuous collapse from **father → mother → sister's apparent escape**.

They must be drafted as a linked sequence even though they are separate chapters.

Exact-date authority: `02-chapters-1-9-day-by-day-continuity.md`.

---

# CHAPTER 4 — UNDER THE ARM

**Dates:** Saturday **23 May** to Thursday **28 May 1349**  
**Location:** Hendre Gelli / burial route  
**Ieuan:** 14

## Chapter job

Destroy Ieuan's assumption that Dafydd always knows what comes next.

The emotional horror is not simply seeing Dad die. It is watching competence leave the strongest person Ieuan knows.

---

## SCENE 1 — WORKING SICK

**Date:** Saturday 23 May

### Characters

- Dafydd
- Ieuan
- Angharad
- Gwenllian

### Practical action

Dafydd tries to continue ordinary work despite fever/weakness.

Possible task:

- cattle movement;
- fence/bank repair;
- water/fodder-related job;
- tool repair.

### Ieuan wants

For Dad to stop being ill by behaving normally.

### Dafydd

He is irritable partly because he knows he cannot do what his body usually does.

He sends Ieuan to complete something he would normally supervise.

### End change

Ieuan returns and finds Dafydd seated/lying when he should not be.

That is more frightening than dramatic collapse.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE SWELLING

**Date:** Sunday 24 May

### Purpose

Move from denial to recognition.

### Physical observation

Choose final bubo location to match Chapter 3 end — **under the arm** is strongest because title and gesture are already seeded.

Ieuan sees:

- painful swelling;
- Dafydd protecting the arm;
- heat/fever;
- difficulty moving normally.

### Angharad

She becomes the organiser:

- water;
- cloth;
- remedies available to household;
- prayer/help-seeking;
- instructs Ieuan and Gwenllian away/from tasks as needed.

### Ieuan wants

To be useful enough that this stops happening.

### Failure

Every job he completes leaves another undone.

---

## SCENE 3 — DAD'S JOBS

**Date:** Monday 25 May

### Practical load

Ieuan must now handle things Dafydd normally structures:

- animal water;
- feed/grazing decision;
- fire/fuel;
- carrying messages/help requests;
- checking a gate/animal alone.

### Core emotional beat

Nobody tells him whether he has done enough.

### Dafydd lucid moment

Dafydd corrects one practical detail from bed/resting position.

For example:

- knot wrong;
- animal placed badly;
- tool not cleaned;
- gate secured in a way cattle can push.

Ieuan is briefly annoyed at being corrected.

That annoyance will later hurt more than a sentimental goodbye.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE WET NIGHT

**Night of 25/26 May**

### Purpose

Remove Angharad as Ieuan's quiet safety net before she physically dies.

Ieuan wakes wet.

Normally Mam would notice/help organise drying/straw.

This time she is with Dafydd and exhausted.

Ieuan handles everything alone.

### Emotional layer

He initially feels abandoned by Angharad, then sees what she is doing and feels ashamed for resenting her.

No one needs to mention the accident.

### Symbolic change

The little end becomes the first place where Ieuan realises childhood help is disappearing.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE LAST CORRECTION

**Date:** Tuesday 26 May

### Dafydd state

Mostly weak/delirious with one clearer interval.

### Important rule

No prophecy about Ieuan becoming a great man/knight.

No "look after your mother" speech that conveniently summarises the plot.

### Best interaction

Dafydd notices something mundane Ieuan has done incorrectly and corrects him one final time.

Ieuan fixes it.

Dafydd sees that he understood.

A tiny nod/word is enough.

This becomes Ieuan's real inheritance from him: practical trust.

---

## SCENE 6 — WEDNESDAY 27 MAY

### Event

Dafydd dies.

### Staging

Keep physical death specific but not theatrical.

Ieuan may be present for the final breaths or return moments after; choose the version that avoids repeating later bedside deaths.

### Ieuan reaction

Task mode.

He asks/looks for:

- cloth;
- help;
- what to do with the animals;
- burial assistance.

Emotion is delayed.

### Knife

He does **not** immediately take it as an heirloom.

It remains among Dafydd's things until a job requires it.

### Chapter false ending

For several paragraphs the reader may think Dafydd's death is the chapter's endpoint.

It is not.

---

## SCENE 7 — BURIAL

**Date:** Thursday 28 May

### Purpose

Give Dafydd more burial dignity than Gwenllian will later receive.

### Practical reality

Ieuan now learns how exhausting burial is:

- moving a body;
- carrying weight;
- digging/earth;
- reduced number of helpers;
- abbreviated ritual/community strain.

### Meurig

If present, he should be exhausted and stretched thin.

If absent, another minimal religious arrangement may be used. Keep consistency with the community-outcomes file.

### Knife transfer

After burial / upon returning to work, Ieuan needs a knife.

He reaches for Dafydd's.

That is all.

### True chapter end

Back at Hendre Gelli, Angharad shivers.

Ieuan notices.

She says she is tired.

End.

---

# CHAPTER 5 — WHITSUN

**Dates:** Friday **29 May** to Tuesday **2 June 1349**

## Chapter job

Destroy the household's final adult organisational structure and make Ieuan responsible for Gwenllian.

This chapter should feel **too busy for grief**.

---

## SCENE 1 — MAM IS TIRED

**Date:** Friday 29 May

### Opening

Angharad wakes/continues with obvious fever.

Ieuan denies the pattern because recognising it means knowing the ending.

### Ieuan wants

For this illness to be something else.

### Gwenllian

She reads Ieuan's face better than his words.

### Practical conflict

Animals/fire/food/water still demand time while Angharad requires care.

Ieuan physically cannot be in all places.

---

## SCENE 2 — EVERYTHING AT ONCE

**Date:** Saturday 30 May

### Structure

This can be one of the most frantic domestic sequences in the novel.

Tasks collide:

- Angharad needs water;
- Gwenllian is hungry/frightened;
- fire is low;
- animal gets loose/refuses movement;
- food scorches/spills;
- somebody comes to door or nobody comes when Ieuan expected help.

### Core rule

Ieuan fails through **scale**.

He is not stupid.

A household required several people before plague.

### Angharad

Between fever episodes she still notices what Ieuan forgot.

Her competence persists longer than her strength.

---

## SCENE 3 — WHITSUNDAY

**Date:** Sunday 31 May

### Historical/religious contrast

Seven weeks after Easter.

The family should normally be within the Christian/community rhythm.

Instead:

- Angharad cannot leave;
- church support is uncertain;
- neighbours are gone/dead/frightened;
- Ieuan cannot even confidently interpret bells.

### Faith

Ieuan may pray with anger/desperation.

He is still praying.

### Gwenllian

She asks whether Mam will get better.

Ieuan gives a bad, over-detailed lie.

---

## SCENE 4 — ANGharad's LAST USEFUL DAY

**Date:** Monday 1 June

### Lucidity

Angharad has a clearer spell.

### Instructions

She gives practical information because she can see Ieuan will need it:

- food storage;
- what he can carry;
- which upland refuge is usable;
- where water/fuel is;
- Gwenllian's needs;
- perhaps which animal to release rather than attempt to save.

### Emotional restraint

She may understand she is dying, but the scene should not become a speech written for the reader.

### Ieuan

He argues with one instruction because following it feels like admitting she will not recover.

Angharad doesn't need to win the argument verbally; the next day proves her right.

---

## SCENE 5 — TUESDAY 2 JUNE

### Event

Angharad dies.

### Difference from Dafydd's death

Dafydd's death removed authority.

Angharad's removes **organisation and shelter**.

Ieuan immediately understands burial as a physical problem because he did it four days earlier.

This time he has:

- fewer helpers;
- Gwenllian to protect;
- greater fear of remaining in the house;
- his own exhaustion.

### Decision

He cannot do everything.

He covers/wraps Angharad as respectfully as possible and chooses to get Gwenllian out first.

This choice should feel morally awful to him even though it is rational.

### Hard continuity

Dafydd is already buried.

Only Angharad's body remains unresolved in the house when they leave.

---

## SCENE 6 — PACKING WRONG

**Late 2 June / before dawn 3 June**

### Purpose

Show that a fourteen-year-old under grief will pack imperfectly despite practical skills.

Ieuan initially takes too much.

Then has to leave things.

Possible painful choices:

- family object with no survival value;
- extra blanket vs food;
- pot vs weight;
- tool vs clothing.

Dafydd's knife stays because it is both useful and small.

### Gwenllian

She wants to bring something useless that matters to her.

Ieuan must decide whether to allow one comfort object or force survival efficiency.

Allowing one small thing may humanise him.

### Chapter end

Ieuan tells Gwenllian they are going to the cattle shelter.

She asks:

**"When is Mam coming?"**

Ieuan lies.

End.

---

# CHAPTER 6 — UP THE MOUNTAIN

**Dates:** Wednesday **3 June** to Sunday **7 June 1349**  
**Location:** familiar route toward Mynydd Gelli / upland refuge

## Chapter job

Give Ieuan a brief, believable victory — **he thinks he saved Gwenllian** — before taking it away.

If the chapter is grim from the first paragraph, her illness has less force.

---

## SCENE 1 — LEAVING

**Date:** Wednesday 3 June, early light

### Location

Hendre Gelli → familiar upland track

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Gwenllian

### Ieuan wants

Distance from the house and everyone who has died.

### Practical movement

Track physical limits:

- pack weight;
- Gwenllian's walking pace;
- pauses;
- water;
- steep ground;
- weather exposure.

Ieuan knows the route.

Do not manufacture getting lost for drama.

### Looking back

One glance back at Hendre Gelli is enough.

Avoid repeated cinematic turning around.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE HAFOD

**Date:** 3 June, later

### Purpose

Competence produces safety.

Ieuan knows:

- where the roof leaks;
- how to clear/fire the hearth;
- nearest reliable water;
- fuel location;
- animal enclosure;
- what was left/stored there if anything plausible remains.

### Gwenllian

She complains about the shelter being worse than home.

This is good.

A child who complains feels alive.

### Ieuan emotional beat

For the first time since Dafydd became sick, he completes a list of jobs and there is no new catastrophe waiting immediately afterward.

---

## SCENE 3 — ONE GOOD DAY

**Date:** Thursday 4 June

### Essential tonal rule

This scene needs actual relief.

Possible beats:

- food works;
- fire works;
- Gwenllian laughs/argues;
- they find/manage an animal or inspect cattle area;
- Ieuan tells her what they will do tomorrow;
- he starts imagining several days ahead.

### Wetting beat

Night/early morning accident can occur around this point.

Gwenllian notices.

She does **not** tease him.

Ieuan is unsettled because this is unlike her.

She may simply help move something or say nothing.

The scene reveals that she understands the world is serious now.

---

## SCENE 4 — FEVER

**Date:** Friday 5 June

### Opening

Gwenllian is slower/cold/tired.

Ieuan initially rationalises:

- climb exhausted her;
- poor sleep;
- cold shelter.

### Recognition

When he checks/finally sees the swelling, he knows.

Do not require Gwenllian to know immediately.

### Ieuan response

The most frightening difference is that he now recognises the pattern **before an adult can tell him what it means**.

### Scene exit

He starts doing things — water, cloth, prayer, remembered remedy — because action is the only alternative to terror.

---

## SCENE 5 — SATURDAY 6 JUNE

### Purpose

Ieuan's desperate attempt to become every adult he lost.

He imitates:

- Angharad's care;
- Meurig's prayers;
- Dafydd's practical steadiness.

None are enough.

### Penrhys thought

Ieuan may consider Ffynnon Fair/Penrhys.

Reasons not to attempt a desperate carry/journey must be practical and immediate:

- Gwenllian too ill;
- distance/terrain;
- danger below/on routes;
- no guarantee anyone is there;
- moving her may worsen suffering.

He can still pray toward/think of the holy place without the plot forcing an impossible pilgrimage.

### Gwenllian

Give one lucid sibling exchange that is **not** a polished farewell.

She may ask about:

- home;
- Mam;
- food;
- whether Ieuan is going to sleep;
- something tiny and ordinary.

That is stronger than "promise me you'll live."

---

## SCENE 6 — SUNDAY 7 JUNE

### Event

Gwenllian dies.

### Difference from parent deaths

There is no other adult to observe Ieuan now.

He can react badly, irrationally, quietly or all three in sequence.

### Possible immediate actions

- refuses to move her;
- keeps water beside her after she no longer needs it;
- talks as if she can answer;
- gets angry because she will not wake;
- resumes a practical task then stops because it is meaningless.

Avoid melodramatic screaming unless earned by prose/character; Ieuan tends toward stillness when frightened/grieving.

### Sacred Sunday

He knows it is Sunday or recognises the rhythm, but the world gives him no functioning community around it.

### Final question

Eventually practical reality arrives:

A body cannot remain indefinitely in the shelter.

Dafydd had helpers/rites.

Angharad at least had a house and might be found by survivors.

Gwenllian has only Ieuan.

### Chapter end

He realises:

**There is nobody to tell him where to bury her.**

End.

# CROSS-CHAPTER CONTINUITY AUDIT — 4–6

## Dafydd

- first clear illness: 23 May
- dies: 27 May
- buried: 28 May

## Angharad

- fever: 29 May
- severe illness through Whitsun 31 May
- lucid interval/instructions: 1 June
- dies: 2 June
- body left respectfully covered when children flee 3 June

## Gwenllian

- apparently healthy through escape 3 June
- ordinary/healthy enough for relief scene 4 June
- first fever 5 June
- severe illness 6 June
- dies 7 June

## Ieuan

By Chapter 6 end he has gained:

- Dafydd's work responsibilities;
- Dafydd's knife;
- Angharad's practical survival instructions;
- full responsibility for Gwenllian;
- the knowledge that all three adults/child he loved could not be saved by work, prayer or effort.

He has **not** yet chosen life for himself.

That belongs to Chapter 7.

---

# DRAFTING TRAPS TO AVOID

- Do not infect Angharad the instant she touches Dafydd.
- Do not make every sick person cough blood; bubonic disease need not present as pneumonic plague.
- Do not give Dafydd/Angharad perfect final speeches.
- Do not make Ieuan perform adult-scale labour without mistakes/exhaustion.
- Do not leave Dafydd's corpse in Hendre Gelli through 2 June.
- Do not have Ieuan casually abandon Angharad; make the conflict between burial duty and Gwenllian's safety explicit.
- Do not make the upland refuge a surprise cabin.
- Do not make Gwenllian sombre from the instant they flee; she needs the 4 June relief scene.
- Do not make Penrhys a magical cure quest.
- Do not imply Ieuan knows why he survives.
