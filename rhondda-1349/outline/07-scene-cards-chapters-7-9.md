# Scene Cards — Chapters 7–9

These chapters complete the **plague-survival half** of the novel.

They move Ieuan through three distinct states:

1. **waiting to die**;
2. **learning to survive among the dead**;
3. **choosing, reluctantly, to live among people again**.

Exact-date authority: `02-chapters-1-9-day-by-day-continuity.md`.

---

# CHAPTER 7 — THE WAITING

**Dates:** Sunday **7 June** to Sunday **14 June 1349**  
**Location:** upland refuge / slope near Mynydd Gelli  
**Ieuan:** 14

## Chapter job

Remove Ieuan's last external purpose.

Until now, every terrible action has still had a reason:

- help Dad;
- help Mam;
- get Gwenllian away;
- keep Gwenllian alive.

After Gwenllian dies there is no job left except **existing**.

Ieuan does not immediately choose that job.

---

## SCENE 1 — AFTER

**Date:** Sunday 7 June, immediately after Gwenllian's death

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Gwenllian's body

### Practical action

Very little.

Ieuan remains beside her longer than is useful.

He keeps performing obsolete care:

- straightening the blanket;
- placing water nearby;
- adjusting her position;
- checking for breath despite knowing;
- speaking as though she might answer.

### Ieuan wants

For one of the adults to arrive and tell him what happens next.

Nobody does.

### Continuity

Ieuan becomes unnaturally still under fear/grief rather than theatrical.

### Scene end

He notices evening coming and understands the body cannot simply remain where she died.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE GRAVE

**Date:** Monday 8 June

### Practical action

Ieuan buries Gwenllian near the refuge.

The grave should be **bad by Ieuan's own standards**, not absurdly shallow for drama.

Problems:

- poor digging tool / improvised tool;
- stony upland earth;
- exhaustion;
- one boy moving a body;
- no coffin;
- no priest;
- no consecrated churchyard;
- fear of remaining exposed too long.

### Religious layer

Ieuan tries to reproduce what he remembers from Christian burial:

- prayer fragments;
- sign of the cross;
- words he has heard Meurig use but does not know correctly;
- perhaps a remembered psalm phrase without presenting Ieuan as literate in Latin.

### Core guilt

The horror is not that he thinks Gwenllian is discarded.

It is that he knows **this is not what should have been done for her**.

### Object

He may place one small personal thing with her only if it is something already established and genuinely hers.

Do not invent a sentimental trinket at the grave scene.

### Scene end

Ieuan piles the last earth and immediately thinks the grave is not deep enough.

That thought follows him for years.

---

## SCENE 3 — WAITING FOR THE SWELLING

**Dates:** 9–10 June

### Chapter mode

Psychological survival horror.

### Ieuan behaviour

He repeatedly checks:

- armpits;
- groin;
- neck;
- temperature by touch;
- headache;
- weakness;
- every ache from digging/walking.

Every normal bodily sensation becomes evidence that the pestilence has started.

### Food

He has food but eats little.

Important distinction:

**He is not starving yet because food is unavailable. He is starving because grief/fear has stopped him using what he has.**

### Faith

Ieuan moves through:

- prayer;
- bargaining;
- apology;
- anger;
- silence;
- prayer again.

Do not make this a clean religious crisis with a conclusion.

### Ieuan wants

Certainty.

Either become ill or know he will not.

The world gives him neither.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE WET NIGHT

**Working night:** 10/11 June

### Purpose

Make the central premise emotionally meaningful without turning it into biology.

Ieuan wakes wet.

His immediate response is old, automatic shame.

For several moments he is simply fourteen again:

- check the blanket;
- smell;
- panic about somebody seeing;
- remember Mam;
- remember Gwenllian teasing.

Then the absurdity catches him.

Nobody is there to discover it.

Everyone who knew is dead or gone.

### Physical realisation

He has:

- slept deeply;
- woken normally;
- no obvious fever;
- no swelling.

The wet bedding is **not proof of immunity**.

It is merely the first ordinary bodily event strong enough to interrupt his expectation of death.

### Emotional response

Ieuan can laugh once, unexpectedly, and then immediately hate himself for laughing beside Gwenllian's grave.

That tiny involuntary reaction is useful because it proves life has restarted before he consents to it.

---

## SCENE 5 — HUNGER

**Dates:** 11–13 June

### Practical conflict

Stored food at the refuge declines.

Water/fuel require movement.

Ieuan finally cannot ignore hunger.

### First planning thought since Gwenllian

He inventories:

- what remains;
- what can be carried;
- what probably still exists at Hendre Gelli;
- neighbouring stores;
- animals left below.

### Dafydd memory

A practical instruction returns to him rather than a sentimental memory.

Something like:

> Count what you have before deciding what you lack.

Do not write this as a polished aphorism unless seeded earlier; the principle is what matters.

### Scene end

Ieuan decides to go downhill **only for food**.

He does not call this choosing to live.

---

## SCENE 6 — SUNDAY 14 JUNE

### Action

Ieuan prepares to leave the refuge.

He checks Gwenllian's grave before going.

### Crucial rule

Do not have him say goodbye as though he believes he will never return.

He fully intends to come back.

### Physical transformation

One week after her death:

- dirty;
- lighter from poor eating;
- exhausted;
- probably smelling of smoke and damp wool;
- carrying Dafydd's knife;
- no adult knowledge of where he is.

### Chapter end

He starts down the mountain toward the houses.

This mirrors Chapter 6's ascent but with the entire purpose reversed.

---

# CHAPTER 8 — EMPTY HOUSES

**Dates:** Sunday **14 June** through late June 1349  
**Locations:** Hendre Gelli / Madog's holding / fractured Ystradyfodwg orbit

## Chapter job

Teach Ieuan the rules of a world where property, labour, danger and ownership have all become unstable.

This is the most overt **survival-horror** chapter.

The horror should come from familiarity made wrong, not from invented gore.

---

## SCENE 1 — HOME WITHOUT THEM

**Date:** 14 June

### Location

Hendre Gelli

### Ieuan wants

Food, cloth, tools — nothing emotional.

That is what he tells himself.

### Practical action

He enters through the familiar door and cannot stop reading the room by its former occupants:

- Dad's place;
- Mam's work area;
- Gwenllian's sleeping place;
- little end.

### Angharad continuity

Her body is gone.

Unknown survivors have removed/buried her while Ieuan was away.

Do not immediately reveal who.

### Effect

This should initially feel like a second loss rather than relief.

Ieuan does not know where Mam was buried.

### Important object use

Dafydd's knife is now just the knife he reaches for during practical work.

Its emotional significance remains mostly subconscious.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE STORES

### Practical inventory

Ieuan assesses what survived:

- grain/pulses;
- salt if available;
- dried food;
- pottery/containers;
- blanket/cloth;
- fuel;
- tools;
- animal equipment.

### Continuity rule

No treasure-house abundance.

The family was modest and spring stores were already being consumed.

### Problem

Some food may be:

- damp;
- spoiled;
- rodent-damaged;
- difficult to carry;
- useful only if he stays near the house.

### Emotional beat

He takes food from his own house and still feels like a thief because Mam used to decide what he was allowed to eat.

---

## SCENE 3 — MADOG'S HOUSE

**Mid June**

### Purpose

Cross the moral threshold into taking property from the dead.

### Ieuan wants

Something Hendre Gelli lacks — perhaps usable grain, a better vessel, rope, or access to livestock.

### Familiarity

Ieuan knew this place while Madog and Nest lived here.

Specific memory beats are stronger than generic creepiness:

- Madog always put one tool in the wrong place;
- Nest would have objected to muddy boots;
- Elen's presence is visible through ordinary household trace.

### Unknown fate

Nest and Elen fled.

Ieuan does **not** know if they are dead.

That makes taking things harder.

### Decision

He takes only what he can justify to himself.

Later survival will erode that boundary.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE COW

### Practical discovery

One manageable cow, perhaps from the family/neighbouring stock network, has survived and can plausibly be recovered.

### Why one cow matters

It offers:

- milk;
- routine;
- an animal Ieuan understands;
- future fodder obligation;
- something alive that depends on him.

### Animal handling

Use Dafydd's lesson:

**watch before moving**.

Ieuan succeeds because he reads the animal rather than overpowering it.

### Emotional echo

This is the first living thing since Gwenllian whose needs structure Ieuan's day.

Do not over-sentimentalise the cow into a pet.

It is livestock and survival.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE CHURCH WORLD

### Location

Ystradyfodwg orbit / church approach

### Purpose

Show institutional fracture without claiming everybody vanished.

Possible observations:

- church shut/open with nobody expected there;
- grave earth recently disturbed;
- reduced bells;
- missing Meurig;
- survivor giving contradictory information;
- burial activity remembered rather than shown as anonymous corpse pits.

### Ieuan wants

Information:

- Where is Meurig?
- Who buried Mam?
- Who survived?

He receives fragments, not answers.

### Crucial emotional beat

His community has not ended cleanly.

It has **scattered**.

That is harder to understand.

---

## SCENE 6 — GORONWY

**Late June**

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Goronwy ap Tudur

### Goronwy

Another survivor/scavenger, older/stronger than Ieuan but not a stock villain.

He has his own losses and logic.

### Conflict

They converge on the same useful resource:

- food;
- rope/tool;
- livestock;
- shelter;
- cart/pack item.

### Ieuan's assumption

Another living person should mean safety.

### Reality

Another living person means another claim.

### Tone

No melodramatic knife duel unless absolutely necessary.

Better:

- threats;
- bargaining;
- one shove/grab;
- both watching the other's hands;
- each leaving with less than wanted.

### Lesson

The dead are predictable.

The living are not.

---

## SCENE 7 — HAY

**Late June**

### Trigger

Ieuan sees pasture growing / grass at the stage when future fodder must be considered.

### Realisation

The cow surviving **today** is not enough.

Winter requires:

- hay;
- shelter;
- labour;
- storage;
- time.

### Core developmental beat

For the first time since plague began, Ieuan plans several months ahead.

That is more important than any heroic declaration that he wants to live.

### Chapter end

He looks at the work one household requires and finally understands:

**one boy cannot do it all.**

---

# CHAPTER 9 — ONLY TO WORK

**Dates:** early July through autumn 1349  
**Locations:** Hendre Gelli / recovering holdings / routes toward Llantrisant

## Chapter job

Move Ieuan from solitary survival into social dependency **without making it feel like rescue**.

Rhys does not save Ieuan.

He gives him work.

For Ieuan, accepting work is almost harder than accepting food because it means admitting that Hendre Gelli cannot remain the whole of his life.

---

## SCENE 1 — RHYS ARRIVES

**Working date:** early July

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Rhys ap Llywelyn

### Rhys's reason for being there

He is connected to practical estate/stock recovery:

- locating surviving animals;
- identifying dead/absent tenants;
- moving livestock;
- assessing what can still be worked;
- reconnecting routes and supplies.

### First impression

Rhys does not say:

> You poor orphan.

He asks practical questions.

Ieuan can answer them.

### Ieuan wants

Rhys to leave without taking anything or ordering him anywhere.

### Rhys sees

- boy alone;
- useful cow/stock handling;
- surviving stores;
- neglected work;
- competence beyond what he expects from someone this age;
- scale Ieuan cannot possibly manage.

### End beat

Rhys offers food/work for several days.

Ieuan refuses.

---

## SCENE 2 — PROVING HE DOESN'T NEED HELP

### Purpose

Let Ieuan fail naturally rather than immediately accepting sensible advice.

### Practical problem

Choose a task that genuinely requires more labour than one boy can provide:

- haymaking window;
- moving several cattle;
- roof/structure repair;
- harvesting/carrying stores;
- sick/injured animal.

### Ieuan behaviour

He works too long and badly overextends himself.

His flaw is pride, not incompetence.

### Result

He completes part of the job but damages/wastes something through exhaustion or simply cannot finish before weather/time wins.

---

## SCENE 3 — TEMPORARY

### Rhys returns / crosses Ieuan again

Rhys does not gloat.

He modifies the offer:

- temporary;
- specific work;
- food while working;
- possibly help managing/moving one animal;
- no promise of adoption/security.

### Ieuan condition

He agrees only if he can return to Hendre Gelli.

Rhys is fine with that.

This unsettles Ieuan because he expected a struggle.

### Chapter title use

Ieuan's internal explanation becomes:

**Only to work.**

Not leaving.

Not joining anyone.

Not starting another life.

Only to work.

---

## SCENE 4 — WORKING BESIDE MEN AGAIN

**July/August**

### Purpose

Reintroduce ordinary human friction.

Ieuan has to deal with:

- instructions;
- jokes he doesn't understand immediately;
- people touching his tools/food;
- shared meals;
- being called boy;
- arguments over animals;
- somebody complaining about his smell/clothes in a completely non-plague way.

### Emotional importance

Annoyance is evidence of normal society returning.

### Rhys

He judges Ieuan by work:

- does he listen?
- does he overclaim?
- can he handle an unfamiliar animal?
- does he return what he borrows?

---

## SCENE 5 — FIRST JOURNEY TOWARD LLANTRISANT

### Ieuan's world expands

The route introduces:

- more travellers;
- surviving officials;
- carts/pack animals;
- different speech;
- evidence that the wider world did not end with Glynrhondda.

### Ieuan reaction

Part relief, part resentment.

How dare ordinary life continue?

### Language seed

He hears more English/Anglo-Norman administrative speech than at home and understands little.

Do not accelerate language learning yet.

---

## SCENE 6 — THE SHARED NIGHT

### Purpose

Bring the wetting problem back the instant society returns.

Ieuan has to sleep in a shared servant/work space.

He remains awake far too long because he is terrified of an accident.

Possible outcomes:

- dry night but terrible sleep;
- slight accident hidden successfully;
- wakes early and checks obsessively.

Do **not** have Rhys discover it yet; that belongs later in their relationship.

### Emotional point

During plague, wetting became irrelevant because nobody was there.

Human society restores shame instantly.

---

## SCENE 7 — BACK TO HENDRE GELLI

### Ieuan returns home after the first work period

He expects relief.

Instead the holding feels smaller and emptier than before.

### Key question

Is home still a place he lives, or somewhere he returns to?

He will not consciously answer yet.

### Gwenllian

He returns to/checks her grave on the next opportunity.

The guilt remains.

### Angharad

Still no certain grave/location.

This unresolved absence should remain available for later emotional use.

---

## SCENE 8 — AUTUMN: CHOICE WITHOUT ADMITTING CHOICE

**Late summer/autumn 1349**

### Practical facts

Ieuan increasingly works with Rhys because:

- food is steadier;
- work is shared;
- animals/routes interest him;
- Hendre Gelli is impossible alone;
- winter is coming.

### Important rule

No formal adoption.

No speech where Rhys asks him to become family.

The relationship grows through repeated work.

### Final image/beat

Ieuan sleeps one night away from Glynrhondda **by choice rather than necessity**.

Before sleep he tells himself:

> only to work.

The reader understands this is the first step out of his old life.

End plague-survival movement.

# CROSS-CHAPTER CONTINUITY AUDIT — 7–9

## Gwenllian

- dies 7 June;
- buried by Ieuan 8 June near the upland refuge;
- grave is outside consecrated ground;
- depth/quality remains a lifelong guilt point;
- Ieuan intends to return/check it and does so later.

## Ieuan illness expectation

- has seen three close family plague deaths;
- remains symptom-free through the waiting week;
- repeatedly checking lymph-node areas is behaviour, not modern diagnosis;
- wetting never becomes medical proof of immunity.

## Food

- refuge food declines but is not instantly exhausted;
- spring/early-summer household stores at Hendre Gelli are limited and imperfect;
- abandoned stores supplement survival;
- wild food remains secondary;
- milk from one manageable cow becomes valuable but does not solve all nutrition needs.

## Angharad

- body left respectfully covered when children flee 3 June;
- gone when Ieuan returns 14 June;
- removed/buried by unknown survivors;
- Ieuan does not know exact burial place.

## Meurig

- missing/presumed dead from Ieuan's viewpoint;
- do not confirm unless later structure needs it.

## Nest / Elen

- fled before Ieuan's scavenging period;
- fate unknown;
- possessions at their holding therefore raise genuine moral ambiguity.

## Goronwy

- survivor/scavenger, not villain or mentor;
- proves that surviving humans create competing claims.

## Rhys

- enters early July;
- practical stock/route worker;
- knows nothing yet about Ieuan's wetting;
- sees skill before biography;
- offers temporary work, not rescue/adoption.

## Hendre Gelli

By the end of Chapter 9 it remains emotionally **home**, but is no longer a viable self-contained future for Ieuan.

That distinction should survive into later chapters.

# PLAGUE-HALF END STATE

At the end of Chapter 9, Ieuan is still fourteen.

He has lost:

- father;
- mother;
- sister;
- confidence in ordinary parish/community continuity;
- the assumption that home alone can sustain him.

He retains:

- Dafydd's knife;
- cattle/land/weather competence;
- Welsh language and faith framework;
- bed-wetting shame;
- Gwenllian's grave;
- Hendre Gelli;
- an emerging relationship with Rhys;
- the ability to work.

Most importantly, he has not decided to become anything.

He is simply doing the next job.

That is the correct handoff into Chapter 10.
