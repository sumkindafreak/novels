# Scene Cards — Chapters 10–12

These chapters must solve the novel's largest time-compression problem.

Ieuan cannot jump from **fourteen-year-old plague survivor** to **twenty-year-old armed household servant** through summary alone.

The structure therefore uses a series of **representative work crises**. Each scene must change what Ieuan is trusted to do.

The reader should feel years passing through:

- seasons;
- changing horses/tasks;
- language acquisition;
- sleeping arrangements;
- equipment he is first forbidden to touch, then ordered to maintain, then eventually permitted to use;
- the slowly changing way older men address him.

Continuity authority: `04-chapters-10-18-continuity-spine.md` and Pass Three `bridge/` files.

---

# CHAPTER 10 — THE HORSE BOY

**Dates:** winter 1349/50 through approximately 1352  
**Ieuan:** 14–17  
**Locations:** Llantrisant orbit / Glamorgan estate routes / occasional return to Glynrhondda

## Chapter job

Turn Ieuan's inherited cattle/weather competence into professional horse and transport skill.

The chapter must prove that his later military usefulness begins with **years of boring work**.

---

## SCENE 1 — FIRST WINTER AWAY

**Date:** winter 1349/50

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Rhys ap Llywelyn
- other fictional stable/stock servants

### Practical action

Ieuan works through his first winter inside a larger labour system.

Tasks:

- feed;
- water;
- mucking out;
- carrying hay;
- keeping tack dry;
- moving stock in poor weather;
- learning that horses require different handling from cattle.

### Ieuan wants

To prove he could leave whenever he chose.

### Reality

He increasingly depends on:

- regular meals;
- shared labour;
- access to tools/animals;
- other people's route knowledge.

### Wetting continuity

Shared sleeping becomes a nightly source of dread.

He deliberately:

- drinks less late when he can;
- sleeps lightly;
- wakes early;
- hides/changes straw when necessary.

Do not make this medically effective or magically curative.

### Emotional beat

The first time somebody complains that Ieuan is slow/tired from poor sleep, he nearly tells the truth and cannot.

---

## SCENE 2 — HORSE IS NOT COW

**1350**

### Practical lesson

Rhys gives Ieuan responsibility for a difficult but ordinary riding/pack horse.

Ieuan initially applies cattle logic too bluntly and gets corrected.

### Skills introduced

- grooming;
- picking/inspecting feet in period-appropriate practical terms;
- noticing heat/swelling/lameness;
- tack fit;
- leading without fighting the animal;
- basic work riding.

### Dafydd echo

Ieuan's instinct to **watch before moving** still helps once Rhys teaches him what to watch for.

### Important rule

Ieuan is not a natural horse whisperer.

He improves because he spends hundreds of hours doing it.

### Scene end

Rhys leaves him responsible for several animals while he goes elsewhere.

It is the first adult task Ieuan receives that does not feel temporary.

---

## SCENE 3 — A ROUTE HE DOESN'T KNOW

**1350–51**

### Purpose

Expand the map beyond Ieuan's childhood competence.

### Action

Ieuan accompanies a pack/stock movement on an unfamiliar Glamorgan route.

He is no longer the boy who knows every stream and ridge.

### Failure

He makes a small but costly navigation/assumption error:

- takes a poorer crossing;
- misjudges distance to water;
- assumes a route remains passable;
- misunderstands an instruction given partly in unfamiliar speech.

### Learning

He begins deliberately memorising:

- landmarks;
- inns/holdings;
- bridges/fords;
- whose land is whose;
- words used by different servants/officials.

### Language seed

English and Anglo-Norman administrative/military words enter as **work vocabulary**, not classroom learning.

---

## SCENE 4 — RETURN TO HENDRE GELLI

**Working placement:** 1351

### Purpose

Prevent the Rhondda from disappearing from the novel for six years.

### Action

Ieuan returns during work/leave/stock business.

### What has changed

- Hendre Gelli is still physically recognisable;
- parts are deteriorating or used differently;
- surviving people have rearranged claims/work;
- the valley has life again, but not the old pattern.

### Gwenllian

Ieuan checks her grave.

He is taller/stronger than the boy who dug it.

The grave looks smaller than memory.

That disturbs him.

### Angharad

He still has no reliable grave location for Mam.

### Emotional change

Ieuan realises returning home does not rewind time.

---

## SCENE 5 — SIR MATTHEW'S HORSES

**1351–52**

### Historical/fictional interface

Sir Matthew de Soor enters the story through the wider Glamorgan administrative/knightly work network.

Do not stage a grand introduction.

Ieuan first knows **Matthew's animals/equipment/servants before he knows Matthew personally**.

### Practical action

Ieuan is assigned to better horses and more valuable equipment.

Consequences:

- mistakes cost more;
- hierarchy is sharper;
- servants are expected to look presentable/useful;
- military gear becomes part of the work environment.

### Ieuan wants

To be trusted with the better animals.

### Class lesson

A horse can have more status/value than Ieuan feels he does.

### End beat

Matthew or a senior servant specifically asks for **the Welsh boy who handled that horse** on a later journey/task.

Ieuan has become personally useful rather than interchangeable labour.

---

## SCENE 6 — THE HORSE BOY

**Approx. 1352, Ieuan 16–17**

### Purpose

Close the chapter by showing genuine progress without social fantasy.

### New position

Ieuan is now trusted enough to:

- travel with better mounts;
- carry messages/equipment;
- supervise younger/lower servants occasionally;
- prepare horses for men who carry arms;
- sleep/eat within a more structured household service environment.

### What he is still not

- esquire;
- man-at-arms;
- knight's social equal;
- expert fighter.

### Final beat

A fighting man tosses Ieuan a piece of armour/weapon equipment and tells him to clean/prepare it properly.

Ieuan catches it.

For the first time, war enters his hands as **work**.

---

# CHAPTER 11 — A MAN'S PLACE

**Dates:** approximately 1352–1354, with 1355 mobilisation approaching  
**Ieuan:** 17–19  
**Locations:** Glamorgan / Matthew de Soor's wider service network / travelling household environment

## Chapter job

Show Ieuan crossing from **servant near armed men** to **servant expected to become useful in armed situations**.

This is the social rung that makes the ending possible.

---

## SCENE 1 — ARMING ANOTHER MAN

**1352/53**

### Practical action

Ieuan helps prepare a man for armed duty.

Tasks may include:

- cleaning/drying mail;
- handling padded protection;
- checking straps/buckles;
- carrying helmet/shield/weapon;
- preparing horse/tack.

### Sensory lesson

Military equipment is:

- heavy;
- oily;
- expensive;
- uncomfortable;
- constantly maintained.

### Ieuan wants

To be allowed to handle the weapon, not merely carry it.

### Adult response

He is told, essentially:

> When you can maintain it without ruining it, perhaps somebody will care what you can do with it.

No exact line required.

---

## SCENE 2 — IORWERTH AP MORGAN

**Working date:** 1353

### Introduction

Iorwerth enters as an already-established Welsh uchelwr/esquire in the same broader military world.

### First impression

Ieuan expects shared language to mean solidarity.

It does not.

Iorwerth sees:

- capable servant;
- low-born boy becoming too aware of armed status;
- somebody who needs discipline more than encouragement.

### Relationship rule

No instant mentorship.

Iorwerth makes Ieuan work.

### Small humiliation

Iorwerth corrects Ieuan publicly on something practical/social.

Ieuan stores the humiliation for weeks.

---

## SCENE 3 — FIRST STRUCTURED WEAPON WORK

**1353–54**

### Reason it happens

Ieuan is increasingly travelling in contexts where servants may need to defend:

- horses;
- baggage;
- household members;
- route parties.

Training is therefore **useful labour**, not knight school.

### Skills

Start with practical weapons/formation concepts:

- staff/spear;
- shield/guarding;
- basic sword handling later;
- moving on command;
- holding position;
- not crowding another man.

### Important rule

Ieuan is bad/average at first.

His advantage is:

- stamina;
- balance on rough ground;
- listening when he suppresses pride;
- comfort around animals/confusion.

### Iorwerth's lesson

The first real martial lesson should be about **where to stand**, not how to kill.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE DANGEROUS ERRAND

**1354**

### Purpose

Give Ieuan his first situation where carrying a weapon matters before full war.

### Possible task

A small travelling/escort duty involving:

- valuable horses;
- money/documents;
- supplies;
- disputed route/rough men;
- threat of theft/violence.

### Conflict

Ieuan wants to demonstrate courage.

Iorwerth/Matthew's people need him to **obey and protect the objective**.

### Result

Ieuan nearly ruins things by moving toward the threat too soon, or must actively restrain himself from doing so.

He learns that armed service is not permission to chase danger.

This lesson pays off in 1355 and Poitiers.

---

## SCENE 5 — BETTER CLOTH, BETTER STEEL

**Late 1354**

### Status shown materially

Ieuan receives access to better protection/equipment because his duties now justify it.

Possible progression:

- padded garment;
- simple helmet;
- spear;
- later hand-me-down mail elements.

### Emotional conflict

He is proud.

Then he realises somebody else used/wore much of it before him.

That is historically/socially more fitting than a custom hero armour reveal.

### Class friction

A better-born servant/esquire resents Ieuan receiving equipment/status.

Do not turn this man automatically into a villain.

His objection can be socially intelligible:

**Ieuan is crossing a boundary people were raised to believe mattered.**

---

## SCENE 6 — THE OLD SECRET

**1354**

### Wetting continuity

Ieuan has an accident during a period of closer household travel/sleeping.

He successfully conceals it, but at greater social risk than before.

### Key point

His body has changed:

- older;
- stronger;
- more capable;
- increasingly treated as a man.

The problem remaining makes him feel fraudulent.

### Psychological consequence

His desire for higher status is partly sharpened by the fear that **one childish secret could prove everyone who doubts his place correct**.

---

## SCENE 7 — ORDERS COMING

**Late 1354 / early 1355**

### External change

The household/military network begins preparing for overseas service.

Ieuan hears:

- Gascony;
- ships;
- horses;
- supplies;
- Warwick;
- prince;
- wages/service obligations.

### Matthew

Matthew's real military/administrative connections now become central.

Ieuan is retained because he can:

- handle horses;
- travel;
- obey;
- maintain gear;
- fight enough to be useful if pressed.

### Ieuan wants

To be chosen.

This is important.

He is no longer merely swept into events.

### Chapter end

When told he will be part of the preparations, Ieuan says yes too quickly.

Later that night he realises **France means leaving Gwenllian's grave across the sea**.

---

# CHAPTER 12 — THE SEA

**Dates:** spring 1355 through autumn arrival in Gascony; campaign leaves Bordeaux **5 October 1355**  
**Ieuan:** 20  
**Locations:** Wales/Glamorgan mobilisation → English south-coast horse shipping → Gascony/Bordeaux

## Chapter job

Make the overseas expedition feel like a huge logistical undertaking before it becomes war.

Ieuan's first great military value is **getting horses to France alive and usable**.

---

## SCENE 1 — GATHERING

**Historical anchor:** **26 April 1355**

Sir Matthew de Soor and historical Welshman Ieuan ap Ruyn are documented gathering supplies in Wales for Warwick's retinue.

### Fictional placement

Our Ieuan works inside this broader mobilisation world without replacing the historical Ieuan.

### Practical action

- assembling animals;
- checking loads;
- moving fodder/equipment;
- identifying unsuitable horses;
- handling complaints/shortages;
- long days on familiar Welsh roads now serving a continental war.

### Emotional contrast

The same landscape that once contained Ieuan's entire universe has become a supply source for an army abroad.

---

## SCENE 2 — LAST RHONDDA RETURN

**Spring/summer 1355**

### Purpose

Give Ieuan one deliberate return before he leaves Britain.

### Gwenllian

He visits the grave.

This time he may attempt to improve/repair it:

- stones;
- earth;
- marker remembered privately rather than elaborate monument.

### Hendre Gelli

Ieuan recognises that he no longer expects to live there soon.

This is the first time he can admit that to himself.

### Dafydd's knife

He checks/maintains it before leaving.

It remains a work knife, not ceremonial relic.

### No goodbye certainty

Ieuan expects to return.

War has not yet taught him otherwise.

---

## SCENE 3 — THE HORSE SHIPS

**Late summer 1355**

### Historical texture

Campaign preparations included special shipboard arrangements for horses, including hurdles/gangplanks.

Warwick/Suffolk horse shipping is tied to the south-coast transport network; exact vessel/harbour details should stay within sourced limits during prose drafting.

### Practical horror

Horses dislike:

- confined ramps;
- unstable footing;
- noise;
- strange smells;
- crowding.

Ieuan's work matters immediately.

### Risk

One animal panics during loading.

Ieuan's response establishes why experienced horse servants are military assets before anyone draws a sword.

---

## SCENE 4 — AT SEA

### Sensory focus

Avoid romantic sea imagery.

Ieuan's crossing is:

- cramped;
- wet;
- foul-smelling;
- noisy;
- seasick men;
- distressed animals;
- constant concern for footing, water and injury.

### Wetting

Sea travel/shared quarters make concealment difficult.

Do not force an accident if the chapter already has enough tension, but Ieuan's fear of one should exist.

### Emotional realisation

At some point he understands there is literally water between him and Wales.

That is a bigger psychological threshold than seeing a map ever could be.

---

## SCENE 5 — GASCONY

### Arrival

Ieuan encounters:

- unfamiliar speech;
- larger military concentrations;
- expensive equipment;
- foreign architecture/agriculture;
- men from many regions;
- horses exhausted by transport.

### Edward le Despenser

Edward may appear **at distance or in a brief formal context** as a real young knight within the Prince's army.

No meaningful personal meeting yet.

### Iorwerth

He is now more important to Ieuan's day-to-day military education as Matthew's broader service network merges into Warwick's campaigning environment.

---

## SCENE 6 — WHAT THE ARMY EATS

### Purpose

Ground the army before the raid.

Ieuan learns logistics at a scale beyond household travel:

- fodder requirements;
- ration/supply pressure;
- horses needing shoeing/attention;
- baggage order;
- who is responsible for what;
- theft/pilfering/discipline tensions.

### Theme

An army is not thousands of swords.

It is thousands of mouths and animals that must keep moving.

This is a world Ieuan understands better than many glamorous young fighters.

---

## SCENE 7 — 5 OCTOBER 1355

### Historical anchor

The grande chevauchée leaves Bordeaux.

### Ieuan's status

Trusted mounted household servant / armed valet-level retainer.

He is **not yet formally/functionally an esquire/man-at-arms**.

### What he carries

Final prose must track:

- horse;
- practical weapon(s);
- protection appropriate to current status;
- tools/horse gear;
- Dafydd's knife.

### Ieuan's expectation

He imagines war primarily as danger from enemy soldiers.

### Reader-facing irony

The real test will be:

- distance;
- hunger/fodder;
- exhaustion;
- burning;
- obedience;
- the moral reality of chevauchée.

### Chapter end

The column starts moving away from Bordeaux.

Ieuan watches the horse in front of him rather than the banners.

That is who he still is.

# CROSS-CHAPTER CONTINUITY AUDIT — 10–12

## Ieuan's age/status

- winter 1349/50: 14–15, working boy;
- 1350–52: groom/pack/route servant;
- 1352: enters Matthew de Soor's wider service network;
- 1353–54: mounted valet / armed-household trajectory;
- spring–summer 1355: trusted armed household servant;
- 5 October 1355: **not yet full man-at-arms**.

Formal/functioning man-at-arms promotion remains winter **1355–56** after campaign proof.

## Skill progression

Do not skip:

1. cattle competence;
2. ordinary horse handling;
3. better-horse responsibility;
4. route/pack experience;
5. equipment maintenance;
6. structured weapons training;
7. armed escort/service;
8. campaign service.

## Language

Welsh remains Ieuan's emotional/default language.

English/Anglo-Norman/French acquisition is practical and uneven:

- commands;
- horse/tack terms;
- ranks;
- food/payment/travel words;
- repeated household phrases.

He should not become implausibly fluent through montage.

## Wetting

It persists across adolescence, perhaps becoming less frequent, but never disappears because the story moves into a more adult setting.

Social danger increases as his rank rises.

Rhys has not yet necessarily discovered it during early work unless later drafting chooses the existing Pass Two planned reveal point; Iorwerth's major discovery remains **winter 1355–56**.

## Hendre Gelli/Gwenllian

The Rhondda must recur through returns/memory so the military half never feels like a different novel.

Ieuan should physically revisit Gwenllian's grave at least once before leaving for Gascony.

## Matthew de Soor

Historical facts may anchor:

- Glamorgan role;
- 1355 Welsh supply mobilisation;
- Warwick connection;
- 1355 army placement.

Private dialogue/mentorship is fiction.

Do not claim Matthew's presence at Poitiers without stronger evidence.

## Iorwerth

Fictional.

Introduced before France so his Poitiers importance does not appear late.

Relationship progression:

- correction/friction;
- professional observation;
- earned respect;
- campaign trust;
- winter secret discovery;
- Poitiers loyalty.

---

# END STATE AFTER CHAPTER 12

Ieuan is twenty.

He has crossed the sea with an army.

He knows:

- horses;
- work;
- household rank;
- basic armed service;
- how to make himself useful.

He does **not** yet know:

- what a chevauchée really does to civilians;
- whether he can kill under pressure;
- whether armed men will accept him as one of them;
- that the 1355 return will earn his promotion;
- that he is only one year away from Poitiers.

The reader should feel the military story beginning, not the hero arriving.
