# Chapters 10–18 — Continuity Spine, 1350–1357

This file controls the **time jumps, status changes and campaign chronology** for the second half of the novel.

Where exact historical dates are secure, use them. Where Ieuan's fictional service history is not documented, use ranges and show enough scenes to make the progression feel earned.

Ieuan is treated as born **early in 1335** for age continuity: fourteen in April 1349, about twenty at the beginning of the 1355 expedition, twenty-one at Poitiers and about twenty-two at the 1357 knighting.

---

# CHAPTER 10 — THE HORSE BOY

**Story span:** 1350–1352  
**Ieuan:** about 15–17  
**Primary world:** Llantrisant / Glamorgan routes / stable and transport work

## Continuity purpose

This chapter must **show compression**, not merely announce it.

Use a small number of representative scenes separated by visible seasonal/time shifts.

## 1350 — first full year in service

Ieuan is still fundamentally a labouring servant.

Show:

- learning that horses react differently from cattle;
- grooming, watering, feeding, mucking, leading, loading and tack care;
- ordinary falls/mistakes;
- one animal he handles badly before learning to read it;
- route work with Rhys;
- language exposure around officials and higher households;
- sleeping among servants and hiding wet bedding when accidents occur.

### Status

No military identity.

He is useful because he works.

---

## 1351

Ieuan becomes reliable enough to be sent without Rhys for small tasks.

Possible scenes:

- trusted with a better horse;
- sent with pack animals or messages;
- first time responsible for a younger/lower servant;
- learns that a lost horse or damaged saddle can be economically disastrous.

The political/administrative personnel of Glamorgan continue changing after the 1349 minority administration; do not make Ieuan understand this in modern institutional terms.

---

## 1352 — Matthew de Soor route opens

Ieuan enters the wider service network associated with **Sir Matthew de Soor**.

He does not become Matthew's personal favourite immediately.

He is one of the competent young men who can:

- move horses;
- carry equipment;
- travel reliably;
- keep his mouth shut;
- complete instructions.

### Chapter end

Ieuan is ordered to travel with Matthew's people rather than return to routine stable work.

His old life does not end ceremonially; the next job simply takes him farther away.

---

# CHAPTER 11 — A MAN'S PLACE

**Story span:** 1352–1354  
**Ieuan:** 17–19

## Purpose

Make the transition from servant to **armed household usefulness** visible.

## 1352–53

New exposure:

- armour cleaning and repair assistance;
- mail care;
- sword/lance handling as equipment before they become weapons he is entitled to use;
- better riding;
- travelling with armed men;
- witnessing violence without necessarily participating centrally.

Ieuan learns hierarchy through daily humiliations:

- where he sleeps;
- where he eats;
- who gets fresh horses;
- who speaks first;
- who may carry what weapon.

## Weapon training rule

He trains because a mounted household servant may become more useful if armed, **not because somebody discovers hidden sword genius**.

Progression:

1. staff/spear fundamentals;
2. safe sword handling;
3. defence/footwork;
4. fighting while tired/encumbered;
5. working around horses while armed.

## Iorwerth ap Morgan

Introduce fictional Welsh uchelwr/esquire **Iorwerth ap Morgan** during this period or late in it.

Contrast:

- same first language;
- similar practical soldiering environment;
- radically different childhood/social assumptions.

Iorwerth initially treats Ieuan correctly according to rank, not cruelly.

## 1354

Ieuan is now an experienced mounted valet/armed servant.

He can:

- ride all day for work;
- maintain a fighting man's equipment;
- stand guard;
- carry spear/sidearm when ordered;
- function around threat;
- obey formation/escort discipline.

He is **not yet an esquire/man-at-arms**.

### Chapter end

News/orders make clear that overseas campaigning is coming.

---

# CHAPTER 12 — THE SEA

**Story span:** spring–September 1355  
**Ieuan:** about 20

## Historical anchor — 26 April 1355

Sir Matthew de Soor and a historical Welshman, **Ieuan ap Ruyn**, are documented gathering supplies in Wales for Thomas Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick's retinue.

Our fictional Ieuan ap Dafydd can therefore plausibly work inside the same mobilisation environment without being confused with the real man.

## Spring–summer 1355

Ieuan helps with:

- horse acquisition/movement;
- supplies;
- pack loads;
- transport organisation;
- practical Welsh routes and local labour.

This is his last extended period in Wales before Gascony.

## Embarkation

Warwick/Suffolk horse transport evidence supports **Southampton** as a strong retinue shipping anchor.

Do not automatically put Ieuan on the Prince's Plymouth transport simply because it is famous.

Horse transport scenes should include:

- gangplanks;
- hurdles/partitions;
- frightened animals;
- slipping/weight problems;
- feed/water constraints;
- human seasickness and animal stress.

Ieuan's horse competence is more important aboard ship than his weapon skill.

## September 1355 — Gascony

Ieuan enters the enormous military world around Bordeaux.

Edward le Despenser may be seen/identified as a real knight in the army, but there is no personal relationship yet.

### Chapter end

The army prepares to ride.

# CHAPTER 13 — FIRE

**Exact campaign anchor:** **5 October 1355** — the Prince's army leaves Bordeaux on the grande chevauchée.  
**Ieuan:** 20

## 5 October–8 November — outward march

Historical campaign scale:

- roughly 300 miles from Bordeaux to Narbonne;
- **23 marching days** within 35 total days;
- average about **13 miles per marching day**;
- rest days matter for horses and men.

## Ieuan's lived reality

Repeated tasks:

- wake before movement;
- water/feed/check horses;
- repair tack;
- redistribute loads;
- find/secure forage under orders;
- keep pace;
- care for sore backs/feet/lameness;
- sleep badly;
- repeat.

Avoid montage that makes these tasks look effortless.

## Moral rupture

Ieuan sees deliberate destruction of:

- fields;
- stores;
- homes;
- animals/resources not taken by the army.

The connection to 1349 is internal and sensory rather than speechified:

- smoke;
- abandoned bowls/tools;
- displaced children;
- winter food destroyed.

He recognises the aftermath because he once lived inside it.

## Sunday 8 November — Narbonne

Historical anchor: army reaches Narbonne and halts there/nearby before turning back.

### Chapter end

Ieuan looks at the destruction and understands that surviving Death has led him into a profession that manufactures ruin.

---

# CHAPTER 14 — THE RETURN

**Historical span:** 9 November–2 December 1355

## Campaign pressure

The return is harder than the outward march:

- roughly 289 miles in 24 days;
- 19 marching days;
- average about **15 miles per marching day**;
- fewer rests;
- growing baggage;
- pursuit/threat;
- short late-autumn daylight.

Historical scholarship estimates about **9.7 hours of daylight around Narbonne on 10 November**, declining to about **8.9 hours by La Réole on 2 December**.

This is a useful concrete pressure: lost daylight is logistical danger.

## Late-November fictional promotion incident

Place the skirmish/alarm **after the army has turned homeward and fatigue has accumulated**, but before re-entry into secure territory.

Ieuan:

1. reads a horse/route blockage correctly;
2. moves animals in the necessary order;
3. obeys an order to hold when armed pressure arrives;
4. kills/fights because the job requires it;
5. does not chase retreating enemies;
6. returns to the animals/equipment.

### Why witnesses matter

Iorwerth and/or other household men must see enough of this that later promotion is not based on hearsay from Ieuan himself.

## 28 November

Historical campaign reaches/enters friendly Gascon territory; some Gascon participants disperse.

## Wednesday 2 December 1355 — La Réole

Historical end point for the hard march.

### Chapter end

Ieuan expects another stable job.

Instead, his status is being discussed by men above him.

# CHAPTER 15 — WINTER ARMS

**Span:** 2 December 1355–early July 1356  
**Ieuan:** 20–21

## Historical frame

Winter/spring is not dead time.

The army/retinues deal with:

- repairs;
- wages/accounts;
- resupply;
- horse shoeing/replacement;
- garrison/frontier operations;
- preparation for renewed campaigning.

## Promotion lock

During winter quarters Ieuan becomes **functionally/formally an esquire/man-at-arms** within Warwick's wider military world.

The exact bureaucratic phrase may vary in prose, but the change must be visible through material consequences.

## Status consequences

Ieuan receives/uses:

- better campaign horse;
- substantial armour;
- lance/spear;
- sword/sidearm;
- higher pay/standing;
- expectation to fight in the men-at-arms line;
- some responsibility for lower servants.

## Bed-wetting continuity scene

A winter accident is discovered by Iorwerth.

Ieuan expects social destruction.

Iorwerth treats it as a bedding/logistics nuisance:

> Change it before it freezes.

The scene matters because it is the first outsider who knows and continues to judge Ieuan primarily by competence.

## Early 1356

Use one smaller armed operation/escort/garrison incident to prove Poitiers will not be Ieuan's first fight after promotion.

### Chapter end

Orders for the next major northern campaign arrive.

# CHAPTER 16 — THE ROAD NORTH

**Historical span:** **6 July–17 September 1356**  
**Ieuan:** 21

## Wednesday 6 July 1356

The Prince leaves Bordeaux to join the army assembling at **La Réole**.

Historical scholarship places the army around **8,000–10,000** at assembly, though exact totals remain debated.

## Around 22 July

Army moves from La Réole toward Bergerac rather than repeating the 1355 southern route.

## 14 August

Working campaign-phase anchor: army reaches/crosses toward the Vienne/Manot phase.

## 14 August–5 September

Movement through the Romorantin phase.

Use:

- longer campaign experience making Ieuan less dazzled than in 1355;
- worsening strategic uncertainty;
- destruction now familiar enough to be more morally troubling, not less;
- Iorwerth treating him as a colleague/subordinate man-at-arms rather than promoted servant.

## 5–17 September — manoeuvre toward Poitiers

French pressure tightens.

The army is no longer simply raiding at will.

Need dominates:

- food;
- water;
- horse condition;
- information;
- route choice;
- avoiding being trapped by a larger French force.

## Saturday 17 September

Armies are close enough that major confrontation is effectively unavoidable.

### Chapter end

Ieuan understands that tomorrow or the day after may be the largest battle he has ever seen.

He checks his father's knife among equipment that now includes a real sword.

# CHAPTER 17 — MAUPERTUIS

**Dates:** Sunday **18 September** and Monday **19 September 1356**

## Sunday 18 September — crisis before battle

Historical framework:

- Anglo-Gascon army needs water/provisions;
- French forces are close;
- negotiations/movement delay immediate full battle;
- terrain around Nouaillé-Maupertuis shapes the coming fight.

Use Sunday for dread rather than action overload.

Ieuan has lived through another Sunday where death is imminent.

Do not force him to consciously compare it to Gwenllian's Sunday death unless prose earns the association naturally.

## Monday 19 September — Battle of Poitiers

### Secure large-scale anchors

- strong difficult/hedged position;
- archers contribute decisively against opening mounted attacks;
- French assaults become sustained close fighting;
- Warwick commands the vanguard/first battle;
- King John II of France is captured later in the battle.

### Ieuan lock

Ieuan fights **dismounted as an esquire/man-at-arms in Warwick's wider vanguard**.

### Personal sequence

1. fighting remains collective, crowded and confusing;
2. Iorwerth is badly wounded;
3. Ieuan does not irresponsibly abandon his place the instant he sees it;
4. when formation/opening/order permits, Ieuan helps recover/protect Iorwerth;
5. fictional French knight **Sir Guillaume de la Motte** presses the group;
6. Ieuan defeats/overmasters him in plausible close combat with help/context rather than superhero duelling;
7. Guillaume yields;
8. Ieuan accepts surrender;
9. Ieuan gets Iorwerth and prisoner through the surviving battle chaos.

### Emotional rule

The climax is not "Ieuan discovers he is brave."

He has been brave repeatedly.

The payoff is that **terror no longer prevents him doing the next necessary thing**.

### Chapter end

Not the knighting.

The battle is won, Iorwerth is alive for now, Guillaume is Ieuan's prisoner, and Ieuan is still simply exhausted and filthy enough that the word "knight" feels absurdly distant.

# CHAPTER 18 — SIR IEUAN

**Span:** 20 September 1356–spring/summer 1357

This chapter needs enough space to prevent the ending from feeling like instant reward.

## 20 September–2 October 1356 — return south

Historical route returns toward Bordeaux after Poitiers.

The army is now moving with:

- famous prisoners;
- wounded men;
- booty;
- administrative/ransom burdens;
- victory stories already changing shape in retelling.

Ieuan is responsible for a living prisoner, not a bag of money.

## Autumn/winter 1356–57 — ransom reality

**Sir Guillaume de la Motte ransom lock:** 100 marks (£66 13s 4d).

The ransom is negotiated/secured over time.

Ieuan does not instantly receive 100 marks in a purse.

After superior shares, costs and obligations his effective benefit is about **40–50 marks**.

This becomes capital.

## Iorwerth

He survives the wound but recovery should be slow enough that rescue has consequences.

Do not restore him instantly for a cheerful ending scene.

## Edward le Despenser

Edward is historically:

- approximately Ieuan's generation;
- already a knight;
- present at Poitiers;
- moving into full adult control/status in **1357** after his minority.

This is when the two Glamorgan trajectories finally become personally useful to one another.

## Reward package

Working fictional Despenser patronage:

- **£20 annual life fee/annuity**;
- productive Glamorgan estate office/lease or equivalent recurring income;
- Ieuan's ransom capital;
- future military pay as a knight.

The exact legal form of the estate reward can remain plain in prose. Ieuan does not need to deliver a lecture on feudal finance.

## Knighthood — spring/summer 1357

**Dubber:** Edward le Despenser.

The ceremony should be dignified but not fairy-tale extravagant.

Ieuan's reaction includes:

- pride;
- disbelief;
- awareness of cost/obligation;
- memory of people who cannot see it.

## Final image options

Strongest current direction:

After the formal accolade, strip the ending down.

Ieuan is alone or nearly alone.

He has:

- knightly spurs/status;
- good military equipment;
- income he once could not imagine;
- Dafydd's worn work knife.

The final emotional movement returns not to Poitiers but to:

- the little end;
- Mam changing straw;
- Gwenllian laughing;
- the belief that his shame was the largest problem in the world.

Do **not** let Ieuan conclude as scientific fact that wetting saved him.

A stronger final thought is uncertainty:

> perhaps the little end saved him; perhaps luck did; perhaps God did; perhaps nothing chose him at all.

What matters is that the boy who spent his childhood wishing that part of himself away lived long enough to become Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd.

# SECOND-HALF CONTINUITY RULES

1. No sudden sword mastery.
2. No automatic title of squire merely because Ieuan is a teenager.
3. Matthew de Soor's **1355** connection is historical; his Poitiers presence is not claimed.
4. Iorwerth is fictional and carries day-to-day military continuity where historical evidence should not be abused.
5. Warwick's retinue/vanguard is the institutional chain that survives personnel uncertainty.
6. Ieuan is a man-at-arms **before** Poitiers.
7. Poitiers produces reputation/ransom opportunity, not instant knighthood.
8. Guillaume remains minor/fictional; Ieuan does not capture King John or another famous person.
9. Ransom is delayed and shared, not cash-on-battlefield treasure.
10. Despenser's patronage solves the recurring-income problem of knightly status.
11. Knighthood happens in 1357, after the material/social case exists.
12. Ieuan's bed-wetting may diminish but remains episodically present through the Gascony winter; status does not cure it.
13. Dafydd's knife survives unless a future plot event deliberately makes its loss meaningful.
14. Welsh remains Ieuan's emotional first language even as he gains working English/French military vocabulary.
15. The war chapters retain the novel's horror identity: organised violence replaces pestilence as the force emptying homes.
