# Scene Cards — Chapters 1–3

These are the first **draft-ready scene cards** for the novel.

They are not finished prose. Their purpose is to make each chapter writable without improvising continuity, history or character motivation mid-draft.

---

# CHAPTER 1 — THE LITTLE END

**Date:** Thursday **2 April 1349**  
**Ieuan:** 14  
**Location:** Hendre Gelli and nearby working ground

## Chapter job

Make the reader care about the ordinary household **before** plague enters it.

The chapter must feel like a family/work chapter with one faint shadow at the edge — not a plague prologue announcing doom every paragraph.

---

## SCENE 1 — BEFORE ANYONE WAKES

**Time:** pre-dawn / earliest household waking  
**Location:** the little end

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Angharad enters late in scene
- Gwenllian may be heard before seen

### Practical action

Ieuan wakes and immediately checks bedding.

It is wet.

He tries to:

- lift the worst straw quietly;
- separate wet cloth from anything salvageable;
- keep smell/noise from carrying into the warmer main space;
- get outside before Gwenllian notices.

### Ieuan wants

**Nobody to know.**

More specifically: Dad must not wake before he fixes it.

### What changes

Angharad catches him.

Not dramatically. She simply sees what he is doing and begins helping because the household needs to start moving.

### Character texture

Angharad should not give modern reassurance.

Her care is practical:

- move that;
- not there;
- put the cloth near the heat later;
- fetch clean straw;
- stop making more noise than necessary.

That practical discretion is love.

### Gwenllian beat

Gwenllian knows anyway.

One small teasing comment is acceptable **only because this is ordinary sibling life before catastrophe**.

Ieuan snaps harder than necessary.

### Sensory palette

- stale/cold air in the little end;
- wet straw against fingers;
- colder floor/draft than main hearth space;
- first faint smoke/fire smell when Angharad begins the day;
- cattle/animals audible outside.

### Do not include yet

- plague exposition;
- narrator explanation of enuresis;
- claims that wet straw kills fleas;
- saintly patient parents;
- cruelty from Dafydd.

### Scene exit

Dafydd calls for Ieuan because cattle/work will not wait for embarrassment.

---

## SCENE 2 — BREAKFAST IS WORK

**Time:** early morning  
**Location:** main domestic space / yard

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Dafydd
- Angharad
- Gwenllian

### Practical action

Family prepares for the day's work.

Food should be simple and not over-described as a feast:

- grain/oat/barley preparation or bread if already baked;
- dairy/ale/water as appropriate;
- leftovers/stores rather than modern breakfast categories.

### Ieuan wants

Dafydd **not to mention the bedding**.

### Dafydd beat

He notices the replacement straw/cloth situation anyway.

His reaction:

- brief frustration about work/waste/drying;
- perhaps a tired "again?";
- then immediate shift to the day's actual jobs.

This establishes that Ieuan's belief that Dad is deeply ashamed of him is larger than Dafydd's actual response.

### Gwenllian beat

She wants something Ieuan considers childish/annoying — perhaps permission to come farther with the cattle than she is allowed.

This gives her a desire unrelated to future death.

### Angharad beat

She remembers three practical things at once and expects everybody else to remember them too.

### Scene exit

Dafydd gives Ieuan his first job involving the cattle.

---

## SCENE 3 — WATCH THE BEAST

**Time:** morning  
**Location:** slope/pasture above Hendre Gelli

### Characters

- Ieuan
- Dafydd
- cattle
- possibly Gwenllian at the lower edge briefly, then sent back

### Practical problem

One animal refuses/is difficult to move through a gate, bank or narrow track.

Ieuan tries to force the problem too quickly.

### Dafydd's lesson

Not a grand speech.

Something like the working principle:

**Watch the beast before you move it.**

Dafydd makes Ieuan stop and notice:

- where the animal is looking;
- where its weight is;
- what it is frightened of;
- what route it thinks is open.

Ieuan then solves the movement with less force.

### Father's knife

Dafydd uses his ordinary knife during the work:

- cutting cord;
- trimming something;
- freeing tangled material;
- another mundane job.

No close-up destiny object language.

### Ieuan wants

Dafydd's approval without asking for it.

### What changes

Dafydd trusts him with a slightly more responsible task for the return/afternoon.

That matters more to Ieuan than praise.

### Historical texture

Use landscape as worked space:

- river below;
- higher rough pasture;
- wooded slopes in places;
- familiar track/ridge references;
- no industrial Rhondda imagery.

### Scene exit

They see another local person approaching/returning with news from outside the immediate holdings.

---

## SCENE 4 — SOMETHING PEOPLE ARE SAYING

**Time:** late morning / midday  
**Location:** working track / edge of holding

### Characters

- Dafydd
- Ieuan
- **Madog ap Rhys** works well here as familiar neighbour
- perhaps Nest mentioned, not necessarily present

### Information

Madog has heard fragmented reports through a traveller/market/route connection.

Keep details uncertain:

- deaths in households farther away;
- too many burials;
- servants leaving;
- contradictory stories about where it began.

Nobody says "the Black Death."

### Adult reactions

Madog is sceptical of exaggeration.

Dafydd does not dismiss everything, but practical obligations remain immediate.

Ieuan listens because adults are talking, not because he understands significance.

### Ieuan's actual concern

He is still thinking about whether Gwenllian might tell somebody about the wet bedding.

This contrast is central to Chapter 1.

### What reader learns

The outside world exists and is moving toward them.

### Scene exit

Madog leaves; Dafydd returns immediately to work.

No ominous staring into the distance.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE KNIFE IN IEUAN'S HAND

**Time:** afternoon  
**Location:** holding / small repair job

### Characters

- Ieuan
- perhaps Gwenllian nearby
- Dafydd may be working separately

### Practical action

Ieuan completes a job using Dafydd's knife temporarily.

Important:

Dafydd hands it over casually and expects it back.

This creates physical familiarity with the object before inheritance gives it meaning.

### Gwenllian character beat

Give her one competent thing.

Possible options:

- remembers where something is stored when Ieuan does not;
- handles poultry better;
- finds dropped cord/tool;
- knows which pot Angharad wanted;
- notices weather changing first.

She must not exist only as a mouthy little sister.

### Ieuan/Gwenllian beat

They bicker, then cooperate without apology.

### Scene exit

The day's light begins going and the family regathers.

---

## SCENE 6 — ORDINARY BELL

**Time:** evening  
**Location:** Hendre Gelli

### Characters

Whole family

### Practical texture

- food/fire;
- drying damp things;
- tired bodies;
- checking animals;
- preparation for sleep.

Ieuan's bedding situation remains mildly embarrassing but no longer the centre of everybody's attention.

### Bell

A distant church bell carries through the valley.

Ieuan hears it.

He may identify its ordinary purpose incorrectly or simply fail to care.

### Chapter emotional endpoint

At dawn Ieuan believed wetting the bed was catastrophe.

By evening nothing has corrected him yet.

### Final beat

**The bell rings. Ieuan keeps doing whatever small job is in his hands.**

End.

# CHAPTER 2 — EASTER

**Dates:** Sunday **5 April** to Sunday **12 April 1349**  
**Locations:** Hendre Gelli / Ystradyfodwg church world / route with Penrhys connections

## Chapter job

Give the reader a complete memory of the family/community functioning together.

This chapter must contain genuine pleasure.

If every scene is ominous, later grief will have nothing to destroy.

---

## SCENE 1 — PALM SUNDAY

**Date:** Sunday 5 April

### Characters

- family
- Meurig ap Cynan
- Madog/Nest/Elen
- Gruffudd ap Ieuan
- other unnamed parish people sparingly

### Ieuan wants

To move through the social space without Gruffudd discovering anything embarrassing.

### Social texture

This is where we see:

- boys measuring each other;
- Elen unimpressed by performance;
- Gwenllian talking when Ieuan wishes she would stop;
- adults exchanging work/news/favours.

### Meurig

Make him ordinary and human:

- remembers somebody's family issue;
- corrects a practical church matter;
- is tired/annoyed/amused at something small.

He must exist as a person before crisis turns him into "the priest."

### Rumour level

Still incomplete.

A person has heard of sickness, but nobody present has seen it locally.

### Scene exit

Ieuan and Gruffudd have a tiny adolescent conflict that feels important to Ieuan.

---

## SCENE 2 — HOLY WEEK AT HOME

**Dates:** 6–9 April, compressed scene/time passage

### Purpose

Show work continuing around religious observance.

### Texture

- animals do not care that Holy Week is approaching;
- stored food choices/faster meals/fasting expectations affect routine;
- Angharad organises household preparation;
- Dafydd has practical work that cannot be postponed.

### Wetting continuity

Ieuan has at least one **dry night** during the week.

He notices far more than anybody else does.

### Ieuan belief

He begins privately hoping prayer/effort has fixed the problem.

Do not have him announce this.

---

## SCENE 3 — GOOD FRIDAY

**Date:** Friday 10 April

### Purpose

Make faith emotionally real before plague tests it.

### Ieuan

He participates because this is his reality, not exotic ritual.

He may pray specifically about the wetting because it genuinely matters to him.

### Rumour beat

A traveller or local person relays something much more alarming:

- a place where multiple people died in one household;
- burials too frequent;
- a priest/servant/market connection affected.

Keep place names only where historically safe. It is enough that the information comes from the south/east / wider Glamorgan route world.

### Adult discussion

Possible explanations include:

- divine judgement;
- corrupted air;
- providence;
- ordinary disease exaggerated by frightened people.

Nobody knows.

### Scene exit

Angharad tells the children to stop listening to adult talk and do something useful.

---

## SCENE 4 — EASTER MORNING

**Date:** Sunday 12 April

### Purpose

Create the family memory the reader will later mourn.

### Ieuan's private win

He wakes dry again.

For one morning he believes something has changed.

### Family material

Keep celebration proportionate to status.

The warmth comes from:

- better/shared food than a fasting day;
- teasing;
- clean(er) clothing;
- tiredness;
- church/community presence;
- people being alive.

### Gwenllian

Give her a ridiculous small concern that Ieuan mocks.

It should become painful in retrospect without being written as foreshadowing.

### Dafydd

Allow one moment of unmistakable pride in Ieuan's work that Ieuan almost misses because Dafydd expresses it indirectly.

### Angharad

Let her laugh.

She needs at least one strong moment where she is not organising illness, bedding or food.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE TRAVELLER

**Date:** Easter afternoon/evening

### Location

Track/church departure / route connected toward Penrhys or wider travel

### Figure

Fictional traveller/packman/servant.

### Important rule

He is not a horror-movie prophet.

He is tired, frightened and trying to continue his journey.

### Information

He has personally seen enough death that scepticism irritates him.

He does not understand cause either.

### Final line/beat

In response to somebody saying those deaths are far away, he gives a simple warning:

**"It is coming this way."**

End Chapter 2.

---

# CHAPTER 3 — THE FIRST BELL

**Dates:** roughly Friday **15 May** to Friday **22 May 1349**  
**Locations:** Gelli/Ystradyfodwg holdings and parish world

## Chapter job

Turn the threat from story into **someone Ieuan knows**.

This chapter should feel different from Chapters 4–6: it is still community-scale, not yet intimate-family collapse.

---

## SCENE 1 — A MONTH LATER

**Date:** around 15 May

### Opening method

Do not begin with exposition saying "a month passed."

Show seasonal/work difference:

- more growth;
- longer days;
- cattle work shifted;
- hay preparation becoming relevant;
- different household stores;
- warmer daylight but damp/cold still possible at night.

### Ieuan's state

The dry-night hope did not permanently fix the wetting.

At least one accident has happened since Easter.

Life continued anyway.

### Local wrongness

A familiar holding has:

- no normal movement;
- unattended animal;
- shut/open door at the wrong time;
- missing smoke.

People now change routes/behaviour around sickness.

### Scene exit

Ieuan learns **Madog is ill**.

---

## SCENE 2 — NEST'S HOUSE

**Date:** 18–19 May

### Characters

- Ieuan may accompany Dafydd/Angharad for practical help but should not crowd the sickroom unnecessarily
- Madog
- Nest
- Elen
- Meurig may arrive/leave

### Purpose

First close view of the disease.

### Physical observation

Through Ieuan:

- Madog hot/cold/weak;
- painful swelling;
- guarded movement;
- altered voice/attention;
- smell/sweat/room conditions.

No modern clinical labels.

### Nest

Her defining confidence is cracking.

She alternates between:

- practical orders;
- irritation at help;
- fear.

### Ieuan wants

To leave.

He is ashamed of wanting to leave.

### Dafydd beat

Dafydd helps with an ordinary task rather than pretending confidence can cure Madog.

### Scene exit

Meurig asks people to pray / prepares for death seriously enough that Ieuan understands adults expect Madog may die.

---

## SCENE 3 — ROGATION DAYS

**Date:** 18–20 May, integrated/overlapping as needed

### Purpose

Show the Christian calendar continuing under pressure.

Do not invent a giant local procession.

Possible texture:

- prayers for land/crops/community;
- people absent who would usually be present;
- conversations cut short;
- households reluctant to mix.

### Ieuan's psychological shift

He has started checking himself for lumps after hearing/seeing Madog's.

He does this secretly.

This is the first seed of the obsessive symptom-checking that dominates Chapter 7.

---

## SCENE 4 — ASCENSION

**Date:** Thursday **21 May**

### Event

Madog dies.

### Handling death

Ieuan should not witness every physical second if that duplicates later family deaths.

Stronger option:

He learns Madog is dead when ordinary work around Madog's holding has stopped and the bell/word comes.

### Gwenllian question

This is a good place for her question about the dead knowing they are dead.

Angharad answers inside Christian belief rather than philosophical uncertainty designed for modern readers.

### Ieuan

Madog's death makes the disease **local**.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE BURIAL

**Date:** Friday **22 May**

### Purpose

Show strain without civilisation instantly collapsing.

### Burial texture

- Christian burial remains meaningful;
- fewer people attend/help;
- distance between mourners may be instinctive/fearful;
- Meurig is tired;
- work normally shared by community has fewer hands.

### Ieuan wants

To stay close to Dafydd without admitting it.

He notices Dad as physical protection: bigger, capable, alive.

### Key continuity point

Dafydd may already have been exposed to the same local plague ecology before/during this week. Do not have Ieuan witness a specific flea and narratively mark it as THE infection.

---

## SCENE 6 — NOTHING

**Date:** evening Friday 22 May or early Saturday 23 May

### Location

Hendre Gelli

### Purpose

End with the smallest possible family threat.

Dafydd is tired.

He rubs beneath his arm / shifts because something hurts.

Ieuan notices.

Dafydd says it is nothing.

### Important

Do not reveal a giant bubo immediately if Chapter 4 needs escalation.

The horror is recognition before proof.

### Chapter end

Ieuan watches where Dafydd's hand rested.

End.

# DRAFTING CHECKLIST — CHAPTERS 1–3

Before prose is accepted, confirm:

- [ ] Chapter 1 feels primarily like ordinary life, not plague setup.
- [ ] Gwenllian has competence/personality beyond teasing Ieuan.
- [ ] Dafydd is loving/imperfect, not cruel or saintly.
- [ ] Angharad's care is shown through action.
- [ ] The wetting is intimate but not sensationalised.
- [ ] Dafydd's knife appears as a normal tool.
- [ ] "Watch the beast" is introduced without obvious future-hero foreshadowing.
- [ ] Medieval Rhondda appears as worked pastoral landscape, not fantasy wilderness.
- [ ] Easter is warm enough that the reader will miss it later.
- [ ] Religion is lived reality, not exposition.
- [ ] Nobody says "Black Death" as a normal contemporary term.
- [ ] Chapter 3 observes plague physically without germ-theory narration.
- [ ] Madog's burial remains organised/Christian under strain.
- [ ] Dafydd's first pain lands only at the end of Chapter 3.
- [ ] No scene gives Ieuan knowledge he could not possess.
