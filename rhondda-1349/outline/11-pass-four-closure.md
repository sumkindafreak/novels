# Final character continuity locks

## Ieuan

Across eight years he remains recognisable through:

- practical/short speech;
- becoming still when frightened;
- bad lies with too much detail;
- automatic attention to food, animals, waste and weather;
- class sensitivity;
- shame around nocturnal wetting;
- light sleeping in shared quarters;
- distrust of sudden fever/coughing after plague;
- tendency to return to work when emotion is overwhelming.

## Wetting

The novel never:

- diagnoses it;
- cures it for symbolism;
- claims urine protects against plague;
- treats it as moral weakness.

It changes meaning over the story:

**shame → altered exposure → solitary bodily continuity → renewed social danger → private fact that status cannot define.**

## Dafydd's knife

The knife remains:

- ordinary;
- useful;
- maintained;
- emotionally important without becoming magical/heirloom fantasy.

It survives beside Ieuan's increasingly expensive military gear.

## Gwenllian's grave

The grave remains:

- outside consecrated ground near the upland refuge;
- a lifelong guilt/memory point;
- physically revisited before Gascony and at/near the ending.

Do not erase the moral weight through an effortless later reburial.

## Faith

Ieuan remains a medieval Christian whose relationship with God changes through grief, anger, fear and survival.

He does not become a modern atheist or a caricatured zealot.

## War

Ieuan is neither morally clean nor sadistic.

He:

- serves willingly by 1355;
- participates in destructive campaigning;
- benefits from military profit;
- is disturbed by what the army does;
- accepts Guillaume's surrender;
- carries unresolved unease into knighthood.

# Historical-fiction discipline during prose

Every drafting session should remember:

- characters know **period explanations**, not modern science;
- narrator stays close enough to Ieuan that modern diagnosis does not leak into viewpoint;
- real historical figures appear only where evidence gives them a legitimate role;
- real people receive fictional private dialogue/personality only when clearly treated as fiction;
- no fake exact Poitiers map certainty;
- no modern inherited Welsh surnames for Ieuan's family;
- no plague-doctor beaks;
- no modern food/anachronistic crops;
- no fantasy-medieval generic tavern culture where a specific Welsh/household environment is available.

# Knighting ceremony lock

The final ceremony should remain restrained.

Research supports:

- formal dubbing persisting in the fourteenth century;
- sword girding as a central knightly investiture element;
- spurs as an important mark of knightly status;
- religious preparation being available/appropriate;
- elaborate bath/vigil ceremonial existing but not being necessary for every ordinary knight bachelor.

Therefore working Chapter 18 ceremony:

1. confession/religious preparation and Mass/prayer;
2. formal spurs/sword association;
3. Edward le Despenser performs the accolade/dubbing;
4. witnesses from military/Glamorgan patronage world;
5. no invented modern investiture wording.

# Prose drafting order

Draft sequentially.

Do **not** draft favourite later scenes first.

Reason:

- later memory depends on exact words/actions established earlier;
- Dafydd's sayings must originate naturally before being recalled;
- Gwenllian's personality must exist before her grave has emotional meaning;
- Iorwerth's Poitiers importance depends on years of accumulated professional trust;
- the final Rhondda return should echo images actually written in Chapter 1.
