# Opening Movement — Motifs, Knowledge and Drafting Rules

Applies primarily to **Chapters 1–9 (April–autumn 1349)**.

The purpose is to make recurring details **change meaning** as Ieuan changes. A motif should never simply be repeated because readers remember it.

---

# 1. BELLS

## Chapter 1

Bell = ordinary structure.

Ieuan hears a bell and barely notices.

## Chapter 2

Bell = church/community rhythm.

Easter and ordinary worship still organise the week.

## Chapter 3

Bell = death entering local consciousness.

The first bell associated with somebody Ieuan knows has emotional weight.

## Chapters 4–5

Bell = uncertainty.

Ieuan cannot always know who it is for, or whether the normal pattern is being followed.

## Chapter 7

High on the mountain, a distant bell may be heard or imagined.

Ieuan no longer trusts it to mean help.

## Chapter 8

Silence can be more frightening than ringing.

### Rule

Do not ring a bell at every death. The motif loses power if mechanical.

---

# 2. DAFYDD'S KNIFE

## Chapter 1

A tool.

Dafydd uses it without ceremony.

## Chapter 4

After Dafydd dies, Ieuan takes it because a job requires a knife.

No inheritance speech.

## Chapter 6

It cuts cord, food/fuel material and practical things during flight.

## Chapter 8

It becomes Ieuan's default working object.

The reader begins attaching emotional meaning before Ieuan consciously does.

## Later novel

The knife remains physically inferior to military weapons but emotionally superior.

### Rule

Ieuan should not constantly stare at it and think about Dad. Most of the emotional force comes from **using it automatically the way Dafydd did**.

---

# 3. "WATCH THE BEAST BEFORE YOU MOVE IT"

## Chapter 1

Literal cattle lesson.

## Chapter 6

Ieuan reads terrain/weather/animal behaviour before moving Gwenllian uphill.

## Chapter 8

Helps him choose/manage one cow rather than attempting an impossible herd.

## Chapters 10–15

Becomes horse craft, then leadership/observation.

## Poitiers

The lesson may echo indirectly in reading movement/men/horses, but **do not turn it into a cheesy repeated catchphrase before every heroic act.**

### Rule

Dafydd's influence survives in habits more than quotations.

---

# 4. WET BEDDING / WATER

This is the novel's most unusual recurring image and must be handled carefully.

## Chapter 1

Pure humiliation.

Ieuan believes discovery would be catastrophic.

## Chapter 2

A dry night briefly becomes private religious hope.

## Chapter 4

An accident during Dafydd's illness becomes additional labour when Mam cannot help him.

## Chapter 6

Gwenllian notices and does not tease him. Her changed reaction communicates fear/love.

## Chapter 7

A wet night after Gwenllian's death becomes an obscene sign of continued ordinary bodily life.

It does **not** become medical evidence.

## Chapter 9

Shared servant sleeping arrangements turn the secret into a social survival problem again.

## Later novel

The wetting may become less frequent with age/stability but should remain episodic enough to preserve continuity through the Gascony winter.

### Rule

Never use the wetting for cheap comedy after Chapter 1.

Gwenllian may tease him early because siblings do; narrator/other adults should not invite the reader to laugh at the condition.

---

# 5. FOOD

Food tracks power and safety.

## Chapters 1–2

Food = household routine and shared labour.

Nobody is starving, but waste matters.

## Chapters 4–5

Food = task.

Ieuan has to remember who needs feeding while adults are sick.

## Chapter 6

Food = what he can physically carry.

## Chapter 7

Food is available but grief/fear suppress appetite.

## Chapter 8

Food = moral problem.

Taking another dead family's grain is materially necessary and emotionally theft.

## Chapter 9

Food = wage/security.

Rhys can offer something Ieuan can no longer guarantee alone.

### Rule

Track stores. Do not generate bread, meat or ale when no process/person has produced or preserved them.

---

# 6. HOME — HENDRE GELLI

## Chapter 1

Small, cramped, annoying, safe.

Ieuan resents the little end.

## Chapter 4

House becomes sickroom.

## Chapter 5

House becomes work he cannot manage.

## Chapter 8

The physical house survives while the household is gone.

That distinction matters:

> the building is not home simply because it is still standing.

## Chapter 9

Leaving it becomes harder than surviving inside it.

### Rule

Never burn Hendre Gelli merely to simplify the plot. Ieuan must confront the harder truth that **some places survive the people who made them matter.**

---

# 7. GWENLLIAN

She must not feel like a child introduced for the purpose of dying.

## Before illness

Give her:

- repetitive habits;
- opinions;
- annoying questions;
- a favourite task/food/object;
- one thing she is better at than Ieuan;
- one private joke with Angharad;
- moments where Ieuan genuinely wants her to go away.

## During escape

She is still herself on 3–4 June.

Do not make her solemnly prophetic because the reader knows she will die.

## Illness

As fever rises, personality should disappear unevenly, which is part of the horror.

## After death

Do not summon sentimental memories every chapter.

Her absence should often appear in **missing routine**:

- nobody talking while he works;
- too much food in one bowl;
- no reason to save a nicer bit;
- an empty place beside the fire;
- nobody knowing his secret who has permission to joke about it.

---

# 8. GOD / FAITH

## Chapter 1

Faith is ordinary, inherited reality.

## Chapter 2

Christian year provides safety and meaning.

## Chapter 3

Adults interpret crisis through providence, sin, prayer, bad air, inherited ideas and incomplete information.

## Chapters 4–6

Ieuan prays because that is what reality means to him, not because the plot is setting up a theological debate.

## Chapter 7

He bargains, accuses, apologises and prays again.

He does not suddenly become an atheist.

## Chapter 8

Practical survival coexists with religious guilt, especially around property and burial.

### Core question

Ieuan's deepest early theological wound is:

> Why was the shameful one spared?

The novel does not answer this.

---

# 9. WHAT IEUAN KNOWS VS WHAT THE READER KNOWS

## Plague

The reader may know:

- *Yersinia pestis*;
- flea/rodent transmission;
- bubonic/pneumonic forms;
- incubation periods.

Ieuan knows:

- fever;
- pain;
- swellings;
- some households die rapidly;
- proximity seems dangerous but unpredictably so;
- prayer/remedies often fail;
- he remains alive for reasons he cannot identify.

### Rule

Never let narrator language slip into invisible modern diagnosis if the viewpoint is close to Ieuan.

Use physical observation.

Not:

> The infected lymph node had enlarged.

But something like:

> The lump beneath his father's arm had grown hard and hot, and Dafydd would not let cloth touch it.

---

# 10. DEATH VARIETY

Four family/community deaths must not feel like the same scene repeated.

## Madog

**Function:** proof the rumour is real.

Ieuan observes mainly from outside another household.

## Dafydd

**Function:** collapse of authority/skill hierarchy.

Ieuan loses the man who always knew the next job.

## Angharad

**Function:** collapse of protection/organisation.

Ieuan becomes responsible for Gwenllian.

## Gwenllian

**Function:** collapse of purpose.

There is nobody left to survive for.

### Rule

Do not chase increasing gore. Increase **personal consequence** instead.

---

# 11. IEUAN'S SPEECH

Opening Ieuan is not eloquent.

He:

- gives short answers;
- lies with too much unnecessary detail;
- becomes quiet when frightened;
- notices work/animals/weather before abstract emotion;
- avoids naming shame directly.

### Example behavioural principle

If asked whether he is frightened, he is more likely to say:

> The cow needs bringing in.

than explain his emotional state.

Do not make every conversation cryptic. He is a teenager, not a minimalist literary oracle.

---

# 12. PHYSICAL DEVELOPMENT

At fourteen Ieuan is useful but not invulnerable.

Track:

- hunger;
- cold/damp;
- blisters/cuts;
- exhaustion;
- lifting/carrying limits;
- sleep debt;
- diarrhoea/food spoilage risk without overusing illness;
- animal injuries.

He can do more than a modern sheltered fourteen-year-old because work is normal to him, but he cannot perform the labour of several adults indefinitely.

That failure of **scale** is what makes Rhys's offer necessary.

---

# 13. OPENING MOVEMENT PACING

The plague should not start on page one.

Recommended emotional distribution:

- **Chapters 1–2:** enough ordinary life that the family becomes real;
- **Chapter 3:** dread arrives;
- **Chapters 4–6:** household collapse accelerates;
- **Chapter 7:** deliberately slow, isolated grief/terror;
- **Chapter 8:** survival mechanics widen the story again;
- **Chapter 9:** cautious return to human systems.

This alternation prevents unbroken misery from becoming emotionally flat.

---

# 14. HORROR RULE

The horror should come from:

- waiting for symptoms;
- familiar routines failing;
- people becoming physically unrecognisable through sickness;
- silence;
- bodies requiring practical decisions;
- food remaining in houses whose owners are dead;
- animals continuing ordinary needs while people die;
- uncertainty over who is alive;
- faith structures becoming overwhelmed;
- Ieuan's inability to know why he lives.

Avoid relying on:

- endless gore;
- plague-doctor masks;
- supernatural plague monsters;
- piles of corpses in every road;
- everyone instantly becoming lawless.

---

# 15. CHAPTER-END ESCALATION

## Chapter 1
Bell heard, ignored.

## Chapter 2
"It is coming this way."

## Chapter 3
Dafydd touches the pain beneath his arm.

## Chapter 4
Angharad shivers.

## Chapter 5
"When is Mam coming?" / Ieuan lies.

## Chapter 6
Nobody exists to tell Ieuan where to bury Gwenllian.

## Chapter 7
He chooses to walk downhill.

## Chapter 8
He realises summer abundance cannot feed an animal through winter.

## Chapter 9
He sleeps away from Glynrhondda by choice.

The escalation therefore moves:

**rumour → disease → father → mother → sister → self → society → future → departure.**
