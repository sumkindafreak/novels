# Rhondda 1349 — 18-Chapter Architecture

This is the first complete structural outline for the novel.

The chapter titles are working titles and may change during prose drafting.

---

# CHAPTER 1 — THE LITTLE END

**Date:** early April 1349, before Holy Week  
**Ieuan's age:** 14  
**Location:** Hendre Gelli, western Rhondda Fawr / Ystradyfodwg–Gelli orbit

## Purpose

Introduce Ieuan's ordinary world **before** it is destroyed.

## Main beats

- Ieuan wakes after another wet night in the colder service-end sleeping bay known inside the family as **the little end**.
- He tries to strip the straw before Gwenllian notices; she notices anyway.
- Angharad handles the problem briskly and discreetly.
- Dafydd is frustrated about the labour/wasted bedding but does not humiliate Ieuan.
- Ordinary cattle work gives us the boy's competence: steep ground, stubborn animals, weather judgement, Dad's knife, short practical instructions.
- We meet Gwenllian as funny, nosy and irritating rather than future tragedy.
- Ieuan's greatest current fear is somebody outside the family discovering the wetting.
- The wider world is tiny: home, Ystradyfodwg, upland grazing, Penrhys ridge, Llantrisant as distant authority.
- A fragmentary rumour arrives through somebody who has heard of deaths farther south/east — too vague to command belief.

## Emotional change

Ieuan begins convinced his private shame is the worst thing that could happen to him.

## Continuity

- Dafydd's work knife introduced casually.
- 'Watch the beast before you move it' established as Dafydd's work lesson.
- Ieuan's bad lying habit introduced.

## Evidence

- **A/B:** Rhondda pastoral setting, Welsh naming/language, house comparators, April Christian calendar.
- **C:** family dialogue, exact holding, little-end arrangement.

## End beat

Ieuan hears the church bell in the distance and assumes it is ordinary.

# CHAPTER 2 — EASTER

**Date:** 5–12 April 1349; climax Easter Sunday, **12 April**  
**Age:** 14  
**Locations:** Hendre Gelli / Ystradyfodwg church / route toward Penrhys

## Purpose

Give the family one complete religious/community chapter so the later silence has something to destroy.

## Main beats

- Holy Week rhythms: fasting/expectation/prayer rather than modern Easter imagery.
- Ieuan sees Gruffudd ap Ieuan and worries pointlessly about adolescent status.
- Elen and Gwenllian provide social texture.
- Meurig ap Cynan, the fictional priest, appears as an ordinary familiar man before he becomes part of the crisis.
- News at church is contradictory: sickness in other places, servants leaving houses, excessive burials.
- A traveller connected to the Penrhys route describes settlements where many have died.
- Adults debate whether stories are exaggerated, punishment, bad air, God's will, or simply distant troubles.
- Easter itself contains warmth: food, family teasing, tiredness after observance, small domestic happiness.
- Ieuan has a dry night and privately treats it as an answered prayer.

## Emotional change

The reader knows this is precious; the family does not.

## Historical anchor

**Easter Sunday 1349 = 12 April, Julian calendar.**

## End beat

A traveller leaving the area says something simple rather than prophetic:

**'It is coming this way.'**

---

# CHAPTER 3 — THE FIRST BELL

**Date:** roughly 15–21 May 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Locations:** Ystradyfodwg/Gelli holdings and church

## Purpose

Move the plague from rumour to local fact.

## Main beats

- Spring work continues because cattle/hay preparation cannot stop for rumours.
- A nearby holding goes quiet.
- Madog ap Rhys becomes ill.
- Symptoms are shown specifically but through medieval observation, not diagnostic vocabulary.
- Nest's confidence breaks when she sees the painful swelling/fever.
- Meurig attempts ordinary pastoral duties: confession, prayer, burial arrangements.
- Madog dies around 20–21 May.
- The funeral is still recognisably Christian and organised, but fewer people attend.
- Ieuan sees adults avoiding one another physically while not understanding flea/germ mechanisms.
- Gwenllian asks a question about whether dead people know they're dead; Angharad gives a period-faith answer rather than a modern one.

## Emotional change

Ieuan's fear hierarchy changes for the first time: discovery of the wetting stops being number one.

## End beat

Dafydd rubs under his arm and tells Ieuan it is nothing.

---

# CHAPTER 4 — UNDER THE ARM

**Date:** 23–27 May 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Location:** Hendre Gelli

## Purpose

Destroy the father/son structure.

## Main beats

- Dafydd tries to work through fever.
- Ieuan initially interprets weakness as something Dad can simply overcome.
- The swelling becomes undeniable.
- Angharad attempts available household/religious remedies and seeks spiritual help.
- Ieuan has to perform Dafydd's jobs with no confidence that he is doing them correctly.
- Dafydd becomes delirious/intermittently lucid.
- Avoid sentimental final speech.
- A short lucid moment can involve work: Dafydd correcting how Ieuan ties/handles something, then no longer having the strength.
- Ieuan wets the little-end bedding during the crisis and hides it himself because Angharad is caring for Dafydd.
- Dafydd dies **27 May**.
- His body cannot receive the normal community attention the family expected in ordinary times.
- Ieuan eventually takes Dafydd's ordinary knife because he needs it for work.

## Emotional change

Ieuan becomes the oldest functioning male in the household before emotionally understanding what that means.

## End beat

Angharad shivers while insisting she is only tired.

---

# CHAPTER 5 — WHITSUN

**Date:** 29 May–2 June 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Location:** Hendre Gelli / damaged parish world

## Purpose

Remove the final adult protection.

## Main beats

- Angharad develops fever around 29–30 May.
- **Whitsunday/Pentecost is 31 May**, but the family cannot participate normally.
- Ieuan attempts to care for Mam and Gwenllian while also watering animals, making food and managing the fire.
- Nobody arrives to tell him what should happen next.
- Meurig's whereabouts become uncertain; church systems are visibly failing through mortality/exhaustion.
- Angharad remains recognisably herself between fever episodes.
- She does not know she is giving a final speech.
- Her most useful last instructions are practical: food, where something is stored, Gwenllian, what route to take if they must leave.
- Angharad dies **2 June**.
- Ieuan briefly tries to keep the household operating as though her death is simply another job to manage.
- He realises he cannot leave Gwenllian beside two bodies while the mortality worsens.

## Emotional change

Responsibility replaces grief because grief has no room yet.

## End beat

Ieuan tells Gwenllian they are going up to the cattle shelter.

She asks when Mam is coming.

He lies badly.

---

# CHAPTER 6 — UP THE MOUNTAIN

**Date:** 2–7 June 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Location:** route to Mynydd Gelli / upland hafod refuge

## Purpose

Give Ieuan one brief belief that he has successfully saved somebody.

## Main beats

- Ieuan and Gwenllian climb with only what he can carry/manage.
- His existing route knowledge is what saves effort; no discovery of a convenient cabin.
- He knows water, leaks, firewood, cattle enclosure, shelter weaknesses.
- Gwenllian initially seems healthy.
- They have small, almost peaceful moments away from the lower settlement.
- Ieuan begins to believe separation worked.
- Gwenllian notices one of Ieuan's wet nights and, unusually, doesn't tease him.
- **5 June:** she becomes feverish.
- Ieuan checks her because he has now seen the pattern twice.
- Finds the swelling.
- He tries every action that makes emotional sense to him even though none can cure plague.
- Gwenllian dies **7 June**.

## Emotional change

Ieuan loses the last person he was surviving *for*.

## End beat

There is nobody to tell him where to bury her.

---

# CHAPTER 7 — THE WAITING

**Date:** 7–14 June 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Location:** upland refuge

## Purpose

Pure psychological survival horror.

## Main beats

- Ieuan buries Gwenllian near the refuge because carrying her to consecrated ground is beyond him and the valley is dangerous.
- The shallow/imperfect grave produces lifelong guilt.
- He prays, bargains, apologises and becomes angry with God without becoming a modern atheist.
- For several days he assumes plague symptoms will begin next.
- Constantly checks groin/armpits/neck.
- Eats almost nothing despite available food.
- Every change in temperature feels like fever.
- He wakes after another wet night.
- Initially disgusted, then realises he has slept deeply enough for an ordinary accident and is still otherwise well.
- The wetting becomes not a cure but an obscene little sign that ordinary body function continues.
- Hunger eventually defeats his willingness to wait for death.

## Emotional change

He does not decide to live heroically.

He becomes too hungry to keep waiting to die.

## End beat

He walks downhill toward the houses.

---

# CHAPTER 8 — EMPTY HOUSES

**Date:** mid-to-late June 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Locations:** Hendre Gelli / neighbouring holdings / Ystradyfodwg orbit

## Purpose

Show practical post-plague survival and moral collapse/ambiguity.

## Main beats

- Ieuan returns to Hendre Gelli but cannot comfortably sleep in the main family space.
- Takes supplies from his own home.
- Enters Madog's abandoned holding and faces the moral reality of scavenging from somebody he knew.
- Finds useful grain/pulses/tools rather than fantasy treasure.
- Livestock are loose or unmanaged.
- One manageable cow becomes central because milk is more useful than trying to command an entire abandoned herd.
- Ieuan discovers the parish world fractured: priest missing, doors open/closed unpredictably, burial pressure evident.
- Encounters **Goronwy ap Tudur**, another survivor/scavenger.
- Goronwy is neither rescuer nor villain; each sees the other as competition.
- A conflict over property/food makes Ieuan understand that survival after plague includes human danger.

## Emotional change

He stops thinking the dead world belongs automatically to survivors.

## End beat

Ieuan realises hay must be made soon or the cow/remaining stock will not survive winter.

For the first time he thinks farther ahead than tomorrow.

---

# CHAPTER 9 — ONLY TO WORK

**Date:** late June–autumn 1349  
**Age:** 14  
**Locations:** Gelli/Ystradyfodwg → Llantrisant orbit

## Purpose

End the plague-survival movement and begin social reintegration.

## Main beats

- Ieuan tries to preserve Hendre Gelli alone and fails through scale rather than stupidity.
- **Rhys ap Llywelyn** arrives with estate/stock work connected to recovering animals and holdings.
- Rhys sees a working boy, not a miracle survivor.
- Ieuan initially refuses help/employment because leaving feels like abandoning his family.
- Rhys offers temporary food/work, not adoption.
- Ieuan discovers other survivors, officials and servants are rebuilding systems he thought had ended.
- He reluctantly joins stock recovery/movement work.
- First trips toward Llantrisant expand his world dramatically.
- Bed-wetting becomes difficult in shared servant arrangements; secrecy continues.
- Ieuan tells himself repeatedly that he is not leaving home permanently.

## Emotional change

He moves from solitary survival to dependence on strangers — which frightens him more than hunger.

## End beat

He sleeps one night away from Glynrhondda by choice.

---

# CHAPTER 10 — THE HORSE BOY

**Date:** 1350–1352  
**Age:** 15–17  
**Locations:** Llantrisant / Glamorgan estate routes / upper household service

## Purpose

Compress the years in which Ieuan turns raw competence into professional service.

## Main beats

- Rhys teaches horse handling as a distinct skill from cattle.
- Ieuan becomes a stable/pack servant and route worker.
- Learns better horses, tack, lameness, loading, messaging, household hierarchy.
- His Welsh remains dominant but he acquires working English/French vocabulary through service.
- First encounter with class rules: who eats where, who speaks, who owns what.
- Gruffudd or another old local face may be glimpsed/survive, reminding us the plague did not erase everyone.
- Sir **Matthew de Soor** enters as a real Glamorgan administrative/knightly figure in Ieuan's wider work network.
- Ieuan is useful enough to be retained upward.
- He begins cleaning/maintaining military equipment and riding better horses.

## Emotional change

Ieuan discovers competence can create status — but only inside boundaries set by other men.

## End beat

He is told to travel with Matthew's people rather than return to ordinary stable work.

---

# CHAPTER 11 — A MAN'S PLACE

**Date:** 1352–1354  
**Age:** 17–19  
**Locations:** Glamorgan / household travel / military-affinity environment

## Purpose

Solve the middle rung on-page.

## Main beats

- Ieuan progresses from groom/valet into trusted mounted household service.
- Learns to help arm/disarm fighting men, clean mail, maintain weapons and understand cost of equipment.
- Begins structured weapons practice because armed household usefulness now justifies it.
- Introduce **Iorwerth ap Morgan**, fictional uchelwr-born esquire.
- Iorwerth is socially Welsh but worlds above Ieuan in status.
- Ieuan resents being treated as a servant even when he technically is one.
- A small dangerous duty proves obedience matters more than bravery.
- Ieuan is given/allowed to wear better protective equipment.
- His continuing wetting becomes less frequent perhaps, but never vanishes by plot convenience.
- Matthew's military connections deepen as 1355 approaches.

## Emotional change

Ieuan begins wanting entry into the armed world, not merely food/security.

That ambition frightens him because it resembles pride.

## End beat

Orders arrive for Gascony preparations.

---

# CHAPTER 12 — THE SEA

**Date:** spring–September 1355  
**Age:** 20  
**Locations:** Wales mobilisation → Southampton/retinue shipping → Gascony/Bordeaux

## Purpose

Take Ieuan farther from the Rhondda than he has ever imagined.

## Historical anchors

- 26 April 1355: Matthew de Soor and historical Ieuan ap Ruyn documented gathering supplies in Wales for Warwick's retinue.
- horse transport preparations include shipboard hurdles/gangplanks.

## Main beats

- Ieuan helps gather/move supplies/horses through a real Welsh mobilisation environment.
- The coincidence of another Welsh Ieuan can be left out of prose unless useful; it belongs primarily to our research proof.
- Shipping horses is terrifying practical labour.
- Sea crossing is cramped, wet, noisy and dominated by animal danger rather than romantic horizon-gazing.
- Ieuan is violently aware that there is now an ocean between him and Gwenllian's grave.
- Reaches Gascony and sees a military world larger than anything in Wales.
- Edward le Despenser may be seen at distance/briefly as a real knight in the Prince's army — not yet a personal relationship.

## Emotional change

Ieuan understands that he can no longer pretend he is 'only working for a while'.

He has joined war.

## End beat

The army rides out from Bordeaux.

---

# CHAPTER 13 — FIRE

**Date:** October–mid November 1355  
**Age:** 20  
**Locations:** Grande chevauchée toward Narbonne

## Purpose

Break Ieuan's romantic ideas about military advancement.

## Main beats

- Fast marching and logistics dominate: fodder, water, lame horses, loads, pace.
- Ieuan excels because these are recognisable problems at huge scale.
- Settlements/resources are burned and plundered deliberately.
- Ieuan sees families fleeing and stores destroyed.
- The visual echo with plague-abandoned Glynrhondda is explicit but not melodramatically explained.
- He participates because refusal would mean breaking with the household/army.
- He takes food/animals from civilians and knows exactly what that means for their winter.
- Iorwerth notices Ieuan's reaction but does not give a moral lecture.
- Ieuan realises pestilence killed without hatred; armies can destroy on purpose.

## Emotional change

His ambition survives, but innocence about what armed service means does not.

## End beat

The army turns back toward Gascony with French pressure increasing.

---

# CHAPTER 14 — THE RETURN

**Date:** late November–2 December 1355  
**Age:** 20  
**Locations:** return from the 1355 chevauchée to La Réole

## Purpose

Earn the promotion.

## Main beats

- Shorter days, exhausted mounts, more baggage, fewer rests.
- A fictional but historically plausible alarm/skirmish threatens a Warwick-affinity remount/baggage element.
- An animal/route blockage starts becoming dangerous.
- Ieuan identifies the order animals need moving and gets the route clearing.
- Enemy pressure hits before evacuation is complete.
- Ieuan is ordered to hold.
- First confirmed killing happens without duel glamour.
- He fights because somebody is coming through him toward men/animals he is responsible for.
- He does not pursue retreating opponents.
- Goes straight back to the horses/equipment.
- Iorwerth/Matthew-affinity witnesses understand this as discipline.
- Army returns around 2 December.

## Emotional change

Ieuan discovers he can kill without freezing — and is disturbed that this now counts as professional competence.

## End beat

In winter quarters, he is summoned to discuss his status.

---

# CHAPTER 15 — WINTER ARMS

**Date:** December 1355–June 1356  
**Age:** 20–21  
**Locations:** Gascony winter quarters/frontier operations

## Purpose

Make Ieuan genuinely man-at-arms before Poitiers.

## Main beats

- Formal/functioning regrading to esquire/man-at-arms.
- Better horse; better armour; changed pay; lower servants now answer to him in some contexts.
- He must learn that rank produces obligations, not freedom from work.
- Smaller frontier/garrison operations give repeated armed experience.
- Iorwerth continues forcing Ieuan to care about horses/equipment personally.
- **Bed-wetting scene:** after dry weeks/months Ieuan wets his bedding among men whose respect matters.
- Iorwerth finds him dealing with it.
- Instead of ridicule: practical instruction — replace/dry it before it freezes.
- Ieuan's deepest private fear fails to destroy him.
- That does not magically remove the shame, but changes its power.

## Emotional change

Ieuan finally begins believing he belongs among these men — while knowing belonging can be revoked.

## End beat

The army is ordered north again.

---

# CHAPTER 16 — THE ROAD NORTH

**Date:** 6 July–18 September 1356  
**Age:** 21  
**Route:** Bordeaux → La Réole → Bergerac → Vienne/Manot → Romorantin → Loire/Tours theatre → Chauvigny → Nouaillé-Maupertuis

## Purpose

Build pressure toward the action climax.

## Main beats

- This time Ieuan rides out as an actual man-at-arms.
- Someone else may tend his principal horse, providing a subtle visual of how far he has moved socially.
- He still checks the animal himself.
- Campaign progresses north through Périgord/Limousin.
- Crossing the Vienne marks deeper hostile territory.
- Romorantin and manoeuvre phase increase tension.
- Contradictory rumours: joining another English army, French king nearby, retreat, battle, crossing blocked.
- Ieuan cannot see senior strategy and shouldn't understand it with historian precision.
- Forced movement south as French forces close the route.
- Contact around Chauvigny convinces everyone a battle is near.

## 18 September night

- Negotiations/rumours.
- Men prepare armour/weapons.
- Ieuan attends his horse despite expectation of dismounted combat.
- Confession/prayer if available.
- Ieuan's plague memory returns: lying awake waiting to see whether death has chosen him.
- Dafydd's knife remains with him.

## Emotional change

Fear no longer means helplessness.

## End beat

Morning, **19 September 1356**.

---

# CHAPTER 17 — MAUPERTUIS

**Date:** 19 September 1356  
**Age:** 21  
**Location:** Nouaillé-Maupertuis / Poitiers battlefield

## Purpose

Action climax of the novel.

## Rules

- No superhero kill count.
- No false metre-perfect battlefield map.
- Ieuan's immediate experience is noise, formation, ground and orders — not omniscient military history.

## Main beats

- Warwick's vanguard prepares/receives the opening assaults.
- Archery and difficult ground disrupt mounted French attacks.
- Fighting becomes close and exhausting.
- Ieuan performs like a trained man-at-arms, not a surprise prodigy.
- Iorwerth is badly wounded.
- Ieuan cannot simply abandon his assigned place; he acts when opportunity/order permits.
- He and another man recover/protect Iorwerth.
- **Sir Guillaume de la Motte**, fictional minor French knight, presses the group.
- Ieuan fights him in ugly close combat.
- Guillaume yields once Ieuan gains the advantage.
- Ieuan accepts surrender and protects the prisoner rather than killing him for kit.
- Ieuan gets Guillaume and wounded Iorwerth out/into custody.
- Wider French collapse becomes apparent only gradually.
- King John is captured elsewhere; Ieuan is nowhere near that famous moment.

## Emotional change

Ieuan behaves in a way others recognise as knightly before he carries the title.

## End beat

Someone asks Guillaume who took him.

Guillaume identifies **Ieuan ap Dafydd**.

For once Ieuan's name matters in a world larger than the Rhondda.

# CHAPTER 18 — SIR IEUAN

**Date:** September 1356–spring/summer 1357  
**Age:** 21–22  
**Locations:** return to Bordeaux → England/Wales → Glamorgan/Rhondda

## Purpose

Resolve money, patronage and identity without making knighthood a fairy-tale freeze-frame.

## Part I — aftermath

- Ieuan helps care for/recover Iorwerth.
- Guillaume's identity and ransom are negotiated.
- Working ransom: **100 marks**.
- Ieuan learns he does not personally keep every penny; retinue claims/costs exist.
- Guillaume is shown as a man with family/property obligations rather than a money bag.
- Ieuan's effective eventual benefit is around **40–50 marks**.
- Victory feels materially profitable and morally complicated.

## Part II — 1357

- Edward le Despenser returns after his seven-year minority and does homage/assumes effective control of his inheritance.
- Ieuan's service reaches him through the Warwick/Glamorgan network and Iorwerth's testimony.
- Edward is not discovering a random hero; he is recognising a seven-year service record.
- Working reward package:
  - £20 annual life fee/annuity;
  - productive Glamorgan estate role/lease;
  - ransom capital already in Ieuan's hands/claim;
  - future knight wages/service.

## The accolade

- Spring/summer 1357.
- Edward le Despenser, already a knight, performs the dubbing.
- Ceremony should be period-researched before prose; avoid modern investiture clichés.
- Ieuan kneels as Ieuan ap Dafydd.
- He rises/stands afterward as a knight, but the prose should resist treating the title as magical transformation.

## Final return/image

Ieuan returns into the Rhondda landscape.

He may pass Ystradyfodwg, Penrhys ridge or the route toward Hendre Gelli.

Do not simply hand him his childhood home as a sentimental reward.

Possible final visual:

- good horse beneath him;
- knight's sword;
- Dafydd's battered work knife still kept;
- sees the high ground where Gwenllian lies;
- remembers hearth smoke disappearing in 1349.

The conclusion is not:

> plague made him great.

It is:

> the boy who survived is still present inside the knight.

## Final story state

**Sir Ieuan ap Dafydd, approximately 22, 1357.**

# Structural balance

## Chapters 1–9 — plague / survival

The book spends **half its chapters** inside the world that defines Ieuan.

## Chapters 10–15 — service / transformation

Six chapters earn the social and military transition instead of skipping it in montage.

## Chapters 16–18 — war climax / consequence

Three chapters give Poitiers enough scale while keeping it from replacing the novel's true emotional centre.

---

# Core recurring threads across all 18 chapters

## Dafydd's knife

Ordinary tool → inherited survival object → campaign possession → quiet link beneath knightly equipment.

## Bed-wetting

Shame → altered sleeping arrangement → survival ambiguity → solitary bodily reminder → service secrecy → rank anxiety → outsider acceptance → never a magical cure.

## Gwenllian's grave

Immediate guilt → recurring private memory → influences Ieuan's relationship with burial/dead soldiers → possible final return.

## Horses/cattle

Childhood work → employability → professional identity → military value → promotion → battlefield discipline.

## Faith

Ordinary inherited belief → plague questions → burial guilt → survival anger → war morality → pre-battle fear → knighthood ritual.

## Empty houses

Plague creates them accidentally.  
The chevauchée creates them deliberately.

That echo is one of the novel's central moral structures.
