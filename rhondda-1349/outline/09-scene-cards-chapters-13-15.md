# Scene Cards — Chapters 13–15

These chapters are the **moral and social forge** of the second half.

Ieuan enters Chapter 13 wanting advancement.

He leaves Chapter 15 with the advancement he wanted — and a clearer understanding of what it costs.

The sequence must preserve three truths:

1. the 1355 chevauchée is primarily speed, logistics and destruction rather than endless battle;
2. Ieuan's first killing is ugly and small-scale;
3. his promotion to man-at-arms is earned through years of work plus campaign reliability, not one heroic moment.

Historical/detail authority: Pass Three bridge files `07-chevauchee-1355-ieuan-trial.md` and `08-winter-promotion-1355-56.md`.

---

# CHAPTER 13 — FIRE

**Dates:** **5 October–8 November 1355**  
**Ieuan:** 20  
**Locations:** Bordeaux → southern French campaign route → Carcassonne/Narbonne corridor

## Chapter job

Destroy Ieuan's remaining idea that becoming an armed man means simply defending comrades against enemy soldiers.

The army itself becomes frightening.

---

## SCENE 1 — 5 OCTOBER: MOVING OUT

### Historical anchor

The grande chevauchée leaves Bordeaux.

### Ieuan's work

He starts the campaign doing what he knows:

- horses;
- tack;
- loads;
- keeping his assigned animal fit;
- watching remounts/pack animals;
- following column order.

### Ieuan wants

To prove he deserves to be included among armed household servants.

### Emotional tone

Excitement exists.

Do not pretend he begins the raid already morally horrified.

At twenty, after six years of climbing toward this world, he is proud to be there.

### End beat

The column crosses into a landscape where the orders stop sounding like ordinary travel orders.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE FIRST EMPTYING

**Early campaign**

### Purpose

Create the moral connection to 1349.

### Action

Ieuan witnesses/participates in the stripping of an undefended rural holding or small settlement.

Orders involve things he understands intimately:

- take usable livestock;
- take grain/food;
- destroy what cannot be carried;
- deny resources to the enemy.

### Specific horror

He sees ordinary household details:

- bowl;
- bedding;
- stacked grain;
- child's item;
- cow being driven away.

They map directly onto Hendre Gelli and Madog's abandoned house.

### Ieuan response

He continues working.

No speech.

### Internal rupture

In 1349 he entered empty houses after disaster.

In 1355 he helps create one.

---

## SCENE 3 — THE FIRE

### Practical action

A settlement/resource site is burned under orders consistent with chevauchée practice.

### Ieuan's perspective

He notices fire as a working hazard:

- horse panic;
- sparks;
- smoke direction;
- men carrying plunder;
- animals refusing movement;
- confusion around narrow exits.

### Memory trigger

Smoke and an open doorway briefly place him back in the plague houses.

Do not make him freeze helplessly for a full scene.

His adult survival response is to become **more functional**.

### Iorwerth

Notices Ieuan working too mechanically after the burning.

Does not ask him to confess feelings.

---

## SCENE 4 — WATER / CROSSING

**Mid October**

### Purpose

Show military achievement through logistics.

### Problem

A difficult river/ford/crossing or muddy approach causes baggage/horse congestion.

### Ieuan contributes

- reads animal behaviour;
- identifies which load must move first;
- keeps an animal from fouling the crossing;
- helps maintain flow under pressure.

### Public recognition

A senior man uses Ieuan's judgement in front of others.

This matters more socially than private praise.

### Ieuan's reaction

Pride returns immediately despite everything he saw burned.

That contradiction is essential.

---

## SCENE 5 — WEALTH AND PLUNDER

**Carcassonne-region campaigning**

### Purpose

Show why soldiers found chevauchée economically seductive.

Ieuan sees:

- better houses/suburbs;
- goods worth taking;
- food/wine/equipment;
- men excited by loot;
- hierarchy deciding who gets what.

### Ieuan

He takes something useful when permitted.

Not jewelry for glamour.

Better possibilities:

- serviceable horse gear;
- gloves/clothing;
- tool;
- food;
- equipment component.

### Moral complication

He hates destruction and still benefits from it.

Do not let him keep moral cleanliness the army does not allow.

---

## SCENE 6 — THE CHILD / CIVILIAN MIRROR

### Purpose

Make the war personal without overusing atrocities.

Ieuan sees a civilian child/young person around the age Gwenllian would now be.

The child survives and flees or is pulled away by family.

### Rule

Ieuan does **not** rescue a symbolic French Gwenllian.

That would be too neat.

He simply sees her/him.

### Consequence

For the rest of the chapter, he cannot stop calculating Gwenllian's age if she had lived.

---

## SCENE 7 — 8 NOVEMBER: NARBONNE

### Historical anchor

The army reaches Narbonne.

### Ieuan state

- exhausted;
- more confident in military work;
- disturbed by what he has helped do;
- still not man-at-arms.

### False achievement

The outward march feels like success.

Then orders/conditions make clear they must turn back.

### Chapter end

Ieuan realises the return will be faster and the horses are already tired.

---

# CHAPTER 14 — THE RETURN

**Dates:** November to **2 December 1355**  
**Ieuan:** 20  
**Locations:** Narbonne → hard return toward Gascon security / La Réole

## Chapter job

Make the return more physically frightening than the outward raid and produce the deed that justifies Ieuan's promotion.

This chapter contains his **first confirmed killing**.

---

## SCENE 1 — LESS DAY, MORE ROAD

### Historical pressure

Return pace is faster, rest scarcer, daylight shorter.

### Practical detail

Ieuan's days become a loop:

- wake in darkness;
- check horse;
- move;
- forage/fodder;
- repair;
- move again;
- guard;
- sleep badly;
- repeat.

### Sensory continuity

Wet tack, sore legs, cracked hands, horse sweat and bad temper matter more than banners.

### Ieuan wants

Get the animals back intact.

His ambition temporarily narrows into survival/work again — almost like 1349.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE HORSE THAT IS FINISHED

### Purpose

Show Ieuan's matured judgement.

He identifies an animal that cannot safely continue its current burden.

A better-born or impatient man wants it pushed onward.

Ieuan argues/counters within the limits of rank.

### Payoff

His judgement proves correct.

This is another witnessed example of competence.

### Dafydd echo

**Watch the beast before you move it.**

At home it prevented a stubborn cow problem.

Here it protects military wealth/lives.

---

## SCENE 3 — PURSUIT / ALARM

**Late November**

### Historical frame

Use fictional small-scale attack/harassment inside the documented period of pursuit and limited fighting.

### Situation

A Warwick-affinity remount/baggage group contains:

- tired horses;
- packs;
- valuable gear;
- perhaps wounded/weak men;
- limited escort.

### Trigger

A horse falls/panics on a constrained route and movement starts to jam.

### Ieuan immediately understands

Pulling everything straight forward will worsen the blockage.

He begins directing animals/servants in the useful order.

### Social proof

People follow him because there is no time to debate his birth.

---

## SCENE 4 — HOLD

### Transition

Armed pursuers/raiders press the escort.

### Ieuan's instinct

Get the valuable horses away.

### Order

He is told to hold/stand with the escort while lower servants continue moving animals.

### Critical character beat

He obeys.

Six years earlier he survived by acting independently.

Military adulthood means sometimes **not doing what his survival instinct wants**.

---

## SCENE 5 — FIRST KILL

### Rule

Short. Confused. Physical.

No duel choreography.

### Event

An attacker comes through Ieuan's space / toward the men or animals behind him.

Ieuan uses the weapon training he has actually earned.

The man dies because of what Ieuan does.

### Ieuan's memory

He remembers fragments:

- breath;
- resistance;
- noise;
- weight;
- something on his hand/weapon;
- not necessarily the man's face.

### Immediate aftermath

He cannot process it because the job is not over.

That is important.

---

## SCENE 6 — BACK TO THE HORSES

### Purpose

This is the actual promotion-defining behaviour.

Other young men might chase retreating attackers/plunder.

Ieuan returns to:

- clearing the route;
- checking the animals;
- securing packs;
- getting the group moving.

### Iorwerth

Sees this.

He may tell Ieuan to finish the job rather than praise him.

### Witnesses

Enough people observe the incident that it can later be reported upward.

---

## SCENE 7 — AFTER THE KILLING

**That night or next halt**

### Ieuan attempts

- cleaning weapon;
- eating;
- sleeping.

### Plague comparison

He has seen many dead bodies.

That does not prepare him for knowing:

**this one is dead because I moved my hand.**

### Faith

He may pray/confess in whatever military religious setting is available.

Do not give him instant absolution emotionally even if sacramental practice offers religious structure.

### Iorwerth

Does not tell him killing is nothing.

Better: treats the reaction as normal and the duty as necessary.

---

## SCENE 8 — 2 DECEMBER: LA RÉOLE

### Historical anchor

The campaign reaches relative security / winter phase.

### Ieuan state

He is thinner and exhausted.

He expects to return to his normal valet/horse position.

### Strange change

Men begin addressing/using him differently:

- armed watch;
- responsibility;
- questions about the skirmish;
- someone else told to take the horse job Ieuan would normally perform.

### Chapter end

Ieuan realises decisions are being made about him without him present.

---

# CHAPTER 15 — WINTER ARMS

**Dates:** December 1355 through spring/early summer 1356  
**Ieuan:** 20–21  
**Locations:** Gascon winter quarters / garrison/frontier operations

## Chapter job

Make Ieuan's promotion **material, social and frighteningly real**.

By the end of this chapter he must be fully believable as a junior esquire/man-at-arms who can stand in Warwick's vanguard at Poitiers.

---

## SCENE 1 — SOMEBODY ELSE MUCKS THE STALL

**Early December**

### Purpose

Let Ieuan notice the promotion before it is explained.

He goes to perform ordinary horse-servant work.

A lower servant has already been assigned to it.

Instead Ieuan is ordered to:

- armed watch;
- meeting/briefing;
- escort duty;
- equipment issue.

### Ieuan reaction

He initially assumes someone has made a mistake.

### Iorwerth

Dryly informs him the mistake would be ignoring the order.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE REGRADING

### Structure

No ceremonial dubbing.

A senior Warwick-affinity officer/knight makes the arrangement practical:

- Ieuan will now serve at man-at-arms/esquire level;
- increased duty/pay/status;
- assigned/approved horse;
- weapon/equipment expectations;
- responsibility for lower servant support.

### Matthew

Matthew's prior sponsorship/report can be referenced as part of why Ieuan is accepted upward without requiring Matthew to remain beside him in 1356.

### Ieuan wants

To look as though this is exactly what he expected.

Inside he is terrified.

---

## SCENE 3 — ARMOUR THAT DOESN'T FIT

### Purpose

Show the promotion through discomfort.

Ieuan receives/acquires a practical mixed set of armour.

Some pieces:

- household issue;
- second-hand;
- altered;
- bought partly from wages/booty.

### Physical problem

A plate/strap/mail piece rubs or fits badly.

Iorwerth/armourer corrects it.

### Theme

Ieuan has entered the armed class in equipment originally shaped for somebody else's body.

That is almost too perfect a social metaphor — use lightly.

---

## SCENE 4 — HIS OWN LOWER SERVANT

### Character mirror

Ieuan is given responsibility over a younger/lower groom/servant for some duties.

### Ieuan discovers

He sounds like Dafydd.

Examples:

- checks a strap after the boy says it is done;
- corrects a gate/horse placement;
- pretends not to praise competent work;
- gets angry because the boy could have been hurt.

### Emotional effect

Ieuan hears Dad in himself and hates/loves it simultaneously.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE WINTER ACCIDENT

### Core continuity scene

After a run of dry nights, Ieuan wets his bedding in shared higher-status quarters.

### Panic

He wakes before others and instantly becomes psychologically fourteen again.

He tries to solve it silently.

### Discovery

Iorwerth catches him.

### Important response

No mockery.

No therapy.

No promise to keep a dramatic secret.

Something practical:

> Change it before it freezes.

Then Iorwerth continues talking about duty/horse/armour.

### Ieuan consequence

For the first time outside his family, somebody knows and **his social world does not collapse**.

This is a major psychological victory even though nothing outwardly happens.

---

## SCENE 6 — FIRST DUTY AS MAN-AT-ARMS

**Winter/spring 1356**

### Possible operation

- escort;
- garrison watch;
- patrol;
- frontier raid;
- receiving surrender of a minor place;
- prisoner handling.

### Purpose

Let Ieuan practice the status before Poitiers.

### Difference from late-1355 killing

Now armed duty is expected, not emergency.

He must:

- make decisions;
- hold position;
- manage fear;
- care for horse/equipment after action.

### Prisoner seed

This is a good place to teach that a surrendering enemy may be economically/socially more valuable alive than dead.

That makes Guillaume's later surrender intuitive rather than convenient.

---

## SCENE 7 — MONEY / VALUE

### Purpose

Show Ieuan beginning to possess economic stake.

He has:

- wages;
- equipment;
- perhaps small permitted booty share;
- a horse assigned/maintained through service;
- status with monetary consequences.

### Ieuan's mental arithmetic

He still thinks in childhood terms:

- cows;
- grain;
- winter fodder;
- what Hendre Gelli could have bought.

Use one comparison, not constant calculations.

### Character point

He begins to understand that becoming a knight someday would require **money**, not merely courage.

He still does not expect it for himself.

---

## SCENE 8 — SUMMER ORDERS

**By early July 1356**

### Historical anchor coming next

The next campaign begins, with the Prince leaving Bordeaux **6 July 1356** to join the army at La Réole.

### Ieuan's status

Now fully/functionally:

- junior esquire/man-at-arms;
- armed;
- mounted;
- expected to fight dismounted when ordered;
- integrated into Warwick's wider military world under Iorwerth's day-to-day supervision.

### Emotional change

He no longer asks himself every morning whether he belongs.

He still notices every man who thinks he does not.

### Chapter end

Ieuan prepares his own equipment while a younger servant prepares his horse.

The reversal from Chapter 10 is complete.

# CROSS-CHAPTER CONTINUITY AUDIT — 13–15

## Status

- Chapter 13: trusted armed valet/household servant;
- Chapter 14: campaign-proven but not yet formally advanced;
- Chapter 15: formally/functionally man-at-arms/esquire.

Do not call Ieuan a knight or treat him as socially settled.

## First killing

- occurs late November 1355 during fictional skirmish/alarm;
- not a famous battle;
- close, ugly, defensive;
- followed immediately by continued work;
- creates psychological difference between seeing death and causing it.

## Promotion evidence

No single deed does everything.

Promotion rests on:

- years of reliable service;
- horse/logistics skill;
- route competence;
- weapons training;
- obedience under danger;
- witnessed late-November performance;
- Matthew's prior sponsorship;
- Warwick-affinity need for capable men.

## War morality

Ieuan is neither enthusiastic butcher nor morally pure observer.

He:

- obeys destructive orders;
- benefits from plunder;
- dislikes what he sees;
- understands civilians through his own plague history;
- remains part of the army anyway.

This contradiction must survive through the end.

## Bed-wetting

Winter 1355–56 is the major outsider-discovery scene.

Iorwerth's non-reaction changes Ieuan more than reassurance would.

The condition may be less frequent, but it remains possible beyond this point.

## Iorwerth

By end Chapter 15:

- has corrected Ieuan for years;
- respects him professionally;
- knows his secret;
- trusts him in armed service;
- is not a father substitute.

This history is required so Chapter 17's rescue has emotional weight.

---

# END STATE AFTER CHAPTER 15

Ieuan is twenty-one.

He has:

- crossed the sea;
- helped burn civilian resources;
- killed a man;
- earned advancement;
- become responsible for lower servants;
- been exposed in his deepest shame and survived socially;
- become a genuine junior man-at-arms.

What he still does not know is whether all of this preparation will matter when an entire royal army comes at him.

That is the handoff to the 1356 Poitiers movement.
