# Research Sources

This bibliography is for the medieval plague novel currently working under `rhondda-1349/`.

Evidence should be read with care: a source may be excellent for one claim and unsuitable for another.

## Core Rhondda landscape

### Heneb — The Rhondda Historic Landscape Character Area
https://heneb.org.uk/hcla/the-rhondda/

Use for:
- Glynrhondda political history;
- 1246 seizure by Richard de Clare;
- medieval church organisation;
- Ystradyfodwg/Llanwynno;
- Penrhys grange/shrine/hostelry/Ffynnon Fair;
- platform houses, hafodau and hendre-hafod landscape;
- pastoral farming and cattle;
- ridge routes;
- Castell Nos and Ynysygrug context;
- medieval hunting park reference.

Confidence: **A for the archaeological/historic-landscape claims it directly reports.**

### Heneb — Penrhys character area (archived Rhondda HLCA)
https://heneb.org.uk/archive/ggat/cadw/historic_landscape/Rhondda/English/Rhondda_025.htm

Use for:
- ridgeway access;
- medieval pilgrimage centre;
- Cistercian grange;
- chapel, shrine, hostelry and Ffynnon Fair;
- early-fourteenth-century leasing of more than thirty holdings.

Confidence: **A/B depending on claim; traditions explicitly remain traditions.**

### Cadw — Castell Nos Scheduled Monument report
https://cadwpublic-api.azurewebsites.net/reports/sam/FullReport?id=3886&lang=en

Use for:
- medieval defensive motte classification;
- scarped natural sandstone outcrop;
- watershed route toward Brecon;
- possible post-1246 use.

Confidence: **A.**

## Black Death in Wales

### William Rees — “The Black Death in Wales,” Transactions of the Royal Historical Society
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/transactions-of-the-royal-historical-society/article/black-death-in-wales/9D28A0E282A273EC8DBF62FD05DD50B6

Use for:
- Welsh mortality/economic disruption;
- dead tenants, collapsed rents and uncultivated holdings;
- succession/land transfer after plague;
- court-roll evidence of Madoc ap Ririd and Kenwric entering plague-vacated houses and stealing goods/cattle.

Important caveat: published 1920. Valuable because it cites primary medieval records, but later scholarship may refine its interpretation.

Confidence: **A for quoted/identified primary-record examples; B for broader conclusions until cross-checked with newer scholarship.**

### Museum Wales — Margam / 1349 Glamorgan plague educational material
Museum Wales material records the Black Death reaching Glamorgan in 1349 and affecting Margam Abbey.

Use for:
- dating the Glamorgan outbreak to 1349.

Confidence: **A/B; use as a strong institutional secondary source and continue searching for the underlying documentary basis.**

## Modern plague biology

### CDC — How Plague Spreads
https://www.cdc.gov/plague/causes/index.html

Use for:
- *Yersinia pestis*;
- flea/animal transmission;
- pneumonic droplet transmission;
- avoiding the false idea that ordinary contact with a bubonic patient is automatically person-to-person spread.

Confidence: **A for modern disease biology.**

### CDC — Signs and Symptoms of Plague
https://www.cdc.gov/plague/signs-symptoms/index.html

Use for:
- buboes;
- fever/chills/weakness;
- bubonic incubation around 2–8 days;
- septicemic and pneumonic features.

Confidence: **A for modern disease biology.**

## Burial

### Historic England — Commemorative and Funerary Sites guidance
https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/dssg-commemorative-funerary/heag239-commemorative-and-funerary-ssg/

Use for:
- East Smithfield Black Death cemetery evidence;
- individual graves plus trenches;
- orderly interment rather than the cliché of bodies simply cast chaotically into pits.

Caveat: **London evidence, not direct evidence for Glynrhondda.**

Confidence: **A for East Smithfield; B when used to shape general expectations of burial under mass mortality.**

## Economy, food and money

### The National Archives — Living in the 14th Century
https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/currency-converter/living-in-the-14th-century/

Use for:
- Great Famine background;
- Black Death economic effects;
- rural/market economy context;
- silver penny;
- gold noble introduced 1344 at 6s 8d;
- groat circulation from 1351 at 4d;
- post-plague labour and consumption context.

Confidence: **A/B institutional overview.**

### Historic England — Charred Plant Remains from the Medieval Farmstead at Eckweek
https://historicengland.org.uk/research/results/reports/27-1995

Use for comparative medieval crop possibilities:
- wheat;
- barley;
- rye;
- oats;
- vetches;
- peas;
- field beans;
- hazelnuts.

Caveat: **Avon, not the Rhondda. Comparative evidence only.**

Confidence: **A for that site; B/C for specific Rhondda household use until regional evidence is found.**

### Historic England — Review of animal remains from medieval sites
Search/report used for a cited early-fourteenth-century shepherd-food list including bread, bacon, onions, garlic, leeks, butter, green cheese, ale and other foods.

Caveat: not a Glynrhondda menu.

Confidence: **B comparative texture.**

## Welsh housing comparators

### Museum Wales — Cilewent Farmhouse
https://museum.wales/collections/historic-buildings/5/Cilewent-Farmhouse/

Use for:
- Welsh `tŷ hir` / longhouse concept;
- people and animals under one roof in a later Welsh example.

Date: first built 1470.

Caveat: **121 years later than the novel opening and from Powys/Radnorshire. Do not copy as a 1349 Rhondda plan.**

### Museum Wales — Hendre'r-ywydd Uchaf Farmhouse
https://museum.wales/collections/historic-buildings/13/Hendrer-ywydd-Uchaf-Farmhouse/

Use for later medieval Welsh construction comparison:
- cruck frame;
- wattle and daub;
- limewash;
- open hearth;
- unglazed windows;
- cattle/horse bays.

Date: 1508.

Caveat: **later and from Denbighshire; reconstruction comparator only.**

## Clothing

### Museum Wales — Model of Longbowman
https://museum.wales/collections/online/object/228d8c8e-73b5-3d7b-aab0-c907584af17d/Model-of-Longbowman/

Use for:
- wool and linen clothing materials;
- hood, hose, leather ankle boots;
- evidence that Welsh bowmen in Black Prince service received woollen livery in 1346.

Caveat: the displayed model represents c.1405 even though part of its livery reconstruction draws on 1346 evidence.

Confidence: **B for clothing texture; specific 1346 livery claim is useful for later military chapters.**

## Names

### National Library of Wales — medieval Welsh poetry/manuscript catalogue material
https://archives.library.wales/

Use for:
- attested medieval given names;
- patronymic chains;
- spelling variation.

### Rees, Black Death in Wales
Also useful for names actually appearing in plague-era administrative records, including Madoc ap Ririd, Griffith ap Madoc, Llewelyn, Jeuan/Ieuan, David ap Madoc and Wladus/Gwladus forms.

Confidence: **A for names as recorded; do not assume a named individual came from Glynrhondda unless the record says so.**

- Glynrhondda-specific fourteenth-century tenant names and rents;
- exact tenure patterns around Ystradyfodwg/Penrhys in the 1340s;
- direct fourteenth-century South Wales domestic archaeology closest to the Rhondda;
- local crop evidence and grain supply;
- clothing of poor upland Welsh tenants specifically around 1349;
- parish clergy and church arrangements in Ystradyfodwg/Llanwynno around 1349;
- medieval medical/urine beliefs suitable for the superstition subplot;
- month-by-month agricultural calendar for upland Glamorgan;
- 1349 travel connections between Glynrhondda, Llantrisant and Cardiff.
