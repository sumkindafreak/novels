# Pass One: Micro-Location and Parish Personnel

This file tightens two remaining parts of the opening world:

1. the most defensible **micro-location** for the fictional family holding;
2. what can and cannot be said about the **clergy serving Ystradyfodwg around 1349**.

---

# 1. Recommended micro-location

## Working recommendation

Place the permanent family holding on the **western side of the Rhondda Fawr in the Ystradyfodwg–Gelli landscape**, on rising ground just above the better river-terrace land and below the uplands of **Mynydd Gelli / Mynydd Ton**.

This is still a fictional holding, not an identified archaeological house.

### Why the west side works

Heneb describes Ton Pentre/Gelli as occupying the west bank of the Rhondda Fawr and identifies the area around the medieval Ystradyfodwg church as a widening of the river terraces containing relatively flat, good-quality agricultural land suitable for settlement.

Above this lower settlement zone, the surrounding uplands preserve medieval platform-house/hafod evidence.

This creates a believable vertical household landscape:

- **river/terrace:** water, better cultivated ground, route through the valley;
- **lower slope:** permanent domestic holding;
- **upper slope:** rougher grazing and seasonal cattle work;
- **high ground:** platform houses, tracks, weather exposure and emergency refuge.

Evidence rating: **A for the landscape pattern; C for the exact fictional holding.**

Sources:
- Heneb, Ton Pentre and Gelli: https://heneb.org.uk/hcla/the-rhondda/ton-pentre-and-gelli/
- Heneb, The Rhondda: https://heneb.org.uk/hcla/the-rhondda/
- Heneb, Rhondda Uplands: https://heneb.org.uk/hcla/the-rhondda/rhondda-uplands-mynydd-ton-mynydd-penygraig/

# 2. The boy's immediate mental map

If we use this micro-location, his childhood geography becomes elegantly simple.

## Down / toward the river

- better agricultural ground;
- crossing points and local movement;
- neighbouring holdings;
- ordinary contact with the valley community.

## Along the west-side settlement zone

- Ystradyfodwg church/parochial centre;
- other scattered farms/holdings;
- routes eventually leading down-valley toward Llantrisant connections.

## Up behind the house

- Mynydd Gelli / Mynydd Ton uplands;
- cattle grazing;
- seasonal shelter/hafod world;
- rough tracks;
- exposure to mist, rain and wind;
- places he knows far better than an outsider would.

## Across the valley / eastern ridge world

- Penrhys;
- Marian religious landscape;
- Ffynnon Fair;
- Cistercian grange/holdings;
- travellers and cross-valley movement.

Do not guarantee that the actual shrine building is visible from the fictional doorstep. The **ridge/location** can dominate his mental geography without every structure being in direct line of sight.

---

# 3. Why this micro-location improves the plague story

The boy begins in a place that is neither isolated wilderness nor town.

He is close enough to:

- church;
- neighbours;
- travellers' news;
- livestock exchange/work;
- the religious world of Penrhys;

but far enough from dense urban life that news of the pestilence can arrive before the disease visibly enters his immediate household circle.

When he later flees upward, he moves into terrain he already knows rather than escaping into an invented wilderness.

The landscape therefore creates a natural progression:

**home → church/community → upland refuge → wider world.**

# 4. Do we have a named priest at Ystradyfodwg in 1349?

**Not yet.**

No sufficiently reliable source located so far identifies the individual priest/chaplain serving Ystradyfodwg in 1349.

The absence of a recovered name is not evidence that the church lacked clergy.

It means only that the current research has not recovered a secure personal identification.

### Novel rule

Until a near-contemporary clergy list, episcopal register, tax record or presentation record is found:

- do not use a supposed real historical priest's name;
- create a fictional cleric if the story needs a recurring priest;
- describe his formal status cautiously as a priest/chaplain serving Ystradyfodwg rather than inventing a precise benefice title.

---

# 5. What we do know about Ystradyfodwg's church status

The strongest located evidence says that medieval **Ystradyfodwg functioned as a parochial chapelry within the huge parish of Llantrisant**.

The local church had the right to:

- baptise;
- marry;
- bury local people.

This is crucial.

The family does not need to travel to Llantrisant for every major sacramental event.

Ystradyfodwg is their normal religious centre.

Source:
- “An unusual miniature font at Penrhys,” University of South Wales repository: https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/ws/files/31136338/59085_Glamorgan_History_PENRHYS_1.pdf

Later evidence also preserves Ystradyfodwg as a chapelry dependent upon Llantrisant, supporting continuity of that relationship.

---

# 6. Penrhys clergy: an important caution

The Penrhys font study gives a useful distinction.

The Cistercian grange's lay brothers were probably served spiritually by visits from the **`magister conversorum`**, the senior monk responsible for overseeing lay brothers on outlying granges.

A resident/identified priest at Penrhys is documented in the **later fifteenth century** in connection with the pilgrim hostel.

### Do not back-project carelessly

The novel should therefore **not automatically install a resident parish-style priest at Penrhys in 1349** simply because one is documented a century later.

Safer options in 1349 include:

- Cistercian personnel connected to the grange;
- periodic monastic oversight;
- lay brothers/tenants;
- religious travellers;
- the chapel and shrine functioning without being treated as an independent parish church.

---

# 7. A strong fictional parish-priest model

If Pass Two requires a named priest, the safest character model is:

> **a fictional Welsh-speaking priest/chaplain who regularly serves the Ystradyfodwg church under the wider Llantrisant ecclesiastical structure.**

He can plausibly know local families personally.

He may:

- baptise children;
- witness marriages;
- hear confession;
- celebrate Mass;
- bury the dead;
- carry news from outside the immediate valley;
- know enough Latin for liturgical/formal use;
- live within a Welsh-speaking pastoral community while participating in the diocesan Church of Llandaff.

His exact residence, income and appointment mechanism should remain vague in prose unless further evidence is found.

---

# 8. Plague consequences for parish structure

When the mortality arrives, the church is not merely a building.

It is a service network dependent on surviving humans.

As deaths rise:

- burial demand increases;
- confession/last rites become exhausting;
- ordinary worship is disrupted;
- the priest may have to visit multiple scattered holdings;
- fewer neighbours remain to carry bodies or dig graves;
- if the cleric becomes ill, the community loses both a familiar person and a key ritual function.

This gives the novel a powerful escalation without needing the church institution to behave irrationally.

# 9. Proposed story geography to lock in Pass Two

Unless new evidence overturns it, use:

### Family home

A fictional modest tenant holding on the **lower western slope above the Rhondda Fawr terraces in the Gelli/Ystradyfodwg orbit**.

### Family upland work

Access/responsibility for cattle grazing and a simple seasonal shelter higher toward **Mynydd Gelli / Mynydd Ton**.

### Ordinary church

**Ystradyfodwg.**

### Sacred place / outside-news magnet

**Penrhys.**

### Lordship administration / serious outside journey

**Llantrisant.**

This is now precise enough to build character routines without claiming an archaeological certainty that does not exist.

# 10. Evidence grading

### A

- Ystradyfodwg/Gelli has a medieval settlement focus on favourable river terraces.
- medieval platform-house evidence survives on surrounding Rhondda uplands.
- Ystradyfodwg functioned as a parochial chapelry with baptism/marriage/burial rights.
- Penrhys was a separate Cistercian grange/shrine landscape.

### B

- a modest permanent household below Mynydd Gelli with upland cattle access;
- a local priest/chaplain regularly serving the chapelry;
- familiar local routes connecting household, church and upland work.

### C

- exact house coordinate;
- exact priest name;
- exact priest residence;
- exact holding boundaries;
- exact path taken from the house to each site.

These details can be fictionalised conservatively during Pass Two.
