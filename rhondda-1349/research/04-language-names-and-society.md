# Pass One: Language, Names and Society

## 1. Language landscape

The protagonist should begin life primarily as a **Welsh speaker**.

Museum Wales notes that medieval Wales was multilingual: Welsh remained the language of much of the native population, while Anglo-Norman was spoken and written by a smaller ruling elite and appeared in administrative/legal contexts. Cambridge scholarship also notes that English-speaking settlement expanded strongly in parts of south Wales after Norman conquest, especially the Vale of Glamorgan, Gower and south Pembrokeshire.

Glynrhondda, an upland Welsh territory only brought firmly under marcher control in the thirteenth/fourteenth centuries, should therefore feel much more Welsh-speaking than the lowland Vale.

### Story rule

- Family and neighbours: Welsh first.
- Church liturgy: Latin at Mass, with pastoral explanation/instruction potentially mediated through Welsh.
- Lordship/legal/elite contexts: Latin and Anglo-Norman can appear in records and higher-status communication.
- English becomes increasingly relevant as the boy leaves the valley and enters military/household networks.

Do not make him conveniently fluent in several languages at fourteen.

## 2. Naming practice

Fourteenth-century Welsh documentary names frequently use patronymics:

- `ap` = son of
- `ferch` = daughter of

Examples appearing in plague-era Welsh records include names such as:

- Madoc ap Ririd
- Griffith ap Madoc
- Llewelyn
- Jeuan / Ieuan
- David ap Madoc
- Jenan / Ieuan forms
- Wladus / Gwladus

National Library collections and medieval Welsh poetry preserve many recurring given names across the period, including Dafydd, Gruffudd, Ieuan, Llywelyn, Rhys, Hywel, Maredudd and others.

### Naming rule for the novel

The family should not be given modern fixed hereditary surnames unless a specific social/contextual reason supports it. A boy may be identified by:

- his given name;
- his father's name (`X ap Y`);
- locality;
- occupation or nickname;
- later, a different identifying label in an English/Anglo-Norman military environment.

Spelling can vary in records. Dialogue does not need to reproduce manuscript spelling mechanically.

## 3. Names not yet locked

Do not choose the protagonist's final name until we build a short list based on:

1. names demonstrably current in fourteenth-century Wales;
2. pronunciation/readability for a modern audience;
3. avoiding names that feel accidentally comic or strongly modern;
4. avoiding overuse of famous princely names if it makes the character sound grander than his station.

The same applies to parents and sister.

## 4. Marcher lordship

Glynrhondda sits within the Lordship of Glamorgan, a marcher lordship with extensive local administrative and judicial autonomy.

Heneb's landscape history records:

- Welsh control of the uplands before 1246;
- Richard de Clare's seizure of Meisgyn and Glynrhondda in 1246;
- continuing native resistance;
- marcher rule established by the later 1330s;
- succession through the de Clares and Despensers.

This produces a useful social tension: the boy is native Welsh, but the legal/military power structure above him is bound into Anglo-Norman/English lordship.

## 5. Llantrisant's role

Llantrisant functions as an important administrative and ecclesiastical anchor south of Glynrhondda. The Rhondda's medieval churches were organised within the wider Llantrisant parochial system.

For the story, Llantrisant can plausibly represent:

- lordship administration;
- market/trade contact;
- legal business;
- a gateway between upland Glynrhondda and the more anglicised/administrative world to the south.

We should verify specific 1349 offices and routes before placing named officials there.

## 6. Social status

Current working family status: **poor free Welsh tenant household**.

This is deliberately between two clichés:

- not a completely unfree peasant caricature with no possessions or agency;
- not prosperous yeoman/gentry with implausible resources.

The family can owe rents/services and still possess animals, tools, household goods and practical standing in a small community.

Welsh plague records show both free and 'native' tenures affected by mortality. Post-plague holdings were reassigned, rents changed and surviving heirs/widows negotiated new realities.

## 7. Post-plague opportunity

The Black Death radically reduced labour supply. The English government responded with the Ordinance of Labourers (1349) and Statute of Labourers (1351), attempts to control wages and labour mobility.

The novel should not portray the plague as instantly ending class hierarchy. It did, however, create vacancies, disrupted tenures and increased the value of surviving labour.

This can help explain why a capable orphan finds openings that would have been harder to access before the mortality.

## 8. Religion and language

The boy may understand prayers, biblical stories and religious teaching in Welsh while experiencing the formal liturgy in Latin.

Do not write every priest as an educated English outsider. Local clergy in Wales could be embedded in Welsh communities even though the wider Church and its documents operated in Latin.

## 9. Dialogue strategy

The novel should be written in readable modern English, not faux-medieval dialect.

Use occasional Welsh terms where they carry social/geographical meaning, but context must make them understandable.

Good approach:

- modern readable prose;
- period-appropriate concepts;
- restrained vocabulary;
- no modern slang/psychological jargon in medieval characters' mouths;
- no endless 'thee/thou' performance unless there is a precise linguistic reason.

The reader should feel that the characters are speaking Welsh even though the novel renders it in English.

## 10. Social knowledge at fourteen

The protagonist knows:

- his valley;
- kin/neighbours;
- parish/church routines;
- land boundaries and local tracks;
- practical farming obligations;
- who has authority locally.

He does **not** begin with a modern map-like concept of Britain, France, disease ecology or national identity.

His geographical and political world should expand as the novel does.
