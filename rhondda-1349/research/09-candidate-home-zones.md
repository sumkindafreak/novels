# Pass One: Candidate Home Zones

This file ranks the strongest locations for the protagonist's **fictional family holding**. The goal is not to put a pin on a documented medieval person's house; that evidence does not survive. Instead, we choose a fictional holding inside a landscape whose settlement, agriculture, church access and movement patterns are archaeologically defensible.

## Current recommendation

**Working favourite: the Ystradyfodwg–Gelli orbit in the Rhondda Fawr, with the permanent holding on or just above the better river-terrace land and access to a seasonal upland cattle holding on Mynydd Gelli / Mynydd Ton / nearby higher ground.**

This is not yet a final coordinate. It is the strongest *zone*.

---

# Zone A — Ystradyfodwg / Gelli / Mynydd Gelli–Mynydd Ton

## Evidence

Heneb describes the area around the medieval church of Ystradyfodwg as a widening of the river terraces with **flatter, good-quality agricultural land ideal for settlement**. The density of earlier and later settlement around this point strongly implies an early medieval settlement focus.

On the higher ground around it are known medieval settlement remains, including:

- platform houses on **Mynydd Gelli**;
- platform houses on **Mynydd Ton**;
- platform houses at **Cwm Cesig above Ton Pentre**;
- platform houses at **Nant-y-Gwiddon**.

Heneb repeatedly interprets these upland medieval platforms/hafodau as part of a pastoral landscape associated especially with cattle and seasonal exploitation of upland grazing.

## Strengths for the novel

### 1. A defensible permanent settlement zone
The river terraces provide exactly the sort of land where a more permanent `hendre` or valley-side holding makes sense, even though later industry has erased much direct medieval archaeology from the valley floor.

### 2. Real upland archaeology nearby
The family can plausibly have grazing rights, work obligations or access to a modest seasonal upland dwelling without us inventing a completely hypothetical economic system.

### 3. Parish life works cleanly
Ystradyfodwg is the local parochial church centre for baptism, marriage and burial. The family can have an ordinary, believable relationship with church life before plague makes the site emotionally central.

### 4. Penrhys is close enough to matter without being on the doorstep
The ridge toward Penrhys can connect the family to travellers, the Marian shrine and the cross-valley route while preserving the boy's primarily rural home world.

### 5. Excellent survival geography
The boy can move between:

- river terraces;
- wooded valley sides;
- upland pasture;
- church settlement;
- Penrhys ridge;
- routes farther up/down the Rhondda Fawr.

### 6. Strong visual storytelling
After the deaths, climbing above the permanent settlements lets him look back across the valley and read survival through **hearth smoke — or its absence**.

## Cautions

- Industrial development has destroyed/buried much valley-floor medieval settlement evidence.
- We therefore cannot claim that a particular later farm occupies the protagonist's exact 1349 holding.
- The family house itself must remain a historically informed fictional reconstruction.

## Rating

**9.5 / 10 — strongest permanent-home zone.**

---

# Zone B — Penrhys / Mynachdy Penrhys

## Evidence

Penrhys was a medieval Cistercian grange and religious centre. Heneb records that from the early fourteenth century the Cistercians abandoned direct large-scale sheep farming there and divided the manor into **more than thirty leased holdings**, producing substantial boundary construction.

It also possessed the chapel, Marian shrine, Ffynnon Fair and hostelry and sat in an upland route network.

## Strengths for the novel

- unusually strong early-fourteenth-century evidence for multiple holdings;
- direct connection to travellers and religious belief;
- powerful location for plague rumours and desperate attempts at healing;
- natural crossing between Rhondda Fawr and Fach;
- striking exposed ridge environment.

## Problems

### Too narratively convenient
If the family lives immediately beside the shrine, virtually every major local ingredient is at their door. That risks making the setting feel constructed for the plot.

### Tenure remains unclear
We know the manor was divided into holdings, but we have not yet recovered a 1340s tenant list identifying the exact people, rents and holding sizes.

### Pilgrimage fame caution
Penrhys's great Welsh pilgrimage fame is best documented in the fifteenth century. It existed as a sacred/grange landscape earlier, but we should not automatically give 1349 the crowds known from later evidence.

## Best use

**Not the main family home.** Use Penrhys as a place the family knows, occasionally visits, crosses or works near — and later as a psychologically loaded location during the pestilence.

## Rating

**8 / 10 as a major story location; 6.5 / 10 as the permanent home.**

# Zone C — Upper Rhondda Fawr / Blaenrhondda–Saerbren orbit

## Evidence

Heneb records a dense medieval upland landscape farther up the Fawr, including:

- longhut settlements at Cwm and Nant Saerbren;
- hafodau at Garreg Lwyd, Blaenrhondda;
- hafodau at Blaenycwm;
- medieval settlement at Penrhiw Castell Llaeth;
- stock-management enclosures/pounds such as Tarren Saerbren.

These fit the cattle-centred seasonal pastoral model particularly well.

## Strengths

- strongest feeling of isolation;
- very strong survival-horror landscape;
- abundant evidence for upland seasonal settlement and stock management;
- woodland, rough pasture, watercourses and high ground provide practical survival settings;
- abandoned hafodau could become important after the plague.

## Problems

- farther from the Ystradyfodwg parish centre;
- Penrhys becomes a more deliberate journey rather than part of ordinary nearby geography;
- a permanent lower holding still has to be reconstructed because surviving archaeological evidence is mainly upland.

## Best use

This is an excellent candidate for **the family's summer grazing / hafod world**, or a place the boy flees toward after the household collapses.

## Rating

**8.5 / 10 for survival chapters; 7.5 / 10 for the permanent home.**

# Zone D — Rhondda Fach / Carn-y-wiwer–Craig Rhondda Fach

## Evidence

Carn-y-wiwer contains one of the best-preserved medieval upland settlement complexes in the Rhondda: paired house platforms, with nearby cairns and possible ploughing evidence whose precise relationship remains uncertain.

Other medieval settlement survives at:

- Craig Tir Llaethdy;
- Craig Rhondda-fach;
- Mynydd Ty'n-tyle;
- nearby upland enclosure/pound sites.

Heneb again associates the landscape with seasonal cattle exploitation.

Farther north, Castell Nos controls a significant watershed approach toward Brecon.

## Strengths

- extremely strong visible medieval settlement archaeology;
- excellent cattle/hafod setting;
- dramatic northern approach and Castell Nos;
- easy to make the boy's immediate world feel remote without making it ahistorical.

## Problems

- moves the family away from the especially useful Ystradyfodwg church geography;
- ecclesiastical ties may lean more naturally toward Llanwynno depending on the exact location;
- Penrhys remains accessible but becomes a cross-valley destination;
- would require reworking several of our emerging story relationships.

## Best use

Keep as a **secondary landscape** that the boy may know through cattle movement, relatives, travellers or later flight. It is also an excellent benchmark for what the lost medieval upland settlement elsewhere probably looked like.

## Rating

**8 / 10 historically; 7 / 10 for our current story architecture.**

# Recommended family geography

The cleanest reconstruction now looks like this:

## Permanent home (`hendre` world)

A **fictional modest holding in the wider Ystradyfodwg–Gelli landscape**, positioned above the immediate floodplain but within practical reach of the flatter river-terrace agricultural land.

The exact site must not overwrite a known archaeological monument or claim a later named farm as certainly medieval.

## Upland work (`hafod` world)

The family has **access to or responsibility for** a much simpler seasonal cattle site on the higher ground — probably in the Mynydd Gelli / Mynydd Ton orbit, or potentially farther up-valley depending on the final tenancy model.

Do not automatically say they own both sites outright. Grazing/customary/tenurial rights remain to be researched.

## Church

Ystradyfodwg.

## Sacred/travel location

Penrhys.

## Administrative world

Llantrisant.

## Outside world

Cardiff/Llandaff to the south; upland routes and ultimately Brecon-side country to the north/east.

---

# Why this is especially good for the bed-wetting plot

The household can have a cramped permanent dwelling with animals/storage immediately adjacent and a believable reason to allocate the fourteen-year-old a separate sleeping place after repeated accidents.

Later, the boy already knows how to sleep and work in a rough upland shelter because seasonal pastoral life is part of his upbringing.

This means his survival skills do **not** materialise after the plague. The plague forces him to use ordinary childhood competencies under extraordinary circumstances.

That is much stronger than turning him into an instant wilderness expert.

**Working lock:** Ystradyfodwg–Gelli orbit.

**Not yet locked:**
