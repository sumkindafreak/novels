# Chapter Seventeen — Maupertuis

**19 September 1356**

The horses knew before the men did.

Ieuan saw it in their eyes.

Sweat darkened coats despite the morning air. Reins pulled against hands. Animals shifted sideways, sensing masses of men, unfamiliar movement, the metal smells of armour and the rising noise nobody could explain to them.

Tomas held Brân.

"He's pulling."

"Let him feel the rein. Don't fight his head."

"I know."

"Then do it."

Ieuan put one hand on Brân's neck.

The horse trembled beneath it.

"Easy."

The word meant nothing.

The tone mattered.

Around them men prepared to leave horses behind and fight on foot.

Ieuan checked Tomas's grip.

Checked the lead line behind.

Checked a strap that did not need checking.

Then stopped himself.

Iorwerth stood several paces away, helmet under one arm.

"Ieuan."

"Aye."

"Come."

Ieuan looked at Brân.

For years, horses had been his reason for being anywhere important.

Now the horse stayed behind.

Ieuan stepped away.

Brân called once after him.

He did not turn.

---

Waiting in armour was worse than wearing it while moving.

Weight settled into shoulders.

Mail pressed at the neck.

Heat gathered beneath layers while hands stayed cold.

Men shifted and then pretended they had not shifted.

Someone near Ieuan vomited quietly into the grass and wiped his mouth with the back of one gauntlet.

Another man prayed without stopping for breath.

Robert complained that his left boot had been rubbing since July.

Iorwerth said, "I'm glad you waited until now to mention it."

"Never seemed important before."

"And today?"

"Still isn't. I just wanted somebody else annoyed."

A few men laughed.

Ieuan did not.

He had become very still.

Iorwerth looked at him.

"There you are."

Ieuan frowned.

"Where?"

"That thing you do."

"What thing?"

"Become a fence post when you're frightened."

Ieuan flexed one hand.

"I'm not frightened."

Iorwerth looked at him.

Ieuan exhaled.

"Fine."

"Good."

"Why is everyone pleased when I admit I'm frightened?"

"Because it saves time."

Ieuan looked ahead.

The ground was not a neat field made for stories.

Hedges.

Ditches.

Uneven approaches.

Places where men disappeared from view because earth and vegetation broke the line.

He could not see the whole battle.

That fact remained true even when it began.

---

The first great movement came as sound.

Shouting.

Hooves.

A rolling violence somewhere beyond the men in front.

Then horses appeared through gaps and broken sightlines.

French mounted men pressing toward the position.

Ieuan's body reacted before his mind.

Horse first.

Always horse first.

He saw one animal fighting the bit, another striking forward, another already wrong in the stride because fear had shifted its balance.

Archers loosed.

The sound of arrows moving together was unlike anything Ieuan had heard closely before.

Then horses screamed.

That was worse.

Men around him shouted with excitement or fear.

Ieuan felt sick.

He understood exactly what a horse did when pain overtook training.

A wounded animal tried to turn where there was nowhere to turn.

Another stumbled into it.

A rider went down beneath hooves.

Some archers were shooting at the animals because a horse could bring down a man in armour more efficiently than any heroic duel.

Ieuan knew the logic.

He hated the logic.

A horse broke partly free and ran sideways near the line, reins loose, one flank bloodied.

Ieuan's whole body wanted to move toward it.

Help.

Turn it.

Get it clear before it knocked men down.

Iorwerth's hand struck his arm.

"Stay."

Ieuan stayed.

The horse went down twenty paces away.

He watched it try to rise.

Could do nothing.

This was the inverse of every lesson his father had taught him.

Watch the beast.

Know what it needs.

And sometimes stand still while it dies because your place is somewhere else.

---

The mounted attack broke into confusion.

Ieuan never knew exactly how large it had been from where he stood.

Later men would explain everything.

Where the French had come.

Where archers stood.

Which movement mattered.

At the time, he knew only that horses had come and then fewer horses were still coming.

Then the next pressure began.

Men on foot.

Armour.

Shields.

Weapons.

The distance disappeared.

Somebody shouted an order Ieuan heard only halfway.

The man beside him moved.

Ieuan moved with him.

Impact arrived.

The first clash was not one clean meeting.

It was bodies compressing into whatever space ground and fear permitted.

Ieuan's spear became useful for seconds.

Then useless because there was no room.

He lost it.

Not dramatically.

Someone struck the shaft, another body shoved through, and the weapon was suddenly somewhere below feet where retrieving it would have meant bending into death.

Ieuan drew his sword.

He had imagined sword fighting when he was seventeen.

This was not that.

No clear opponent waited politely.

Steel appeared from directions.

A shoulder struck his chest.

A helmet hit his.

Someone screamed very close.

Ieuan used the shield of the man beside him as part of his own safety without meaning to, then felt that man lean on him in return.

Formation.

Where to stand.

Iorwerth's first lesson.

Not how to kill.

Where to stand.

Ieuan stayed.

---

Time stopped making sense.

There were moments of impossible noise followed by seconds where Ieuan heard only his own breathing inside the helmet.

He struck a man and did not know what happened to him.

Blocked something badly and felt the impact through his whole arm.

Lost sight of Iorwerth.

Found him again.

Robert was somewhere to the right until he wasn't.

A Frenchman fell in front and Ieuan stepped on part of him because there was nowhere else to put a foot.

Mud pulled at boots.

Blood made ground slick in patches.

The hedge he had noticed earlier ceased to be scenery and became simply something stopping men from moving sideways.

At one point Ieuan thought the line had broken.

Then realised only the six men around him had shifted.

The battle beyond his sight could have been won or lost already.

He had no idea.

That frightened him less than expected.

His world had become ten feet wide.

Stay upright.

Stay with the men beside him.

Breathe.

Don't overreach.

Don't chase.

Move when they move.

At fourteen, survival had meant no one was coming to tell him what happened next.

At twenty-one, survival meant obeying men who did.

---

Iorwerth went down without Ieuan seeing the blow.

One moment he was upright.

The next Ieuan saw the top of his helmet disappear behind bodies.

Ieuan's stomach dropped.

Every instinct screamed *go*.

He stepped.

The man beside him shouted, "Hold!"

Ieuan stopped.

Iorwerth was down.

Maybe dead.

Maybe being trampled.

Ieuan stayed.

The French pressure in front had not vanished because his friend was hurt.

This was the lesson he had resented for years.

The dangerous road.

The baggage attack.

Hold the place or make everything worse.

Ieuan fought.

He hated every heartbeat of it.

Then movement changed.

A command passed along the line.

Men shifted forward and sideways.

The pressure opened just enough.

Ieuan saw Iorwerth again.

On one knee.

Trying to rise.

Blood darkened the lower side of his armour.

"Robert!"

Robert appeared from somewhere Ieuan had assumed he was dead.

"What?"

Ieuan pointed.

They moved together.

Not abandoning formation now.

Moving within it as the line changed.

They reached Iorwerth.

"I'm fine," Iorwerth said immediately.

"You're sitting in mud."

"Strategic."

Ieuan got one arm beneath him.

Iorwerth hissed.

"Where?"

"Side."

"Can you stand?"

"If you stop asking."

Robert took the other side.

They lifted.

Armoured men were heavy.

Ieuan knew this with a knowledge older than war.

Bodies did not help carry themselves when badly hurt.

Tad's weight.

Gwenllian's.

The memory entered his muscles without needing words.

"Move," Ieuan said.

Then a French knight came through the chaos toward them.

---

Ieuan knew he was a knight before he knew anything else.

Equipment.

Bearing.

The way other men had moved near him.

The Frenchman was tired too.

That mattered.

No shining champion had arrived fresh for the final duel.

The man's surcoat was torn and filthy. His breathing came hard. One gauntlet looked damaged.

He saw Iorwerth wounded.

Saw Ieuan and Robert holding him.

Saw opportunity.

Robert lowered Iorwerth awkwardly.

Ieuan stepped forward.

The French knight said something.

Ieuan caught only part.

Not enough.

The sword came.

Ieuan blocked late.

The impact jarred his wrist.

He gave ground.

The Frenchman pressed.

Ieuan tried to create space and almost backed into Iorwerth.

No room.

Always no room.

He moved sideways instead.

A blow scraped his shoulder plate.

Ieuan struck back.

The knight turned it.

Better swordsman.

Ieuan understood that quickly.

That was useful information.

He stopped trying to be better.

He tried to stay alive.

The Frenchman advanced again.

Ieuan let the ground help.

A churned patch where footing failed slightly.

Not a trap.

Not clever hero work.

Just the same habit he had with cattle and horses.

See where weight is going.

The knight stepped.

His foot slid half a pace.

Ieuan hit him hard with the shoulder and sword together.

Both men nearly fell.

Robert shouted.

Another fighter crashed past behind them.

The French knight recovered and struck Ieuan's helmet hard enough that light flashed in his vision.

Ieuan staggered.

The next blow came.

He caught the arm instead of the blade.

They collided.

For several seconds the fight became wrestling in armour.

Ieuan smelled the man's breath.

He heard him grunt.

Felt metal edges and weight.

The Frenchman tried to drive him down.

Ieuan was stronger than he had been at seventeen.

Stubborner than he had ever stopped being.

He twisted.

The man went partly to one knee.

Ieuan brought the sword point toward a gap.

The Frenchman froze.

Then shouted a word Ieuan understood.

Yield.

Ieuan did not move.

The battle roared around them.

The easiest thing would have been to thrust.

Finish.

No prisoner to guard.

No danger behind him later.

The thought existed for less than a heartbeat.

Then all the winter lessons returned.

A man who yields and is accepted changes the rules.

Ieuan pulled the point back slightly.

"Your name."

The Frenchman breathed hard.

"Guillaume. Guillaume de la Motte."

The words came in broken overlap of French Ieuan understood well enough through years of military work.

"Knight?"

"Oui. Chevalier."

Ieuan grabbed his arm.

"Then you're mine."

The absurdity of that statement nearly made him laugh.

Guillaume looked as if he might kill him for it if the sword were not still at his throat.

Ieuan lowered the weapon.

"Help me."

Guillaume stared.

"What?"

Ieuan pointed at Iorwerth.

"Help."

The French knight looked offended by captivity almost immediately.

Good.

That meant he intended to live.

---

Between Ieuan, Robert and a prisoner who had been trying to kill them seconds earlier, they got Iorwerth moving.

That was the feat later men told badly.

They made it sound elegant.

Ieuan protected his wounded superior.

Defeated a French knight.

Took a valuable prisoner.

Brought them clear.

In reality, Iorwerth swore constantly.

Robert slipped twice.

Guillaume tried to insist on some point of honour Ieuan could not understand while carrying half another man's weight.

Ieuan told him to shut up in three languages.

A horse without a rider blocked one route and Ieuan nearly abandoned everything to catch it from instinct.

He stopped.

Different job.

They moved through broken ground toward men of their own company.

At one point Iorwerth sagged badly.

"Stay awake," Ieuan said.

"I'm awake."

"You stopped talking."

"Enjoy it."

"Not today."

Iorwerth made a sound that might have been laughter.

That was enough.

Other Warwick men reached them.

Hands took Iorwerth.

Someone seized Guillaume's arm.

The French knight said sharply, "I yielded to him."

Ieuan heard that clearly.

The man holding Guillaume looked at Ieuan.

"Did he?"

Ieuan nodded.

"Aye."

"Your prisoner then."

The words meant almost nothing in the moment.

Ieuan turned toward Iorwerth.

"Is he alive?"

"For now."

Ieuan sat down where he stood.

His legs had stopped asking permission.

---

The battle did not end because Ieuan sat.

He was pulled back into duty.

Moved again.

Waited.

Fought in smaller fragments.

Helped where ordered.

Only later did the scale of what happened begin reaching him.

The French king captured.

King John himself.

Nobles taken.

An impossible victory, men said.

Ieuan was too tired to understand impossible.

He wanted water.

He wanted his helmet off.

He wanted to know whether Brân was alive.

He wanted Iorwerth not to die.

Tomas appeared near evening with Brân.

Ieuan almost hugged the horse.

He settled for putting his forehead briefly against the animal's neck.

"He pulled all day," Tomas said.

"Good."

"Good?"

"Means he's alive."

Tomas looked at the blood and dirt on Ieuan's armour.

"You?"

"Also."

Ieuan found Iorwerth.

Alive.

Badly wounded.

Angry at everyone.

That was promising.

Then he went to see Guillaume.

The French knight sat under guard, helmet removed.

Without it he looked younger.

Early thirties perhaps.

Hair stuck to his forehead. A cut marked one cheek. He looked exhausted rather than noble.

Ieuan stood in front of him.

Guillaume looked up.

"Your man?"

Ieuan understood.

"Alive."

Guillaume nodded.

"Good."

That surprised Ieuan.

"You tried to kill him."

Guillaume gave a tired shrug.

"Today."

Ieuan almost smiled.

"Your name again."

"Sir Guillaume de la Motte."

The title came with stubborn emphasis.

Ieuan nodded.

"Ieuan ap Dafydd."

Guillaume repeated it badly.

Ieuan let him.

A retainer approached and asked questions about Guillaume's standing and lands.

The Frenchman answered carefully.

Afterward the man looked at Ieuan with new interest.

"He may be worth something."

Ieuan looked at Guillaume.

The French knight looked back.

A human being.

A tired man with blood on his face.

A family somewhere perhaps waiting to learn if he was alive.

Worth something.

Ieuan thought of cows.

Grain.

Horses.

Everything war converted into value.

"How much?" he asked.

The retainer laughed.

"Now you sound like a soldier."

Ieuan did not feel like one.

He felt fourteen again, standing inside an empty house and deciding what he had the right to take.
