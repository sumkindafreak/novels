# Ieuan — Voice, Habits and Memory Continuity

This file locks the small behavioural details that should keep Ieuan recognisably the same person from fourteen-year-old plague survivor to twenty-one/twenty-two-year-old fighting man.

These are **C — character/story choices**, constrained by the historical world already built.

---

# 1. How Ieuan speaks

Ieuan is not naturally eloquent.

His speech tends to be:

- short;
- concrete;
- practical;
- more comfortable describing what something is doing than what he is feeling;
- defensive when embarrassed;
- occasionally dry in the way Dafydd was dry.

He should not sound like a modern introspective teenager translated into archaic vocabulary.

When frightened he often says **less**, not more.

When caught lying he does the opposite: he adds unnecessary practical detail.

### Example behaviour

A simple lie from Ieuan becomes suspicious because he explains:

- where he went;
- which path;
- why the ground was wet;
- what the cow did;

when nobody asked.

This is useful across the novel, including when hiding wet bedding later.

---

# 2. Internal viewpoint

Ieuan notices the world through work.

Before he notices that a man is wealthy, he may notice:

- the quality of his horse;
- whether leather is cracked;
- whether a servant is carrying too much;
- whether boots suit the ground;
- whether an animal has been brushed recently.

Before he describes weather poetically, he notices what it will do to:

- hay;
- tracks;
- fire;
- animals;
- visibility.

This gives the narration a consistent rural working perspective even as his social world expands.

---

# 3. Fear tells

Ieuan tries to hide fear, but his body gives it away.

Working tells:

- becomes very still before acting;
- checks where the nearest exit/path/clear ground lies;
- rubs the seam/edge of whatever he is holding — later often Dafydd's knife sheath;
- swallows before answering questions about himself;
- avoids eye contact when shame is involved, but holds eye contact too aggressively when challenged publicly.

He does not tremble theatrically every time he is frightened.

---

# 4. Anger

Ieuan's anger is usually **cold first, explosive second**.

He stores humiliation.

He is especially vulnerable to anger when somebody:

- laughs at something he cannot control;
- implies he is a child;
- dismisses his family/background;
- questions whether he belongs in a higher-status environment;
- handles Dafydd's knife without permission.

One long-term risk is that military confidence gives him better tools for acting on an anger he already had at fourteen.

---

# 5. Bed-wetting continuity

The wetting must not appear only when the plot wants to mention the premise.

## At fourteen

It occurs often enough to shape sleeping arrangements and shame — **several episodes in a bad week, fewer in a good one**.

Stress, exhaustion and unfamiliar sleeping places can make it worse, but the story does not claim a single cause.

## During solitary survival

He can hide it completely from other people, but managing bedding becomes his own responsibility.

## Early service

This is psychologically the hardest stage because he returns to shared sleeping spaces.

Behaviours:

- volunteers for edge-of-room/stable sleeping positions;
- wakes early;
- checks straw/blankets before anybody else rises;
- removes wet straw under the excuse of cleaning;
- avoids discussing how deeply he sleeps;
- worries constantly that smell will expose him.

## Later adolescence/adulthood

Frequency may reduce with age but **does not need to vanish completely**.

By the military years, occasional stress-associated accidents remain possible.

This matters because knighthood must not magically cure the private problem that shaped his childhood.

The narrative should use the detail sparingly enough to remain human rather than gimmicky, but consistently enough that it never feels forgotten.

---

# 6. Dafydd memories

Avoid constant sentimental flashbacks.

Use a few repeatable sensory/behaviour memories.

## The knife

Ieuan remembers Dafydd using the same knife for ridiculous ordinary things:

- cutting cord;
- trimming a damaged piece of leather;
- slicing food;
- scraping dirt from something that should probably not have been scraped with the food knife.

That ordinariness is the point.

## Animal lesson

Dafydd's basic teaching principle:

> Watch the beast before you move it.

Exact wording can change in final Welsh/English handling.

Ieuan later applies the same principle to horses — and eventually, without consciously noticing, to people and battle.

## Approval

One of Ieuan's strongest father memories is **not praise**.

It is a day Dafydd trusted him to bring stock back without supervision.

Ieuan later understands that trust was praise.

---

# 7. Angharad memories

Angharad is remembered through work and discretion.

## Wet bedding

She never gives a modern speech about self-esteem.

Her kindness is:

- noticing;
- removing wet straw;
- finding dry cloth;
- doing it before Gwenllian can make a fuss;
- saying almost nothing.

Later, when Ieuan cleans his own bedding before other servants wake, he unconsciously copies her efficiency.

## Hands

He remembers her hands more often than her face during grief:

- kneading/mixing;
- mending;
- wringing cloth;
- checking Gwenllian's hair;
- touching Dafydd's forehead during fever.

## Faith

Angharad is the parent whose ordinary faith most shapes Ieuan.

When he tries to pray years later and cannot find words, he often remembers the **rhythm** of hearing her pray rather than a perfect quotation.

---

# 8. Gwenllian memories

Gwenllian's memories should change with Ieuan's age.

At fourteen he remembers the immediate irritation:

- too many questions;
- touching his things;
- following when told not to;
- noticing everything embarrassing.

Later he begins remembering smaller things he did not value enough:

- how easily she laughed;
- how quickly she forgave an argument;
- the exact way she filled silence with nonsense;
- the fact that after becoming ill she stopped teasing him about the wetting.

## The grave

For years, guilt about Gwenllian is tied to one concrete thought:

> she should have been in the churchyard.

Whenever Ieuan sees an orderly Christian burial later, this can return without requiring a full flashback.

---

# 9. Food behaviour after starvation/fear

Ieuan does not become a caricature who hoards every crust forever.

But he develops habits:

- notices remaining food automatically;
- dislikes seeing edible food wasted;
- saves a small amount longer than necessary;
- eats quickly when uncertain whether more is coming;
- later has to learn the manners expected in higher-status households.

This creates subtle class friction without constant exposition.

---

# 10. Sleep

After 1349 Ieuan is a lighter sleeper around unfamiliar people.

He is unusually aware of:

- doors;
- animals becoming restless;
- coughing;
- somebody moving close to his bedding.

A cough in darkness can trigger disproportionate fear long after the first plague wave.

He knows intellectually only what medieval people know, but emotionally his body remembers fever entering a household.

---

# 11. Humour

Ieuan must retain humour or the character becomes exhausting.

His humour is:

- dry;
- occasional;
- practical;
- often funniest when he is annoyed.

Dafydd is the source of much of this style.

As Ieuan becomes comfortable with Rhys and later companions, humour is one of the first signs that he is living rather than merely surviving.

---

# 12. What never completely leaves him

At twenty-two, even if knighted, Ieuan still carries:

- automatic awareness of food;
- discomfort sleeping in crowded spaces;
- shame around wetting;
- sensitivity to coughing/fever;
- the habit of studying animals before touching them;
- dislike of having no useful task;
- fierce attachment to Dafydd's knife;
- guilt about Gwenllian's grave;
- difficulty believing high-status acceptance is permanent.

These are the threads that connect the last chapter to the first.
