# Working Family Death Chronology — 1349

This is the **planning chronology**, not final prose.

It uses the May 1349 outbreak lock from Pass One and modern plague biology only to prevent impossible timing. Characters themselves do not understand incubation, fleas, bacteria or disease forms.

## Core biological constraints

For bubonic plague, modern CDC guidance gives a typical incubation of roughly **2–8 days** before acute symptoms such as fever/chills/weakness and painful swollen lymph nodes.

The household sequence must therefore allow family members to have shared exposure at different times rather than implying that each person instantly 'catches it' by touching the previous victim.

Pneumonic complications can alter transmission, but they should not be inserted merely to make every death chain convenient.

---

# Sunday 12 April — Easter

**All four family members alive and apparently healthy.**

This is the last major feast the complete family shares.

Story purpose:

- show ordinary parish life at Ystradyfodwg;
- establish food, clothing, neighbours and family jokes;
- let the reader meet everyone before fear dominates them;
- no heavy-handed foreshadowing.

---

# Late April — rumours

Around the final week of April, stories arriving through Penrhys / the route from the south become harder to dismiss.

Rumours may include:

- deaths farther south;
- unusually rapid sickness;
- priests and household servants dying;
- fear in market settlements;
- contradictory explanations.

Dafydd is sceptical of exaggeration.
Angharad is more worried.
Ieuan is interested until adult anxiety makes the stories frightening.
Gwenllian asks inappropriate questions.

---

# 18–21 May — Rogation / Ascension week

**Working point at which the mortality becomes undeniably local.**

A neighbouring household or familiar person develops the characteristic fever/swelling.

This gives the family's fear a religious-calendar context without claiming that plague historically arrived on a feast day.

Potential visual:

A landscape/processional season traditionally concerned with fields, boundaries and divine protection collides with the first obvious sign that something is deeply wrong.

---

# 23–24 May — Dafydd becomes ill

Dafydd begins with:

- sudden exhaustion;
- fever/chills;
- pain he initially attempts to dismiss;
- then discovery of a painful swelling, probably groin or armpit.

He tries to continue working longer than he should because livestock and household obligations do not stop because he feels ill.

### Exposure logic

Do not identify a single cinematic 'infection moment'.

Dafydd may have been exposed days earlier through ordinary movement, animals, bedding/environment or contact with an infested place.

The family will search for moral/medical explanations that make sense to them; the narrator does not need to reveal an exact flea.

---

# 26–27 May — Dafydd dies

Dafydd deteriorates quickly.

Working death: **27 May 1349**.

The exact hour remains open.

### Emotional sequence

Ieuan initially believes his father will recover because Dafydd has always been physically reliable.

When that certainty breaks, Ieuan attempts to become useful:

- water;
- fire;
- errands;
- tending stock;
- doing whatever adults tell him.

None of those jobs fixes the problem.

### Burial

A Christian burial is attempted through the Ystradyfodwg parish system while it is still functioning, although attendance may already be reduced.

Ieuan does **not** inherit the knife in a ceremonial speech.

He simply finds/takes Dafydd's working knife among his things because somebody still needs a knife.

Its emotional weight comes later.

---

# 29–30 May — Angharad becomes ill

Angharad develops fever several days after Dafydd's sickness began.

This falls immediately before **Pentecost/Whitsunday, 31 May**.

She initially tries to conceal how bad she feels because Gwenllian is frightened and Ieuan is already attempting too much work.

### Exposure logic

Again, the story does not claim she simply contracted bubonic plague through caregiving contact.

She lived in the same household/environment and may have experienced shared or later exposure. If her symptoms eventually include respiratory signs, pneumonic progression can be considered, but only if medically and narratively justified.

---

# 31 May — Whitsunday

Angharad is too sick to attend church.

This is an important psychological marker for Ieuan because missing a major feast due to ordinary work is one thing; being unable to go because the household is collapsing is another.

Somebody may suggest Penrhys/Ffynnon Fair, prayer, a remedy or help from clergy.

Whether Ieuan actually makes a desperate journey for help remains a chapter-structure decision.

---

# 1–2 June — Angharad dies

Working death: **2 June 1349**.

By now burial capacity and neighbourly help are more fragile than they were for Dafydd only days earlier.

This difference should show the epidemic accelerating.

### What changes immediately

Ieuan is fourteen and now the oldest surviving person in the household.

He does not become 'the man of the house' in a triumphant sense.

He is simply the person Gwenllian looks at when she asks:

> What do we do now?

And he has no adult to ask.

---

# 2–3 June — Ieuan takes Gwenllian away

Ieuan decides the house is dangerous.

His logic is medieval/practical rather than germ-theory-based:

- everybody who sleeps here is dying;
- neighbours are dying;
- the air/place may be corrupted;
- they need distance;
- he knows an upland shelter/seasonal cattle site.

He takes:

- father's knife;
- food he can carry;
- blanket/clothing;
- vessel for water;
- fire-making materials if available;
- whatever small item Gwenllian refuses to leave.

They head toward familiar higher ground rather than randomly disappearing into wilderness.

---

# 5 June — Gwenllian becomes ill

For several days she appears healthy enough that Ieuan begins to believe leaving worked.

Then she becomes tired/cold/feverish.

The moment of recognition should be restrained.

He has already seen the disease twice.

When he finds the swelling, he does not need anybody to tell him what it means.

### Incubation logic

A symptom onset around 5 June remains compatible with an exposure during the final days at home in late May / early June.

Her later onset also explains why Ieuan genuinely believes for several days that he has saved her.

---

# 6–7 June — Gwenllian dies

Working death: **7 June 1349**, Trinity Sunday.

This date is intentionally powerful but remains **fictional**, not derived from a historical local death record.

If the symbolism feels too constructed during chapter outlining, move the death by a day or two. Medical plausibility matters more than liturgical neatness.

## The crucial difference from the parents

Ieuan had adults around him, however overwhelmed, when Dafydd and Angharad died.

With Gwenllian he is essentially alone.

He must:

- keep her warm;
- obtain water;
- listen to her fear;
- decide whether to move her or stay;
- face the possibility that seeking other people exposes them both to danger;
- remain with her when there is nothing useful left to do.

He cannot outsource the experience to adults.

This is the death that ends his childhood.

---

# After 7 June — alone

Ieuan is fourteen.

He has survived the immediate household outbreak.

He does not know whether he is:

- lucky;
- protected;
- cursed;
- simply waiting to become ill later.

For several days he should reasonably expect symptoms every time he feels tired, hot, cold or sore.

That fear is essential.

He does **not** instantly conclude he is immune.

---

# Bed-wetting during the deaths

The wetting must not disappear conveniently because the plot becomes serious.

Stress can make the issue more psychologically prominent.

Possible sequence:

- while Dafydd is sick, Ieuan is horrified by an accident because Angharad has no spare attention to hide it;
- after Angharad dies, he wakes wet and for the first time must deal with the bedding himself;
- while sheltering with Gwenllian, she notices but does **not** tease him;
- after Gwenllian dies, an accident becomes devastating because there is nobody left who knows his secret except him.

The condition may improve/change later with age, stress and lifestyle, but there is no magical overnight cure.

# Status

**Working chronological lock for Pass Two:**

- Easter together: 12 April
- local crisis clearly underway: 18–21 May
- Dafydd ill: 23–24 May
- Dafydd dies: 27 May
- Angharad ill: 29–30 May
- Whitsunday crisis: 31 May
- Angharad dies: 2 June
- siblings flee: 2–3 June
- Gwenllian ill: 5 June
- Gwenllian dies: 7 June

Dates may shift slightly during chapter architecture, but the biological intervals and emotional order should remain unless research requires revision.
