# Ieuan ap Dafydd

## Core identity

**Name:** Ieuan ap Dafydd  
**Age at opening:** 14  
**Birth:** c. 1335  
**Home:** fictional modest tenant holding in the Ystradyfodwg–Gelli orbit, Rhondda Fawr  
**Language:** Welsh first; very limited exposure to English/Anglo-Norman administrative speech at the opening  
**Religion:** ordinary medieval Latin-Christian parish life understood through Welsh speech and local custom  

## Physical impression

Ieuan should look like a working upland teenager, not a tiny modern schoolboy and not an adult warrior trapped in a child's body.

Working description:

- lean rather than muscular;
- wiry legs from steep ground and livestock work;
- hands already callused;
- dark brown hair, usually badly cut by household standards rather than fashionably styled;
- grey-brown or hazel eyes;
- a face that still looks unmistakably young when frightened or asleep;
- several small work scars on hands/shins by fourteen;
- clothing patched rather than ragged-for-effect.

No distinctive heroic birthmark, unusual eye colour or other destiny marker.

## Temperament before the plague

Ieuan is:

- observant;
- quick to notice weather and animal behaviour;
- stubborn;
- embarrassed easily when mocked;
- protective of Gwenllian even while finding her irritating;
- wary of strangers;
- not naturally eloquent;
- capable of lying badly when frightened;
- very sensitive to perceived humiliation;
- quietly competitive with his father.

He is **not** fearless.

His courage later in the novel grows from acting while afraid, not from losing fear.

## The bed-wetting

Ieuan has persistent nocturnal enuresis that has continued well beyond the age at which his family expected it to stop.

### Story rule

The novel never diagnoses the cause.

For modern plausibility it can be understood as persistent primary nocturnal enuresis with no single identified cause, but no modern label enters the prose.

### What it means to Ieuan

At fourteen it is his deepest private shame.

He believes:

- he is too old for it;
- other boys would destroy him socially if they knew;
- his father is disappointed by it;
- his mother pities him;
- his sister knows too much about him;
- prayer should have stopped it by now if he were better or more deserving.

Those beliefs are not necessarily what his family actually thinks.

### Practical household consequences

Because bedding, cloth, drying time and clean straw all cost labour, accidents create real household work.

The current home reconstruction gives Ieuan a **separate pallet and sleeping corner near the outer/service side of the dwelling rather than in the tightest family sleeping cluster**. When weather permits, damp/soiled bedding is removed quickly and his straw is changed more often than the family's other bedding.

If later architectural work supports a small adjacent service/storage shelter, he may sometimes be moved there after repeated accidents, but this must remain a defensible reconstruction rather than a plot-built isolation chamber.

### Plague rule

His wetting does not make urine medicinal and does not make him immune.

It merely changes:

- where he sleeps;
- who shares bedding/space with him;
- how often his bedding/straw is disturbed and replaced;
- his proximity to the family's tight sleeping cluster.

Whether any of that actually saves him remains unknowable inside the story.

## Skills at fourteen

Ieuan can plausibly already:

- move and watch cattle;
- recognise several animals individually by behaviour;
- find local streams/springs and judge routes in poor weather;
- climb steep ground safely;
- collect and stack fuel;
- help cut, turn and carry hay;
- help with simple fences/banks/enclosures;
- carry water;
- use a small knife safely for ordinary work;
- start and tend a domestic fire;
- recognise spoiled versus usable stored food reasonably well;
- help with grain/pulse preparation;
- navigate between home, Ystradyfodwg, upland grazing and familiar ridge routes;
- spend a night in a rough upland shelter without treating it as an expedition.

He cannot yet:

- fight expertly;
- use a sword;
- ride a trained warhorse;
- hunt like a nobleman;
- read fluently;
- understand wider politics;
- navigate unfamiliar country;
- magically identify every edible wild plant.

## Relationship with work

Ieuan complains about work because he is fourteen.

But work is also how he understands his place in the family.

Dafydd trusting him to bring stock home alone matters more to him than praise.

After the plague, the first frightening sign that he is truly alone is not silence.

It is realising that **nobody has told him what job comes next**.

## Relationship with God

At the opening Ieuan's faith is ordinary and mostly unexamined.

He prays because people pray.
He confesses because Christians confess.
He knows feast days through family/community rhythm.
He believes saints, sin, grace, punishment, mercy and the afterlife are real categories.

The plague does not instantly make him atheist or zealot.

Instead it makes him ask increasingly unbearable questions inside the religious framework he already possesses.

The most important early question is:

> Why did God spare the shameful thing in the family?

## Fear hierarchy at fourteen

Before the plague, Ieuan fears:

1. somebody outside the family discovering the wetting;
2. disappointing Dafydd;
3. supernatural judgement / dying unshriven;
4. getting lost on the high ground in bad weather;
5. violent strangers;
6. serious animal injury.

By the end of the plague movement, that hierarchy has been completely rewritten.

## Objects

### Father's working knife

After Dafydd dies, Ieuan takes his father's ordinary utility knife.

It is:

- practical;
- worn;
- not a fighting dagger;
- valuable because iron and good tools matter;
- emotionally invaluable because Dafydd's hand shaped its wear.

Ieuan keeps it through the whole novel if continuity allows.

It becomes a quiet counterpoint to the expensive weapons he later handles in military service.

## Core flaws

Ieuan's survival should not turn him into a flawless prodigy.

Major flaws:

- shame makes him secretive;
- he can be spiteful when humiliated;
- he overestimates what he can carry/manage alone;
- he hates accepting charity;
- grief makes him emotionally withdraw rather than speak;
- later success can produce a dangerous belief that survival proves he is somehow harder or more chosen than other men;
- he remembers insults for years.

## Long arc

### 1349 — boy

Ieuan begins as a useful but dependent son whose world extends only a short distance beyond Glynrhondda.

### Plague — survivor

He loses the structures that made his skills meaningful and has to turn ordinary work into survival.

### Post-plague — servant

He learns that surviving alone and living among powerful strangers are different skills.

### Military household — squire/esquire trajectory

His strengths become:

- animal handling;
- endurance;
- observation;
- reliability under discomfort;
- rapid practical learning;
- unusual calm around death.

His weaknesses remain:

- shame;
- class insecurity;
- anger;
- fear of being exposed as somebody who does not belong.

### 1356 — man

At Poitiers he is not fearless and not untouched by what happened in 1349.

He is effective precisely because terror no longer automatically stops him moving.

Knighthood, if achieved, should not cure his grief or erase the boy from the first chapters.
