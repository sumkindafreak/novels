# Opening Community

These characters give the family a social world before the pestilence isolates Ieuan.

They are fictional unless explicitly marked otherwise.

## Design rule

The community should remain **small enough to feel rural and dispersed**. Do not turn Ystradyfodwg into a bustling fantasy village.

The boy knows people because:

- they share church life;
- holdings lie along familiar routes;
- stock/work brings families together;
- people exchange labour, news and obligations;
- Penrhys and Llantrisant connect the area to strangers and authority.

---

# Madog ap Rhys

**Age:** about 50  
**Role:** neighbouring tenant / experienced cattle man

Madog is the nearest adult outside the family whom Ieuan respects.

Traits:

- speaks slowly;
- notices everything;
- has lost teeth and makes no attempt to hide it;
- knows upland routes exceptionally well;
- distrusts dramatic rumours;
- thinks Ieuan is competent but too proud.

Story use:

- establishes ordinary neighbourly help before plague;
- can be one of the first households affected, forcing Ieuan to understand the danger is local;
- his abandoned cattle later confront Ieuan with the moral/practical problem of taking another man's property after death.

---

# Nest ferch Hywel

**Age:** early 40s  
**Role:** Madog's wife / neighbour

Nest is more socially direct than Madog and one of Angharad's closest local relationships.

Traits:

- blunt;
- capable organiser;
- remembers every favour owed;
- brings news from church before anyone else;
- not easily frightened until she has evidence in front of her.

Story use:

Her household provides a contrast with Ieuan's: similar status, different dynamics.

If plague reaches Madog's holding first, Nest may become the first familiar adult Ieuan sees transformed by terror.

---

# Gruffudd ap Ieuan

**Age:** 15  
**Role:** local boy / occasional rival

Gruffudd is exactly the sort of person Ieuan is terrified will discover his bed-wetting.

He is not a villain.

Traits:

- stronger than Ieuan;
- louder;
- good with sheep, less patient with cattle;
- enjoys showing off around other boys;
- has mocked Ieuan for smaller things before;
- would probably be horrified rather than delighted by the true scale of the plague.

Story use:

The reader initially expects Gruffudd to become a bullying problem.

Then the pestilence renders ordinary adolescent rivalries absurd.

His fate should be decided later; death is not mandatory merely because he exists in the opening.

---

# Elen ferch Madog

**Age:** 13  
**Role:** neighbour's daughter / contemporary

Elen is not automatically a childhood love interest.

She exists to widen the social world and show how a girl of roughly Ieuan's age may have equally serious household responsibilities.

Traits:

- sharper-tongued than Ieuan;
- excellent memory;
- openly unimpressed by Gruffudd's showing off;
- good at dairy/food work but also accustomed to livestock tasks;
- knows when Ieuan is hiding something but not what.

Story use:

Elen gives Ieuan someone his own age with whom conversation does not become a contest of strength.

Whether she survives the plague remains open.

---

# The Ystradyfodwg priest

**Name:** deliberately not locked yet  
**Status:** fictionalised local priest/chaplain unless a defensible historical name is recovered

Character rule:

He must not be:

- a plague-denying fool;
- a secret modern scientist;
- a sadistic fanatic;
- an all-knowing holy man.

He is an educated cleric working inside the explanatory system of his century.

Plausible qualities:

- knows the families in his parochial area;
- conducts/assists baptism, marriage, confession, Eucharistic life and burial;
- worries about sin and proper burial;
- becomes exhausted as deaths multiply;
- understands that he is personally at risk even if he does not understand microorganisms.

He should have at least one ordinary scene with Ieuan before the plague so later church breakdown is personal.

---

# Penrhys religious figure

**Status:** optional fictional chaplain/brother/keeper associated with the grange or chapel

Do not write a full monastery at Penrhys unless research supports that exact arrangement in 1349.

This figure can:

- receive travellers;
- know rumours before Ystradyfodwg households do;
- interpret the mortality through prayer, penance and providence;
- become part of Angharad's desire to seek help when the family is threatened.

---

# First outsider carrying the rumour

This should probably be a **fictional traveller**, not a famous historical person.

Possible type:

- packman;
- servant travelling from Llantrisant/Cardiff direction;
- pilgrim/supplicant passing Penrhys;
- drover or messenger.

He does not arrive shouting 'the Black Death is coming'.

He brings fragmented information:

- houses where everyone has died;
- burials without enough men to dig;
- servants leaving masters;
- priests dying;
- markets disturbed;
- contradictory explanations.

His story initially sounds exaggerated.

That is important.

---

# Community destruction rule

Do not kill every named character on the same page.

The horror should spread unevenly:

- one household falls silent;
- another person survives;
- someone flees;
- someone steals;
- somebody refuses to leave family;
- somebody disappears and is never confirmed dead.

Uncertainty is more believable and more frightening than a perfectly complete death list.

## Evidence grading

All named community characters are **C — fiction**, placed within an **A/B-supported** settlement, parish and pastoral framework.
