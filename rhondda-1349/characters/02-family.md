# Ieuan's Family

The family must feel complete before the pestilence enters the household.

Their deaths are not character descriptions. Each person needs habits, work, irritation, humour and relationships that continue to exist in Ieuan's memory long after the reader loses them.

---

# Dafydd ap Hywel

**Role:** father  
**Age:** approximately 38  
**Birth:** c. 1311  
**Status:** modest Welsh tenant household head in Glynrhondda

## Work

Dafydd's competence centres on livestock and the holding.

He is responsible for or heavily involved in:

- cattle management;
- seasonal grazing decisions;
- hay/fodder planning;
- boundaries and simple structural repairs;
- negotiating household obligations/rent with people above the family;
- slaughter/butchery when necessary;
- tools and transport;
- teaching Ieuan practical work.

He is not a specialist knight, hunter, blacksmith or woodsman.

## Personality

Dafydd is:

- practical;
- sparing with praise;
- dryly funny;
- physically affectionate only occasionally;
- deeply uncomfortable when he cannot solve something;
- respectful of Church practice without being especially mystical;
- suspicious of easy cures and travelling miracle claims;
- proud of Ieuan's competence while worrying about the boy's stubbornness.

## Dafydd and Ieuan

Their relationship has friction because Ieuan is fourteen and wants to be treated as more capable than he is.

Dafydd tends to show approval by **giving responsibility**.

Examples:

- trusting Ieuan to fetch cattle without him;
- handing him a better tool;
- asking what he thinks the weather will do;
- letting him handle a difficult animal first.

Ieuan often mistakes the absence of spoken praise for disappointment.

### The wetting

Dafydd is frustrated by the problem because:

- it causes work;
- the family cannot waste bedding indefinitely;
- he does not understand why it persists;
- he worries Ieuan will be ridiculed if other boys learn.

He has occasionally spoken too sharply after an accident and regretted it.

But he does **not** beat or terrorise Ieuan over it.

This matters because after Dafydd dies, Ieuan remembers both the sharp words and the quieter acts of protection.

## Father's knife

Dafydd owns the worn utility knife that Ieuan later keeps.

It should appear naturally in pre-plague chapters repeatedly:

- cutting cord;
- trimming wood;
- food preparation;
- animal work;
- scraping something clean.

Only later does it become a relic.

# Angharad ferch Madog

**Role:** mother  
**Age:** approximately 34  
**Birth:** c. 1315

## Work

Angharad's work is essential to household survival.

Her responsibilities can plausibly include:

- food preparation and storage;
- dairying;
- poultry/eggs;
- washing and bedding;
- spinning/textile work;
- garden or small cultivated household crops;
- helping with harvest/hay at peak labour times;
- caring for sick household members;
- keeping track of stored food more closely than anyone else.

She is not written as the household's magical herbal witch.

She knows ordinary remedies, food knowledge and inherited practice appropriate to her world, but cannot cure plague.

## Personality

Angharad is:

- quicker to laugh than Dafydd;
- perceptive about people's moods;
- capable of being cutting when annoyed;
- materially cautious;
- more willing than Dafydd to hear stories from travellers;
- strongly attached to parish and Marian devotion;
- protective without being soft.

She is the person most likely to understand exactly how frightened Ieuan is while pretending not to notice.

## Angharad and Ieuan

She helps manage the wetting discreetly.

Important rule: she does not endlessly reassure him with modern therapeutic dialogue.

Her kindness is practical:

- removing bedding before Gwenllian can make a fuss;
- finding dry straw;
- putting wet cloth where strangers will not see it;
- waking him early if she suspects an accident so he can deal with it privately;
- refusing to let Dafydd discuss it where another household can hear.

This practicality is why her death creates an immediate terror beyond grief:

**the one person who quietly managed his shame is gone.**

## Angharad and faith

Angharad is the most natural family link to Penrhys/Ffynnon Fair.

When the mortality approaches, she can become more willing to seek prayer or sacred help than Dafydd.

The narrative should treat that belief seriously, not mock it from a modern viewpoint.

# Gwenllian ferch Dafydd

**Role:** little sister  
**Age:** 8  
**Birth:** c. 1341

## Why eight works

At eight Gwenllian is:

- old enough to remember everything happening;
- old enough to walk significant distances with help/rest;
- capable of useful small jobs;
- young enough to depend overwhelmingly on Ieuan after the parents die;
- still recognisably a child rather than a miniature adult.

## Personality

Gwenllian is:

- talkative;
- nosy;
- observant in a completely different way from Ieuan;
- frightened of cattle larger than she is but unwilling to admit it;
- fascinated by travellers and stories;
- prone to asking questions adults want her to stop asking;
- capable of unexpectedly cruel little-sister teasing followed five minutes later by absolute devotion.

## Gwenllian and Ieuan

They annoy each other constantly.

She knows about his wetting because hiding something inside a cramped household from an eight-year-old sibling is nearly impossible.

She has teased him about it before.

But there is a hard family rule:

**she never tells anybody outside the household.**

That is one of the first ways the reader sees that her loyalty is deeper than her teasing.

After their parents die, their relationship changes violently.

Ieuan becomes:

- brother;
- food provider;
- route finder;
- authority;
- comfort;
- the nearest thing she has left to a parent.

He is completely unprepared for that role.

## Her importance after death

Gwenllian must remain present in Ieuan's internal life.

Her death is not the last time she matters.

Specific memories can recur:

- a phrase she always used;
- the way she hated an ordinary food;
- a nonsense game on the path to church;
- asking him to tell the same story again;
- protecting his wetting secret;
- holding his hand once she becomes ill even though both know she is 'too old' to need to.

When Ieuan eventually enters a noble household, encountering another child around Gwenllian's age should be unexpectedly difficult for him.

# Family dynamics before plague

## At meals

Dafydd tends to speak about work.
Angharad notices who has eaten what.
Gwenllian interrupts.
Ieuan tries to appear older than he is.

## At church

Angharad is attentive.
Dafydd behaves properly.
Gwenllian gets restless.
Ieuan worries somebody will notice anything unusual about him.

## During work

Dafydd and Ieuan pair naturally for heavier outdoor tasks.
Angharad and Gwenllian often work around the holding but roles overlap during hay/harvest pressure.

## Family humour

The family needs jokes the reader sees before catastrophe.

Working examples:

- Dafydd claims Gwenllian can frighten rain away by talking until the clouds flee;
- Angharad mocks Dafydd's inability to find an object placed directly in front of him;
- Gwenllian insists one particular cow hates Ieuan personally;
- Ieuan and Dafydd compete silently over who can finish an unpleasant job first.

The humour should be modest and domestic, not modern sitcom banter.

# What each person leaves Ieuan

## Dafydd

- working knife;
- practical competence;
- habit of looking at a problem before panicking;
- unresolved need for paternal approval.

## Angharad

- knowledge of food/storage/domestic survival;
- religious habits;
- the memory of being protected without being made to feel helpless;
- his deepest association between home and safety.

## Gwenllian

- the emotional reason the first survival movement continues;
- guilt;
- tenderness he later tries to hide;
- proof that survival is not the same thing as deserving to live.
