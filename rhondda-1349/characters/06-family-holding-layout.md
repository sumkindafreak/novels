# The Family Holding — Pass Two Story Lock

This file turns the cautious Pass One house reconstruction into the **specific fictional home** used by Ieuan's family.

The exact holding is fictional. Its materials, landscape and functions are designed around the documented/strongly reconstructed Ystradyfodwg–Gelli and South-East Welsh evidence.

## Working internal name

**Hendre Gelli** — *working story-bible name only*.

This is not being claimed as a documented 1349 farm or medieval place-name. It simply means the family's permanent/lower holding in the Gelli landscape and may be renamed before prose drafting.

---

# 1. Position

The holding sits on the **western side of the Rhondda Fawr in the Ystradyfodwg–Gelli orbit**, on rising ground above the better river-terrace land and below the higher Mynydd Gelli pastoral landscape.

Story geography:

- close enough to Ystradyfodwg church for ordinary parish life;
- low enough for permanent occupation and small-scale cultivation;
- high enough to avoid placing the dwelling directly on the wettest river ground;
- connected by familiar paths to Mynydd Gelli summer grazing;
- Penrhys is a known ridge destination rather than the family's immediate doorstep.

No exact modern coordinate is assigned.

---

# 2. Main dwelling

## Working footprint

Approximately **9–11 m long by 4.5–5.5 m wide**.

These dimensions are **C — fiction**, chosen to fit the scale of a modest household and remain within the general range of known medieval upland rectangular buildings. Do not present them as an excavated Ystradyfodwg house.

## Construction vocabulary

Strong period/regional reconstruction:

- substantial low walling using turf/stone/clay/timber as locally practical;
- timber roof structure;
- steep thatch;
- open hearth;
- earth floor with limited stone/slab hard-standing;
- small shuttered openings;
- no domestic glass;
- no chimney fireplace;
- drainage deliberately managed because of slope and rainfall.

---

# 3. Interior zones

The family does **not** have modern rooms.

## Hearth / work end

The open hearth is the practical centre of the household.

Used for:

- cooking;
- warming;
- drying clothing;
- evening work;
- conversation;
- light after dark.

A pot can hang or stand at the fire according to final scene needs. Smoke blackens the roof structure and upper interior.

## Main sleeping zone

Dafydd, Angharad and Gwenllian sleep close together in the warmer main domestic space.

Their bedding consists of modest straw/plant material, woollen coverings and whatever cloth the household can afford.

They do not own three ornate wooden beds.

## Storage

A chest and raised/drier storage area hold the family's most valuable cloth and small possessions.

Dry grain/food storage should not be placed directly beside Ieuan's sleeping bay because that would unnecessarily undermine the altered-exposure premise through obvious rodent attraction.

---

# 4. Ieuan's 'little end'

This is now the **working story lock** for the bed-wetting arrangement.

Attached to or forming the downhill service end of the dwelling is a rougher, lightly separated work/storage bay.

It is not a bedroom in the modern sense.

Characteristics:

- separated from the main family sleeping group by a partial timber/wattle division or service threshold;
- colder and more draughty;
- close to an outer/service doorway;
- originally intended for tools, baskets, work materials and occasional sleeping;
- no bulk grain storage;
- Ieuan has a straw pallet and one or two coverings there;
- his wet straw can be removed without stripping the family's shared bedding;
- his bedding is replaced/turned more often than the others'.

The family informally calls it **the little end** or equivalent natural Welsh phrase in dialogue once language handling is finalised.

## Emotional meaning

Dafydd's decision to move Ieuan there is practical and frustrated, not sadistic.

Ieuan experiences it as:

- proof that something is wrong with him;
- being pushed out of the family;
- childish humiliation at exactly the age he wants adult status.

Angharad treats it more neutrally and continues to help with washing/drying when needed.

Gwenllian knows exactly why he sleeps there.

## Plague meaning

The separate position, different bedding turnover and reduced close sleeping contact may **change exposure**.

Nothing guarantees safety.

The house is still one domestic complex, and Ieuan remains exposed to the same wider environment.

---

# 5. Yard and outbuildings

Keep the holding modest.

Working features:

## Small yard

Used for:

- animal handling;
- tools;
- drying/repair;
- manure and mud in the appropriate places;
- household work that should not happen inside.

## Simple livestock shelter / pen

Cattle and other animals are close enough to dominate daily life, but the current story lock does **not** require the principal cattle to sleep under the same roof as the family.

A modest adjacent shelter/pen keeps the architecture safer and makes Ieuan's 'little end' a human service space rather than a magical animal loft.

## Poultry

A small number of chickens are plausible and useful for later abandonment scenes.

## Pig

Optional but plausible. If included, only one or a very small number; the family is not a large commercial farm.

## Fuel / hay / fodder

Stored with the constant risk of damp/fire in mind. Exact building form remains flexible.

# 6. Water

The family uses a reliable nearby spring/stream source rather than carrying every drop from the main river.

Exact source is fictional.

Ieuan knows:

- the quickest route;
- where the footing is bad after rain;
- where animals muddy the water;
- how much water a household and livestock consume.

This becomes survival knowledge after the deaths.

---

# 7. Household economy

Working family resources before plague:

- **2–3 cattle**, including at least one milk cow;
- a few sheep or access/responsibility for sheep depending on tenancy;
- several chickens;
- possible single pig;
- small cultivated plots / grain and pulses obtained through own work, dues/exchange and local economy;
- access or customary right/responsibility to use higher summer grazing;
- hay/fodder needs that make June work important;
- very limited cash.

Exact legal ownership versus tenancy/customary rights remains deliberately blurred where the documentary evidence is insufficient.

---

# 8. Key possessions

The house should feel valuable to Ieuan because it contains **few things, each with a purpose**.

Core recurring objects:

- **Dafydd's work knife** — the most important long-term object;
- one better glazed jug, valued because it is nicer than everyday coarse ware;
- cooking vessel;
- wooden bowls/spoons;
- baskets/sacks;
- rope/cord;
- whetstone;
- small axe/hatchet or similar tool;
- agricultural implements too large or specialised for Ieuan to carry;
- chest containing cloth and small valuables;
- blankets;
- straw bedding;
- simple religious object only if later research/story needs justify it.

Do not give the family decorative clutter merely to signal 'medieval'.

---

# 9. The upland refuge

The family's summer cattle shelter remains separate from the permanent holding.

It lies higher toward **Mynydd Gelli / related upland pasture**.

It is rougher than the home:

- platform cut into slope;
- turf/stone walling;
- thatched timber roof;
- hearth;
- minimal sleeping place;
- crude storage;
- nearby water;
- cattle working area/enclosure.

Ieuan has spent enough time there before 1349 that reaching it in fear or poor visibility is plausible.

This is where he takes Gwenllian after Angharad's death and where his first solitary survival period centres.

---

# 10. House after plague

The family holding does not become Ieuan's comfortable apocalypse base.

After returning in June:

- the smell and memory of Angharad make the main dwelling unbearable;
- he can retrieve supplies but cannot emotionally live in the main space;
- the little end is equally loaded with shame and childhood memory;
- he increasingly bases himself at the upland shelter despite its roughness.

This prevents the survival section from becoming too easy simply because a stocked house exists downhill.
