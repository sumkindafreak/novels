# Transition Out of Isolation — First Door Into Service

This file designs the person and circumstance that eventually pull Ieuan out of solitary plague survival.

It deliberately avoids the cliché of a knight discovering a gifted orphan and immediately making him a squire.

---

# Historical logic

Welsh Black Death records show:

- tenements left vacant after tenants died;
- lands reverting into lords' hands where tenants/heirs could not continue;
- rents and services collapsing;
- holdings being transferred/relet;
- administrative officials continuing to account for land, dues and estate income after the mortality.

Manorial/ministerial administration did not simply vanish because plague occurred.

Therefore it is plausible that, once immediate mortality begins to ease enough for movement, **estate servants/local officials inspect damaged holdings, identify surviving tenants and livestock, and try to restore some order**.

The exact visit described below is **C — fiction**, built on that administrative reality.

---

# Rhys ap Llywelyn

**Age:** about 32–36  
**Role:** fictional livestock/horse servant attached to the wider Llantrisant estate-administration world

Rhys is **not a knight**.

He is the first important person from outside Ieuan's childhood world who recognises that the boy is useful.

## Status

Rhys works somewhere between practical estate servant and trusted stock/transport man.

Possible responsibilities:

- moving cattle;
- arranging pack animals;
- checking estate livestock;
- carrying instructions for a bailiff/reeve/other officer;
- accompanying men assessing occupied and deserted holdings;
- travelling between Llantrisant and upland districts.

Do not give him a grand title unless Pass Three research supports one.

He can simply be a man trusted to get animals and people from one place to another.

---

# Character

Rhys should feel like a working man who has survived a catastrophe, not a fantasy mentor.

Traits:

- economical with words;
- very good with horses and pack animals;
- impatient with people who romanticise them;
- can read people better than Ieuan can;
- not especially gentle, but not cruel;
- views food, animals, time and labour in practical terms;
- has lost people of his own during the mortality, but does not immediately tell Ieuan the details.

He does **not** think Ieuan is destined for greatness.

His first assessment is closer to:

> the boy knows cattle, is still alive, and can work.

That is enough.

---

# First sight — early July 1349

Rhys first appears with **one or two other men**, not alone.

They have come into the damaged Glynrhondda settlement landscape to establish basic facts:

- which holdings remain occupied;
- who claims which animals;
- whether tenants/heirs survive;
- where stock has strayed;
- what property may need reporting to an estate officer;
- which routes are safe/passable.

They may carry pack animals or horses.

## Ieuan's response

Ieuan initially hides/watches them.

By now, strangers are potentially dangerous.

He also fears they may take:

- the cow he has been managing;
- food he has scavenged;
- his family's holding;
- him, through some authority he does not understand.

This encounter should remind the reader that Ieuan is still fourteen and has almost no legal power.

---

# The first conversation

Rhys does not say:

> Come with me, boy. You could be a knight.

He asks practical questions:

- Who was your father?
- Who survives in the holding?
- Whose cow is that?
- Where are the other cattle?
- Is there anyone at the next holding?
- Which track reaches the upper grazing?

Ieuan can answer most of them.

That gets Rhys's attention.

## What Rhys notices

Ieuan:

- knows the local paths better than the visiting men;
- knows which animals belonged to which household;
- can handle cattle;
- has kept one cow alive and productive;
- knows where abandoned stock is likely to wander;
- can identify several deserted holdings;
- does not panic around working animals.

These are **labour skills**, not heroic qualities.

---

# First offer: temporary work, not adoption

Rhys needs help gathering/driving stock and navigating between upland holdings.

He offers Ieuan:

- food while working;
- perhaps a small payment or agreed share/allowance depending on final economic research;
- protection of travelling with several adults;
- temporary work for a few days.

Ieuan's first answer should probably be **no**.

Reasons:

- he does not want to leave Gwenllian's grave;
- he distrusts authority;
- he believes leaving means surrendering his family's home;
- he fears somebody will discover his bed-wetting;
- the upland refuge has become his one controllable place.

Rhys does not beg him.

He leaves him with information such as where the men will be next or when they return.

---

# Why Ieuan eventually accepts

The decision must come from practical pressure rather than sudden ambition.

By mid/late July Ieuan understands:

- he cannot preserve the full holding alone;
- winter is coming eventually;
- grain harvest will require labour he does not have;
- lone survival makes every injury dangerous;
- other survivors may simply take what he cannot defend;
- the old community will not restore itself because he waits long enough.

The temporary work therefore becomes attractive because it gives him:

- reliable meals;
- adult company without emotional intimacy;
- movement;
- a reason to use skills he already has;
- access to information about the wider world.

He tells himself it is only temporary.

That is important.

---

# Route toward horses

Ieuan begins with cattle, not cavalry.

Rhys eventually notices that Ieuan:

- stays calm around frightened animals;
- understands pressure, space and herd movement;
- pays attention to an animal before forcing it.

These skills transfer imperfectly to pack animals/horses.

Ieuan initially knows **far less about horses than Rhys does**.

This creates a natural apprenticeship:

1. cattle/route helper;
2. handling pack animals;
3. basic stable work;
4. grooming, feeding, tack and hoof awareness;
5. riding competence develops later;
6. only much later does horse skill become militarily useful.

This is far more credible than giving a poor fourteen-year-old instant knightly horsemanship.

---

# Connection to Llantrisant

Rhys's work ultimately draws Ieuan toward **Llantrisant**, the administrative centre already established in Pass One.

This is the first major expansion of Ieuan's world.

The movement should feel huge to him even though modern readers know the geographic distance is modest.

At Llantrisant he encounters:

- more survivors;
- officials;
- different accents/language balance;
- more horses;
- commerce;
- labour shortage;
- people trying to rebuild routines while the mortality is still recent.

This is where solitary survival begins turning into **service**.

---

# Rhys's long-term role

Rhys should matter, but not replace Ieuan's father.

Possible arc:

- teaches him horses and disciplined work;
- occasionally protects him because losing a useful worker is stupid, not because he is sentimental;
- calls out Ieuan's pride;
- eventually recommends him upward when Ieuan becomes too useful for basic work;
- may remain part of the story for several years or exit naturally when Ieuan moves into another household/military layer.

Do not make Rhys secretly a master swordsman who teaches Ieuan everything.

His gift to Ieuan is **entry into a network**.

That is enough.

---

# Bed-wetting in the transition

Ieuan's biggest private fear about joining other workers is sleeping near them.

This gives the decision extra weight.

At the upland shelter he can hide accidents.

In a shared stable/service environment he cannot.

Possible early service behaviour:

- volunteers for awkward sleeping spots;
- sleeps close to animals or doors under the excuse of watching them;
- wakes before others to inspect bedding;
- hides wet straw;
- becomes obsessive about how much he drinks late in the day even though this does not reliably solve the problem;
- interprets teasing about unrelated smells/straw as discovery.

The problem follows him into his new life instead of disappearing conveniently.

---

# Pass Two endpoint

Pass Two should end when Ieuan makes the decision:

> **he will go with Rhys for work.**

Not forever.

Not to become a knight.

Just for food, company, and because he has finally understood that staying alone on Mynydd Gelli is another way of dying slowly.

That gives Pass Three a clean starting point:

**plague orphan → working servant in the Llantrisant/Glamorgan administrative world.**

---

# Evidence notes

### A/B historical foundation

- Welsh ministers' accounts/court records show land in lords' hands after tenant deaths and lack of heirs/tenants.
- Post-plague estates continued accounting, re-letting and transferring holdings.
- Manorial administration used practical officials/servants such as stewards, bailiffs, reeves and other local officeholders; exact Glamorgan titles/functions must be checked before prose uses them.

### C fiction

- Rhys ap Llywelyn;
- his precise job;
- his visit to Glynrhondda;
- the first work offer;
- the route by which Ieuan enters service.

The fiction is designed to follow documented economic/administrative pressures rather than bypass them.
