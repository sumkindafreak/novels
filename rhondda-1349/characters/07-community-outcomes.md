# Opening Community — Plague Outcomes

This file decides what happens to the fictional people introduced in `03-community.md` during the first outbreak.

The guiding rule is **uneven destruction**.

Ieuan should not receive a neat list of the dead. In a dispersed rural community during mass mortality, some people die, some flee, some vanish into kin networks, and some remain alive without Ieuan knowing it.

---

# Madog ap Rhys — dies early

**Age:** about 50  
**Outcome:** **dead, c. 20–21 May 1349**

Madog is the first person Ieuan knows well whose death makes the pestilence undeniable.

## Sequence

- Madog is seen ill several days before Dafydd.
- His household initially hopes it is an ordinary fever.
- His deterioration is fast enough to terrify Dafydd and Angharad.
- News of his death reaches the family around 20–21 May.

This gives Dafydd only a very short interval between recognising the threat and developing symptoms himself.

## Later importance

Madog's holding is one of the first abandoned places Ieuan enters after Gwenllian's death.

His cattle/property create the moral problem:

> Is taking food from a dead neighbour theft, rescue, or simply what survival has become?

Madog's ordinary relationship with Ieuan before death is essential. Do not introduce him merely to kill him.

---

# Nest ferch Hywel — flees

**Age:** early 40s  
**Outcome:** **leaves Glynrhondda; fate unknown to Ieuan**

After Madog dies, Nest decides remaining beside the holding is more dangerous than travelling.

She gathers what she can and leaves with Elen, probably seeking kin or greater security farther down-valley/toward the Llantrisant world.

## Important rule

The novel does **not** immediately tell the reader whether Nest lives or dies.

Ieuan later finds their holding deserted.

That is all he knows.

A later reappearance remains possible but is not required.

---

# Elen ferch Madog — disappears with Nest

**Age:** 13  
**Outcome:** **unknown**

Elen leaves with her mother after Madog's death.

This prevents every adolescent contemporary from becoming a corpse and leaves open the possibility that another child from Ieuan's old life survives by a completely different route.

## Story value

Before plague, Elen is one of the few people Ieuan's age with whom he can speak without competition.

After plague, her absence creates a question rather than closure.

Do not resolve her fate simply to make Ieuan sadder.

If she returns later, it must serve the larger novel rather than nostalgia.

---

# Gruffudd ap Ieuan — away when the worst arrives

**Age:** 15  
**Outcome:** **missing / likely survives the immediate local outbreak**

Working choice:

Gruffudd has gone down-valley with an adult relative/father on livestock or transport business shortly before the local mortality becomes catastrophic.

He therefore disappears from Ieuan's immediate world before Ieuan learns whether he is safe.

## Why keep him alive?

Gruffudd represents the ordinary adolescent life Ieuan expected to have:

- rivalry;
- showing off;
- work;
- eventual adulthood in the same regional world.

Keeping his fate open lets the novel potentially confront Ieuan later with somebody who remembers **the boy before the plague**.

No later reunion is locked yet.

---

# Meurig ap Cynan — fictional Ystradyfodwg priest

**Age:** about 45–55  
**Status:** fictional cleric, because no securely identified 1349 Ystradyfodwg priest has yet been recovered.

**Outcome:** **disappears during the outbreak; Ieuan assumes he died**

## Character

Meurig is not a saintly oracle or plague-denying fool.

Before the outbreak he is simply part of parish life:

- knows the family;
- has corrected Ieuan for inattentiveness;
- has spoken kindly to Angharad;
- has performed ordinary religious duties for people Ieuan knows.

## During the mortality

He continues working:

- confession;
- Eucharistic care;
- prayers for the dying;
- burial;
- organising what can still be organised.

He becomes exhausted.

Dafydd receives as proper a burial as circumstances still permit, with Meurig involved or present.

By Angharad's death on 2 June, Meurig is unavailable — either ill, dead, fled under instruction, or attending another crisis.

Ieuan never sees his body.

## Story effect

When Ieuan later approaches Ystradyfodwg and finds no familiar priest functioning, the failure feels personal rather than abstract.

Whether Meurig truly died can remain unresolved permanently.

---

# The first rumour-carrier — unnamed traveller

**Outcome:** unknown

This person appears before the local outbreak becomes obvious, likely in **April or very early May 1349**.

He carries fragmented news from the lower Glamorgan/Llantrisant/Cardiff direction.

He should not deliver a perfectly accurate epidemiological report.

He knows things such as:

- whole households have died elsewhere;
- burials are becoming difficult;
- servants/labourers are moving;
- priests are dying;
- people disagree about causes;
- some routes/markets are disturbed.

He then leaves the story.

This preserves the feeling that news travels through people, not omniscient narration.

---

# A surviving scavenger — first post-isolation stranger

**Working name:** **Goronwy ap Tudur**  
**Age:** around 30  
**Role:** survivor from outside Ieuan's immediate cluster of holdings

**Outcome:** survives at least late June 1349

Ieuan encounters Goronwy around **21–24 June** at or near an abandoned holding.

Goronwy is not automatically evil.

He is hungry, wary and already accustomed to taking abandoned property.

## Scene purpose

Both Ieuan and Goronwy initially treat the other as a potential threat.

Goronwy may:

- demand to know whose land Ieuan belongs to;
- laugh bitterly at the idea that the dead still care who owns a pot;
- warn Ieuan about other survivors;
- attempt to claim an animal Ieuan considers his/neighbour's;
- leave rather than fight because neither benefits from injury.

This is the moment Ieuan learns:

> plague survival does not automatically make living people friends.

Goronwy should not become the long-term mentor.

---

# Community state by early July 1349

From Ieuan's point of view:

| Person | Ieuan's knowledge |
|---|---|
| Dafydd | dead |
| Angharad | dead |
| Gwenllian | dead |
| Madog ap Rhys | dead |
| Nest ferch Hywel | fled; unknown |
| Elen ferch Madog | fled; unknown |
| Gruffudd ap Ieuan | absent; unknown |
| Meurig ap Cynan | missing/presumed dead |
| Goronwy ap Tudur | alive when last seen |

The important emotional result is **not** 'everyone is dead.'

It is:

> Ieuan no longer knows where anybody belongs.

The stable social map of childhood has vanished.

---

# Future-use rules

- Nest/Elen may reappear later, but no reunion is mandatory.
- Gruffudd is deliberately preserved as a potential later mirror of Ieuan.
- Meurig's actual fate need never be confirmed.
- Goronwy may reappear once, but should not become an adventure companion unless the story later earns it.
- New named survivors must not repopulate the valley too quickly; first-half loneliness still matters.

## Evidence grade

All character outcomes are **C — fiction**.

The pattern of uneven mortality, flight, abandoned holdings and property transfer/scavenging is grounded in **A/B historical plague evidence** from medieval Wales.
